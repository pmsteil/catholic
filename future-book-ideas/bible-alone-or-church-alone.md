# Future Book Idea: *Bible Alone or Church Alone?*

**Working Title:** Bible Alone or Church Alone?
**Genre:** Catholic apologetics / biblical theology
**Target Audience:** Cradle Catholics seeking deeper roots; Protestants open to following the evidence

---

## Core Thesis

The Protestant instinct — *Scripture alone, no human authority* — and the Catholic claim — *Christ established a living Church to guard and transmit His truth* — are not arbitrary disagreements. They trace back to a single unbroken biblical thread that runs from Moses to the papacy. The question is not "Bible or Church?" but "What did the Author of the Bible actually build?"

---

## The Central Thread (Origin: Chapter 6 of *What Is Love?*)

The idea surfaced while working on the *emet* (Hebrew: truth/faithfulness) section of the Truth chapter. Deuteronomy 32:4 calls God *El emet* — "The Rock... a God of faithfulness." The "Rock" is a recurring OT divine title (Deut 32; Ps 18:2; Ps 19:14; Is 26:4).

Paul makes the Christological turn explicit: **"the Rock was Christ"** (1 Cor 10:4) — retroactively applying the OT divine Rock-title to the pre-incarnate Son.

Then Jesus gives that same title to Simon: **"You are Peter (*Kephas* — Rock), and on this rock I will build my Church"** (Matt 16:18). Catholic exegetes read this not as a nickname but as Jesus sharing His own divine attribute — a participation in what He *is*.

The chain closes with Paul calling the Church **"the pillar and foundation of truth"** (1 Tim 3:15) — Rock-language applied now to the institution built on the rock.

**The full chain:**

> God = The Rock / *El emet* (Deut 32:4)
> → Christ = The Rock / "I am the Truth" (1 Cor 10:4; John 14:6)
> → Peter = named "rock" by Christ, sharing His authority (Matt 16:18)
> → Church = pillar and foundation of truth (1 Tim 3:15)

This is not scattered proof-texting — it is one continuous narrative from Deuteronomy to Vatican I. It shows that the Catholic claim to authoritative teaching is not an addition to Scripture; it is the conclusion Scripture itself is driving toward.

---

## Key Patristic Anchor

**St. Leo the Great** (Pope, 440–461): Peter's authority is a *participation* in Christ's own — not a separate power but a share in the one authority of the Son. What Christ is to the universal Church, Peter is to the College of Apostles. Leo's language is almost mystical: the office outlasts the man because the authority belongs first to Christ.

## Potential Chapter Arc

1. **The Problem** — Why do Christians disagree about authority at all? (The Reformation rupture)
2. **The Rock in the Old Testament** — God as *El emet*, the unshakeable ground of truth (Deut 32; Psalms; Isaiah)
3. **The Rock Was Christ** — Paul's Christological reading of the OT Rock (1 Cor 10:4; 1 Pet 2:4–8)
4. **Christ Names His Rock** — Matthew 16:18 in full context; Aramaic *Kephas*; the keys of the kingdom (Is 22:22)
5. **The Church as Pillar of Truth** — 1 Tim 3:15; the Magisterium as servant of truth, not master of it
6. **Sola Scriptura on Its Own Terms** — Where did the canon come from? Who decided which books are Scripture?
7. **The Two Books** — Scripture and Tradition as the two modes of the one Deposit of Faith (Vatican II, *Dei Verbum*)
8. **Leo the Great and the Living Office** — How the authority of the Rock passes through time
9. **The Question Every Protestant Must Answer** — If not the Church, who guards the truth?
10. **An Invitation** — Not "you were wrong" but "come and see what was always there"
