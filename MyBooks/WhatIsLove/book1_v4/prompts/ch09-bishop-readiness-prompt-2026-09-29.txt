KNOWN ITEMS FOR CH. 9
1. Payment language. Ch. 9 is where it is thickest: "paid" ×20, "pay" ×9, "debt" ×7, "transaction" ×3, "payment" ×2, "performed" ×2 (a banned word). Read each in context; some say what sacrifice is *not*. Ch. 8 now states the Cross as satisfaction that love gives its power (CCC 615–616), as expiation and oblation (CCC 433, 614), and ties it to the Day of Atonement and the mercy seat (Lev 16:14–16; Rom 3:25). Align with that. Cross-reference it; don't repeat it. Line 131, "And every bit of it is paid," is the first test.
2. The Ch. 8 Bridge (chapter_08.md:311) still says sacrifice "proves love is real" and "paying the price it demands." Fix both: payment language, and the proves/shows question settled in Ch. 2 (Ch. 9:71 has the same "proves love real"). This is the one edit in chapter_08.md; it goes in the same pass.
3. Todo 43: chapter_09.md:43 inherits the Aquinas overreach fixed in Ch. 5 ("sacrifice is what makes it *for another*, willed at cost to me"); *alicui* carries no notion of cost. Todos 45 (the retranslation "to will good to another") and 44 (sacrifice vs. self-gift) touch the same sentence. Fix line 43 to match Ch. 5 now. For 44 and 45, tell me what Ch. 9 needs and whether the book-wide decision should be settled here, before we edit.
4. Divine simplicity (todo 61), caution 1. Sacrifice is not said of the divine essence the way truth, justice and mercy are. In the Trinity there is total self-gift without loss; sacrifice is what that self-gift becomes in a fallen world, supremely at the Cross (Ch. 3:133). Test the title's "Eternal Self-Gift" and every sentence about sacrifice *in God* against that. Don't overcorrect into "sacrifice is God's essence."
5. The Mass. "Why We Have Mass" must say plainly that the Mass makes present the one sacrifice of the Cross and does not repeat it (CCC 1366–1367; Trent XXII). Ch. 10 carries the Eucharist as communion; don't pre-empt it.
6. Martyrs section. Verify every fact: Stephen; Polycarp; Ignatius; Maximilian Kolbe; the 21 Coptic martyrs of Libya (2015; entered in the Roman Martyrology in 2023). Tertullian's "the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church" is the traditional paraphrase of *Apologeticum* 50 ("semen est sanguis Christianorum"). Present it as such.
7. Marriage and family sections ("What Children Know," "Sacrifice vs. Selfishness: The Destruction of the Family," "The Grain That Refuses to Fall: Barrenness and Betrayal," "The Difference Between Sacrifice and Self-Destruction"). They must match Chs. 7–8: separation may be permitted for grave danger or adultery while the bond remains (CCC 1649; can. 1151–1153, in the cache). Sacrifice never means staying in harm's way. Never charge a motive, and remember the parent reading it (entry 42). Nothing may imply that a couple with infertility is barren in love (CCC 1654).
8. Alignment with Ch. 8, so the chapters don't repeat. Ch. 8 now uses Rom 12:21 in the Divine Mercy Chaplet paragraph. Ch. 8's counterfeit-mercy and empathy passage now cites Leo XIV and Barron ("To love is to will the good of the other"). Ch. 8's "When the Thread Is Cut" has "Mercy without sacrifice." Ch. 7's Binding Check closes on Matt 23:23, which Ch. 9's "Disciple at the Teacher's Feet" also uses. Keep each chapter's use distinct.
9. Robustness and progression (open since the April 20 review). Ch. 9 has 24 ## sections. Check whether each does distinct work and where repeated treatment can be joined without weakening the chapter. Propose, don't cut, and keep my phrases.
10. Footnotes. Only one note. Every quotation from a magisterial document, a saint or a modern author gets a numbered note, as in Chs. 7–8.
11. Em-dashes (todo 30). Ch. 9 is the book's worst case (319). After the doctrinal redlines are written, one redline per the todo's method: convert roughly a third to a half, keeping the dash where it marks a real interruption, and never swap in hyphens.
