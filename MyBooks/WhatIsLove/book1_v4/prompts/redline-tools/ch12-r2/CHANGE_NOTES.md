## (a) Plan

The frame stays as Patrick approved it: the H1, both epigraphs, "The Question That Won't Go Away" (line 23's gravity/lawgiver premise untouched), "The Classical Moral Argument" with its syllogism and the CCC 31–36 sentence, the Divine Command section, Natural Law, Integration, Personal Encounter, and everything from "The Law of the Gospel" through the Bridge (byte-identical). Inside the argument, the premises are now taken in the order that makes the honesty possible. **Premise 2 comes first**, because almost everyone lives by it. Its facet paragraphs keep every original sentence and gain the lab's concrete data (A1–A7). These are: bound to the truth; wrong for everyone, which no consent can make right; the betrayed dead friend and the guilt that faces a person; the surgeon and the five, with the line the Church holds as natural law (EV 57, 65); and the equal worth of the infant no one holds. The Ratzinger/EV paragraph now closes Premise 2, because it answers the one who denies that premise. **Premise 1 follows**, stated plainly as "where the real debate lies". It is defended the lab's way: by comparison and best explanation, not by "every attempt fails". It gives one steelmanned paragraph each to the materialist and debunker, the "we make our own values" family, the utilitarian, respect for persons (Kantian/contractualist, absorbing the old "noblest attempt" sentence), and the godless realist (conceded parity, "reach and fit, not a refutation"). The Eastern traditions get one short paragraph under *Nostra Aetate* 2 and LG 16. "Why Only God Can Ground Love" becomes **"Where the Roads Part"**: five short subsections for the five places the rivals stop (the infant no one holds; a law that calls each of us by name; the law and the mind that reads it; guilt that asks to be forgiven; the longing). Patrick's approved paragraphs (orig 95, 99, and most of 77) were moved there, unchanged or minimally fixed. "The Atheist's Dilemma" is replaced by **"The Weight on the Other Side"**, which treats evil, hiddenness, the unbaptized infant, hell, 1 Samuel 15 in the lab's exact formula, and Patrick's invitation. The syllogism's conclusion moves after that section as **"Conclusion: Therefore, God Exists"**, so the verdict comes after both sides have been weighed. The overclaims the brief lists are all gone. So are the repeated gravity/lawgiver lines (orig 27, 133, 137, 140 callout); line 23 is kept, and orig 77 keeps one gravity contrast that argues rather than repeats.

**Touched, with the reason** (in each case the point is kept and only what is wrong was fixed):
1. **Orig 27** (R1 CCC 31/35 sentence). "it requires a lawgiver" became "it points to a Lawgiver". The old wording repeated line 23's approved premise and claimed proof where the chapter now offers a converging case. The CCC 31 clause gained "which 'allow us to attain certainty about the truth,' though they are not proofs of the kind the natural sciences give". As it stood, the half-quotation underclaimed against CCC 31 (CC-6). The CCC 35 clause is unchanged but now begins its own sentence.
2. **Orig 47's lead-in.** The paragraph's "This" pointed back to orig 45 ("every attempt … fails"), which is cut. Three new sentences on the relativist now stand before it, so the approved text reads verbatim.
3. **Orig 77** (R1 "sacrifice offered"). Three fixes:
   - (i) "The crucial question: If these facets are built into us" became "If the facets of love are built into us". The sentence now opens a subsection.
   - (ii) "and to refuse the Lawgiver you must explain away every layer of that order at once;" became "The one who refuses a Lawgiver must take each layer of that order as given on its own;". The realist does not explain the order away; he takes it as given (v13 C).
   - (iii) "An impersonal ground might explain a law of things; it cannot explain a law whose entire content is love." became "An impersonal ground might account for a law of things; a law whose entire content is love points to Someone who loves." "Cannot explain" overclaims against the sacred-order and realist rows; v13 W3 says only a person can love us.
4. **Orig 81.** The first sentence opens the new Conclusion; its last three sentences close it. Both parts are verbatim. The middle is new, because the conclusion is now a weighing.
5. **Orig 103** (not recorded as Patrick's, listed for completeness). "And it proves something real—that we cannot help *treating* ourselves as precious. But a necessity of our practice is not yet a truth about the world" is kept in the respect-for-persons paragraph. "only a ground outside the practice can close that gap" is replaced by what those views take as given.
6. **Orig 107**, after its first sentence (R1 approved). "The atheist's stops at moral facts that simply exist—uncommanded, unexplained, hanging in mid-air" is removed; no realist would sign it (v13 C). "a personal Good explains at one stroke the three things an impersonal fact never touches: why the law obligates…" is kept in point at new 91. "never touches" and "why the law obligates" go: the realist and the Kantian do explain obligation, and on guilt God "answers but does not explain better" (v13 C last row).

8. **Orig 119** (1b, "bind all people in all circumstances"). "Atheists can recognize that murder is wrong, but, as we have just seen, they cannot ground that wrong in anything with the authority to bind all people in all circumstances." became "Atheists can recognize that murder is wrong, and the best of them hold that it binds all people in all circumstances. "asks us to believe that objective moral truth emerged from mindless matter and that universal obligations arose from evolutionary accidents" became "asks us to believe that mind, meaning and the moral law are, at bottom, matter in motion". The usual materialist denies objective moral truth, so he would not sign the old description. "When you weigh it honestly, that takes far more faith than belief in God ever could—…" is cut (brief-listed overclaim). Patrick's sentence on the materialist is untouched and now ends the paragraph.
