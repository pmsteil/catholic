# Premise 2: Love's Failures, Sinners and Saints

## How the Section Would Work

Premise 2 would open by quoting back the Introduction's first sentence, which already names what is breaking: "the lies, the partiality and cruelty, the indifference, the breakdown of social cohesion and the family."

Each facet of love then gets three things. First, the urgency: what a society becomes when that facet is lost. Second, a sinner: a well-known failure that believer and unbeliever condemn alike. Third, a saint: someone who lived the opposite. The reader judges the sinner and the saint with the same certainty, and neither judgment is a matter of taste. That is the objective argument. The urgency makes it personal.

The sinner and saint in every pair are ones an atheist would judge as we do, so Premise 2 holds. The urgency lines are the author's warning about where a society goes, and an atheist may dispute some of them (abortion, euthanasia). They are kept anyway, because that warning is the point.

## Truth: The Lies

**Urgency.** When truth is lost, a society stops asking the one question that matters: *what is it?* From conception, the child in the womb is a new, living human being. The honest debate is whether the mother's rights outweigh the right to life of that innocent human being. A society that will not face that question honestly has ended an estimated sixty million lives in America alone, the figure Chapters 15 and 16 also use.<sup>1</sup> Most who take part are sincere, like the young mother in Chapter 1. And we have seen where it leads when a society decides that some human beings don't count. Everyone knows what Hitler's lie did.

**Sinner.** Adolf Hitler. His lie was that the Jew is not fully human: a "subhuman" with no claim to the dignity of a person made in the image and likeness of God. The same lie is told about the unborn child: *it is not a human being.*

**Saint.** St. Gianna Beretta Molla (Italy, died 1962; canonized 2004). A doctor and mother who, pregnant and gravely ill, refused treatment that would have ended her baby's life, and died a week after giving birth. She answered "what is it?" with her life: *my child.*

## Justice: The Partiality and the Cruelty

**Urgency.** When justice is lost, the strong take what they want and the weak become property. About 28 million people worldwide are trapped in forced labor today, and more than 6 million of them in forced sexual exploitation.<sup>2</sup> If justice is only what the powerful can get away with, none of them is wronged.

**Sinner.** Jeffrey Epstein, who trafficked underage girls for years, shielded by money and powerful friends.

**Saint.** St. Josephine Bakhita (Sudan and Italy, died 1947; canonized 2000). Kidnapped as a child, she was sold and resold, and beaten. Once freed, she became a nun, forgave her captors, and is now the patron saint of trafficking victims.

## Mercy: The Indifference

**Urgency.** When mercy is lost, the helpless become a burden, and a society that sees them as a burden soon finds a way to be rid of them. It starts with indifference: we film the drowning man instead of jumping in. It ends by calling death mercy. Canada legalized assisted death in 2016 for the dying. By 2023 it was about one death in twenty, and the law has been widened to people whose natural death is not reasonably foreseeable.<sup>3</sup>

**Sinner.** Jamel Dunn, a 32-year-old disabled man who drowned in a Florida pond in 2017 while teenagers filmed him, laughed, and called no one. Police called their conduct inhumane but not illegal.<sup>4</sup>

**Saint.** St. Teresa of Calcutta (India, died 1997; canonized 2016). She picked the dying up off the streets so they could die loved, and took in abandoned babies, so that the unwanted were held instead of thrown away.

## Sacrifice

**Urgency.** When sacrifice is lost, the children pay first. Divorce became an option before sacrifice did, as Chapter 9 showed. Nearly one child in four now grows up without a father in the home.<sup>5</sup> Ask any child of divorce who paid for the adults' freedom. This needs no theory: almost everyone knows a child who carried it, or was one.

**Sinner.** The no-fault divorce revolution. In 1969 California became the first state to let one spouse end a marriage without naming any wrong, and within about fifteen years nearly every state had followed. The governor who signed it, Ronald Reagan, is reported by his son Michael to have called it one of the worst mistakes of his political life.<sup>6</sup> Even the man who signed it saw what it cost.

**Saint.** St. Monica (North Africa, 4th century). She stayed faithful in a hard marriage to a hot-tempered, unfaithful husband and won him to the faith before he died. Then she wept and prayed for her wayward son for seventeen years, until he became St. Augustine.

**Caution to write in.** The Church does not ask anyone to stay in danger; separation is permitted when there is abuse (CCC 2383). Monica's sacrifice was faithfulness, not enduring harm, and the text should say so in one line.

**Duplication.** The book already treats divorce three times. Chapter 9 uses it as the example of sacrifice failing ("Divorce becomes an option before sacrifice does"), Chapter 15 calls it "our divorce culture," and Chapter 16 gives rows to "No-Fault Divorce" and "Divorce with Children." Here it would be a short callback whose job is different: to show that this loss is a wrong everyone feels. The Reagan detail and St. Monica as a model of faithfulness in marriage are new to the book. Chapter 13 mentions Monica only as Augustine's praying mother.

## Covenant, Life-giving, and Communion: The Breakdown of the Family

**Urgency.** When the covenant is lost, life stops being welcomed. Birth rates have fallen below replacement across most of the developed world, the "demographic winter" Pope Francis has warned of.<sup>7</sup> A people that no longer binds itself to anyone stops making room for anyone new.

**Sinner.** China's one-child policy (1980 to 2015). For thirty-five years a state rationed children, enforced with forced abortions and sterilizations. One demographer estimates that about 20 million girls went missing between 1980 and 2010,<sup>8</sup> and a generation grew up with no brothers, sisters or cousins. China ended the policy in 2015.

**Saints.** Sts. Louis and Zélie Martin (France, 19th century; canonized together in 2015, the first married couple canonized together). A watchmaker and a lacemaker, they were faithful to each other, welcomed nine children, buried four, and raised St. Thérèse of Lisieux. They are the covenant lived whole, the opposite of a state rationing children.

**Set aside.** The Ulma family (Poland, killed 1944; beatified 2023). Józef and Wiktoria hid eight Jews in their farmhouse and were killed for it with all seven of their children, including the baby Wiktoria was carrying, who was beatified with them. They tie back to Truth: an unborn child beatified, killed by the lie that some human beings don't count.
