## The Question That Won't Go Away

**1.** "and in Chapter 11 how prudence…"
- Problem: The verb is left out ("we've seen"), so the ear loses the thread. Commas restore the pause the missing verb needs.
- Judgment.
- Old: `and in Chapter 11 how prudence puts it into practice in the hardest cases.`
- New: `and, in Chapter 11, how prudence puts it into practice in the hardest cases.`

**2.** "love's objectivity, its binding demands, its sacred character"
- Problem: These are abstract nouns. "Objectivity" is also used before it is defined.
- Must-fix.
- Old: `Everything we've explored—love's objectivity, its binding demands, its sacred character—only holds if God actually exists.`
- New: `Everything we've explored—that love is real for everyone, that its demands bind us, that it is sacred—only holds if God actually exists.`

**3.** "the entire architecture collapses. The question is not peripheral…"
- Problem: This is wordy. "This is not a detour" has already said "not peripheral". "Architecture" is abstract where the foundation image wants a building.
- Must-fix.
- Old: `If that claim is wrong, the entire architecture collapses. The question is not peripheral: it is the question on which everything else depends.`
- New: `If that claim is wrong, the whole building falls. Everything else depends on this question.`

**4.** "an objective law with the characteristics we've discovered"
- Problem: "With the characteristics we've discovered" is filler. "Objective" carries the whole argument (Premise 1 and Premise 2) and needs a plain definition here, at its first formal use.
- Must-fix.
- Old: `if love exists as an objective law with the characteristics we've discovered, it points to a Lawgiver.`
- New: `if love is an objective law—true and binding whether anyone agrees or not—it points to a Lawgiver.`

**8.** "The Church holds that line at every number…"
- Problem: Three clauses are piled on one another ("and even against…", "and she teaches…", "which reason itself can know"). This is also the first use of "natural law", and it is glossed in catechism phrasing.
- Must-fix.
- Old: `The Church holds that line at every number, and even against a person's own request to die, and she teaches that it belongs to the natural law, the moral order written on every human heart, which reason itself can know (*Evangelium Vitae* 57, 65).`
- New: `The Church holds that line at every number, and even against a person's own request to die. She teaches that it belongs to the natural law: the right and wrong God wrote into human nature itself, which reason can know on its own (*Evangelium Vitae* 57, 65).`

**9.** "would let a dying man ask for death"
- Problem: The phrase is imprecise. The rival position is not that a man may *ask*, but that his request may be *granted*. Its defenders would sign the new wording.
- Must-fix.
- Old: `many who would never hang the innocent man would let a dying man ask for death.`
- New: `many who would never hang the innocent man would grant a dying man's request to die.`

**12.** "This is the full philosophical weight of what we called the *dictatorship of relativism*…"

- Must-fix.
- Old: `This is the full philosophical weight of what we called the *dictatorship of relativism* in Chapter 5, on how love binds, and traced through truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice in Chapters 6–9—Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger's warning, on the eve of his election as Pope Benedict XVI, of a relativism "that does not recognize anything as definitive and whose ultimate goal consists solely of one's own ego and desires."<sup>1</sup>`
