# Censor's Report: Chapter 12, "The Lawgiver Is Love" (proposed text)

## Scope

**What I read.** All of `/tmp/claude-0/ch12/chapter_12.proposed.md`, every sentence, from the two epigraphs through the Further Study lists and Notes 1–2. I spent the most time on the material from "## The Question That Won't Go Away" through "## From Moral Argument to Personal Encounter". Per the brief, I opened nothing else under /tmp/claude-0.

**What I checked it against.**
- Sacred Scripture (RSVCE).
- The *Catechism*, using the vatican.va archive pages fetched this session.

- *Evangelium Vitae*, *Veritatis Splendor*, *Dignitas Infinita*, *Verbum Domini*, and John Paul II's General Audience of 28 July 1999.
- St. Thomas: ST I q.20 a.2; I-II q.91 a.2, q.94 a.5, q.100 a.8; II-II q.64 a.6, q.171 a.5. I read these on newadvent.org (Benziger).

## Bottom line

**No Errors.**

Nothing in the chapter is contrary to faith or morals.
- **1 Samuel 15.** The chapter states St. Thomas's position accurately, calls it a permitted and undefined opinion, rules out voluntarism, and closes the door to any present-day claim of such a command.
- **Forgiveness, satisfaction and reparation.** These are sound in substance.
- **Hell, grace and freedom.** These stay within Trent VI and CCC 1033–1037, and the chapter respects the limits the Church set on the *de auxiliis* controversy.
- **The unbaptized infant.** This matches CCC 1257 and 1261 exactly.
- **Inviolability of life.** This is correctly called natural law.

There are **18 points that Need care**. They should be fixed before print. Four of them go to reason's reach (CCC 31–38, *Dei Filius*): findings 1, 2, 3 and 4. One is a real omission in the handling of 1 Samuel 15: the Magisterium's own guidance on the "dark passages" (DV 15; VD 42).

**Checked and sound (OK).** These were the passages most likely to draw a censor's pen.

- **1 Samuel 15 and St. Thomas.** The quotation of I-II q.100 a.8 ("admit of no dispensation whatever") is verbatim. The account of ad 3 is accurate: the precept forbids *undue* killing; all die by God's sentence "on account of the sin of our first parent"; the agent is "the executor of that sentence by Divine authority". The parallels at q.94 a.5 ad 2 and II-II q.64 a.6 ad 1 check out.
- **Abraham.** The use of II-II q.171 a.5 is accurate ("most certain of the Divine revelation").
- **Labelling and safeguards.** The chapter labels the Thomist position as permitted and undefined. It explicitly rejects voluntarism ("God does not make a wrong right by willing it"). It excludes any present claim of such a command (CCC 67, 2258).
- **Forgiveness and satisfaction.** "God alone forgives sins" (1441), sin as "before all else an offense against God" (1440), satisfaction after Baptism made through Christ (1459–1460), and reparation even after forgiveness (2487) are all correct. Temporal punishment (1472–1473) is not named, but nothing denies it.
- **Evil.** CCC 309, 310, 311, 312, 314, 324 and 412 are quoted or paraphrased accurately. "Suffered in His own flesh" keeps divine impassibility intact. CCC 29 and 847 are used in a balanced way on hiddenness, and the chapter does not presume the guilt of seekers.
- **Unbaptized infants.** CCC 1257 and 1261 are quoted exactly. The chapter states a hope, not a certainty, and says nothing against the theological opinion of limbo.
- **Hell, grace and Mary.** CCC 1033, 1035 and 1037 are quoted exactly. The Trent VI references (ch. 5, ch. 11, can. 4) are accurate. CCC 490 supports grace bringing about Mary's free assent. "Why one will is won and another refuses … the Church does not explain" respects the limits Paul V set in 1607 on the *de auxiliis* dispute. The General Audience of 28 July 1999 is quoted accurately.
- **Inviolability of life.** "At every number" and "against a person's own request" are correctly placed in the natural law. Double effect is stated soundly ("side effects may be weighed, but persons may not be spent"). Nothing touches legitimate defense or the death penalty in a way that conflicts with CCC 2263–2267.
- **Reason's reach.** Apart from findings 1–4, the treatment is balanced and Catholic:
  - CCC 31, 35, 36, 37 and 156–159 are used accurately.
  - The epigraph is Romans 1:20.
  - "Not because the evidence is insufficient" agrees with *Dei Filius*.
  - "An honest reader may weigh it all differently" is covered by LG 16 ("without blame on their part") and CCC 847 and 1861.
- **Rivals.** These are described charitably and, apart from findings 12 and 13, accurately. "They are not saying relativists are wicked; most are not" is exemplary.
- **Salvation of non-Christians.** CCC 846, 847, 848, 1257 and 1260 and LG 16 are all quoted exactly. Pelagianism is excluded ("That love does not save by itself; it is grace already at work").

## 3 Verdict

- **As the chapter stands:** I would **withhold the *nihil obstat* for the present**. Nothing is contrary to faith or morals. But findings 1–4 (reason's reach against *Dei Filius* and CCC 31–38) and finding 11 (the missing magisterial framework for 1 Samuel 15) are exactly the passages a diocesan censor is appointed to correct. Several smaller points (5, 7, 8, 14, 16) could be read amiss.
- **With the eighteen fixes above:** I **would grant the *nihil obstat*** to the whole chapter. Before print, EV 57, 65 and 66 and the Benedict page number should be rechecked against the full texts, since the fetches this session were cut off or unavailable.
