## Bottom line

**No Errors.**

Nothing in the chapter is contrary to faith or morals. The hard places are handled within what the Church allows:
- 1 Samuel 15, which the chapter labels as a permitted Thomist opinion, not defined doctrine;
- hell, which keeps the self-exclusion of CCC 1033–1037 and leaves *de auxiliis* undecided;
- the unbaptized, where the chapter stays exactly at CCC 1261;
- other religions, handled under NA 2 and LG 16, with no presumption of guilt.

There are **24 Needs care** findings. The weightiest are:
- N14 and N15: *Dei Verbum* 11 is cut so that inerrancy can be read narrowly, and inspiration is defined loosely.
- N8: satisfaction is defined without its expiatory sense.
- N16 and N17: the necessity of the Church and of faith is weakened by partial quotation of CCC 846 and 848.
- N4: "could have been otherwise" can be read against the freedom of creation.
- N12 and N13: the definitions of mortal sin and private revelation are incomplete.
- N3: divine command theory is defined loosely.

**Passages examined and found sound.** These are listed so that later readers do not reopen them.

- **Reason's reach.**
  - The chapter's "weighing" sits alongside CCC 31, 36, 37–38 and *Dei Filius* ch. 2, stated in full in the Conclusion.
  - "None of them is a proof that compels … Taken together, they converge" matches CCC 31's "converging and convincing arguments".
  - Saying evil "weighs against" the conclusion is framed within reason alone, and faith's greater certainty is affirmed (CCC 157, 159).
  - Nothing here underclaims, given N23.
- **1 Samuel 15.**
  - The Thomist account is quoted accurately: I-II q.100 a.8 and ad 3; q.94 a.5 ad 2; II-II q.64 a.6 ad 1; q.171 a.5.
  - It is labelled as an opinion the Church has not defined.
  - The chapter holds that God does not make wrong right by willing it, so it is not voluntarist.
  - The line about the innocent stays absolute for us (CCC 2258; EV 57).
  - DV 12, DV 15 and VD 42 are used accurately, given N14 and N15.
- **Hell.**
  - CCC 1033, 1035, 1037 and 1058 are accurate.
  - Trent VI ch. 5 and 11 and can. 4 are accurate.
  - Mary: CCC 488 ("free co-operation") and 490 ("wholly borne by God's grace", "free assent of her faith") support the sentence.
  - The *de auxiliis* question is rightly left undecided.
  - The 1999 General Audience is quoted accurately.
- **The unbaptized.** CCC 1257 and 1261 are accurate and stay exactly within the Church's hope.
- **Forgiveness.**
  - CCC 1440, 1441, 977, 1484, 1497, 1452, 1458 and 2487 are all accurate.
  - "Takes away the whole guilt … without waiting on her pardon" is sound.
  - Reconciliation with the one wronged is rightly left as hers to give.
- **Other religions.** NA 2 and LG 16 are quoted verbatim. The "flight to God with love and trust" is NA 2's phrase about Hinduism, and the chapter rightly says "other traditions". The chapter presumes no one's guilt (CCC 847, 1861).
- **Natural law.** The definitions at the "Sacrifice" paragraph and in the Natural Law section are adequate (CCC 1954–1955; EV 57). St. Thomas I-II q.91 a.2 is verbatim.

## 3 Verdict

**As it stands:** I would grant the *nihil obstat*. Nothing in the chapter is contrary to faith or morals, and every opinion it adopts is marked as an opinion.

**With the fixes:** I would grant it without reservation. I would make N14–N17 (inerrancy, and the necessity of the Church and of faith), N8 (satisfaction) and N4 (the freedom of creation) conditions of printing. The rest should also be corrected before print.
