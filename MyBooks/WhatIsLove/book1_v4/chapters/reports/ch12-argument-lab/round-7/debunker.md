# Round 7 (final): Moral Error Theory + Evolutionary Debunking + Cognitive Science of Religion vs. v7

## 1. School and voice

This is the same school as Rounds 1–6. It draws on the error theorists (Mackie, Joyce, Olson), the debunkers (Street, Ruse, Greene, with Clarke-Doane on what a debunking argument must show), and the cognitive science of religion (Barrett, Boyer, Bering, Kirkpatrick, Norenzayan). Our claim is that morality claims a categorical authority that would fit a Lawgiver, that neither the authority nor the Lawgiver exists, and that evolved minds in evolved cultures explain why we believe in both. This round checks whether v7 took my three Round 6 repairs, and whether its new wording (the conclusion's two contrasts, A4's parenthesis, the A6 Christ and Kolbe sentence, the care-of-the-dying sentence in W1(2), the CCC 2258 sentence, the Evil section's "faith adds") broke anything.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Justice.** Nothing is owed categorically. Justice is a set of conventions among creatures whose sympathy is limited (Hume, Mackie). We keep it as a useful fiction (Joyce) or drop it (Garner). → **Coherent; borrows nothing.**

**2. Hard case.** A dying man volunteers for a lethal experiment that would save thousands. We let him go ahead, because welfare and his own choice both point that way. → **Coherent; borrows nothing.** A6 still records this view fairly ("would let ... a volunteer give his life to an experiment") and labels its own answer "this book's claims."

**3. Equal dignity.** We deny it as a truth and keep it as a commitment that came late, mostly through Christianity. → **An honest denial.** The symmetry of genealogies binds us. If that origin discounts equal worth, it discounts our will to truth too (Nietzsche row).

**4. Ultimate good.** There is none. Our working ideal is lucid truthfulness. → **It breaks at "ultimately,"** and it leans on a norm of inquiry the school cannot ground (A7, W2). Unchanged since Round 3.

### 3b. Exchanges

**Exchange 1: The conclusion's two contrasts, tested for validity.**
*Opponent (Singer; McMahan's time-relative-interest account):* Last round the premise varied strength and held mind fixed. Now it has two contrasts. The strong newborn differs from her in strength only. The grown man differs in strength and in mind. If she was wronged no less than the first, worth does not follow strength. If no less than the second, worth does not follow mind. So far it is valid. But the elimination is not complete. All three can suffer, so "what she could feel" survives. On Marquis's view, her loss of a future is greater than the grown man's, which would make her wrong *greater*, not equal. So "it follows what she is" is not the only ground left standing.
*Reply:* Granted, and v7 does not claim otherwise. It says "and then, *this book answers*, it follows what she is." The strict inference ends at "never followed her strength or her mind." What follows the comma is offered as the book's answer, not as what the elimination forces. The suffering route is met in W1(2) ("others can always suffer more"), and Marquis's view grants the premise. It says she was wronged at least as much, which still rules out graded worth by mind.
*Rejoinder:* Then the only open premise is the Moorean one: that leaving her was no lesser wrong than leaving the grown man. I deny it.
*Reply:* And the conclusion asks you exactly that: "which you are surer of ... or any argument that says ... it was a lesser wrong." It "cannot compel" you, and says so.
**Result: Held.** The step is now valid as far as it claims, and labeled where it goes further. The Round 6 Serious item (Realist) and my Minor item are closed. There is one read-aloud point: "though she was weak and not yet able to know herself" now comes after "a grown man left there to die," so "she" must reach back past two other people (§5 Minor 1).

**Exchange 2: Is the equal-wrong datum debunked? (new, prompted by the grown-man contrast)**
*Opponent (Street's Darwinian dilemma; Daly and Wilson, and Hrdy, on infant abandonment, paraphrased, verify):* The new contrast puts the weight on one intuition: abandoning a weak newborn is no lesser wrong than abandoning a grown man. Evolution explains well why we care about the grown man, through reciprocity, coalitions and kin. It also explains why many societies abandoned weak infants when resources were short. So the equal-wrong intuition is not innate. It is cultural, and its cultural cause is known: the Christian norm, which spread for reasons of group success (Norenzayan, paraphrased, verify). A belief with a known cause that is unrelated to its truth is debunked.
*Reply:* Notice first what you have conceded. Of all A's data, this is the one that selection least predicts. A4 says so itself: "where instinct fails, for the infant no one is drawn to hold." So the biological half of the debunking story does not reach it. That leaves the cultural half. Here A's own standard applies. A belief is defeated when we are shown that "what produced the belief had nothing to do with its truth." Cultural transmission through a tradition that claims to have *seen* the truth is testimony, and testimony can track truth. Group success explains why the norm spread, not why it was held. The fairness rule (C, opening; Nietzsche row) binds both sides: the same ancestry belongs to the will to truth that drives your argument.
*Rejoinder:* So we are at parity on genealogy again.
*Reply:* Yes, at parity, and the conclusion asks only which you are surer of.
**Result: Held at parity.** This is a standoff, not a defect. If anything, the two-contrast form helps the book against this school. The datum it now isolates is the one that is hardest to explain as an adaptation.

**Exchange 3: "Whatever voice he claims to have heard in His name" (W1(2), CCC 2258) and the cognitive science of religious voices.**
*Opponent (Luhrmann; Barrett on agency detection, paraphrased, verify):* Minds like ours hear voices and sense agents where none are. v7 now says that no one, "whatever voice he claims to have heard in His name," may claim the right to take an innocent life. Good. But the same paragraph says that "He who gives life ... may call it home." Then the rule has a gap. If God has that right and voices claiming His name cannot be checked, the absolute stands on an epistemic accident (Genesis 22).
*Reply:* The sentence closes the gap rather than opening it. What God may do in His own providence, by natural death, is not a right He delegates to any human claimant. CCC 2258 says no one may "claim for himself" the right directly to destroy an innocent. The text applies this exactly where your science says it should apply, to voices. That is the same epistemic humility Kant urged about Abraham: a man can be surer that he must not kill his son than that the voice is God's (paraphrase, verify). For the Church the Akedah ends in the ram, and Hebrews 11:19 reads it as faith in the resurrection, not as a license. The conquest texts remain on the weight side (the Evil school's standoff).
*Rejoinder:* So your rule holds even against your own religious experience.
*Reply:* Yes. That is why it is a rule of justice and not of private revelation.
**Result: Held.** The new sentence concedes to CSR what it should and gives up nothing.

**Exchange 4: W3's Smith sentence, as repaired.**
*Opponent (Kirkpatrick):* "That tells us how we long, not whether there is Someone to long for, and the longing alone settles nothing." That is the concession I asked for. Smith is now the subject of the next sentence, and "a longing to be judged truly could be explained that way." Then: "It does not as easily explain a longing to be told, before we have earned anything, that it is good that we exist." "It" now most naturally means "that way," Smith's account. That is true: the impartial spectator concerns praiseworthiness, not unearned acceptance. A reader could still stretch "it" back to the science two sentences earlier.
*Reply:* Even on that stretched reading, the clue no longer rests on it. The concession "the longing alone settles nothing" comes first, and the paragraph closes with "A longing does not prove its answer" and "That is not evidence." The comparative is a description of Smith's reach, and the case puts no weight on it.
**Result: Held.** The optional tightening ("Smith's account does not as easily explain") is under §5 Minor 2.

**Exchange 5: A6 on Christ and Kolbe, re-tested against Greene.**
*Opponent (Greene; also Mackie on ad hoc distinctions):* "Accept the death that comes ... but he may not aim at his own death." When Christ "laid down His life of His own accord," death was the very means of redemption. If it was not aimed at, the line between aiming and accepting is drawn after the fact to fit the cases you want.
*Reply:* Greene's general point was answered in Round 2 (Berker: the neural story needs a normative premise that is the very point in dispute). On the case: CCC 609 says Jesus "freely accepted" His Passion and death. Out of love He embraced the Father's will to save, and the killing was the act of others, "those who wrongly kill him." v7's verb ("accept") is the Catechism's own. Kolbe aimed at saving Franciszek Gajowniczek; the Nazis did the killing. Above all, A6 labels the whole teaching "this book's claims ... not shared ones," and it now puts the dissenters' own question in their own voice ("why may he not ask for death?").
*Rejoinder:* The line still looks thin to me.
*Reply:* A6 lets you say so. "Many who hold the rest of A would let a dying man ask for death."
**Result: Held.** The new wording is more accurate than v6's ("as Christ did" placed Christ among those who "bear the death" from "those who wrongly take it," which blurred John 10:18). Now it is exact.

**Exchange 6: A4's parenthesis and the A heading, checked against their labels.**
*Opponent:* A4 keeps "nor 'for his own good' without his asking," which still suggests that asking might make it right. The A heading now says many "deny the Church's teaching that the line holds at every number and against a man's own request." Do the labels still agree?
*Reply:* The parenthesis now states the Church's denial outright ("which the Church denies (CCC 2277; *Evangelium Vitae* 65–66)") and sends both questions, numbers and asking, to A6 "as this book's claims." The heading names the same two. "Without his asking" is now plainly the shared floor (no non-voluntary killing "for his own good"), and Singer, who denies even that for some newborns, is listed in the heading as denying "parts of ... A4." A4, A6, W1(2), the heading and the God row agree.
**Result: Held.** One read-aloud point: the nested parenthesis "(... which the Church denies (CCC 2277; EV 65–66), A6 names ...)" is hard to read aloud (§5 Minor 3).

**Exchange 7: "Faith adds" in the Evil section, tested by Mackie.**
*Opponent (Mackie):* "Faith adds that He permits no evil from which He will not draw a good." This cannot be falsified. Any evil you cannot explain gets a promised good you cannot see.
*Reply:* That is why v7 puts it under "faith adds" and keeps it apart from "so far as reason can see." The case for reason does not use it. The text still says "not seeing God's reasons is weak evidence that He has none. It is not no evidence," and "whether it is worth this world's cost is part of the weighing, not settled by it." CCC 312 blocks the instrumental reading: the evil never becomes good, and the good "is never the evil's wage."
*Rejoinder:* So my 1955 argument stays on the scale.
*Reply:* It does, and v7 counts it there (Mary, CCC 491–493, 412).
**Result: Held at parity.** The new sentence is labeled correctly, and "God says no to the wound and yes to her" is a gain.

**Exchange 8: The source-connection test, carried over a last time.**
*Opponent:* Does any new v7 sentence lean on a seeming that our genealogy would discount, against C's fairness rule?
*Reply:* The new seemings are the equal-wrong datum (Ex. 2, which the conclusion offers only as a Moorean question), Smith's Judge (now "the longing alone settles nothing"), and the hope for the unbaptized infant (CCC 1257, 1261, labeled as hope). The steps that do the work are inferences from A, judged on their premises. W2's reply to me ("he does not distrust the proof") is unchanged and still at parity.
**Result: Held.**

**Minor (wording only; the case rests on none of them):**

**Doctrinal note for the censor (not this school's domain, flagged for accuracy only).** W1(2): "the ordinary care owed to the sick, food and water among it, may not be withdrawn." CCC 2279 says ordinary care "cannot be legitimately interrupted." The CDF's 2007 responses on nutrition and hydration, which *Samaritanus Bonus* follows, call food and water "in principle" ordinary and proportionate, and so obligatory for as long as they achieve their proper purpose (hydrating and nourishing the patient). The CDF allows exceptions where they can no longer be assimilated or would cause significant physical discomfort. As written, the sentence could be read as admitting no exception. Verify against *Samaritanus Bonus* V.3 and the CDF 2007 Responses on vatican.va. A possible wording is "... food and water among it, so long as they nourish and do not burden, may not be withdrawn." I leave that to the censor.

## 7. New insights and lines worth keeping

**Insights:**
- **One contrast for each ground eliminated.** The conclusion now does what an argument by elimination must do, and it marks where elimination ends and the book's answer begins. This is the model for every "therefore" in the chapter.
- **The two-contrast form isolates the datum hardest to debunk.** Selection explains our care for the grown man and predicts discounting the weak infant. So the claim that she was wronged *no less* is the very belief evolution least explains. Against this school, that is the strongest place the case could end.
- **A rule that holds against your own religious voices is a rule of justice.** "Whatever voice he claims to have heard in His name" concedes to the cognitive science of religion what it should, and costs the case nothing.
- **Keep "so far as reason can see" and "faith adds" apart.** The Evil section now does this in so many words. It is the honest form of theodicy for a reader like Mackie.
- **Across seven rounds, the largest gain against this school was consistent labels.** A4, A6, W1(2), the God row, the heading and now the conclusion all agree on what is shared, what is this book's claim, and what is faith.
