# Round 1 synthesis — v1 against 11 schools (2026-10-18)

**Scores (as written → with that school's repairs):** Realist 6→8, Debunker 6→8, Aristotelian 6→8, Kantian 6→8, Contractualist 5→7, Utilitarian 6→8, Expressivist 5→7, Nietzschean 6→8, Buddhist 5→7, Confucian 6→8, Evil 4→7. Mean 5.6 → 7.6. No school found a Fatal objection to the case as a whole; every school found Serious faults in v1's wording.

## What every school agreed on
1. **W1 is invalid as written (11 of 11).** "A claim no human can cancel was not laid by any human, so Someone laid it" does not follow. Each school filled the gap differently: the neighbor holds the claim (contractualist); reason legislates it (Kant); the relationship lays it (Confucian); the nature roots it (Aristotelian); no one lays it, it simply holds (realist); impersonal karma (Buddhist); attitudes (expressivist); a human legislator (Nietzsche). **And the release test proves necessity, not personhood:** by Aquinas God cannot dispense from the Decalogue either (ST I-II q.100 a.8).
2. **What survives and wins at the edges:** the *relational* shape of wrongdoing. A wronged person needs forgiving, not just correcting ("worse vs. wronged"); guilt toward the dead or the unknown victim has no human left to forgive; a standing that is "ours but not ours to cancel" looks received; every wrong also offends God (CCC 1850), and only He can forgive without injustice because only He can make the victim whole. Anscombe's fork (keep morality as law and accept a Lawgiver, or drop "law" and keep only excellence of a kind) frames it honestly.
3. **A3 conflated two things.** Victims can and should forgive. What no one can do is make the wrong right, or consent it into being right. "Forgiveness heals the bond; it does not remake the law."
4. **The A heading overclaimed.** Nietzsche, Singer and the error theorists honestly deny A2, A4 or A6. New frame: what almost everyone finds they cannot give up, with the cost of giving each up shown.
5. **A4 (equal dignity) is our strongest ground and was underused.** Capacity grounds are unequal; kind grounds borrow "what we are for"; Kantian, contractualist and utilitarian views each fail at the newborn, the profoundly disabled or the dying. Reason can see equal dignity (DI 1), but history shows how easily it is lost (Seneca approved infant exposure), as CCC 37–38 teaches.
6. **C straw-manned or omitted the strongest rivals.** Missing: reason alone (Kant), what we owe each other (contractualism), well-being (utilitarian), sentiment (expressivist), an impersonal sacred order (Confucian Heaven, Stoic Logos, karma), power and rank (Nietzsche), and nothing binds (error theory). "Brute moral facts explain at most A1" was false: they *accommodate* much; the honest line is accommodate vs. explain. God is also a stopping point.
7. **Evil got one line.** The problem-of-evil school scored v1 lowest (4). C must give evil and hiddenness real weight, say what our answers do and do not cover (free will answers the evil we do, not the fawn in the fire), and show why the weight does not overturn the case.
8. **W2 overreached.** "It is luck" is answered in part by the third-factor reply; "most surely" is false to history; mercy-to-enemies can be explained as herd self-protection. Rest W2 on truth (we want truth even when a lie would comfort and no one would know) and on the dilemma: either the human form is a passing product (and A1 becomes "wrong only for creatures shaped like us") or there is an eternal order of ends. W2 reaches one Source; personhood is carried by W1 and W3. "Never contradict" becomes "never require us to do evil."
9. **A5 (Patrick's distinction) holds, refined.** The Aristotelian showed Aquinas puts the "for" *inside* our nature as an inclination (ST I-II q.91 a.2; q.94 a.2). So: what we are able to do does not settle what we ought to do; the ought lives in what we are for; the real question is who made us for it.
10. **A7/W3 are a clue, not a premise;** hiddenness must be faced inside W3 (CCC 37, Isaiah 45:15, LG 16, "a light without yet knowing its name").

## The four standing challenges, across schools
- **Starting point for justice:** strongest secular answers are the contractualist (what no one could reasonably reject) and Confucian (relationships); both assume equal standing rather than derive it. Hume admits creatures too weak to make us feel their resentment are owed gentleness, not justice. Nietzsche: justice starts in exchange between rough equals; the weak are owed nothing.
- **Hard cases:** most schools resolve cases coherently, often reaching the book's verdicts; several (Foot, utilitarian) permit euthanasia or the clean-case torture/transplant. They differ at the limit case, not the ordinary one.
- **Equal dignity:** the decisive divider. Denied (Nietzsche, Singer, error theory), asserted as brute (realist), assumed (Rawls, Scanlon), or grounded in kind membership, which borrows teleology (Aristotelian, Kantian repairs).
- **Ultimate good:** coherent but bounded everywhere (eudaimonia within a life, nirvana with no one at the end, primary goods, amor fati willing every evil again); several reach for eternity at their summit (Aristotle NE X.7; "all joy wants eternity").
