# Argument Lab, Round 6: Utilitarianism / Consequentialism vs. v6

## 1. School and voice

The opponent speaks for realist, impartialist consequentialism at full strength: Sidgwick's axioms, Hare's two levels, Parfit's Triple Theory, de Lazari-Radek and Singer, Railton's sophisticated consequentialism, and Hooker's rule consequentialism, with threshold deontology called in as a neighbour. This round checks whether v6 closed my two Round 5 Serious objections (A4's "not for the many" on the killing clause; the Kolbe line) and my four Minors, and whether v6's new wording broke anything. The new wording is A4's split clauses and parenthesis, A6's intention-based statement with *Evangelium Vitae* 66, and the God row.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges (short)

1. **Justice:** each counts for one, and justice is the set of rules that protect the most vital interests. **Coherent and borrows nothing. It breaks** where "owed" comes second to "best."
2. **Hard case:** the hijacked airliner over the stadium: shoot it down. **Holds as a decision.** It is a parity case, since Catholic moralists divide on it. The school pays its price at the secret transplant.
3. **Equal dignity:** equal *consideration*, yes. Equal *worth* whatever one's capacities, no. **Breaks at the ground, honestly.** W1(2) and the conclusion answer this directly.
4. **Ultimate good:** well-being, seen impartially. **Coherent, but thin at the summit.** Impartial reason can see that each one counts. Only a person can want each one (W3).

## 3. Debate highlights

The killing clause reads "never, if innocent, to be deliberately killed for others' sake," and the parenthesis sends the numbers to A6 by name: "Whether that line holds however vast the numbers … A6 names as this book's claims." - **S2 (the Kolbe line): Fixed**, by a different route from mine. It also keeps the authority clause in its last words: "no one may kill him because he asks." Christ and Kolbe are now named as cases of the permitted act, not counterexamples to it. Closed (Exchanges 2–5 test it).
- **Minor 1 (A6 "since" clause; "make his death right"): half taken.** "Make it right to kill him" is in. The single "since" still gives the firefighter as the reason for both dissents (carried, Minor).
- **Minor 2 (C, utilitarianism: "as if they had no exceptions" vs. Hooker's disaster rule): not taken** (carried, Minor; Exchange 7).
- **Minor 3 (God row overstated what threshold rivals share): Fixed.** It now reads "one person may not be spent for others' totals, which many of the best rivals share and rest on a standing they take as given," and the exceptionless line is kept separately as "as the Church teaches and this book argues."
- **Minor 4 (flourishing row): Fixed.** It now reads "as though, the Church would say, a person owned his life … (CCC 2280; this book's claim, A6)."
- **A heading (add those who let the line bend at vast numbers): not taken.** Now that A4 itself names the numbers question as A6's claim, this has dropped from part of a Serious item to a Minor consistency point (Minor 4 below).

**Exchange 1: "Not for the many" on the mere-means clause: the terror bomber.**
*Utilitarian:* In my Round 5 note I warned that the mere-means clause is not wholly safe either. A terror bomber uses civilians' deaths as a means to make a people surrender, and so to save many. Plenty of people who defend Hiroshima would deny "never used as a mere means … not for the many."
*Defense:* The parenthesis comes after the whole list, and "that line" covers both the mere-means clause and the killing clause. "The many" is the five, the crowd, the town. "However vast the numbers" is the city and the war, and A6 calls that the book's claim. Read in order, the passage says: most hold it for the many; whether it holds at any number is argued later.
*Result:* **Held as written.** The reading is natural, and it matches A6's "Yet even those who would cross the line for a city will not cross it for five."

**Exchange 2: Did Kolbe "make his own death the thing he chooses"?**
*Utilitarian:* Kolbe stepped forward knowing that the only outcome was death by starvation. If foreseen certainty counts as choosing, the new sentence condemns him again.
*Defense:* The test of what a will chooses is what it would count as success. Suppose the guards had accepted the swap and then let all ten live. Kolbe would have had everything he wanted. What he chose was the other man's life. His own death was what unjust men did to him, and the sentence says exactly that: "bear the death that comes, even at the hands of those who wrongly take it." Now suppose a lethal experiment's subject survived. The data the experiment needed would be lost. His death is part of the means.
*Result:* **Held.** The counterfactual test sorts the firefighter, Kolbe, the lethal-experiment volunteer and the euthanasia request correctly.

**Exchange 3: The closeness problem returns, since v6 rests the line on intention.**
*Utilitarian:* In Round 5 (Exchange 4) I said that an intention-based line runs into double effect's closeness problem (Hart; Foot's fat man in the cave). The volunteer says he intends the data, not his death. v6 chose intention anyway.
*Defense:* v6 does not rest the line on intention alone. Its last clause, "no one may kill him because he asks," binds the one who kills, whatever the volunteer intends. W1(2) grounds that clause: "no number of others has any right over her life," and her life is "given to her keeping and not to her disposal." So the researcher is forbidden on grounds of authority, and the closeness problem reaches only the edges of the volunteer's own guilt. That standoff belongs to every double-effect ethic, and the book does not claim to settle it.
*Result:* **Held at parity.** The sentence now has both grounds, which is better than my authority-only wording.

**Exchange 4: Kolbe asked them to take him. Then "no one may kill him because he asks" makes his request an invitation to a wrong.**
*Utilitarian:* The guards killed Kolbe *because he asked* to take Gajowniczek's place. By v6's own clause they did wrong, and he asked them to do it. Then a priest who takes a prisoner's place in a lethal Nazi experiment is just as heroic. So why is my volunteer in a sanctioned trial forbidden?
*Defense:* Two points. First, the clause binds the one who kills, and the guards did do wrong; nothing in the sentence denies that. Kolbe's request added no killing to the world. It turned a killing already decreed away from a father and onto himself, and what his will sought was the other man's life. Second, the priest in the Nazi experiment is a Kolbe case, and the book would honour him. What is forbidden is not dying in an experiment. It is a researcher claiming the right to kill because the subject consented. The line runs through the right, not through the setting.
*Result:* **Held.** The sentence says this as it stands: "at the hands of those who *wrongly* take it."

**Exchange 5: Captain Oates, St. Apollonia, Samson.**
*Utilitarian:* Here are the stock cases against "may not make his own death the thing he chooses." Oates walked into the Antarctic blizzard to stop slowing his companions. Apollonia leapt into the fire prepared for her, and the Church venerates her. Samson pulled the temple down on himself.
*Defense:* Oates: what freed the party was his absence, not his death; had he somehow survived apart from them, his aim would have been met. Moralists dispute the case, and it is a standoff of the same kind as Exchange 3. Apollonia and Samson: the tradition has long answered these by a special divine prompting (Augustine, *City of God* I.26; ST II-II q.64 a.5 ad 4). That answer can be contested, but it is the Church's old answer to old cases, not a new fault in v6.
*Result:* **Held at parity.** None of these is a defect of the text as written.

**Exchange 6: "Those who wrongly take it, as Christ did" against the book's own "given, not taken."**
*Utilitarian:* A6 says Christ bore the death that came "at the hands of those who wrongly take it." Evil, *Second* says "What Christ gave was given, not taken," and cites John 10:18 ("No one takes it from me"). A hostile reader will set the two side by side.
*Defense:* Both are true. Acts 2:23 says He was killed "by the hands of lawless men," and John 10:18 says it was not taken against His will. But the shared verb invites a gotcha for no gain.
*Result:* **Held; Minor** wording (§6).

**Exchange 7: The C row for this school still says "as if they had no exceptions" for rule consequentialism.**
*Utilitarian:* That is Hare's critical/intuitive split. Hooker's ideal code (*Ideal Code, Real World*, 2000) includes an overriding rule to prevent disaster, so rule consequentialism holds its rules firmly but expressly *with* an exception. The book's brief asks us to flag where a school is misstated, and this is a small misstatement.
*Result:* **Held; Minor** (§6). The rest of the row is exact: CCC 1754, "in principle … (A4, A6)", which is now fair because A4 no longer claims the line at every number, the A2 letter, Sidgwick, and de Lazari-Radek and Singer.

**Exchange 8: "Without it, no innocent is safe from being spent."**
*Utilitarian:* A threshold deontologist keeps every innocent safe below the threshold.
*Defense:* "Safe from being spent" means safe at any number. On a threshold view, each innocent can be spent in principle once the stakes are high enough. That is what the sentence says, and the sentence before it ("The absolute can cost lives, even many") pays the honest price.
*Result:* **Held as written.**

**Exchange 9 (brief): A4's new "nor 'for his own good' without his asking."**
*Utilitarian:* Singer (*Practical Ethics*) and Hare defended some non-voluntary euthanasia of severely disabled newborns.
*Defense:* The A heading already says "Singer denies parts of A4." The clause leaves the case where the person asks to the parenthesis and A6.
*Result:* **Held.** It is consistent with the parenthesis and with A6.

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: NONE.**

Both of my Round 5 Serious objections are closed. A4 no longer claims the line at every number as shared; it sends the numbers to A6 by name. A6 no longer condemns Kolbe or Christ. It names them as the permitted case, states the forbidden case by what the will chooses, and keeps the authority clause ("no one may kill him because he asks") that keeps the volunteer case out of the closeness problem. v6's new wording broke nothing this school can press to Serious. I tested it against the terror bomber, the closeness problem, Kolbe's own request, Oates, Apollonia and Samson, the threshold "no innocent is safe," and Singer on non-voluntary euthanasia (Exchanges 1–5, 8, 9).

**Minor**
1. A6: "since he may lay down his life for others, as a firefighter does" still gives one reason for two different dissents. Those who accept the dying man's request reason from mercy, not from the firefighter. (Carried from Round 5, half taken.)
2. A6: "even at the hands of those who wrongly take it, as Christ did" sits against Evil, *Second*'s "What Christ gave was given, not taken" (John 10:18). Both are true, but the shared verb invites a gotcha (Exchange 6).
3. C, utilitarianism: "hold them as if they had no exceptions (Hare's two levels; rule consequentialism)" fits Hare. Hooker's rule consequentialism keeps an overriding rule to prevent disaster (Exchange 7; carried).
4. A heading: "which A6 names as this book's claim" (singular) covers only chosen death. A4 now says "this book's claims" (plural), covering the numbers too. The heading should match.
5. (Optional, typographic.) A6: "a wrong done to *her*" has no feminine antecedent inside A6, whose cases are a patient, an innocent man, a prisoner and "a man." This is harmless given the book's habit of "her" for the victim, and it was already present in v5.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**

- The closeness problem for double effect at its edges (Oates; the volunteer's own guilt). A6's authority clause and W1(2) keep the researcher's act out of it.

## 7. New insights for the book

- **The success test is the plainest way to tell Kolbe from the volunteer.** Ask what each would count as success if he lived. If the guards had spared all ten, Kolbe would have had everything he wanted. If the experiment's subject survives, the data is lost. That one question, which a reader can ask of any case, does the work of the double-effect machinery without its jargon. It could go in Chapter 9 (sacrifice) with a cross-reference from Chapter 12.
- **v6's A6 now has both grounds, and that is its strength.** The will's choice ("may not make his own death the thing he chooses") answers for the one who dies. The trust ("no one may kill him because he asks"; W1(2)) answers for the one who kills. Each covers the other's hard edge: intention alone meets the closeness problem, and authority alone says nothing about suicide by one's own hand.
- **The A–W–C seam held this round.** Every "never," "every" and "not even" in A is either shared or sent to A6 by name. That is the check v6 says it ran, and it shows.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "He may give his life for another; he may not make his own death the thing he chooses."
- "No one may kill him because he asks."
- "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent."
- "What protects her is not a weight that enough lives could outweigh; it is that no number of others has any right over her life."
- "Given to her keeping and not to her disposal."
- "The trust forbids that her death be the thing aimed at." (shorter: "The trust forbids aiming at her death, not easing her dying.")
- "His life kept A7 better than his theory could" (Sidgwick).
