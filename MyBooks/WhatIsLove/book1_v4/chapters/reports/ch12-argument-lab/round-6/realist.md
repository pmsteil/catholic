# Argument Lab, Round 6: Non-Naturalist Secular Moral Realism vs. v6

## 1. School and voice

Godless normative realism: Parfit (*On What Matters*), Enoch, Shafer-Landau, Wielenberg, Huemer, with Fine on essence and necessity, directed duties (Darwall, Wallace, Thompson), Kamm and Nagel on the shape of constraints, and McMahan as this school's voice on the moral status of infants. Moral truths are objective, necessary, known by reflection, and need no God. This round the school checked its eight Round 5 repairs (N1–N8) against v6 and tested the new wording the assignment names, plus three new phrases v6 added on its own.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

1. **Justice.** What each person is owed, much of it owed *to* her, fixed by necessary truths about persons. *Holds; it posits its ground.*
2. **A hard case.** Re A (the conjoined twins): Mary may not be killed as a means; Jodie is not bound to keep sustaining her. *Coherent; it borrows justice and mercy in all but name (Rom 2:14–15).*
3. **Equal dignity.** Kind-based essentialism (worth follows the rational kind), or, for McMahan's wing, it is denied: infants are wronged, but less than persons. *The first holds but posits the measure; the second is consistent and pays A4.* This school's split on this point is what Exchange 6 turns on.
4. **Ultimate good.** An objective list lived with integrity; death ends it. *Coherent for a life; breaks on unity, and on the child who died before any of it reached her.*

### v6's new wording under pressure

**Exchange 1: the Kolbe sentence. Does the intention line survive the realist's next case? (Held)**
*Realist:* Grant Kolbe: he chose to take a man's place, and his death was his captors' act. Now take a volunteer in a certainly lethal experiment whose death is foreseen but not aimed at, for example a vaccine trial that will kill him as a side effect. By your intention test he does not "make his own death the thing he chooses." Is he Kolbe?
*Defense:* No, for two reasons the sentence already carries. First, the experimenter's own act causes the death, and "no one may kill him because he asks" binds the one who acts, not only the one who asks. With Kolbe, no one acted on his request; the killers acted on their own wicked will. Second, CCC 2295, cited in the same list, forbids exposing even a consenting subject to disproportionate or avoidable risk to life, so the certain-death trial fails on proportion before intention is reached.
*Realist rejoinder:* Then the line is two tests, intention and who does the act, and the sentence names both. That is coherent. It remains the book's claim, and the text labels it so.
*Result:* **Held.** No repair. The sentence also honors the Church's martyrs, which "hand it over" did not.

**Exchange 2: "gave her life to her keeping and not to her disposal" and "remains its sovereign Master." Does Master language bring back a property wrong? (Held, at parity)**
*Realist:* In Round 5 I welcomed v5 for dropping possession language about God. v6 restores "sovereign Master" (CCC 2280 does say this). If her life is His and she is its keeper, then killing her looks like a trespass against the Master, and the directed wrong to *her* becomes secondary, which is the very thing Thomson and the directed-duty theorists object to in property accounts of the right to life.
*Defense:* The text closes that door three ways. (a) The order of explanation is unchanged: "The wrong is wrong because of what it does to her, and so it offends Him who loves her" (W1 closing), and guilt is "owed to her, truly and in her own right, and not to God alone" (W1 frame). (b) The Master "willed her for her own sake ... and never spends her as a means." The keeping is *for* her; it is not a lease from a landlord. (c) Master language does only one job here: it explains why the line is not hers to waive (keeping, not disposal), which no directed-duty account explains. It does not explain why others may not kill her; that is done by "no number of others has any right over her life," which is a claim about her standing.
*Realist rejoinder:* So the Master explains non-waivability, and her own standing explains the constraint on others. That division is clean. Whether a life willed for her own sake may still not be ended by her own considered judgment is the old standoff (does this life bound her good?), now narrowed by CCC 2278–2279 to its true size.
*Result:* **Held, at parity.** The new wording is also more precise than v5's "holds her life for her, never against her," which the Aristotelian rightly said a beneficiary could use to claim the trust.

**Exchange 3: the God row's "rest on a standing they take as given." (Held)**
*Realist:* Not every rival rests the rule on a standing. Foot rests it on doing versus allowing; some say it is "simply what justice is" (W1(2) says so itself).
*Defense:* Foot grounds doing versus allowing in the difference between negative and positive rights, which is a claim about what persons may demand, that is, a standing. "Simply what justice is" is the bare form of the same stopping point. W1(2) gives the full list; the God row compresses it fairly, and "many of" leaves room for those, Parfit among them, who do not hold the rule strictly.
*Result:* **Held.** No repair.

**Exchange 4: "no number of others has any right over her life." Does it clash with "side effects may be weighed"? (Held)**
*Realist:* The book allows proportionate side-effect harm (A6). In a just war the many may act in ways that foreseeably kill her. Then they do have something over her life.
*Defense:* They have a right to act, not a right over her life. A right over her life is a right to dispose of it, to use her or to aim at her death; that is what "spent" means, and what A6 and W1(2) both forbid. Side-effect harm takes nothing as theirs. "No one may take what is not his to take" is Nagel's and Kamm's agent-centered constraint, given a ground instead of posited as a status.
*Realist rejoinder:* Kamm would say her inviolability *is* the ground. You say it is a gift. Each is a stopping point.
*Result:* **Held, at parity** (the standoff already recorded: posited standing against given standing).

**Exchange 5: two phrases v6 added unannounced. (Held)**
(a) *A4: "nor 'for his own good' without his asking."* Shared by nearly everyone. It is denied for infants by McMahan and Singer (non-voluntary euthanasia of newborns in severe untreatable suffering), but Singer is named in the A heading as denying parts of A4, and the list is illustrative. The "without his asking" implicature is caught by the parenthesis, which names the Church's teaching on a requested death as the book's claim. **Held.**
(b) *W1(2): "no voice that claims His name can make it ours to take (CCC 2258)."* A Wielenberg-style test: does the line have an exception for a genuine divine command (Abraham)? Aquinas holds that one who kills at God's command does not sin, because the death is God's sentence and the agent His instrument (ST I-II q.100 a.8 ad 3; II-II q.64 a.6 ad 1). The sentence survives that: on Aquinas's account the taking is still God's, never "ours," and "claims His name" points at pretenders, as CCC 2258 ("no one can under any circumstance claim for himself the right ...") does. **Held.** A note for the censor, not this school: if the book ever discusses Abraham, it should use Aquinas's "God's sentence" explanation so this line is not read as denying it.

**Exchange 6: the conclusion's "wronged no less." Does the new comparison reach the view it was written to answer? (Failed as written: Serious, one-clause repair)**
*Realist (McMahan's voice):* Round 5's synthesis made this fix to answer graded views, and named Singer and McMahan among them. v6 asks whether the abandoned infant was "wronged no less than a strong child would have been, though she was weak and not yet able to know herself." I grant that at once. On my account, the time-relative interest account, a healthy newborn and a weak one are both wronged, and wronged roughly alike, because neither is yet psychologically connected to her future. What I deny is that either is wronged as gravely as you would be, since you are a person and fall under the morality of respect, and they do not yet. So the comparison you chose holds mind fixed: the strong child is also "not yet able to know herself." It varies only strength. From "no less than a strong child," it follows that her worth never followed her strength. It does not follow that it "never followed ... her mind," and it does not follow that "she is that as fully as you are." The concessive "not yet able to know herself" names her lack of mind, but nothing in the comparison tests it.
*Defense:* The conclusion is a best-explanation appeal, not a deduction. Strength, mind and relations are the candidate grounds; relations are ruled out because no one was drawn to hold her; and a worth grounded in sentience would make her equal to a puppy, not to you.
*Realist rejoinder:* That last step is not in the text. In Round 5 I let it carry the bare "If she was wronged," and I was too generous: the defense was mine, not the book's. Now that v6 has made "no less than" the measure, the gap is exact. The one reader the paragraph addresses ("the one who gives up A4") is Singer's or McMahan's reader, and the comparison offered is one he grants. The "If ... then" therefore fails against exactly him. The fix is one clause: add a comparison that holds kind fixed and varies mind. Then he has to choose, and the text already gives him the honest exit ("or that it was a lesser wrong"). That is a fair standoff.
*Result:* **Failed as written. Serious, because it is the last step of the case and it is invalid against its named target. One-clause repair (R1).** It is the remainder of Round 5's Serious item 3, on the axis Singer and McMahan actually grade by. It is not a new line of attack, and it is not a regression: v5 had the same gap more loosely, and I missed it.

**Exchange 7: SCG III.122, checked again.** I still could not reach a critical text. A secondary source (Aleteia, 2025) gives the same sentence as *Somme contre les Gentils* III, c. 122, so the chapter attribution is confirmed at the secondary level. The chapter's subject (why simple fornication is a sin, that is, a sin that seems to harm no one else) still needs checking before the line is used for the victim's good. **Held; verification note stands.**

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**Fatal against v6 as written:** none.

**Serious against v6 as written:** one.

- **S1-R6. Conclusion, the "no less" comparison holds mind fixed.** The only comparison offered is with "a strong child," who is equally "not yet able to know herself." A reader on McMahan's time-relative interest account, or Singer's person/non-person line, grants that the weak infant was wronged no less than the strong one, and holds that both were wronged less than a person would be. For that reader, "her worth never followed ... her mind" and "she is that as fully as you are" do not follow. He is the reader the paragraph names ("the one who gives up A4"). The objection is valid, and its premises are true of the views it cites. As written, our side cannot answer it at parity, because the reply that would answer it (eliminate mind as the ground) is not in the text. It does not touch W1(2), A4 or C, which argue the point on other grounds. It touches only the closing inference, and one clause repairs it (R1). It is the remainder of Round 5's Serious item 3 on the second axis, and it is my own correction of Round 5 Exchange 9.

**As written: one Serious item (S1-R6). Not NONE.** With R1 applied, this school would report NONE.

**Minor:** none against the text. For the censor, not this school: (a) if the book ever discusses Abraham, it should give Aquinas's "God's sentence" account (ST I-II q.100 a.8 ad 3) so that "no voice that claims His name" is not read as denying it; (b) SCG III.122 still needs a critical text, and the chapter's subject (fornication) should be checked before the line is applied to the victim's good.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- *The dead:* the tragic view (a real debt, forever unanswered) against "she is still someone." Guilt's request to be forgiven fits the second better; the first is coherent and honestly held.
- *Where necessity lives:* in essences, nowhere at all (Parfit), or first in a Mind. Our side's edge is breadth (law, natures and minds from one Source), not fewer axioms, and v6 says exactly that.
- *Reach:* reliable general reasoning against the access problem shared with mathematics. "It weighs, it does not settle."
- *Why the line cannot be waived:* the realist allows waiver; the Church does not. "Keeping, not disposal" explains non-waivability only for a reader who grants the datum, and v6 claims no more than that.
- *Posited standing against given standing:* Kamm's inviolability as a status, or "no number of others has any right over her life" because her life is a gift. Each is a stopping point (Exchange 4).
- *The dying woman:* whether a trust that forbids aiming at her death can be for her good. With CCC 2278–2279 the gap is narrowed to its true size: whether this life bounds her good.
- *Whether wrongness binds of itself* or needs a Lawgiver: Anscombe's question, stated openly.
- *Equal worth:* kind-based essentialism posits the measure; the theist gives a reason. Our side leads, narrowly. After R1, McMahan's wing faces an honest choice, not a non sequitur.
- *Natural evil:* the fawn and the parasite. Carried as weight, with the teleological line drawn honestly. Parity only by concession.

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact wording)

**R1. Conclusion.** Replace

> that she was wronged when she was left there, and wronged no less than a strong child would have been, though she was weak and not yet able to know herself; or any argument that says she was not, or that it was a lesser wrong, or that you cannot know it. If she was wronged no less, her worth never followed her strength or her mind.

with

> that she was wronged when she was left there, and wronged no less than a strong child would have been, or a grown man left there to die, though she was weak and not yet able to know herself; or any argument that says she was not, or that it was a lesser wrong, or that you cannot know it. If she was wronged no less than either, her worth never followed her strength or her mind.

Why this works: the strong child holds kind and mind fixed and varies strength, and the grown man holds kind fixed and varies mind. Each eliminated ground now has its own contrast, so the "If ... then" is valid. The next sentence ("Then her worth follows what she is, and she is that as fully as you are") then follows, because the grown man is what the reader is. The graded reader keeps his honest exit ("or that it was a lesser wrong"), and now has to take it openly against a case most readers are sure of.

No other repairs.

## 7. New insights; lines worth keeping

**Insights**
1. **An argument by elimination needs one contrast for each ground it eliminates.** The conclusion eliminates strength and mind, so it needs a case that varies strength with mind held fixed (the strong child) and a case that varies mind with kind held fixed (the grown man). v6 supplied the first. The same check should be run on any "her worth never followed X" sentence in the chapter.
2. **The chosen-death line has two tests, and v6 now states both:** what the will chooses (not his own death) and who does the act (no one may kill him because he asks). Together they handle Kolbe, the firefighter and the side-effect volunteer, with CCC 2295 adding proportion.
3. **"Keeping, not disposal" divides the work cleanly.** The gift explains why the line is not hers to waive. Her standing explains why it binds others. That answers the directed-duty theorist's fear that Master language turns murder into trespass.
4. **"A right over her life" is narrower than "a right to act in ways that cost her life."** Keeping the two apart is what lets "no number of others has any right over her life" sit beside "side effects may be weighed."
5. **"No voice that claims His name can make it ours to take" survives the Abraham problem on Aquinas's own terms.** The taking at God's command remains God's, never ours. This is a better answer to Wielenberg's divine-command worry than most theistic texts give.
6. **Chapter text (open since Round 2):** chapter_12_current.md still has "something only a person can do" and "Only a Someone can." Directed-duty realism grants that persons lay claims, namely the victim. Rework both along the W1 frame when the chapter is redlined.

**Lines worth keeping**
- From v6: "Gave her life to her keeping and not to her disposal." "No number of others has any right over her life." "He may not make his own death the thing he chooses ... and no one may kill him because he asks." "No voice that claims His name can make it ours to take." "Honesty may cost a life but can never finally be the enemy of life." "Wronged no less than a strong child would have been."
- New (with R1): "Wronged no less than a strong child would have been, or a grown man left there to die."

**To verify before use:** SCG III.122 against a critical text (the chapter attribution is confirmed only at the secondary level, by Aleteia 2025; check the context); ST I-II q.100 a.8 ad 3 and II-II q.64 a.6 ad 1 (newadvent) if Abraham is ever discussed; CCC 2295 wording on "disproportionate or avoidable risks" (vatican.va); McMahan, *The Ethics of Killing* (2002), on the time-relative interest account and the morality of respect. All non-magisterial material is paraphrased.
