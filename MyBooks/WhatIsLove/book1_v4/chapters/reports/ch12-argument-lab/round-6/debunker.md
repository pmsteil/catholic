# Round 6: Moral Error Theory + Evolutionary Debunking + Cognitive Science of Religion vs. v6

## 1. School and voice

This is the same school as Rounds 1–5. It draws on the error theorists (Mackie, Joyce, Olson, Streumer), the debunkers (Street, Ruse, Greene, with Clarke-Doane on what a debunking argument must show), and the cognitive science of religion (Barrett, Boyer, Bering, Norenzayan, Kirkpatrick). Our claim is that morality claims a categorical authority that would fit a Lawgiver, that neither the authority nor the Lawgiver exists, and that evolved minds in evolved cultures explain why we believe in both. This round checks whether v6 took my four Round 5 Minor points and whether its new wording (Kolbe, the strong child, Smith's Judge, Mackie's 1955 form, the Neo-Confucian contemplatives) broke anything.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Justice.** Nothing is owed categorically. Justice is a set of conventions among creatures whose sympathy is limited (Hume, Mackie). We keep it as a useful fiction (Joyce) or drop it (Garner). → **Coherent; borrows nothing.**

**2. Hard case.** A dying man volunteers for a lethal experiment that would save thousands. We let him go ahead, because welfare and his own choice both point that way. → **Coherent; borrows nothing.** A6 records this view honestly and still labels its own answer "this book's claims ... not shared ones."

**3. Equal dignity.** We deny it as a truth and keep it as a commitment that came late, mostly through Christianity. → **An honest denial.** The symmetry of genealogies (Nietzsche row) binds us. If that origin discounts equal worth, it discounts our will to truth too.

**4. Ultimate good.** There is none. Our working ideal is lucid truthfulness. → **It breaks at "ultimately,"** and it leans on a norm of inquiry the school cannot ground (A7, W2). This is unchanged since Round 3.

### 3b. Exchanges

**Exchange 1: A4's new placement of "not for the many," tested by the threshold deontologist.**
*Opponent (Moore's threshold deontology; Greene in support):* "Never to be used as a mere means, not for the many and not for the few" still reads as "at no number." Threshold deontologists hold that the constraint against using a person as a means gives way past a catastrophe threshold. A6 says that they are "many." So the absolute has only moved from one clause to the next.
*Reply:* The parenthesis that closes A4 now says, of "that line," that whether it "holds however vast the numbers ... A6 names as this book's claims." On that reading "the many" means numbers like the five and the riot, which the threshold view also refuses. "However vast" is the threshold, and A4 openly sets it aside. The Round 5 problem was a shared premise that silently asserted what A6 calls unshared. Here the premise asserts the shared part and points to A6 for the rest in the same sentence.
*Rejoinder:* "Not for the many" with no limit is still the stronger reading.
*Reply:* Then the parenthesis cancels it, in the same sentence and by name. A reader cannot take A4 as asserting the every-number line, because A4 itself says that is not what it asserts.
**Result: Held.** The fix works. The order of A4's clauses (mere means, then the shared killing clause, then "nor 'for his own good' without his asking," then the parenthesis for the rest) matches A6's labels exactly.

**Exchange 2: The God row, checked against its own labels.**
*Opponent:* Does the God row still take explanatory credit for something it calls shared?
*Reply:* It now credits theism with three things. (a) It grounds "the rule that one person may not be spent for others' totals." The row says many rivals share that rule, and they do. The credit is for the *ground*, which the rivals take as given (W1(2): "a standing they take as given"). (b) Exceptionlessness, "as the Church teaches and this book argues, though not every reader grants it." (c) The longing. None of these is a double count.
*Rejoinder:* "Others' totals" carries no number either.
*Reply:* It says "many of the best rivals," not "the best rivals." So it claims what the threshold views actually grant at their stopping point, and it labels (b) as the book's own claim.
**Result: Held.** The Round 5 self-contradiction is gone.

**Exchange 3: The new consent wording in A6 (Christ, Kolbe), tested against John 10:18.**
*Opponent:* A6 now says a man "may not make his own death the thing he chooses," yet holds up Christ, who said that no one takes His life from Him and that He lays it down of His own accord (John 10:18, which v6 cites in the Evil section). If that is not choosing one's death, the distinction is made to fit. Greene would add that the means/side-effect line is an artifact of how we plan actions.
*Reply:* Greene's point was answered in Round 2 (Berker: the neuroscience needs a normative premise that is the very point in dispute), and I do not reopen it. On John 10:18: what Christ chose was to give Himself to the Father's will for us and not to escape. The killing was done by "those who wrongly take it," which is exactly the clause A6 now carries. A6 separates laying down one's life, which includes not escaping a death others inflict, from making one's death the thing chosen, by one's own hand or another's at one's request. That is the distinction of CCC 2280–2282, and the Kolbe case fits it: he chose to take another man's place, and the Nazis did the killing. Above all, A6 labels this whole teaching "this book's claims, argued in W1(2), not shared ones," so no shared premise rests on it.
*Rejoinder:* A reader may still find the line thin.
*Reply:* He may, and A6 lets him: "many who hold the rest of A would let a dying man ask for death."
**Result: Held.** The new wording is true of the cases it names, and it is labeled.

**Exchange 4: "Wronged no less than a strong child." The repair moves the gap; it does not close it.**
*Opponent (Singer; McMahan's time-relative-interest account):* I accept the new Moorean premise. The weak newborn left on the hillside was wronged no less than a strong newborn would have been. On my view that is true: both can suffer equally, and neither is yet a person. Now take the inference. "If she was wronged no less, her worth never followed her strength or her mind." The first half follows, since the two infants differ in strength. The second half does not. The strong child is also "not yet able to know herself," so the two have the same infant mind, and comparing them cannot show that worth does not follow mind. Then "she is that as fully as *you* are" brings in a third party. The premise compared one newborn with another. The conclusion compares a newborn with an adult reasoner. Everyone in my school, and every graded-worth view, grants the first equality and denies the second. So against the very reader this paragraph addresses, the step is still invalid. Round 5 Ex. 5 made the same point about the route through suffering. v6 fixed the Nietzschean's version (rank by strength) and left mine.
*Reply:* Granted on validity. The honest Moorean question has to stand where the A4-denier stands: would putting her to death without pain have been a lesser wrong than putting *you* to death? He says yes. Then ask which he is surer of: that, or his argument. If it is no lesser wrong, her worth followed neither her strength nor her mind nor what she could feel. Even then the step to "what she is" should be offered as the book's answer, since rivals can name other grounds (Marquis's "future like ours," which would make her death worse than yours, not equal).
*Rejoinder:* With that wording the close is valid and asks for exactly what is in dispute. It will not move me, but it does not argue past me.
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.** It is not Serious, for three reasons. The case's conclusion ("best explanation ... a Lawgiver who is Love") does not rest on this paragraph. The equal-worth case is carried by A4 as a premise and by W1(2). And the conclusion itself says it "cannot compel the one who gives up A4" and "says only" this. But it is the one sentence in v6 that is formally invalid as written, and a graded-worth reader will see it at once. Schools whose whole position is graded worth (Nietzschean, Utilitarian) may rate it higher.

**Exchange 5: Smith's Judge and the science of attachment.**
*Opponent (Kirkpatrick):* The new W3 reads: "Science can trace the paths ... attachment, the wish not to die, a mind that pictures being seen. That tells us how we long ...; Adam Smith traced how ... we appeal to the all-seeing Judge; that explains a longing to be judged truly. It does not as easily explain a longing to be told, before we have earned anything, that it is good that we exist." If "it" means Smith's account, fine: the impartial spectator is about praiseworthiness, not unearned acceptance. But the sentence follows a list that names attachment first, and the natural reading of "it" covers the paths just named. Attachment theory is precisely about a caregiver who accepts the child before the child has earned anything: the secure base. In my account, God is an attachment figure of exactly this kind. So on that reading the comparative claim is what I retracted in Round 5.
*Reply:* The intended "it" is Smith. The fair sentence names him as the subject and then concedes, as W1(1) does of speaking to the dead, that the science can explain this longing too, so the longing alone settles nothing. The paragraph's own weight already matches that concession ("A longing does not prove its answer," "That is not evidence"), so the clue does not depend on the comparative.
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.** This is ambiguity, not a claim the clue depends on. A source note: TMS III.2.33 has the appeal to a higher tribunal when the judge within is shaken by the "man without." C's Smith paraphrase ("when that judge within is shaken") is closer to it than W3's "when every human judge misjudges us."

**Exchange 6: "A wronged person needs forgiving," a third time.**
*Opponent:* A2 and the first sentence of W1(1) still say it. Read in ordinary English ("the car needs washing"), it means the victim needs pardon. A2 is the premise on which "guilt faces a person" rests.
*Reply:* Granted. It is the same one-word fix as last round: "a wrong done to a person needs forgiving."
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.**

**Exchange 7: Mackie's 1955 argument, now in the Evil section.**
*Opponent (Mackie, "Evil and Omnipotence"):* v6 now states my founder's strongest form, and states it against itself. God "kept Mary free of every sin her whole life long" (CCC 493). So a free creature who never sins is possible, and God can bring it about. Then why not everyone? The answer, "Christ's grace gave us more than the devil's envy took away" (CCC 412), is a second-order good that needs first-order evil, and that regress was my reply to the greater-good defense. There is also a tension. If Mary's life was one in which love "could be refused," God gave that good with no actual sin, which is my premise. If it was not, the good of "a life where it could be refused" was withheld from the holiest creature, which makes it an odd good to buy at this world's price.
*Reply:* The text does not claim to answer you. It says "the Church answers *only* with what He brought from it," and it leaves the rest on the weight side: "whether it is worth this world's cost is part of the weighing, not settled by it." CCC 312 blocks the instrumental reading: "the evil never thereby becomes good." On Mary, the Church holds that she was free and sinless by a singular grace given in view of Christ (CCC 491–493). The book claims to know why God gave that grace to one and not to all no more than it claims to know why each evil is permitted. That is where "weak evidence, not no evidence" applies.
*Rejoinder:* So my argument stands, as weight.
*Reply:* Yes, it stands as weight, and v6 counts it there.
**Result: Held at parity.** This is the honesty this school asks for. It is a standoff, not a defect, and v6 states Mackie's argument without misstating it.

**Exchange 8: The contemplatives and expectation-shaped experience.**
*Opponent (Taves; Luhrmann):* Religious experience is shaped by training and expectation. Buddhists find no self, Neo-Confucians find principle, Carmelites find Someone in the dark. This is what the cognitive science predicts if there is no one there to find.
*Reply:* W3 says the same thing in its own terms: "Each tradition can read the other's summit as a stage on its own road; neither reading is evidence, and this book does not count it as such." The new Neo-Confucian sentence adds to the weight against the case; it does not take from it.
**Result: Held.** v6 does not count contemplative report on either side.

**Exchange 9: The source-connection test, carried over.**
*Opponent:* Does any new v6 sentence lean on a seeming that our genealogy would discount, against C's fairness rule?
*Reply:* The new seemings are Confucius's knowing Heaven ("strain ... not that it fails"), Kant's Ruler ("rational faith, not ... evidence"), Smith's Judge (a longing, "not evidence"), and the ancestral rites (the dead "kept by the living," whose memory fails). All are labeled. The steps that do work are inferences from A, judged on their premises.
**Result: Held.**

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: NONE.**

No Fatal or Serious objection stands against v6 as written. My one Round 5 regression is fixed where it did its damage: A4 and the God row no longer state as shared what A6 says is not. The new wording in A6 (Christ, Kolbe), the Evil section (Mackie's 1955 form, CCC 493, CCC 412) and W3's hiddenness passage (the Neo-Confucians) is true of the cases it names, and it is labeled or placed on the weight side honestly. What remains is three wording points. The case does not rest on any of them.

**Minor:**
1. **Conclusion: "wronged no less than a strong child ... If she was wronged no less, her worth never followed her strength or her mind. ... she is that as fully as you are"** (Ex. 4). The premise compares two newborns who share the same infant mind and the same capacity to suffer, so it supports "never followed her strength" but not "or her mind," and it never reaches "as fully as you." Against the graded-worth reader it addresses, the step is invalid as written. It is Minor because the case's conclusion is carried by A4 and W1(2), and because the paragraph says it "cannot compel" this reader. Graded-worth schools may rate it higher.
2. **W3: "It does not as easily explain a longing to be told, before we have earned anything, that it is good that we exist"** (Ex. 5). It can be read as covering attachment research, which explains exactly that longing. It needs Smith named as the subject, plus the concession that the longing alone settles nothing.
3. **A2 and W1(1): "a wronged person needs forgiving"** (Ex. 6). It says the victim needs pardon. This is not taken from Round 5.
4. *Optional:* A2 with a living-victim example (Round 4, item 13).
5. *Source note:* W3's paraphrase of TMS III.2.33 ("when every human judge misjudges us") is looser than C's ("when that judge within is shaken"). Verify before use.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- **Moorean trust against the evolutionary undercutter.** Which is more certain: A1, or a reconstruction of how selection shaped moral belief?
- **Reach and safety.** Projection explains the seeming of universal moral reach as well as it explains the seeming of logical necessity. Darwin's bees vary the part of the law fitted to our kind, not the core.
- **The norm of truth.** We are at parity on belief, and our side has an edge on inquiry.
- **The debt to the dead.** The second road against the third, judged by what is owed. A wish needs no one to answer it; a debt is owed to someone.
- **Genealogy of equal worth, and of theistic belief.** Origin against origin. The fairness rule cuts both ways, and v6 uses it both ways.
- **The absolute.** "What justice is" (Foot) against "held in trust." The case now offers this consistently as conditional.
- **Mackie's free-will objection** (Mary, CCC 412). v6 states it at full strength and answers "only" in part. It stays on the weight side (Ex. 7).
- **Expectation-shaped religious experience.** Neither summit counts as evidence (Ex. 8).
- **The coherent error theorist.** He borrows nothing, and no argument compels him. C states his cost fairly.

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact wording)

**Conclusion.** Replace from "that she was wronged when she was left there, and wronged no less than a strong child would have been, though she was weak and not yet able to know herself;" through "Then her worth follows what she is, and she is that as fully as you are." with:
> that she was wronged when she was left there, and wronged no less than a strong child would have been, though she was weak and not yet able to know herself; and that to put her to death, even without pain, would have been no lesser a wrong than to put you to death; or any argument that says she was not, or that it was a lesser wrong, or that you cannot know it. If it was no lesser wrong, her worth never followed her strength, her mind, or what she could feel. Ask what it does follow, that she had as fully as you. This book's answer is: what she is; and she is that as fully as you are.

Keep "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."

**W3, paragraph 1.** Replace "Adam Smith traced how, when every human judge misjudges us, we appeal to the all-seeing Judge; that explains a longing to be judged truly. It does not as easily explain a longing to be told, before we have earned anything, that it is good that we exist." with:
> Adam Smith traced how, when the judge within us is shaken by the judgments of others, we appeal to the all-seeing Judge; that explains a longing to be judged truly, but Smith's account does not reach a longing to be told, before we have earned anything, that it is good that we exist. The science of attachment may explain even that longing; so the longing alone settles nothing, and it cannot show that no one answers it.

**A2.** Replace "a wronged person needs forgiving." with:
> a wrong done to a person needs forgiving.

**W1(1), first sentence.** Replace "A wronged person needs forgiving, and the pardon that heals the bond between us is hers to give." with:
> A wrong done to a person needs forgiving, and the pardon that heals the bond between us is hers to give.

*Verify before use (all paraphrases):* Smith, *Theory of Moral Sentiments* III.2.33; Mackie, "Evil and Omnipotence," *Mind* 64 (1955); Kirkpatrick, *Attachment, Evolution, and the Psychology of Religion* (2005); Marquis, "Why Abortion Is Immoral," *Journal of Philosophy* 86 (1989); McMahan, *The Ethics of Killing* (2002); Luhrmann, *When God Talks Back* (2012); Taves, *Religious Experience Reconsidered* (2009).

## 7. New insights and lines worth keeping

**Insights:**
- **A comparison class has to match the conclusion.** If the premise compares two newborns, the conclusion can only say what differs between them does not ground worth. To reach "as fully as you," the Moorean question has to compare her with you.
- **To reach the reader who denies A4, the honest question is about the painless death compared with your own.** That is where Singer and McMahan actually stand. Asking it there costs the case nothing and makes the close valid.
- **The label fix now holds everywhere.** A4, A6, W1(2) and the God row agree on what is shared and what is the book's claim. This was the single largest improvement this school saw across six rounds.
- **Stating Mackie against yourself is strength.** The Mary passage concedes his premise and answers "only" in part. That is more credible than a full theodicy would be.
- **Smith's Judge answers worthiness; attachment answers acceptance.** The case's distinction between being judged worthy and being told it is good that we exist is right, but the science covers both, so the clue's weight must not rest on the science failing.

**Lines worth keeping:**
- "He may give his life for another; he may not make his own death the thing he chooses." (Round 5 synthesis; in v6 A6 in longer form)
- "A yes said to whatever happens is said to no one." (v6)
- "Given to her keeping and not to her disposal." (v6)
- "The speaking alone settles nothing; it cannot show that no one does." (v4; still the best line in the case for this school)
- "Grief wishes for her; guilt owes her."
- "Neither reading is evidence, and this book does not count it as such." (v6)
