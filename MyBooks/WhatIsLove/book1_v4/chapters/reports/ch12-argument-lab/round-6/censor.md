# Round 6: Doctrinal Censor's Fourth Read (v6, pre-imprimatur)

**Scope.** v6 only ("v6 — Round 6" in EVOLUTION.md). First I checked every Round 5 finding (E1-5, N1–N12, minor rows 13–24, and the Round 4 optionals still open). Then I read every sentence that is new in v6 (found by a sentence-level diff against v5) against Scripture (RSVCE), the Catechism, Vatican I and II, VS, *Evangelium Vitae*, *Spe Salvi*, *Dignitas Infinita*, *Salvifici Doloris*, *Dominus Iesus*, *Benedictus Deus* and Aquinas. This read is not scored.

**Bottom line: No Errors.** E1-5 is fixed, using the Catechism's and St. Thomas's own distinction. All twelve Needs-care items from Round 5 are fixed. Most of the optional minor items were also taken.

The new text has six Needs-care items. None is a doctrinal error. Each is a place where a careful bishop's reader would stop:
1. **A6, Christ.** "Those who wrongly take it, as Christ did" runs straight into John 10:18: "No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord." "Bear the death that comes" also understates His free self-offering (CCC 609, "freely accepted his Passion and death").
2. **A6, the last clause.** "No one may kill him because he asks" can be read two ways. In the Kolbe sentence it reads oddly, because Kolbe asked and was killed.
3. **A4.** "Nor 'for his own good' without his asking" carries the hint that asking would make it right. The parenthesis that follows should name the Church's denial outright.
4. **W1(2), CCC 2278–2279.** The sentence leaves out the Catechism's own limit: "the ordinary care owed to a sick person cannot be legitimately interrupted." It also turns "even at the risk of shortening their days" into "even if it shortens."
5. **W1(2).** "No number of others has any right over her life" is broader than intended (compare CCC 2310). It should say "right to take."
6. **C, Kant.** "God does what Kant's ruler may not" invites the reply that God then commits Kant's "greatest wrong," impunity. One clause answers it.
