# Argument Lab, Round 6: Nietzschean opponent vs. v6

## 1. School and voice

This is Nietzsche of the mature works (*Beyond Good and Evil*, *Genealogy*, *Gay Science*, *Twilight*, *Antichrist*, *Ecce Homo*), argued as Leiter, Reginster and Bernard Williams argue him. Round 6 first checks what v6 did with my Round 5 Serious and my five Minors. It then tries rank, genealogy and *amor fati* on the new wording: the conclusion, A4's new clause, A6's Kolbe sentence, W1(2)'s "no voice that claims His name," and the new line on the yes in Evil, First. All Nietzsche material is paraphrased, and the fairness rule applies both ways.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges (short)

1. **Justice:** exchange between parties of roughly equal power (*GM* II §8). At its height it overcomes itself in mercy, which only the strong can afford (*GM* II §10). *Coherent, and it borrows nothing. It breaks A4.*
2. **Dilemma:** one ventilator, the same prognosis, a composer of genius and a man with profound disability. It goes to the composer, without remorse (*BGE* §126). *It decides the case. Nearly every reader rejects it once it is said about a named patient.*
3. **Equal dignity:** denied. There is an order of rank. The lower have real claims, including gentleness from the higher (*Antichrist* §57), but their claims are lesser (*BGE* §257). *A consistent graded view. v6's conclusion now makes it pay its real price.*
4. **Flourishing:** become who you are; self-overcoming; *amor fati*. *It is good for a few, with the many as their base (*BGE* §258). It does not answer for humans as such.*

### 3b. Exchanges

**Exchange 1. Can rank say "no less" and still keep rank?**
*Nietzsche:* You ask whether she was wronged no less than a strong child. I can say yes and keep my order of rank. What makes a deed base is what it shows about the doer. Leaving a weak thing to die is the act of a coward, whatever the thing is worth. So the wrong is equal because the baseness is equal, and her worth is still less. Your own Church counts oppressing the defenseless as an aggravating fault.
*Patrick's side:* That move works only by changing what the question asks. The question is whether *she* was wronged no less, which is a wrong done to her. It does not ask whether the deed was as base. Your ethics is about the doer, and the question is about the victim; you have answered a different one. The aggravation also cancels out. Both infants lie on the same hillside, and both are equally unable to defend themselves. The comparison holds the act fixed and changes one thing: weak or strong. Defenselessness is the same on both sides, so it cannot make up the difference in worth.
*Rejoinder:* Then I say the wrong done to her was less.
*Reply:* Then you have said the sentence v6 now asks you to say, "a lesser wrong," about her, by name. That is the price the case was built to show.
**Held.** The repair works as intended.

**Exchange 2. "Her worth follows what she is." So does rank.**
*Nietzsche:* I agree that worth follows what one is. Rank is a matter of type, not of deeds (*BGE* §287). What she is, is a weak and failing type. So your "Then her worth follows what she is" is my thesis.
*Patrick's side:* The premise has already ruled that reading out. If she was wronged no less than the strong child, then the difference between them, which is strength and health and nothing else, made no difference to the wrong. A worth that follows type, where type means strength and health, would make that difference. What is left is what the two children share: "she is that as fully as you are," that is, human. The word "type" cannot get back in what the premise has just shut out.
**Held.** The step from "wronged no less" to "follows what she is" is now valid against rank, which it was not in v5.

**Exchange 3. Borrowed authority.**
*Nietzsche:* Your new horn 2 is fair. But I never claimed to show that anyone *ought* to keep honesty. It is our virtue, the one we free spirits cannot get away from (*BGE* §227). It is fate, and I love my fate. No ought is needed.
*Patrick's side:* Granted, and v6 says exactly that cost and nothing more: on horn 2 your honesty binds those who already have it. Your critique can convict us only by our own A7. It then convicts us on the evidence, which is where the C row now puts the whole question ("whether honest inquiry ends there or at the Lawgiver is the question of the whole case"). One small point: the sentence reads "he denied that it binds every mind. Then either truthfulness binds every reasoning mind ... or it binds only those who hold it." The "Then" makes the dilemma sound as if it followed from his denial, when the case is the one putting the choice. A second "Then" follows one clause later.
**Held at parity.** Minor wording only (section 5).

**Exchange 4. A4's new clause, and the proxy.**
*Nietzsche:* You now forbid killing the innocent "'for his own good' without his asking." But the parents of the disabled newborn ask. The physician who follows a protocol for ending such lives acts at someone's asking.
*Patrick's side:* At *his* asking, the text says, not someone's. A parent's request is not the child's. The clause shuts exactly the door Round 5 found open. It covers the newborn and the severely disabled, who head A4's own list, and it leaves to A6 only the disputed case of a person's own request. It also does not forbid what the Church allows, since refusing burdensome treatment and easing pain (W1(2), CCC 2278–2279) are not deliberate killing. My own line, that the ill-constituted should perish and be helped to (*Antichrist* §2; *Twilight*, Skirmishes §36), is for the sake of ascending life, which is "for others' sake," and that was closed already.
**Held.**

**Exchange 5. The new line on the yes.**
*Nietzsche:* "A yes said to whatever happens is said to no one ... it cannot take her side." Your faith says yes too. *O felix culpa*: you call the first sin a happy fault. You say God permits no evil except to bring good from it (CCC 324). Mary says *fiat*. Your yes is mine with a priest's name on it.
*Patrick's side:* They differ in two places, and v6 states both. The first is who the yes is said to. Your yes is said to the whole of what happens. Ours is said to Someone, and that Someone is on her side ("What He keeps is her claim, and her"). The second is what the yes affirms. *Amor fati* wants nothing to have been different, forward, backward or for all eternity (*Ecce Homo*, "Why I Am So Clever" §10). The Christian is forbidden to want the evil, and the text says so: "the evil never thereby becomes good (CCC 312)." The *felix culpa* praises what God brought out of the fault, which is Christ, and v6 says that too: "the Church answers only with what He brought from it" (CCC 412). The Christian can say what was done to her ought never to have been. You cannot say it without taking back your yes.
*Rejoinder:* My yes includes my own *no*: my contempt for the one who left her is fated too.
*Reply:* Then it includes his deed as well, and you want both again for eternity. That is still not taking her side.
**Held.**

**Exchange 6. Kolbe and free death.**
*Nietzsche:* Your new A6: "he may not make his own death the thing he chooses." Zarathustra teaches the free death, at the right time. Socrates took the hemlock. And your martyrs chose death; I said they harmed the truth by making death look like an argument (*Antichrist* §53).
*Patrick's side:* v6 now states the line by what the will aims at, not by what the body undergoes. Kolbe aimed at the other man's life and bore the death that came "at the hands of those who wrongly take it." Christ "laid down His life of His own accord" (Evil, Second), and He did not kill Himself. The free death aims at death itself. And A6 marks the whole line as "this book's claims ... not shared ones," so it asks nothing of you as a premise. On the martyrs, the book claims no argument from their dying. A7 judges a belief by the evidence, not by who died for it.
**Held at parity.** This is a named standoff, honestly placed, and the v6 wording fixes the earlier problem of saying "hand it over."

**Exchange 7. "No voice that claims His name."**
*Nietzsche:* W1(2) now says "no voice that claims His name can make it ours to take." Good. The priest is the one who says "God wills it" (*Antichrist* §26). You have disarmed your own priests. But your Bible has such a voice: God tells Abraham to sacrifice Isaac. And your Aquinas holds that at God's command death may be given to anyone, guilty or innocent, without injustice.
*Patrick's side:* The sentence is about voices that *claim* His name, and it is aimed at exactly the move you describe. That is the right place to put the anti-priestly line. Genesis 22 ends with the ram: God does not take Isaac. The Church's present teaching is that the ban on deliberately killing the innocent binds everyone, always and everywhere (VS 52, 80; CCC 2261). How that sits with St. Thomas's reading of Abraham is a question for the doctrinal censor, not a gap in the argument. Against my school the sentence is a strength.
**Held.** I flag it for the censor (section 7).

**Exchange 8. The genealogy against the new conclusion.**
*Nietzsche:* You are surer that she was wronged no less because two thousand years of slave morality trained you to be.
*Patrick's side:* The fairness rule answers this as it did in Round 5. Your account gives the will to truth the same ancestry (the C row now says so), so ancestry undercuts both or neither. The A heading's test asks whether what produced the belief "had nothing to do with its truth." Ressentiment predicts the strong called evil and the weak called good. It does not predict a floor that holds for the enemy, or the plain judgment that the weak infant was wronged *no less*, not more. The ancient world that left her on the hillside shows what instinct produces without that correction.
**Held at parity.**

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: NONE.**

My one Round 5 Serious (the conclusion) is fixed exactly. The repaired question separates the believer in A4 from the rank theorist. He cannot grant "wronged no less" while keeping graded worth, since the comparison holds the deed and the defenselessness fixed (Ex. 1) and the premise rules out worth by type (Ex. 2). He must instead say "a lesser wrong" about her, by name. That is a price, not a defect. None of the new v6 wording (A4's clause, A6's Kolbe sentence, W1(2)'s "no voice that claims His name," the Evil, First line) opens a Fatal or Serious gap against this school.

**Minor (as written)**
1. **C, Power and rank, "Then either."** "He denied that it binds every mind. Then either truthfulness binds every reasoning mind ... or it binds only those who hold it. Then his unmasking ..." The first "Then" makes the dilemma sound as if it followed from his denial, and two "Then"s come one after the other. This is wording only.
2. **Conclusion, "a strong child" (optional, very low).** In context it plainly means a strong infant left on the same hillside. "A strong newborn" would make it explicit that only strength differs, which is what makes Ex. 1 work. It is not needed for validity.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- **Rank vs. equal worth.** He is surer of rank. The case now makes him say "a lesser wrong" about the weak infant, by name.
- **Ressentiment genealogy vs. A4** (Ex. 8). Ancestry undercuts both or neither. Our edge is that ressentiment predicts inversion, not a floor that holds for the enemy.
- **Immanent critique.** Whether honest inquiry ends at "God is dead" or at the Lawgiver. The whole case decides it, as the C row says.
- **Honesty as fate vs. honesty as binding** (Ex. 3). On his account the virtue needs no ought. On ours, without one it binds only those who already have it.
- **"Finally."** One Source, or art to bear the truth. Each side carries a hope the other calls unshown.
- **Free death vs. "not his to make the thing he chooses"** (Ex. 6). This is marked as the book's claim.
- **Dionysus vs. the Crucified** on the evidence of nature alone. The book owns this as weight.

## 7. New insights for the book

1. **The repaired question holds the deed fixed.** The two infants share the same hillside and the same helplessness, and only strength differs. That is why the rank theorist cannot buy "no less" with "the deed was equally base." The chapter can keep the comparison concrete for this reason.
2. **The two yeses.** *Amor fati* says yes to what happened. The Christian says yes to Someone who did not want it. The *felix culpa* praises what God brought out of the fault, not the fault. This is a clean way to answer the charge that providence is *amor fati* under a priest's name.
3. **The anti-priestly line.** "No voice that claims His name can make it ours to take" answers *Antichrist* §26 directly. It is worth keeping in the condensed chapter.
4. **For the censor, not an objection:** check "no voice that claims His name" against ST I-II q.94 a.5 ad 2 and q.100 a.8 ad 3 (St. Thomas on Abraham), so the chapter does not seem to contradict St. Thomas without saying so.

- "No voice that claims His name can make it ours to take." (v6)
