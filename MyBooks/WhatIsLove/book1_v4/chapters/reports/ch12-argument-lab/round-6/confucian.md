# Argument Lab, Round 6: Confucian opponent vs. v6

## 1. School and voice

The opponent speaks for the Confucian tradition at full strength: the *Analects* (Slingerland numbering), Mencius against Xunzi, the *Odes*, the *Mean* and the *Great Learning*, the *Classic of Filial Piety*, the school of principle (the Cheng brothers, Zhu Xi), Wang Yangming's learning of the heart, and Mou Zongsan's "immanent transcendence," with Ivanhoe, Angle and Tu Weiming as modern readers. He grants nearly everything the book says about love and denies only that its Source is Someone. This round he audits v6 against his Round 5 repairs (R-a to R-e, and the optional R9) and tests the new wording. Everything is paraphrased; nothing is quoted from a real thinker.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges (unchanged in substance)

1. **Justice.** It begins in repayment (*bao*): we owe first what we received, from parents, from the sages, and from Heaven and Earth; reciprocity is its measure (15.24). *Holds, close to Chapter 4.* But repayment needs a receiver.
2. **A hard case.** Shun carries his murderer father to the sea (Mencius 7A35): he neither bends the law nor abandons his father. *Coherent and honest about the cost.* It strains A4 at one point: the victim's claim yields.
3. **Equal dignity.** Each person carries the whole of principle, as the one moon is whole in ten thousand rivers. *Holds on equality.* It breaks at "for her own sake": drain one river and the moon has lost nothing.
4. **Ultimate good.** Sagehood, forming one body with all things; for the world, the Great Unity in which widow, orphan and disabled are all cared for. *Coherent and noble.* It stops short of communion, and of the dead.

## 3. Debate highlights

**Exchange 1: did v6 take R-a to R-e and R9?**
*Audit:*
- **R-a (the *Analects* gloss):** **taken word for word.** 7.23 (virtue), 9.5 (would not let it perish), 11.9 (bereaved him), 14.35 (alone knew him) now each say what the passage says. One slip is mine, not v6's; see Exchange 2.
- **R-b ("besides me"):** **taken word for word.** "No one besides me who knows me as myself, and no one at all who can release what I did to another or keep the one I lost." That is fair to Mou: the infinite heart knows me, and it does not pardon my wrongs to others.
- **R-c (the God row's modifier):** **taken.** "It is the one that knows and loves us, and is Someone besides ourselves." The ambiguity is gone.
- **R-d (the ancestral cult on road three):** **taken word for word, and placed where asked**, immediately before the Buddhist, which is now "another form of the third road." The two godless forms of the third road stand side by side. Tested in Exchange 3.
- **R-e ("a yes said to everything"):** **taken**, and v6 added a clause: "it must say yes to what was done to her as well as to her, and so it cannot take her side." Against Heaven this does not land, and rightly: Heaven's heart gives life and does not affirm the breaking; Confucius accepts what is appointed (*ming*) but never stops taking the side of rightness. The clause aims where it should, at an affirmation of everything that happens.
- **R9 (Neo-Confucian seekers in W3):** **taken.** Tested in Exchange 6.
*Result: Held.* Every Round 5 point is closed.

**Exchange 2: 9.5 is about the culture, not "his work" (a slip in my own Round 5 wording).**
*Opponent.* "In 9.5, besieged in Kuang, the Master says that since King Wen is gone, the culture (*wen*) is here with him, and if Heaven meant this culture to perish, a later man could not have shared in it. Heaven protects *this culture*, which he carries; 'his work' narrows it into a personal project. I proposed the words, and they are slightly wrong."
*Defense.* "Accepted. The correction also suits the book: Heaven is spoken to as one who keeps something for the sake of others, which is closer to Providence than 'his work' was."
*Result: Held with repair (Minor).* R-f. No step of the argument rests on it.

**Exchange 3: is the ancestral sentence true to those who keep the rites?**
*Opponent.* "Three tests. (a) Popular religion holds that the dead exist in the courts below whether or not they are fed. Feeding gives them comfort, not being. 'Kept by the living' could be read as 'exist only through the living.' (b) In the great lineage halls the founding ancestor is never removed; some are named for thirty generations. (c) In China the line is the father's. A daughter who dies unmarried has no place in her father's hall; hence ghost marriage."
*Defense.* "(a) The sentence says what keeps her *there*, at the offering, as a named ancestor, and it says what happens to the unfed: they 'become the wandering dead.' That grants your point: they go on, but not as anyone's. On Zhu Xi's reading, where the ancestor's *qi* answers through the shared *qi* of her descendants and disperses after some generations, the sentence is true even of being. (b) 'As long as her own line remembers her' covers the founder; memory that lasts thirty generations is still memory. (c) That is true, and it is a datum, not a defect: the rite runs through kinship, and the one who belongs to no line has no one to keep her by name. The book's Keeper is not bound by blood."
*Opponent rejoinder.* "And popular belief adds that before rebirth the dead drink the soup of forgetting."
*Defense.* "Then your own popular tradition agrees with v6's Buddhist sentence: the one born again 'will not, as a rule, know what was done.'"
*Result: Held.* No repair. (b), (c) and the soup go to §7.

**Exchange 4: does the ancestral form satisfy the clue without God?**
*Opponent.* "W1(1) closes: the clue 'weighs as much as the reader finds the third road, on which she is kept as herself, truer than the second.' On my road she *is* kept as herself, by name, for as long as she is remembered. So the clue weighs toward the tablet as much as toward the altar."
*Defense.* "For as long as she is remembered: that is the whole difference, and the text names it ('memory fails'). The clue says only that guilt asks for what it could have only if she is still someone. Your road grants that, which makes you a witness for the clue, not against it. What the clue cannot do by itself, it does not claim: the step to One who forgets no one is made by the Catholic paragraph, and it is offered as a hope that 'promises' what guilt asks, not as a proof. The ancestral reader who wants the debt received by *her* when no one is left who knows her name is asking for a Keeper."
*Opponent rejoinder.* "And I can say the wish for permanence is a wish, not a debt."
*Defense.* "Your own *Odes* say a child's debt to his parents is as boundless as Heaven and cannot be repaid (Ode 202, 'Lu e'; verify). That is A3 in your own voice: the debt outlives every payment. If it is boundless, it outlasts memory too."
*Result: Held.* An honest standoff remains (§5): what keeps her when memory fails. The text states it fairly.

**Exchange 5: A6's new line on chosen death, and a fair tu quoque.**
*Opponent.* "v6 now says a man may lay down his life and bear the death that comes, but may not make his own death the thing he chooses. Mencius would give up life to take rightness (6A10), and 15.9 says the man of *ren* gives up his body to complete *ren*; the tradition honored loyal ministers who died with their dynasty and widows who died to stay chaste. Your Church commemorates holy virgins who threw themselves to death rather than be violated, and Aquinas excuses them by a hidden command of the Spirit (ST II-II q.64 a.5 ad 4, following Augustine, *City of God* I.26; verify). Is your line cleaner than ours?"
*Defense.* "6A10 and 15.9 are laying life down, which v6 grants by name; the chaste-widow suicide and the loyalist suicide are what v6 forbids, and v6 says plainly that many who hold the rest of A disagree ('this book's claims ... not shared ones'). As for the virgins: Augustine argued at length *against* suicide to escape rape, and allowed only that some saints may have acted under a divine command no one else may presume; today's teaching (CCC 2280–2282) names no exception, and v6's 'no voice that claims His name can make it ours to take (CCC 2258)' rules out any *human* claim to such a command. So yes: both traditions honored some who chose death in a way their later teaching does not commend. Your Yongzheng edict and our Augustine both corrected their own. v6 uses neither tradition's honored dead against the other, which is the fairness rule kept."
*Result: Held.* No repair. (For the censor, not a defect: the word "claims" carries the weight in "no voice that claims His name," and it is what keeps the sentence consistent with Augustine and Aquinas. Keep it.)

**Exchange 6: R9's "so did."**
*Opponent.* "'The Buddhist contemplatives ... looked inward for a lifetime and report a peace beyond the self rather than a Person; so did the Neo-Confucians, who ... found a principle rather than a Person.' 'So did' can carry over 'report a peace beyond the self.' Zhu Xi's whole polemic against Chan was that it seeks a mind emptied of the self and of the world, while we seek principle in things, in human relations, and in the heart. To pair us under 'beyond the self' is the conflation our teachers spent their lives refusing. The words are mine, and the relative clause mostly saves them, but one change removes the risk."
*Defense.* "Accepted. Say what they did, not 'so did.'"
*Result: Held with repair (Minor).* R-g.

**Exchange 7: shame in A2 (checked, not re-raised).**
*Opponent.* "'Shame, left to itself, says I am less; guilt says I wronged her.' For Mencius, shame and dislike (*xiu wu*) are the sprout of rightness (2A6): moral shame is directed at the deed."
*Defense.* "'Left to itself' is the hedge. Mencian shame is shame cultivated toward rightness; v6 does not say shame cannot grow into that, only that by itself it looks at the self and does not ask *her* pardon. Mencius would agree that the sprout must be extended."
*Result: Held.* No repair. (Unchanged since v5; I held it then and hold it now.)

**Exchange 8: the classical Heaven, and whether the sacred-order row scopes itself.**
*Opponent.* "The oldest layer of our tradition is not impersonal. In the *Odes* the Zhou kings' ancestors stand at the left and right of the Lord on High (Ode 235, 'Wen Wang'; verify), and Mencius cites the *Documents*: Heaven sees as the people see and hears as the people hear (5A5). Does the row make Confucianism an impersonal order from the start?"
*Defense.* "No. The row names its target: 'Heaven's principle as the Neo-Confucians, above all Zhu Xi, understood it.' It then shows Confucius speaking of a Heaven that acts on him, and says that this is where the account 'strains for its own teachers.' That is the classical Heaven, glossed accurately. The Jesuit Matteo Ricci argued that the Lord on High of the *Odes* and *Documents* was the true God and that the school of principle had naturalized Him (paraphrase; verify). The book need not take that side; it is a contested reading. But it is open to the book to note it."
*Result: Held.* No defect. Optional addition in §6 (R10), for the book's richness, not for the stop test.

**Exchange 9: smaller checks of v6 wording.**
(a) God row: "a ground for the rule that one person may not be spent for others' totals, which many of the best rivals share and rest on a standing they take as given." Mencius 2A2 (not one innocent killed, even for the empire) rests on the doer's rightness, not on the victim's standing. "Many" leaves room for him. No fault.
(b) A4's new parenthesis and "equal worth is not equal duty" still draw the line where graded love (love with distinctions) sits. No fault.
(c) Conclusion, "wronged no less than a strong child": graded love concerns duty, not worth, so the Confucian grants it. Mencius's child at the well (2A6) is any child. No fault.
(d) Evil, *Third*: the Great Unity (*Liyun*) is credited accurately. No fault.
(e) Sima Qian (*Shiji* 61): the brothers who starved and the robber who lived long, and the question whether the Way of Heaven is just. Accurate. No fault.
(f) W3 para 2: "Each tradition can read the other's summit as a stage on its own road; neither reading is evidence." Fair to the Neo-Confucians, who read Chan as a half-truth. No fault.

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: NONE.**

No Fatal or Serious objection from this school stands against v6 as written. v6 took every repair this school asked for in Round 5 (R-a to R-e, and the optional R9), most of them word for word, and placed the ancestral form of the third road exactly where it was asked to go. The new wording (the ancestral sentence, "another form of the third road," "the third road, on which she is kept as herself," the clause after "a yes said to whatever happens," A6's line on chosen death, and the Neo-Confucian seekers in W3) is accurate to Zhu Xi, Mou, Mencius and popular practice, and none of it breaks a step. The strongest new probes (Ex. 3, 4, 5) are answered at parity or better from the text's own premises.

**Minor (both slips are in this school's own Round 5 wording, not v6's choices):**
1. Sacred-order row: "would not let his work perish" narrows 9.5, where Heaven keeps *the culture* (*wen*) that Confucius carries (Ex. 2). R-f.
2. W3 para 2: "so did the Neo-Confucians" can carry "a peace beyond the self" over to a school that rejected exactly that as the Chan error (Ex. 6). R-g.

**Misstatements of this school:** none beyond the two Minor items above.

**Noted for the censor, not a defect:** in W1(2), "no voice that claims His name can make it ours to take" depends on the word "claims," which keeps it consistent with Augustine and Aquinas on the hidden divine command (Ex. 5). Keep the word.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- *The ancestral third road.* The named dead kept by the memory of their kin against the dead kept by One who forgets no one. They part on what keeps her when memory fails.
- *Debts to the dead.* Xunzi's completion of the bond, or Zhu Xi's *qi*-response through the line, against a debt that waits for its creditor. Theism is ahead only where guilt asks for *her* pardon.
- *Equality without capacity.* The moon in the rivers matches the theist ground on equality; theism is ahead on "for her own sake" and "for good."
- *Life held in trust.* Trust from parents (*Classic of Filial Piety*; Mencius 7A2) against trust from the Lord of life. Mencius's absolute (2A2) rests in the doer's rightness; the book's rests in her.
- *Honored deaths.* Each tradition honored some who chose death in ways its later teaching does not commend (Ex. 5).
- *Union vs. communion.* Mou's infinite heart, mine and yours, against Someone besides us.
- *The problem of evil.* A principle that intends no one carries a lighter problem and promises the seeker his own completion; a God who knows each one carries the heavier problem and promises this child.

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

1. **The filial debt is boundless (Ode 202, "Lu e").** The son wishes to repay his parents' kindness, but it is like vast Heaven, without limit. This is A3 in Confucian voice: some debts outlast every payment, and so outlast memory. (Ch. 12, A3 and W1(1); Ch. 4 on gift.) Verify.
2. **The daughter with no hall.** In the patrilineal rite a daughter who dies unmarried has no place in her father's line; ghost marriage arose to give her one. The kinship rite cannot hold the one who belongs to no line. The Church's Keeper is not bound by blood. Use with care, as a datum, never as a jab. (Ch. 12, W1(1); Ch. 10 on communion.) Verify.
3. **The soup of forgetting.** Late Chinese popular belief has the dead drink a soup of forgetting before rebirth. The popular tradition itself confirms that the one reborn "will not, as a rule, know what was done." (Ch. 12, W1(1).) Verify.
4. **The founder who is never moved.** Even a lineage that names its founding ancestor for thirty generations keeps him by memory, and only within its own line. Memory can be long and still fail. (Ch. 12, W1(1).)
5. **Ancestors beside the Lord on High (Ode 235).** In China's oldest layer the great dead stand at the side of the Lord on High: the ancestral road once reached for a Keeper, but only for kings. The Church's Keeper keeps the least. (Ch. 12, C, optional.) Verify.
6. **Augustine, Aquinas and the honored virgins.** Both traditions honored some who chose death and later taught against it. Naming that honestly, if the book ever speaks of other traditions' honored suicides, keeps the fairness rule. (Ch. 12, A6; Ch. 9 on sacrifice.) Verify ST II-II q.64 a.5 ad 4 and *City of God* I.17–27 at newadvent.
7. **Verify before anything enters the book:** *Analects* 9.5, 11.9, 11.10, 15.9; *Mencius* 2A2, 2A6, 5A5, 6A10, 7A2, 7A35; Odes 202 and 235; Zhu Xi's *Family Rituals* (four generations at the shrine); Sima Qian, *Shiji* 61; *Liyun* (the Great Unity). Paraphrase only.

**Lines worth keeping (short, plain, warm):**
- "Heaven and Earth give life to things; God wanted *you*." (v6)
- "On that road the dead are kept by the living, and memory fails." (v6)
- "A yes said to whatever happens is said to no one; it cannot take her side." (v6, condensed)
- "If the debt is boundless, it outlasts memory too."
- "The Keeper is not bound by blood."
- "Something can respond. Only someone can forgive." (Round 4, still true of Zhu Xi's *qi*.)
