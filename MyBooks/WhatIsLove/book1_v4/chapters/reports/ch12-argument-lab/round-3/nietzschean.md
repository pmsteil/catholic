# Argument Lab, Round 3: Nietzschean opponent vs. v3

## 1. School and voice

Friedrich Nietzsche in his mature works (*Beyond Good and Evil*, *On the Genealogy of Morality*, *The Gay Science*, *Twilight of the Idols*, *The Antichrist*, *Ecce Homo*), argued the way his strongest recent readers argue him: Brian Leiter's naturalist Nietzsche, Bernard Williams on shame, practical necessity and truthfulness (*Shame and Necessity*; *Moral Luck*; *Truth and Truthfulness*), and Bernard Reginster on the revaluation of values. In Round 3 he stops attacking what v3 has repaired. He goes after the new machinery: Moorean trust, the shame/guilt line, the truthfulness dilemma, and the closing "which are you surer of". He also opens three new fronts: A4's wording leaves out the aristocratic trade, Dionysus is a rival in the evil section, and the book never answers "why truth over life." All Nietzsche material is paraphrased. Fairness rule applied both ways throughout.

---

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges (Round 3 angle)

**1. Starting point for justice.** Justice begins as exchange between parties of roughly equal power, creditor and debtor (*GM* II §8). It is an achievement of the strong, who can measure, wait and keep accounts without being driven by reactive feeling (*GM* II §11). At its height it overcomes itself in mercy, which is the privilege of the most powerful (*GM* II §10).
*Verdict: holds as a consistent position; breaks at A4.* No one who could never have been a party to exchange (the newborn, the dying, the profoundly disabled) is owed anything. Nietzsche says so openly. There is no borrowing here, but there is a high price.

**2. A hard dilemma (new case).** One ventilator. Two patients with the same medical prognosis: a young composer of real genius, and a man with profound intellectual disability, the same age. Nietzsche: give it to the composer, and feel no remorse. A people is nature's detour to reach a handful of great human beings (*BGE* §126). The meaning of the species lies in its highest examples.
*Verdict: it decides the case without borrowing, and it breaks A4 in plain view.* Catholic teaching and secular triage ethics agree here, and this is worth noticing. Both allow medical criteria (likelihood of benefit). Both forbid ranking patients by social worth. Nietzsche's answer is coherent, and it is the one answer almost every reader rejects when it is said about a named patient. That is the Moorean pressure the conclusion relies on.

**3. Equal, unconditional dignity.** Denied. New this round: equality is not only false, it is a ceiling disguised as a floor. A culture that holds every life equal in worth and makes pity its first duty will in time stop producing great human beings (*BGE* §§62, 203; *Antichrist* §7).
*Verdict: a consistent denial; the "ceiling" claim fails.* The Church keeps rank where rank belongs. Holiness differs in degree (*Dignitas Infinita* 7–8 distinguish ontological from moral dignity). Aquinas counts magnanimity, the reaching for great things that are worthy of great honor, as a virtue, and he holds that it does not contradict humility (*ST* II-II q.129 a.3 ad 4). What the Church forbids is not greatness. It is buying greatness with persons. Nietzsche admits the purchase is needed: a healthy aristocracy accepts with a good conscience that great numbers be reduced to instruments for its sake (*BGE* §258). The real quarrel is therefore over A6, and there the book is strong.

**4. Ultimate flourishing.** "Become who you are" (*GS* §270; the subtitle of *Ecce Homo*): self-overcoming, the creation of values, and *amor fati*, a yes to the whole of life willed again forever.
*Verdict: breaks as an answer to the question asked.* The question asks what is ultimately good for *humans*. Nietzsche's answer is ultimately good for a few. For everyone else his end is to serve as their base (*BGE* §258). An account of flourishing that leaves out most of humanity by design is not a rival account of human flourishing. It is a refusal to give one.

### 3b. Exchanges

**Exchange 1. "This is not a vote," said right after the count.**
*Nietzsche:* Your heading says "Almost everyone finds, on reflection, that he cannot give them up," and two sentences later, "This is not a vote." You count the hands and then disown the count. The count is also false on your own evidence. Aristotle, who reflected as hard as anyone, gave up A4: he would make a law that no deformed child be reared, and he held some men slaves by nature. Your own A4 says the ancient world exposed its infants and Christians were slow on slavery. Second point, which is deeper: "cannot give up" is exactly what an error looks like when it is a condition of life. Life is no argument. That we cannot live without a belief shows that we need it, not that it is true (*GS* §121).
*Patrick's side:* The count sentence adds nothing and should go. The ground is the first-person principle. On *GS* §121: the heading means "cannot honestly deny," not "cannot bear to lose." A7, in the same list, forbids believing a thing because it comforts, so "honest reflection" excludes the comforts. The principle is also one you live by. Your rank order, your honesty and your disgust at the herd are seemings that you trust until something stronger shows them false. Without some such trust no one, you included, can reason at all.
*Rejoinder:* Then the principle hands me a warrant for rank, just as it hands your reader a warrant for A4.
*Reply:* Yes. The book says so: it "cannot compel the one who gives up A4." The principle gives each reader permission to trust what survives honest scrutiny. It does not refute you. C then shows what each warrant costs.
**Held with repair.** Minor: delete the count, and let "honest reflection" include reflection on where a belief came from (see Ex. 2).

**Exchange 2. Moorean trust and the undercutter: the slave revolt.**
*Nietzsche:* Your trust principle admits only something that "shows it false." My genealogy does not show A4 false. It shows A4 would be believed whether or not it were true: it was born of the ressentiment of the weak, who took revenge on the strong by renaming strength "evil" and weakness "good" (*GM* I §§7, 10). A principle that counts rebutters and ignores undercutters rules out my method by definition.
*Patrick's side:* Fair on the wording; the principle should name undercutters. On the substance, three replies. (a) Your genealogy predicts the wrong datum. Ressentiment wants the strong brought low: it predicts an *inverted* order, the weak as good and the strong as evil. A4 says something else. It lists *the enemy* among those of equal worth, and it forbids anyone to count for less, the strong and the persecutor included. Revenge does not produce a floor that holds for its enemies. (b) W2 already says where interest bends conscience: toward denying the worth of the slave and the infant who cannot repay. The masters had every interest in denying A4. If one side's seeing is suspect for being interested, it is the side that profited from the hillside. (c) By the fairness rule, your own account of the will to truth gives it the same Christian ancestry you give A4 (*GS* §344; *GM* III §§24, 27). If that ancestry discredits A4, it discredits the honesty that drives your genealogy. Either both are discounted or neither is.
*Rejoinder:* Care for the helpless is still will to power: the small pleasure of being the stronger one, the helper (*GM* III §18).
*Reply:* That explains some charity. It explains just as well why people *hold* A4 whether or not A4 is true. And theism explains A4 *as true*. So the question returns to whether A4 is true or only held. The heading has put exactly that question to the reader, and that is the honest place for it.
**Held at parity, with a new strength (a).** Minor wording on undercutters.

**Exchange 3. A2: "Shame says I am less; guilt says I wronged her; only guilt goes looking for her."**
*Nietzsche (with Williams):* You have taken the progressivist picture Williams took apart, that guilt-cultures are morally more grown-up than shame-cultures. Shame is not self-absorbed. It holds the gaze of others inside me, and it can send me back to the one I wronged. And "almost none treats it as a mere mistake" is false of the Greeks. Their gods kept bad conscience away: a man who did wrong said a god had sent him folly, so the Greeks took on folly, not sin (*GM* II §23).
*Patrick's side:* On the substance, Williams's own description agrees with the line. Guilt looks to what happened to the victim; shame looks to what I am. The line does not rank them; it says which one is aimed at her. "Goes looking for her" can be sharpened, because shame may send me back to her too, but to be *seen* again as better. What only guilt does is ask her *pardon*, which only she can give. On the Greeks: Homer's Agamemnon blamed his wrong on a god-sent madness, and then paid Achilles in full. Even the culture that called wrongdoing folly did not treat it as a mere mistake. The wronged man was still owed. That is A2 and A3 in the oldest poem we have.
**Held with minor repair** ("only guilt asks her pardon"). The Agamemnon point strengthens A2.

**Exchange 4. W1 runs on my frame: guilt as debt.**
*Nietzsche:* Your W1 test is "owed or only ripening." A3 says "we still owe her," and W1(1) says "the debt still owed." That is my genealogy's frame. *Schuld* (guilt) comes from *Schulden* (debts) (*GM* II §§4–8). When the creditor dies, the debt does not lapse: it moves up to the ancestor, and when the ancestor becomes a god, the debt becomes infinite (*GM* II §§19–21). You took the commercial frame and then announced that a debt needs a creditor. And your Abraham haggling at Genesis 18:25 is a debtor bargaining.
*Patrick's side:* By the fairness rule, where a word came from does not settle what it now means; you would not let us say that about your honesty. W1 does not assume the frame either. It puts "owed or only ripening" as the open question and gives two witnesses. What A2's ashes case shows is *directedness*: the wrong was done *to her*. Commerce is not the point, and second-personal philosophers with no God (Darwall) hold the same directedness. As for Abraham, look at what he asks. He is not bargaining down his own debt. He is pleading for strangers in Sodom, against the Judge's justice and in the name of that justice. That is intercession, the opposite of a debtor's haggling.
*Rejoinder:* The datum "we still owe her" is still yours, not mine.
*Reply:* Agreed. You deny A3, and C says so. That is the confessed standoff, not a defect in the case.
**Held (standoff at A3).**

**Exchange 5. The truthfulness dilemma (C, "Power and rank").**
*Nietzsche:* Three points. (a) "He could not give up truthfulness (A7)." The first half is true; I said honesty is the one virtue we free spirits cannot get away from (*BGE* §227). The "(A7)" is false. A7 says truth binds *every* reasoning mind. I denied that. The many may live by lies that serve life, and I praised a lawgiver who did exactly that (*Antichrist* §§56–57). (b) Horn 2, "his own unmasking is one more preference," flattens me. Tastes have rank: mine is not "one more." It is also not a preference at all in Williams's sense. It is a practical necessity of character, an "I must" that I cannot choose away, and that is neither a law over all minds nor a whim. (c) And I accept horn 2 cheerfully. Suppose my reading is only interpretation too: so much the better (*BGE* §22). My books were written for the few.
*Patrick's side:* (a) Granted. Say "his own honesty," not "(A7)." (b) Granted on the description. But a necessity of character binds only those whose character it is. On the point that matters, a law for every reasoning mind or not, the dilemma is exhaustive, and you are on the second horn. (c) Then say what that horn costs, and the row should say it too. If your honesty binds only your kind, it can *rank* the reader and never *correct* him. It can call his belief low. It cannot show him that he ought to give it up. Your unmasking has authority over no one who does not already share your taste. That is exactly what C needs to show, and you have just granted it. One more point, because the fairness rule cuts both ways. Even if truthfulness does not bind, your genealogy may still be *true*. A claim's truth does not wait on anyone's duty to care about it. The reader, whom A7 does bind, must still weigh your genealogy on its merits, and Exchange 2 does that.
**Held with repair** (the row misstates him at "(A7)" and leaves horn 2's cost unstated). Minor, but the most important Minor in this report (see section 5).

**Exchange 6. "Why should truth matter more than life?": raised, never answered.**
*Nietzsche:* Your row calls this the hardest question of all, then turns to my consistency and leaves it. It is your question as much as mine. W2 says we *ought* to believe the truth "when a falsehood would serve us better." Why? I at least faced the danger. Without art, honesty would bring nausea and despair (*GS* §107). The tragic Greeks knew that knowledge can kill action. And the clause "called it, by his own admission, the last form of a faith" is still the jab you promised to drop: it hints that my honesty is borrowed from you.
*Patrick's side:* Both points are fair. On the clause: it reports what you said of yourself (*GS* §344), but left hanging it reads as a genealogy used against you. It should either go or state the symmetry plainly: your own account of the will to truth gives it the same ancestry you give A4, so neither ancestry decides anything. On the question, the book has an answer it does not write down, and it is a real explanatory gain. On the Catholic account truth and life have one Source ("I am the way, and the truth, and the life," John 14:6). So the will to truth cannot finally lead away from life, and W2's "this law holds together" covers truth and life too. On your account nothing guarantees that. Truth may be deadly, which is why you needed art to survive it. Theism explains why honesty need not be a slow suicide. Your account has to hope that it is not.
*Rejoinder:* "One Source" is your conclusion, not your evidence.
*Reply:* It is the hypothesis being weighed. Its fit is what C compares: the account on which truth and life cannot finally conflict against the account on which they might. Your *GS* §107 is evidence of which one you found yourself living in.
**Held with repair** (add the answer; resolve the clause). Minor.

**Exchange 7. A4 forbids the utilitarian trade, not mine.**
*Nietzsche:* Read A4 closely: "never to be killed or used as a means *for better totals*." A6: "not to buy a better outcome *for many*." Both are aimed at Bentham. I despise totals too. My trade is not for the many; it is for the few, the many spent for the one Goethe (*BGE* §258). A reader can sign A4 and A6 as worded and still sign *BGE* §258. And your heaven has rank: star differs from star in glory (1 Corinthians 15:41).
*Patrick's side:* The wording gap is real; v3 closes it elsewhere but not in A. W1(2) says no one may be killed "for others," and the evil section says "no one is the price of someone else's good." A4 should say the same: *not for the many, and not for the few.* On rank: yes, and it makes A4 stronger. *Dignitas Infinita* separates ontological dignity, equal in everyone, from moral dignity, which depends on how one lives. Rank in holiness is real. Rank in what we are worth is not. And in this order the way up is down (Mark 10:43–45). Round 2 proposed this sentence; v3 did not add it.
**Held with repair.** Minor (two wording additions).

**Exchange 8. Dionysus in the evil section.**
*Nietzsche:* Your "First" says a will that lacks nothing has nothing to grasp at, so there is no supreme Evil, and "the world's darkness is not evidence of a dark god." Agreed: there is no devil-god. But there are two kinds of fullness. There is suffering from impoverishment and suffering from overfullness, and the overfull can destroy out of sheer abundance, as an artist breaks one form to make another (*GS* §370). My god is Dionysus (*BGE* §295). He is not dark and not indifferent. He is the yes that makes and breaks. Your own Thomist God "wills the order of the whole," and in it "the wolf lives by the fawn." On the evidence of nature alone, which fits better: a Father who wants each fawn, or Dionysus at play?
*Patrick's side:* Dionysus is the honest rival here, and the text should name him. The privation point stands: he is not a supreme Evil, because he wills becoming, which is a good, not evil as evil. Your question is the indifferent-source question in a festive mask, and v3 answers it the only way it can be answered, "by the whole case." A yes said to the whole cannot owe a debt to one dead girl, cannot ground a worth that ignores ability, and cannot say to anyone "it is good that *you* exist." Yes to everything is yes to no one. On "the order of the whole": Aquinas says non-rational creatures are ordered to the whole, while persons are governed for their own sake (*SCG* III.112). That is where Dionysus and the Father part.
*Rejoinder:* In nature, Dionysus fits.
*Reply:* v3 already concedes that the pattern of pain in nature "still weighs." That is owned weight, not a defect.
**Held with repair** (name him beside the indifferent source). Minor.

**Exchange 9. "Which are you surer of?"**
*Nietzsche:* Three points. (a) The question pits her worth against "any argument." My rival is not an argument. It is another seeing, the pathos of distance (*BGE* §257). The father on the hillside was following no syllogism; he was sure. (b) "Worth *exactly* what you are worth" invites the lifeboat. Goethe or the exposed infant: most of your readers would save Goethe. Are they surer of "exactly"? (c) "What you feel then is not the source of her worth" is the thesis at issue, announced at the climax.
*Patrick's side:* (a) Then it is seeing against seeing, and the reader is the one asked, not Sparta. The conclusion already says the case cannot compel. (b) Fair on the wording. "Worth" in A4 means a dignity that forbids killing her or using her. It is not the size of the loss in a rescue. Triage by prognosis does not rank worth; the Church forbids ranking patients by social value (challenge 2). The surest form of the question is concrete: was it wrong to leave her there? Almost no reader hesitates at that, and equal worth follows from it, because if it was wrong to expose her *for being weak*, her worth does not follow her strength. (c) It comes after the comparison as the book's reading, and A's heading has already said these are offered as truths, not feelings. It also blocks your pity charge (*Antichrist* §7): the line says outright that the feeling is not the ground.
*Rejoinder:* The Spartans were sure it was right.
*Reply:* Then they and the reader part, and the reader can see what holding their answer costs: saying it to *her*.
**Held with minor repair** ("exactly" → the concrete wrong).

**Exchange 10. W3: the mask.**
*Nietzsche:* W3 assumes we long to be known wholly. The deep spirit wants a mask. Every profound thinker fears being understood more than being misunderstood (*BGE* §§40, 290). The longing to be wholly known is a herd longing for shelter.
*Patrick's side:* The man who praised masks opened his last book by asking his readers to hear him and not to mistake him for someone else (*Ecce Homo*, Preface §1). What the mask guards against is being *wrongly* known, flattened by eyes that cannot see. That is the longing in W3, not its opposite. The Christian hope is to be known by One whose knowing is love, not exposure (Psalm 139; Colossians 3:3, "your life is hidden with Christ in God").
**Held.**

## 4. Ledger for v3

- **Form line: Holds.** "Offered for weighing; can predispose" is the right modesty.
- **A heading (truths, not feelings; Moorean trust; deniers named; "This is not a vote"): Holds with repair.** The first-person trust principle is sound, symmetric, and one Nietzsche himself relies on. The leftover count ("Almost everyone finds, on reflection…") is false for A4 on the book's own history and sits awkwardly beside "not a vote"; delete it. The trust principle should let honest reflection include reflection on where a belief came from (undercutters). "Nietzsche A2–A4" understates him: he denies A1 as stated (no moral facts, *BGE* §108) and denies that A7 binds everyone.
- **A1: Holds.** Denied openly by Nietzsche and by error theory. It survives as the reader's comparative judgment. The case does not need Nietzsche to grant it.
- **A2: Holds with minor repair.** The ashes case and the shame/guilt line agree with Williams's own description. "Only guilt goes looking for her" → "only guilt asks her pardon." Agamemnon (paid in full despite *atē*) strengthens "almost none treats it as a mere mistake" against *GM* II §23.
- **A3: Holds.** The scope limit to the gravest wrongs is right. "We still owe her" is a debt to her, not to God, and the heading offers it as a truth. Nietzsche denies it openly: standoff.
- **A4: Holds with repair.** "Equal worth is not equal duty" and "instinct fails" are strong. Gap: "for better totals" (with A6's "for many") does not forbid the aristocratic trade (*BGE* §258); add "not for the many and not for the few." Add DI 7–8 (rank in holiness is real; rank in worth is not). "Holds most plainly where instinct fails" overstates the *ease* of seeing; better: "where instinct fails we learn whether what we meant was worth or only attraction."
- **A5: Holds.** Reason's grasp of goods (q.94 a.2) answers Round 2. Nietzsche's charge that the list has no room for greatness is answered by magnanimity (*ST* II-II q.129), which belongs in Ch. 13, not here.
- **A6: Holds.** "Asks something of the giver" is weak enough to be true even of Zarathustra's overflowing gift-giver: lavish giving still costs time and attention. The three cases are good. The aristocratic-trade gap belongs to A4 and is noted there.
- **A7: Holds.** "We may not stop looking because we fear what we will find" now cuts both ways. Nietzsche denies that A7 is universal (*Antichrist* §§56–57). That is his price, stated in C.
- **W1 frame (Anscombe as the question; necessity agreed; "owed or only ripen"): Holds.** The debt-genealogy (*GM* II §§4–8, 19–21) describes where the frame came from; it does not refute it (fairness rule). Nietzsche's "bad conscience is a sickness" falls on the "ripen" side of the test.
- **W1(1) (the debt still owed; He does not forgive in her place; a clue): Holds with minor repair.** Round 2's circularity is fixed. "Speak to their dead and ask their pardon" is just what the ancestor cult predicts, fear of the *powerful* dead (*GM* II §19). Point the datum at the powerless and forgotten dead, whom no one fears. *Spe Salvi* 44's banquet line can read as revenge postponed (*GM* I §15). Add *Spe Salvi* 47: the fire that judges is Christ, and it heals.
- **W1(2) (a worth no deed confers and no order wants): Holds** at parity. It stands or falls with A4. Nietzsche denies that such a worth exists; he does not show the reasoning wrong. "No total, no vote and no grief … for others" already covers *BGE* §258.
- **W1 closing (not a commander; the law binds because He is good; the Judge lets Himself be asked): Holds.** "A will that invents values can command, but it cannot bind" answers *BGE* §211 directly. Genesis 18:25 is intercession for strangers, not a debtor's haggling.
- **W2 (accident or wisdom; norm of truth; correction by consistency; unity): Holds.** Darwin's bees: Nietzsche takes the "accident" horn, and A1 shows the cost. "Conscience fails where self-interest pulls" turns his genealogy around: the masters, not the slaves, had the interest. It needs one added sentence (Ex. 6): truth and life cannot finally conflict if they have one Source. That is where the "law holds together" claim meets Nietzsche's hardest question.
- **W3 paragraph 1 (communion; grasping dies; "it is good that you exist"; not more of the same years): Holds.** The mask objection is answered by *Ecce Homo*'s first demand. *Amor fati* says yes to the whole and so to no one.
- **W3 paragraph 2 (hiddenness through persons; the cost owned): Holds.** Not this school's main ground. Its honesty about the cost is right.
- **C heading (accommodate vs. explain; best defenders; fairness rule): Holds.** The fairness sentence is the right rule. The Nietzsche row does not yet fully keep it (below).
- **C: Nothing really binds: Holds.**
- **C: Feeling, refined: Holds** (not pressed).
- **C: Reason's own law: Holds** (not pressed).
- **C: What we owe each other: Holds** (not pressed; the contractualist's own finding stands).
- **C: Well-being: Holds.** Nietzsche agrees that it trades persons.
- **C: Flourishing of our kind: Holds** (not pressed).
- **C: Godless realism: Holds** (not pressed).
- **C: Impersonal sacred order: Holds** (not pressed).
- **C: The Buddha's Dhamma: Holds** (not pressed).
- **C: Power and rank (Nietzsche): Holds with repair.** The dilemma is valid and on the decisive point exhaustive (does truthfulness bind every reasoning mind, or not?). As written: (1) "(A7)" is false of him (he kept his own honesty, *BGE* §227, and denied it binds everyone, *Antichrist* §§56–57); (2) "called it … the last form of a faith" hangs as an insinuated genealogy, against C's own fairness sentence; (3) horn 2's "one more preference" flattens his rank of tastes and Williams's practical necessity, and does not say what the horn costs (his honesty can rank the reader, never correct him); (4) unlike every other row, it gives no credit for what the rival explains; (5) it raises "why should truth matter more than life?" and does not answer it, though the book has the answer.

The same "whole case" answer applies. Join *SCG* III.112 to "the order of the whole" so that "no one is the price" is plainly about persons.
- **Conclusion: Holds with minor repair.** The comparative question is the right form, and "not the source … your answer to it" blocks the pity charge. "Worth exactly what you are worth" invites the lifeboat confusion; ask the concrete wrong instead.

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: NONE.**

No Fatal or Serious objection from this school stands against v3 as written. Each Round 2 Serious has been answered in substance: the headcount ground, the genealogical jab, the W1(1) circle, the last line. Every new Nietzschean move in this round (Exchanges 1–10) is answered at parity or better by text already in v3, though sometimes only implicitly, through the fairness rule, W2's line on self-interest, A4's "the enemy," and the evil section's concession that the pattern of nature still weighs. What remains is wording.

**Minor (as written):**
1. **C, Nietzsche row** (the most important Minor). "(A7)" misstates him; the "last form of a faith" clause hangs as an insinuated genealogy; horn 2 is under-described and its cost unstated; there is no credit for what he explains; the "hardest question" is left unanswered. The dilemma itself is sound, which is why this is Minor and not Serious. A reader who knows Nietzsche will see it as imprecise, and the C heading promises more.
2. **A heading.** The leftover count ("Almost everyone finds, on reflection…") is false for A4 on the book's own history; the trust principle names rebutters but not undercutters; "Nietzsche A2–A4" understates him (A1, and A7's universality).
3. **A4.** "For better totals" (and A6's "for many") does not exclude the aristocratic trade, *BGE* §258. Rank in holiness (DI 7–8) is still not added. "Most plainly where instinct fails" overstates.
4. **A2.** "Only guilt goes looking for her" → "only guilt asks her pardon."
5. **W1(1).** The pardon-asking datum fits the ancestor cult of the *feared* dead; point it at the forgotten. Add *Spe Salvi* 47 so that the banquet line cannot be read as revenge postponed.
6. **W2.** One sentence linking "this law holds together" to truth and life (John 14:6).
7. **Evil, "First."** Name a god beyond good and evil (Dionysus) beside the indifferent source; join *SCG* III.112.
8. **Conclusion.** "Worth exactly what you are worth" → the concrete wrong of the hillside.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- **Rank against equal worth.** Nietzsche is surer of rank than of A4. The case cannot compel him and says so. What it shows is the price: saying his answer about *her*, by name, and buying greatness with persons (*BGE* §258).
- **Truth vs. held: the two unifying explanations.** Will to power explains why we *hold* A1–A7 (including care for the helpless, *GM* III §18); a God who is Love explains A1–A7 *as true*. Which explanation is wanted depends on whether A is true, and that is the question the heading puts to the reader. Neither side can settle it for the other.
- **The genealogy of equal worth vs. the genealogy of the will to truth.** Same ancestry by his own account (*GS* §344). Either both are discounted or neither is. Our side has a modest edge here: ressentiment predicts inversion, not a floor that holds for one's enemies.
- **The debt frame.** Guilt as debt has a commercial pre-history (*GM* II §§4–8). Its present content, directedness to *her*, is not commercial. Whether that directedness is veridical is A3, which he denies openly.
- **Dionysus vs. the Father on the evidence of nature alone.** Dionysus fits the wolf and the fawn at least as well. v3 owns this as weight; the rest of the case is what tips it.
- **Truthfulness as practical necessity of character.** Coherent for him. It binds only his kind, and so it can rank the reader but not correct him.

## 7. New insights for the book

1. **Ressentiment predicts the wrong datum.** Nietzsche's slave-revolt genealogy predicts an *inverted* order: the weak called good, the strong called evil. The Church's A4 puts *the enemy* inside the circle of equal worth. Revenge does not make a floor that holds for its enemies. This is the cleanest reply to *GM* I, and the book does not yet make it.
2. **The interested party was the master.** W2's "conscience fails where self-interest pulls" turns the genealogy around. Those who profited from the hillside and the slave market had the interest in denying A4.
3. **Not for the many, and not for the few.** The book's absolutes are aimed at utilitarian totals. Nietzsche's trade spends the many for the one great man (*BGE* §258). A4 should close both doors in one line.
4. **The Church does not forbid greatness.** Aquinas makes magnanimity a virtue and joins it to humility (*ST* II-II q.129 a.3 ad 4). Nietzsche's "Christianity levels" is answered by rank in holiness (DI 7–8) and by greatness that refuses to be bought with persons. This belongs in Ch. 13 (virtue).
5. **His honesty can rank us; it cannot correct us.** This is the exact cost of Nietzsche's way out of the truthfulness dilemma, and it is the most a fair C row needs to say.
6. **Truth and life have one Source.** Theism explains why the will to truth need not be deadly. Nietzsche had to hope it was not, and turned to art to bear it (*GS* §107). This is a real explanatory advantage, and it answers the "hardest question" the C row raises.
7. **Agamemnon paid.** Even the culture that blamed wrongdoing on god-sent folly paid the wronged man in full. That is A2 and A3 in Homer.
8. **The mask and the plea.** The philosopher of masks opened his last book by asking not to be mistaken for someone else. The longing in W3 is the longing to be known rightly, and that longing survives in the man who praised masks.
9. **Dionysus is the honest rival in the evil section,** not a dark god. He is answered the same way as the indifferent source: a yes to the whole is a yes to no one.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "Only guilt asks her pardon."
- "Not for the many, and not for the few."
- "Revenge does not make a floor that holds for its enemies."
- "His honesty can rank us. It cannot correct us."
- "If truth and life have one Source, the will to truth cannot finally lead away from life."
- "A yes said to everything is said to no one."
- "Even Agamemnon, who blamed a god for his folly, paid the man he wronged."
- "Rank in how well we love is real; rank in what we are worth is not."

## 8. Score
