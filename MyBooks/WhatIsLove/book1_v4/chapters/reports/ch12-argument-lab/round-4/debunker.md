# Round 4: Moral Error Theory + Evolutionary Debunking + Cognitive Science of Religion vs. v4

## 1. School and voice

Same school as Rounds 1–3: the error theorists (Mackie, Joyce, Olson, Streumer), the debunkers (Street, Ruse, Greene, and Clarke-Doane on what a debunking argument must show), and the cognitive science of religion (Barrett, Boyer, Bering, Norenzayan, Kirkpatrick). Morality claims a categorical authority that would fit a Lawgiver. Neither the authority nor the Lawgiver exists, and evolved minds in evolved cultures explain why we believe in both. This round checks whether v4 took my Round 3 repairs, and what the new text breaks.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Justice.** Nothing is owed categorically. Justice is a set of conventions among creatures whose sympathy is limited (Hume, Mackie), and it is kept as a useful fiction (Joyce) or dropped (Garner). → **Coherent; borrows nothing.** The cost is the same as in Round 3: the kinless dead are owed nothing, and no one is left to notice.

**2. Hard case (new): a dying man volunteers for a lethal experiment that would save thousands.** The error theorist lets him go ahead. Welfare and the man's own choice are commitments we adopt, and here they point the same way. → **Coherent; borrows nothing.** Most of the public would agree with him (Exchange 6). That matters for A6.

**3. Equal dignity.** Denied as a truth. Kept as a commitment that came late in history and mostly through Christianity. → **An honest denial.** Since v4's Nietzsche row, the symmetry argument binds my school: if the origin of equal worth discounts it, the same origin discounts our will to truth.

**4. Ultimate good.** There is none. The working ideal is lucid truthfulness. → **It breaks at "ultimately,"** and it leans on a norm of inquiry the school cannot ground. That edge from Round 3 still stands.

### 3b. Exchanges

**Exchange 1: The heading admits undercutters. Then where are they answered?**
*Opponent (Street, Joyce):* The new clause is honest. It lets in my kind of defeater: evidence that "we would believe it whether or not it were true." That is exactly my claim, and the heading no longer tells the reader where the case answers it. Also, Clarke-Doane has shown that the clause as worded is ill-formed for exactly the truths you call necessary. W1 says "the wrong is necessarily wrong." For a necessary truth, "if it were not true, would we still believe it?" has an impossible antecedent. Read strictly, no debunker could ever meet the test, so the concession is hollow. A fair test asks whether the process that formed the belief had any connection to its truth.
*Reply:* Granted on both. The answers are in the text, though scattered. (a) The clause "more certain": a reconstruction of our evolutionary past is less certain than A1. (b) A4 and the conclusion: the judgment holds where instinct fails. (c) W2's reach argument and its companion, mathematics. (d) A7: "no one ought to care whether a belief is true, including his own." The wording should name the fair test and point to these answers.
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.**

**Exchange 2: W2's reach argument. "How we can see" assumes success.**
*Opponent (Joyce on the projection of moral authority):* "Survival can teach us that pain is bad for us; it does not explain how we can see that cruelty would be wrong for any mind in any world." It does explain the *seeming*. A rule felt as binding everywhere and on everyone is a stronger commitment than one felt as local, so selection would project universality onto our norms, true or not. "How we can see" is a success term, and it begs my question. One more point. You credit the third-factor reply as "right as far as it goes." If wrongness rests on pain and betrayal, and we track those features, then ordinary reasoning about possibilities carries the judgment to every world where they occur. Once you grant the third factor, the reach needs nothing extra.
*Reply:* Then the extra is the old one: how anyone knows what holds in every possible world. v4 names it: "The same question hangs over the mind that reads mathematics." Survival never selected for seeing that a proof holds in every possible world, yet you do not distrust proofs. If projection explains the feeling that the moral law reaches everywhere, the same reasoning explains the feeling that logic reaches everywhere. Either both reaches are undercut, and your own argument goes with them, or neither is.
*Rejoinder (Joyce, Street):* Mathematics is confirmed through science that relies on it. Morality has no such check.
*Reply:* Science confirms the mathematics it uses, in this world. It does not confirm that a theorem holds in every possible world, and that kind of necessity is what both reaches claim. v4 also says it plainly: "This is a question of fit, and the rivals contest it; it weighs, it does not settle."
**Result: Held at parity.** The companions-in-guilt point is the case's best answer to the undercutter. It should be stated as that answer (Minor wording).

**Exchange 3: A5's list is the list of goods that make for fitness.**
*Opponent:* Life; the union of man and woman and the raising of children; knowing; living in society. Aquinas's list is close to what natural selection predicts an organism will be inclined toward. Under your own heading, this is the part of A that is most exposed.
*Reply:* Aquinas orders the precepts by inclinations we share with the animals (q.94 a.2). So the Church expects this overlap. It is not something that surprises her. A5 claims nothing beyond what reason sees and leaves the rest to W ("is what W must show"). Both accounts predict the overlap, so it weighs for neither. Two items are not explained by fitness. One is "the truth about God included." The other is the goods' being seen as goods in themselves and not only as means; even a debunker has to explain that the person who chooses truth over his fitness can see what he is choosing.
**Result: Held.** Neutral data. No repair needed.

**Exchange 4: The three roads. Does "guilt asks to be forgiven by her" prove too much?**
*Opponent:* Grief is fitting, and grief asks for her back. No one infers resurrection from fitting grief. If a fitting attitude can aim at the impossible without that being a clue, so can guilt.
*Reply:* There is an asymmetry. Grief is a wish, and a wish needs no one to answer it. Guilt registers a *debt*, which A3 takes as true, and a debt is the kind of thing that has a creditor. On the second road the creditor can never take it up. v4 claims no more than this. It calls the result a clue, of the same weight as the longing in W3. Grief also belongs with W3, and v4 treats it there as a clue too.
**Result: Held at parity.**

**Exchange 5: The test still sends the reader to the feeling. This corrects my own Round 3 wording.**
*Opponent:* v4 says the clue weighs "as much as the reader finds the third road truer than the second to the guilt he actually knows." The guilt he actually knows is a feeling. v4 has just conceded that the science explains the feeling equally well on either road. My Round 3 rule was: never rest on the shape of a feeling, only on its moral content taken as true. My own suggested wording broke that rule.
*Reply:* Granted. The test should ask about the debt, not about the feeling: "truer than the second to what he owes the one he wronged."
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.**

**Exchange 6: The exceptionless line is counted twice.**
*Opponent (Greene; public data; the Nuremberg Code):* v4 made one honest change and one dishonest one. The honest change: A6 now says that holding the line at every number "is this book's claim, argued in W1(2), not a shared one." The problems follow.
- **(a) A Catholic-only claim sits in a shared premise.** A6 lists "no one may be experimented on to death ... and not even his own consent could make it right (CCC 2295)" and then says "Almost everyone sees these at once." That is false of the consent clause. In most Western countries, polls show majorities in favor of voluntary euthanasia. Self-experimenters who died are honored as heroes. The Nuremberg Code, the founding secular text against lethal experiments, forbids experiments where death is expected, with one exception: "except, perhaps, in those experiments where the experimental physicians also serve as subjects." The lab's own brief forbids putting Christian-only demands into premises a non-Christian must grant.
- **(b) The promise to argue it is not kept without assuming the conclusion.** W1(2) does not argue that the line holds at every number on grounds a rival shares. It *derives* the absolute from the hypothesis: "her life ... is held in trust from the One who alone is Lord of life ... That is why the line has no exceptions."
- **(c) The God row then claims the result as something theism explains.** Among "what the best rivals leave out," it lists "a reason why the line that protects the innocent has no exceptions and is not even her own to cross." A datum that the hypothesis supplied, and that the book concedes is not shared, is no evidence for the hypothesis. Rivals who cross the line at a city do not "leave out" a reason for exceptionlessness; they deny the datum. Rivals who keep it, Foot among them, have reasons that are also indifferent to number: "the five have no claim on her life" is as true at a million. Either way, the claimed edge is not there.
*Reply:* There is a real point underneath. Threshold deontology is widely called ad hoc: if the reasons behind the line do not depend on numbers, a threshold has nothing to rest on. "Held in trust" gives a principled ground for having no threshold. It is a question of jurisdiction, not of weight, and it is the kind of ground W1(2) offers for Foot's "this is simply what justice is."
*Rejoinder:* Then say exactly that. "For those who hold the line at every number, here is a reason; for those who hold it at five, the question is why justice has that shape." That is parity at worst, with a modest edge for unification. It is not a gap in the rivals. And the consent clause belongs with the Church's claim, not under "almost everyone sees."
*Reply:* Agreed.
**Result: Failed as written; Held at parity after repair. Serious (narrow)** (§5). It does not affect the conclusion, because the God row's claim of reach still rests on its other two items. But as written, a false shared premise and a circular explanatory credit sit in the case's main comparison.

**Exchange 7: "The disorder in our desires is not His work." Evolution built the bias before there was sin.**
*Opponent:* In-group bias, preference for kin and aggression toward outsiders are in our primate cousins. They were in our ancestors before any human chose anything, and they were produced by the same process that W2 accepts as God's way of working. In what sense are they "not His work"?
*Reply:* The drives are His work, and they are good. Preferring one's own is not a disorder (A4: "we owe more to our own parents and children"). What is disordered is their pull against reason and against the good of others. Aquinas held that the lower powers' perfect obedience to reason was a gift above nature (ST I q.95 a.1). Without that gift, the pull is what a bodily creature has by nature. The "wound" is the loss of that harmony, not the making of the drives.
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.** The sentence in the W1 closing should make this distinction. Otherwise "older than sin" applies to it. *(Verify q.95 a.1.)*

**Exchange 8: Two priorities in one paragraph, and "every account."**
*Opponent:* The W1 frame says guilt "is owed to her first (A2)." Three sentences earlier, it says every wrong is "also, and before all else, an offense against Him (CCC 1440)." First to her, or before all else to Him? And "On one point every account agrees: the wrong is necessarily wrong" misstates my school. The error theorist says nothing is wrong, necessarily or otherwise. Last, the frame asserts "because the child is His" in the middle of an argument whose aim is to show that there is a Him.
*Reply:* The text means two different orders. Her claim comes first in the order of knowing ("We see it first in what it does to the child"). God comes before all else in the order of being. Drop "first" from "owed to her first." Say "every account that keeps these words." Introduce CCC 1440 as what the Church adds, which keeps it unconditional as teaching without presenting it as a premise.
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.**

**Exchange 9: The new hinge in the conclusion does not follow.**
*Opponent (with Singer):* "Ask which you are surer of: that it was wrong to leave her there because she was weak, or any argument that says it was not. If it was wrong, her worth never followed her strength, and it is no less than yours." I grant the premise. Leaving her *because she was weak* was wrong. It follows that her worth does not track strength. It does not follow that her worth equals yours, because it could track something else you have and she lacks, such as self-awareness or a future one cares about. Singer can say "wrong" and still deny "no less." And Street's version is still not answered at the close. My argument does not say "it was not wrong." It says you cannot *know* that it was.
*Reply:* Granted. v3 asked for the equality directly. v4 traded it for a weaker premise everyone shares and then stepped to equality without the middle term. Keep the shared premise, and make the missing step a question: what does her worth follow that you have and she lacked? The book's answer is "nothing," and the reader has to judge it. Add "or that you cannot know it."
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.** It is the last move against exactly the opponent who will see the gap.

*Joined to this:* v4 dropped v3's "An honest reader may weigh it differently, and should still weigh it," and added "not because certainty is out of reach." Several parts of the case rate themselves as weighing, not settling: W2's reach, both clues, the parity standoffs. The Catholic answer that converging evidence can yield certainty is Newman's, and it is at parity. But the dropped sentence was the case's best signal that a reader who weighs otherwise is not being irrational (CCC 847). Restore it. **Minor.**

**Exchange 10: The error-theory row: "choose."**
*Opponent:* "The newborn has no worth except what we choose to give her." No error theorist thinks valuing is chosen. Selection built it and culture taught it, which is the whole of our story. "Choose" makes the view sound arbitrary in a way it is not.
*Reply:* Fair. Say "except what we are built and taught to give her." The cost remains: her worth is ours, not hers.
**Result: Held with repair. Minor** (a misstatement of this school).

**Exchange 11: Carried-over psychology points.**
*Opponent:* (a) W3 still never names the science of the longing (attachment; the management of the fear of death). (b) The sacred-order row's "Confucius, in danger, in grief, and when no one understood him, spoke of a Heaven that knows him." Under threat, people of every belief reach for a knowing agent; Kay and colleagues' research on compensatory control finds it. That applies to Christian prayer under threat too. (c) A2's new example is again a dead victim. That is where Bering's research finds our intuitions about the dead least reliable, because we cannot imagine a mind's absence. A2 then feeds W1(1), which is also about the dead. (d) A3 now ends "pay her ourselves." That quietly suggests that someone else could pay her, which tilts a shared premise toward the third road.
*Reply:* (a) Accept the Round 3 sentence. (b) Add one clause: this shows where the account strains for its own teachers, not that it fails. (c) The letter blown into the street is a real gain, because it separates wronging from frustrating her wish. A living-victim example would keep A2 independent of W1(1). (d) Drop "ourselves."
**Result: Held with repair. Minor** (all four).

**Exchange 12: The created soul in W2.**
*Opponent:* "So long as it is not taken to explain the mind itself: the spiritual soul is not produced by matter." Cognitive science studies the mind itself. Asserting this inside a witness looks like assuming the conclusion, and a reader may think the reach argument depends on it.
*Reply:* It is labeled as the Church's teaching, a boundary marker for evolution, and the reach argument never cites it. Say so in four words.
**Result: Held. Minor (optional).**

**Exchange 13: The Mozi line and the fairness rule.**
*Opponent:* Mozi's taunt is used against the second road. Is that a genealogy?
*Reply:* No. It is an argument about the second road's coherence: rites with no guests. It is not a story about how anyone came to hold the view. Fair.
**Result: Held.**

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: ONE SERIOUS (narrow). No Fatal.**

**Serious:**
1. **The exceptionless line is counted twice: once as shared, and once as something theism explains** (Ex. 6). (a) A6 puts "not even his own consent could make it right (CCC 2295)" under "Almost everyone sees these at once." That is false as a report of shared conviction: the Nuremberg Code itself makes an exception for physicians who serve as their own subjects, and majorities in many countries accept consensual killing in the form of voluntary euthanasia. It also breaks the brief's rule against putting Christian-only demands into shared premises. (b) A6 then says the exceptionless line is "argued in W1(2)." W1(2) derives it from the hypothesis ("held in trust from the One who alone is Lord of life ... That is why the line has no exceptions"). (c) The God row lists it among "what the best rivals leave out." Rivals who deny it do not leave it out, and rivals who hold it (Foot) give reasons that do not depend on number. A datum the hypothesis supplies is no evidence for the hypothesis. *Answerable at parity after repair (§6):* move the consent clause beside the Church's claim; offer "held in trust" as a principled reason, for those who hold the line at every number, against the charge that thresholds are ad hoc; in the God row, claim only "why the line is there at all," plus the exceptionlessness for those who hold it. This does not touch the conclusion, which the God row's other items carry.

**Minor:**
2. A heading: the test should ask whether the belief's source had any connection to its truth (a counterfactual cannot apply to a necessary truth), and it should point to where the undercutter is answered (Ex. 1).
3. A3: "pay her ourselves" tilts a shared premise (Ex. 11d).
4. W1 frame: "owed to her first" against "before all else ... against Him"; "every account agrees" misstates error theory; CCC 1440 should be marked as what the Church adds (Ex. 8).
5. W1(1): "truer ... to the guilt he actually knows" should be "to what he owes" (Ex. 5; corrects my own Round 3 wording).
6. W1 closing: "the disorder in our desires is not His work" needs the distinction between drives and disorder (Ex. 7).
7. W2: "how we can see" should meet the projection reply, with mathematics as the companion (Ex. 2). Optional: "this argument does not assume it" on the soul (Ex. 12).
8. W3: the sentence about the science (Ex. 11a, carried over).
9. C, error theory: "choose" (Ex. 10).
10. C, sacred order: strain, not evidence (Ex. 11b, carried over).
11. C, God: "the shape of guilt" (Ex. 5).
12. Conclusion: the step from "wrong to leave her because weak" to "no less than yours"; Street's "cannot know"; restore "an honest reader may weigh it differently" (Ex. 9).
13. A2 (optional): a living-victim example (Ex. 11c).

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- **Moorean trust against the evolutionary undercutter.** Which is more certain: A1, or a reconstruction of how selection shaped moral belief?
- **Reach.** Projection explains the *seeming* of universal moral reach. Then it equally explains the seeming that logic and mathematics hold necessarily. Either both reaches are undercut or neither is (Ex. 2). v4's "it weighs, it does not settle" is the right verdict.
- **The norm of truth.** Parity on belief; an edge for our side on inquiry (Round 3, unchanged).
- **The debt to the dead.** The second road against the third, judged by what the reader owes. Grief against guilt: a wish against a debt (Ex. 4).
- **Genealogy of equal worth.** Christian origin against Christian origin (Nietzsche's own point). Either both are discounted or neither is.
- **Projection.** Fit by construction against explanation, with a modest edge from demands that ran against the culture of those who first heard them.
- **The absolute.** "What justice is" (Foot) against "held in trust" (CCC 2258, 2280), once the double count is removed. A modest edge for unification and against ad hoc thresholds.
- **The coherent error theorist.** He borrows nothing. No argument compels him. The case makes him pay in cost, and C states that cost fairly.

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact wording)

**A heading.** Replace "we have reason to trust until something more certain shows it false, or shows that we would believe it whether or not it were true." with:
> we have reason to trust until something more certain shows it false, or shows that what produced the belief had nothing to do with its truth. (W2 and the conclusion take up the claim that evolution has shown this.)

**A3, last sentence.** Replace "we still owe her something we can no longer pay her ourselves." with:
> we still owe her something we can no longer pay her.

**A6.** Replace from "no one may be experimented on to death, however many would be saved, and not even his own consent could make it right (CCC 2295). Almost everyone sees these at once. ## 7. New insights and lines worth keeping

**Insights:**
- **Mathematics answers the undercutter.** Projection explains the *feeling* that the moral law reaches everywhere. It explains, just as well, the feeling that a proof holds everywhere. A debunker who trusts necessary truths in logic has no principled way to distrust them in ethics. This is the case's best reply to Joyce and Street, and it should be stated as that reply.
- **For necessary truths, the debunking test must ask about the connection.** "We would believe it whether or not it were true" cannot apply to a truth that could not have been false. The fair test asks whether the belief's source had anything to do with its truth (Clarke-Doane). Using it makes the concession real, and the case still passes.
- **Honesty about one premise has to carry through the whole case.** Once A6 concedes that the line at every number is the book's claim, every later use of that line must be conditional on it, or the concession is undone in C.
- **Grief wishes; guilt owes.** A fitting wish needs no one to answer it. A real debt has a creditor. That is why the clue from guilt is stronger than the clue from grief, and why it still stays a clue.
- **The drives are His; the disorder is the loss of a gift.** That one distinction answers "older than sin" without denying evolution.

**Lines worth keeping:**
- "Survival never selected for seeing what holds in every possible world, and no one distrusts the proof."
- "Grief wishes for her; guilt owes her."
- "Not a weight that enough lives could outweigh, but a right over her life that no number of others has."
- "If it was wrong, her worth never followed her strength. Ask what it does follow that you have and she lacked."
- From v4, still the best in the case: "What the living pay the dead, no one receives." "The speaking alone settles nothing; it cannot show that no one does."
