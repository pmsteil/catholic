# Round 4: Doctrinal Censor's Second Read (v4, pre-imprimatur)

**Scope.** v4 only (EVOLUTION.md, "v4 — Round 4"). I checked it against my Round 3 findings (round-3/censor.md, #1–#51 and R1–R19), then read all new text against Scripture (RSVCE), the Catechism, Vatican I and II, VS, *Spe Salvi*, *Dignitas Infinita*, *Salvifici Doloris*, *Dominus Iesus* and Aquinas. This read is not scored.

**Bottom line.** All five Round 3 Errors are fixed, and fixed correctly. Nearly every Needs-care item is fixed too. The new W1(1) paragraph is now sound Catholic teaching: only God forgives sins, through the Church, amends are still owed, the soul lives, the communion of saints, the resurrection. **Two new Errors**, each a one-clause fix:
- **E1.** W1 says guilt "is owed to her *first*." Three sentences earlier, the same paragraph quotes CCC 1440: every such wrong is "before all else, an offense against Him." The paragraph contradicts itself and the Catechism.
- **E2.** The Evil section says God does not intend natural harms "either as an end or as a means," and cites ST I q.19 a.9 and q.49 a.2. Those articles say the opposite. In willing the natural order, God "wills some things to be naturally corrupted." He causes corruption "as it were by accident."

There are also seven Needs-care items and a few minor ones. The most important is *Spe Salvi* 47: v4 presents as the Church's own statement ("She says") what the encyclical presents as the opinion of "some recent theologians," which Benedict adopts.

## 4. Replacement wording (every Error and Needs-care item)

**R-E1 (W1, replaces "and this book agrees: it is owed to her first (A2).").**
> … and this book agrees: it is owed to her, truly and in her own right, and not to God alone (A2).

**R-E2 (Evil, *Second*; replaces "What a free creature does is his own act, not God's (Chapter 11); and the harms that follow in a world of real causes God does not intend, either as an end or as a means, though He wills the order they come with (ST I q.19 a.9; q.49 a.2).").** This also covers N8.
> The evil a free creature does is his own, not God's: God is in no way the cause of moral evil (CCC 311; Chapter 11). The losses that come in a world of real causes God never wills for their own sake; He wills them only as bound up with the order of the whole He made, the order in which the wolf lives by the fawn (ST I q.19 a.9; q.48 a.2 ad 3; q.49 a.2).

**R-N1 (W1(1), replaces from "She says that grace" through "(*Spe Salvi* 47).").**
> She says that grace "does not make wrong into right," and that the wrongdoer does not sit at the eternal banquet beside his victim as though nothing had happened (*Spe Salvi* 44). Pope Benedict, with some recent theologians, sees the fire that both burns and saves as Christ Himself: before His gaze "all falsehood melts away," and the touch of His heart heals whoever is open to Him (*Spe Salvi* 47).

**R-N2 (W1(1)).**
> And no one dies with no one left to speak for her: at every Mass the Church prays for the dead, and she commends to God even those whose faith He alone has known.

**R-N3 (W1(2), replaces "It is not added later and cannot be lost, not even by her own refusal (DI 15).").**
> It is not added later, and it cannot be lost, not even by her own sins (DI 15, 22): sin can stain how worthily she lives, never what she is worth (DI 7).
