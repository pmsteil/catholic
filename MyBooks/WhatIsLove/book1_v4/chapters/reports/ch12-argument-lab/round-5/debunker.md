# Round 5: Moral Error Theory + Evolutionary Debunking + Cognitive Science of Religion vs. v5

## 1. School and voice

Same school as Rounds 1–4: the error theorists (Mackie, Joyce, Olson, Streumer), the debunkers (Street, Ruse, Greene, with Clarke-Doane on what a debunking argument must show), and the cognitive science of religion (Barrett, Boyer, Bering, Norenzayan, Kirkpatrick). Morality claims a categorical authority that would fit a Lawgiver. Neither the authority nor the Lawgiver exists, and evolved minds in evolved cultures explain why we believe in both. This round checks v5's repair of my Round 4 Serious objection and Minor points, and whether the new wording broke anything.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Justice.** Nothing is owed categorically. Justice is a set of conventions among creatures whose sympathy is limited (Hume, Mackie), kept as a useful fiction (Joyce) or dropped (Garner). → **Coherent; borrows nothing.** The kinless dead are owed nothing.

**2. Hard case: a dying man volunteers for a lethal experiment that would save thousands.** We let him go ahead: welfare and his own choice both point that way. → **Coherent; borrows nothing.** v5 now records this view honestly in A6 ("many ... would let ... a volunteer give his life to an experiment").

**3. Equal dignity.** We deny it as a truth and keep it as a commitment that came late, mostly through Christianity. → **An honest denial.** The symmetry of genealogies (Nietzsche row) binds us: if that origin discounts equal worth, it discounts our will to truth too.

**4. Ultimate good.** There is none. Our working ideal is lucid truthfulness. → **It breaks at "ultimately,"** and it leans on a norm of inquiry the school cannot ground (A7, W2). Unchanged since Round 3.

### 3b. Exchanges

**Exchange 1: The fix for the double count, and the two phrases that undo part of it.**
*Opponent (Greene; the threshold deontologists):* A6 now says honestly that holding the line at every number is the book's claim and not a shared one: "Fewer hold the line when the numbers grow vast." W1(2) repeats it: "Many let the line bend when the numbers grow vast." Two new phrases say otherwise. (a) A4 is a shared premise, and the heading says most people "cannot give [it] up." Its new wording is "never, if innocent, to be deliberately killed for others' sake, not for the many and not for the few." "Not for the many" with no limit is the line at every number. In v4 it was not there. (b) In the God row, theism grounds "the rule that no total may be weighed against one person, which the best rivals share and take as given." No total, at any size, is the absolute. The rivals who bend the line at vast numbers do weigh totals against one person, and A6 says they are many. The very next clause then limits exceptionlessness to "those who hold it with the Church." Read literally, the row contradicts itself.
*Reply:* Both phrases were meant to name the line most people hold: the surgeon and the five, the riot. W1(2)'s grounding claim is made at that level: the rivals draw the line, and at their stopping point they take its shape as given. The conditional clause shows the intent.
*Rejoinder:* Then write what you mean. As written, A4 states as shared what A6 says is not, and the God row credits theism with grounding an absolute that it says the rivals share. That reopens the gap your Round 4 fix closed.
*Reply:* Granted. Neither phrase now carries an explanatory credit that is not also stated conditionally. In the God row, exceptionlessness is claimed only "for those who hold it with the Church," and in W1(2) only "on this account." So the conclusion is not inflated. But the text is internally inconsistent, and the repair is two short edits (§6).
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.** It is a regression of Round 4's Serious objection, but the conditional clauses limit it to wording.

**Exchange 2: The source-connection test, turned on theism.**
*Opponent (Barrett, Boyer, Norenzayan):* The A heading now admits defeat for a belief when "what produced the belief had nothing to do with its truth." Apply it to belief in God. Agency detection, minds built to imagine other minds, and moralizing gods that kept large groups cooperative all produce belief in a knowing, watching moral agent, whether or not one exists. So by the book's own test, the Lawgiver is debunked.
*Reply:* The test governs A, which is a list of things that still seem true after reflection. The Lawgiver is not on that list. He is the conclusion of an inference from A1–A7, and an inference is judged by its premises and its logic, not by the origin of the arguer's tendencies. C's fairness rule says this both ways: no genealogy is used against a rival that the book would not let him use against it. Where v5 does lean on a seeming of this kind (Confucius's knowing Heaven, the longing in W3, Kant's moral Ruler), it labels it "strain," "not evidence," or "a clue," and W1(1) concedes outright that "the speaking alone settles nothing."
*Rejoinder:* The God row still ends with "Someone ... is what guilt's request to be forgiven ... suggest[s]." Minds feel guilt as being seen by an agent (Bering), and that is the feeling this test should discount.
*Reply:* The request in A2 is addressed to the victim, not to a watcher: "I did this to her." The step to Someone at the ground goes through W1(1)'s dead victim and the debt, which is moral content offered as true, not a feeling of being watched. "Suggest" is the right weight.
**Result: Held.** The test is honest, and the case passes it on its own terms.

**Exchange 3: W2's new reach sentence. Do Darwin's bees break the mathematics parallel?**
*Opponent (Street, Joyce):* "Survival no more selected for seeing what holds in every possible world when we read a proof." Granted about reach. But the debunker's sharper point is about safety: we could easily have believed otherwise. W2's own example shows it. Darwin's hive-bee humans would hold fratricide a sacred duty. Nothing comparable holds for arithmetic: creatures who could not count predators did not survive. So moral belief is less safe than mathematical belief, and the parallel fails before reach even comes up.
*Reply:* The bees vary the part of the law that W2 already concedes is "fitted to our kind." For them, killing brothers serves the hive; it is not gratuitous. W2's reach claim is about the core: gratuitous cruelty, betrayal, treating a person as a thing. A creature that finds those fine is as easy to imagine as one that finds modus ponens invalid. Neither shows that our faculty could easily have been theirs. And survival favored getting counting right in this world, never seeing that a theorem must hold in every world. Reach against reach, the parallel stands.
*Rejoinder:* The third-factor point from Round 4 also stands: once wrongness rests on pain and betrayal, ordinary reasoning about possibilities carries the judgment everywhere.
*Reply:* Then the question is how reasoning reaches necessity at all, in logic as in ethics. That is the question W2 leaves "weigh[ing], not settl[ing]."
**Result: Held at parity.** The new wording broke nothing. "He does not distrust the proof" is fair: debunkers who are nominalists doubt mathematical objects, not the validity of proofs.

**Exchange 4: Does "what he owes" favor the second road?**
*Opponent:* v5 took my wording: the clue weighs "as much as the reader finds the third road truer than the second to what he owes the one he wronged." But forgiveness is never owed to the wrongdoer. What he owes is apology and repair, and the second road provides both: memory, honoring her wishes, care for those she loved. So the test I proposed now favors my side.
*Reply:* A3, which this school treats as a shared premise only for argument's sake, says "we still owe her something we can no longer fully pay her." What is missing is not the apology. It is her receiving it. An apology that no one can ever hear is the second road's own admission, "what the living pay, she is not there to take up." Whether a debt is paid when no creditor can take it up is exactly what the reader must judge. The grief/guilt asymmetry from Round 4 is still the hinge: a wish needs no one to answer it; a debt is owed to someone.
**Result: Held at parity.** The test asks about moral content, not a feeling, and it favors neither road by its wording.

**Exchange 5: "Then it follows what she is." The middle term again.**
*Opponent (with Singer, whom the A heading names as denying parts of A4):* The close asks whether I am surer "that she was wronged when she was left there, weak and not yet able to know herself." I grant it. Leaving an infant to die of exposure is cruel, because she can suffer. From that, v5 concludes "her worth never followed her strength or her mind. Then it follows what she is, and she is that as fully as you are." That does not follow. Her being wronged may follow her capacity to suffer, which she has. That is a mind, and the passage's "or her mind" either covers it, in which case the inference is false, or leaves it out, in which case the disjunction is incomplete. And an account that grounds the wrong in suffering need not give her a worth equal to yours: on it, a painless death is a lesser wrong. My Round 4 repair turned this into a question ("what does it follow that you have and she lacked?"). v5 asserts the answer. Also, "Then it follows what she is" reads at first as a logical "it follows," which claims exactly the validity that is missing.
*Reply:* The premise has to close the route through suffering, or the close argues past the one reader it addresses. Put the Moorean question where the A4-denier actually stands: would she have been wronged if she had been left to die without pain? He denies it. Then ask which he is surer of: that, or his argument.
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.** It is the last move against exactly the reader who will see the gap. The main conclusion does not rest on it: A4 and W1(2) carry the case for equal worth.

**Exchange 6: My own W3 sentence overclaims.**
*Opponent (Kirkpatrick, Bering):* v5 took my Round 3 sentence, and I now have to retract its last clause: "those paths explain a longing for a protector more easily than a longing to be known wholly and told that it is good that we exist." That is not what the research shows. Attachment theory is about a secure base that accepts the child without conditions, which is very close to "it is good that you exist." Kirkpatrick's account of God as an attachment figure is an all-knowing, always present, wholly accepting one. Bering's work concerns the sense of being known and of one's life meaning something. These paths explain the longing to be known wholly and accepted at least as easily as the longing for a protector.
*Reply:* Fair. W3 already says "That is not evidence." The sentence should make the same concession that W1(1) makes about speaking to the dead: the science explains the longing either way, so the longing alone settles nothing, and the science cannot show that no one answers it.
**Result: Held with repair. Minor** (a correction of my own wording, as in Round 4, Ex. 5).

**Exchange 7: "A wronged person needs forgiving."**
*Opponent:* A2: "a wrong sum needs correcting; a flawed character needs reforming; a wronged person needs forgiving." W1(1) says it again. In English, "X needs forgiving" means X needs to be forgiven, as in "the car needs washing." Read that way, the victim is the one who needs pardon. The wording has been there since v2. It matters because A2 is the premise that guilt faces the victim.
*Reply:* Granted. The intended sense is "a wrong done to a person needs forgiving."
**Result: Held with repair. Minor.**

**Exchange 8: The new consent distinction.**
*Opponent:* "A man may lay down his life, but he may not hand it over to be destroyed." That is a distinction made to fit: the firefighter, yes; the volunteer subject, no.
*Reply:* A6 labels it the Church's teaching and the book's claim, and the case nowhere counts it as shared. The distinction itself is principled. One man accepts a death that comes as a side effect of saving others; the other makes his death the means. A6 already says that side effects may be weighed and that persons may not be spent. A reader may reject the distinction without rejecting anything in the shared premises.
**Result: Held.** This is how the Round 4 fix was meant to work.

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: NONE.**

No Fatal or Serious objection stands against v5 as written. My Round 4 Serious objection (the exceptionless line counted as shared and as explained) is fixed where it did its damage. A6 labels the teaching as the book's claim, W1(2) offers "held in trust" only "on this account," and the God row claims exceptionlessness only "for those who hold it with the Church." Two new phrases contradict that labeling, but every explanatory credit that depends on the absolute is stated conditionally, so the problem is wording, not a circular credit.

**Minor:**
1. **A4 and the God row put the absolute back among the shared claims** (Ex. 1). The two phrases are "not for the many and not for the few," and "no total may be weighed against one person, which the best rivals share." This regression is new in v5.
2. **Conclusion: "Then it follows what she is"** asserts the middle term. Her being wronged may follow her capacity to suffer, which the premise leaves open, so the step does not reach the A4-denier it addresses (Ex. 5).
3. **W3: "explain a longing for a protector more easily than a longing to be known wholly"** overclaims the science. This is my own Round 3 wording (Ex. 6).
4. **A2 and W1(1): "a wronged person needs forgiving"** says the victim needs pardon (Ex. 7). The wording dates from v2.
5. *Optional:* A2 with a living-victim example (Round 4, item 13).

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- **Moorean trust against the evolutionary undercutter.** Which is more certain: A1, or a reconstruction of how selection shaped moral belief?
- **Reach and safety.** Projection explains the seeming of universal moral reach as well as it explains the seeming of logical necessity. Darwin's bees vary the part of the law fitted to our kind, not the core (Ex. 3).
- **The norm of truth.** Parity on belief; an edge for our side on inquiry.
- **The debt to the dead.** The second road against the third, judged by what is owed. A wish needs no one to answer it; a debt is owed to someone (Ex. 4).
- **Genealogy of equal worth, and of theistic belief.** Origin against origin. The fairness rule cuts both ways, and the case uses it both ways (Ex. 2).
- **The absolute.** "What justice is" (Foot) against "held in trust," now offered conditionally. A modest edge for unification and against ad hoc thresholds.
- **The coherent error theorist.** He borrows nothing, and no argument compels him. C states his cost fairly.

If she was wronged, her worth never followed her strength, her mind, or what she could feel. Ask what it does follow, that she had as fully as you. ## 7. New insights and lines worth keeping

**Insights:**
- **A label has to hold everywhere in the text.** v5 placed the absolute correctly in A6, W1(2) and the God row's conditional clause, and two new phrases elsewhere undid it. Every sentence that touches the line at every number should be checked against A6's "not shared."
- **The source-connection test does not debunk the conclusion.** It governs seemings, and the Lawgiver is an inference. The case keeps faith with this by labeling every seeming it uses as strain, clue, or "not evidence."
- **Darwin's bees attack content, not reach.** What evolution could easily have varied is the part of the law fitted to our kind, which the case already concedes. The core (gratuitous cruelty) is as hard to imagine reversed for a reasoner as modus ponens is.
- **To reach the reader who denies A4, the premise must exclude pain.** Otherwise the wrong rests on suffering and equal worth does not follow. The honest Moorean question is about the painless death.
- **The science explains the longing either way.** W3 should say so as plainly as W1(1) says it of speaking to the dead.

**Lines worth keeping:**
- "A man may lay down his life, but he may not hand it over to be destroyed." (v5)
- "Not a second injury beside hers, but His love refused in her." (v5)
- "The speaking alone settles nothing; it cannot show that no one does." (v4; still the best line in the case for this school)
- "Grief wishes for her; guilt owes her."
- "Survival no more selected for seeing what holds in every possible world when we read a proof." (v5)
