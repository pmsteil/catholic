# Argument Lab, Round 5: Nietzschean opponent vs. v5

## 1. School and voice

Nietzsche of the mature works (*Beyond Good and Evil*, *Genealogy*, *Gay Science*, *Twilight*, *Antichrist*, *Ecce Homo*), argued as Leiter, Reginster and Bernard Williams argue him. Round 5 first checks what v5 did with the five Round 4 repairs. It then turns his order of rank (*Rangordnung*), his genealogy and his immanent critique on the new wording. All Nietzsche material is paraphrased, and the fairness rule applies both ways.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges (short)

1. **Justice:** exchange between parties of roughly equal power (*GM* II §8). At its height it overcomes itself in mercy, which only the strong can afford (*GM* II §10). *Coherent, and it borrows nothing. It breaks A4.*
2. **Dilemma:** one ventilator, the same prognosis, a composer of genius and a man with profound disability. It goes to the composer, without remorse (*BGE* §126). *It decides the case, and nearly every reader rejects it when it is said about a named patient.*
3. **Equal dignity:** denied. There is an order of rank. The lower have real claims, including gentleness from the higher, which is the higher man's duty (*Antichrist* §57), but their claims are lesser (*BGE* §257). *A consistent graded view. This round it is the key to Ex. 7.*
4. **Flourishing:** become who you are; self-overcoming; *amor fati*. *Good for a few, with the many as their base (*BGE* §258). It is not an answer for humans as such.*

### 3b. Exchanges

**Exchange 1. "Before all else," now dressed as identity.**
*Nietzsche:* The priest has learned subtlety. You say the offense against God is "not a second injury beside hers … but His love refused in her." Then why "before all else"? If it adds no injury, the priority is empty. If it is not empty, God has again taken the front of the queue, and the victim's claim is a shadow of His (*GM* II §§21–22). And W1(1) now opens: "The Catholic answer begins with God."
*Patrick's side:* "Before all else" is about how grave the wrong is, not about who collects. A wrong against her is graver than a wrong against a thing because the Love that made her precious is refused in it. That deepens her claim and does not compete with it. The test of the priest's shift is the one you set: does the debt to God swallow the debt to her? v5 answers in four places: "owed to her, truly and in her own right, and not to God alone"; absolution "does not cancel what we owe her" (CCC 1459); "God does not take her place in giving the pardon that is hers to give"; "What He keeps is her claim, and her." "Begins with God" is the order of explanation. The paragraph then spends most of its length on her.
*Rejoinder:* An empty priority is still a priority.
*Reply:* It is not empty. It says why the wrong is grave. What would make it the priest's move is God taking her place, and the text forbids that by name.
**Held.** This is now the book's clearest answer to *GM* II §22.

**Exchange 2. Horn 2 and *GM* III §27 now say opposite things.**
*Nietzsche:* You added my hope, that truthfulness would at last turn on Christianity itself. But one sentence earlier you still say that on horn 2 my unmasking "cannot correct us … cannot show anyone that he ought to give it up." Both cannot be right. If your own honesty can bring you to give up God, then my unmasking can correct you. It shows you that you ought to give it up by your own ought. That is the whole method of *GM* III §27 and *GS* §357.
*Patrick's side:* Granted. The new sentence states your method correctly, and the old clause is now a leftover that contradicts it. The dilemma still holds once horn 2's cost is stated properly. An immanent critique uses our honesty. It can correct us only as long as we hold that honesty binds. It cannot show that anyone ought to keep that honesty, his own included. If honesty binds every mind, we are on horn 1 and A7 stands. The cost of horn 2 is borrowed authority, not no authority.
*Rejoinder:* Borrowed authority still convicts you.
*Reply:* Then it convicts us by A7, and the case is decided on the evidence, which is what the new sentence already says.
**Held with repair (Minor; it still misstates his method in one clause).**

**Exchange 3. "Honesty need not be fatal."**
*Nietzsche:* Your martyrs' honesty was fatal. And you count that to their credit.
*Patrick's side:* As in Round 4: the claim the book needs is that truth can never finally be life's enemy, not that honesty never costs a life. Both sides hold that something is worth more than survival (*BGE* §13). The phrase should say what the book means.
**Held with repair (Minor, carried over).**

**Exchange 4. A4's new "for others' sake."**
*Nietzsche:* You narrowed A4 to keep chosen death out of the shared premises. Does it still close my door? It does, I grant. When I say the ill-constituted should perish and that we should help them (*Antichrist* §2; *Twilight*, Skirmishes §36), it is for the sake of ascending life. That is for others. But look at what you opened. A physician who ends a disabled newborn's life "for her own good," with no request because she can make none, kills her neither for others' sake nor at her own asking. A4 no longer forbids that. A6's named claim covers only "a man's own request or consent." The newborn and the severely disabled head A4's own list, and this is the threat they actually face today.
*Patrick's side:* Fair. It is a side effect of moving consent out of A4, and it is chiefly Singer's ground, not yours. The fix is to keep the shared floor exactly where the drafters wanted it: forbid killing without the person's own asking, and leave the disputed case of a request to A6. One clause does it (section 6).
**Held with repair (Minor; caused by new wording).**

**Exchange 5. The new debunking test, turned on equal worth.**
*Nietzsche:* Your heading now distrusts a belief when "what produced the belief had nothing to do with its truth." Good. Belief in equal worth was produced by the slave revolt in morality, the ressentiment of the weak (*GM* I §§7, 10). Ressentiment has nothing to do with truth. Under your own test, A4 is undercut.
*Patrick's side:* Take the genealogy at its word and see what it predicts. Ressentiment predicts inversion: the strong are evil, the weak are good. It does not predict what A4 asserts, a floor that holds for the enemy and that a saint cannot rise above in worth. W2 also names a process that has to do with truth: conscience corrects itself against interest, "toward those who can never repay," by reason holding itself to consistency. And by the fairness rule, your account gives the will to truth the same Christian ancestry, a point the C row already makes, so ancestry undercuts both or neither.
*Rejoinder:* The consistency you praise is ressentiment learning to argue.
*Reply:* Then it argues well, and an argument is judged by its steps, not by who first made it. That is A7.
**Held at parity.** v5's source-connection wording is fair to the debunker and costs the case nothing here.

**Exchange 6. The lion and the parasite.**
*Nietzsche:* "In willing the order He wills some things to be naturally corrupted." Then He wills the child's body to be corrupted by the parasite. "A person it never gives away" is a theologian's distinction. Dionysus at least admits he made the parasite.
*Patrick's side:* The distinction is about direction, not wording. In the animal order, one creature's loss is ordered to another's life. A person is governed for her own sake (SCG III.112), so the loss that reaches her is never ordered *to* the parasite. It is ordered to her own good, which the Third names: "this child, herself, body and all, will be raised." Whether that hope is true is the whole case. v5 states it as a setting, not an explanation ("Beyond that she claims no explanation").
**Held at parity** (mostly the evil school's ground).

**Exchange 7. The conclusion: "wronged" is not yet "wronged no less."**
*Nietzsche:* Last round I gave you "wronged." Now I take it back, because I have read your new sentence closely. "Ask which you are surer of: that she was wronged when she was left there … If she was wronged, her worth never followed her strength or her mind." My order of rank grants it at once. Yes, she was wronged. The lower have claims, and the higher owe them gentleness. Her claim was simply less than his. Wronging a being shows that it has *some* claim. It does not show that the claim is *equal*, or that it does not grow and shrink with strength. Your own Catechism forbids cruelty to animals without making the dog the equal of the man. Singer grants that leaving the infant to suffer wronged her, and still holds that infants count for less. McMahan and Kagan argue for graded status in detail. Your premise no longer separates the believer in A4 from the people the paragraph is addressed to, "the one who gives up A4." v4 at least said "*because* she was weak." You have dropped even that.
*Patrick's side:* The objection is valid. Being wronged establishes a claim. It does not establish an equal claim, and "Then it follows what she is" overstates the step. W1(2) argues for equal worth on separate grounds ("an equal call … explains an equal worth"), and this objection does not touch that. But the conclusion's own "if … then" fails against a graded view, and that is exactly the view of the reader it is speaking to. The repair is to ask the question that does separate the views: was she wronged *no less gravely* than a strong child would have been? Nearly everyone is surer of that than of any argument against it. Killing a disabled newborn is not a lesser murder. The rank-theorist must then say "a lesser wrong" about her, by name. That is the true price, and it is far higher than saying "not wronged," which he never has to say.
*Rejoinder:* I will say it. She was worth less.
*Reply:* Then the case has done what it promised. It cannot compel you. It shows you what your certainty costs, said about her.
**Failed as written. Held with repair.** **Serious:** the last step of the case is invalid against its own named audience, and the new wording widened the gap.

**Exchange 8. "Or that you cannot know it."**
*Nietzsche:* That is my perspectivism. There are no facts, only interpretations.
*Patrick's side:* v5 does well to name that exit, and the question applies to it as much as to the others. Are you surer that you cannot know whether she was wronged than you are that she was? Your own perspectivism ranks perspectives, so it does not treat them all alike.
**Held.**

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: one Serious. No Fatal.**

**Serious**
1. **Conclusion, the step to equal worth (Ex. 7).** "If she was wronged, her worth never followed her strength or her mind. Then it follows what she is, and she is that as fully as you are."
   - The step is invalid against a graded or rank view, which is the very view of "the one who gives up A4" whom the paragraph addresses. Being wronged shows that she had *a* claim. It does not show that the claim was equal or that it did not vary with strength and mind.
   - Nietzsche's order of rank (*BGE* §257; *Antichrist* §57), Singer, McMahan and Kagan all grant that leaving her wronged her and deny equal worth. The Church itself holds that cruelty to animals is wrong without granting them equal worth (CCC 2418).
   - v5 widened the gap by dropping v4's "because she was weak." My own R4 repair ("a claim her weakness did not lessen") had a milder form of the same gap. I missed it then, and I correct it now.
   - It is not Fatal. W1(2) grounds equal worth separately (an equal call), and this objection does not touch that ground. But the case's final sentence to the A4-denier should not contain a non sequitur. Section 6 gives a one-clause fix that makes the step valid and leaves the rank-theorist a higher price to pay.

**Minor (as written), in order of importance**
1. **C, Power and rank, horn 2** (Ex. 2). "Cannot correct us … cannot show anyone that he ought to give it up" contradicts the new *GM* III §27 sentence and misstates his method. The true cost of horn 2 is borrowed authority.
2. **A4, "for others' sake"** (Ex. 4). Non-voluntary killing "for her own good" now falls outside both A4 and A6's named claim, and the newborn and the disabled head A4's own list.
3. **C, Power and rank, "honesty need not be fatal"** (Ex. 3). The martyrs answer it. Say "can never finally be the enemy of life."
4. **C, Power and rank, "A1 through A4"** does not match the heading's "A1 through A4, the second half of A6."
5. **A heading, the count sentence.** "Most people find …" still sits beside "This is not a vote." It is low priority, and this is the third time I have raised it.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- **Rank vs. equal worth.** He is surer of rank. Repaired, the case shows him the price: saying "a lesser wrong" about her, by name.
- **The ressentiment genealogy vs. A4** under the new source-connection test (Ex. 5). Our edge is that it predicts inversion, not a floor for the enemy. The fairness rule cuts both ways.
- **Immanent critique.** Whether honest inquiry ends at "God is dead" or at the Lawgiver. v5 now says this correctly; the whole case decides it.
- **"Finally."** One Source, or art to bear the truth. Each side carries a hope the other calls unshown.
- **Dionysus vs. the Crucified** on the evidence of nature alone, and the lion and parasite distinction (Ex. 6). The book owns this as weight.
- **Free death vs. "not hers to dispose of."** Now explicitly the book's claim, not a shared premise.

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact wording)

**Conclusion (Serious).** Replace:
> ask which you are surer of: that she was wronged when she was left there, weak and not yet able to know herself, or any argument that says she was not, or that you cannot know it. If she was wronged, her worth never followed her strength or her mind.

with:
> ask which you are surer of: that she was wronged when she was left there, and wronged no less than a strong child would have been, though she was weak and not yet able to know herself; or any argument that says she was not, or that it was a lesser wrong, or that you cannot know it. If she was wronged no less, her worth never followed her strength or her mind.

Keep what follows ("Then it follows what she is, and she is that as fully as you are. What rises in you then …"). The step is now valid. If the wrong does not shrink with her weakness, the worth it answers to does not follow her strength.

**C, Power and rank, horn 2.** Replace:
> or it binds only his kind, and his unmasking can rank us but cannot correct us: it can call a belief low, but it cannot show anyone that he ought to give it up.

with:
> or it binds only those who hold it. Then his unmasking can turn our own honesty against us, as he meant it to, but it cannot show that anyone ought to keep that honesty, his own included.

(The next sentence, "He hoped that truthfulness would at last turn on Christianity itself …", then follows naturally.)

**C, Power and rank.** Replace "so that honesty need not be fatal; on his account it might be," with:
> so that honesty may cost a life but can never finally be the enemy of life; on his account it might be,

Replace "It stays consistent by giving up A1 through A4 openly," with:
> It stays consistent by giving up A1 through A4, and the second half of A6, openly,

**A4.** Replace "never, if innocent, to be deliberately killed for others' sake, not for the many and not for the few," with:
> never, if innocent, to be deliberately killed for others' sake, not for the many and not for the few, nor "for his own good" without his asking,

(The parenthesis that follows, on "a life that its owner asks to end," then covers exactly the disputed case and nothing more.)

**A heading.** Delete "Most people find, on reflection, that they cannot give these up."

**Evil, First (optional, carried over).** After "A yes said to everything is said to no one." add:
> It must say yes to what was done to her as well as to her, and so it cannot take her side.

## 7. New insights for the book

1. **Being wronged at all versus being wronged no less.** Almost every denier of equal worth grants that the exposed infant was wronged. What they deny is that she was wronged *as gravely*. The climax should ask the question that separates the views. The conviction it relies on is nearly universal: killing a disabled newborn is not a lesser murder.
2. **"Before all else" as gravity, not precedence.** "Not a second injury beside hers, but His love refused in her" turns the Catechism's order into a direct answer to *GM* II §22. God's priority is what makes her injury grave, not a claim that crowds hers out.
3. **The ressentiment genealogy meets the source-connection test.** Ressentiment predicts inversion. Reason's consistency, which W2 names as the engine of correction, is a process connected to truth. The book can use the same test it lets the debunker use.
4. **Borrowed authority (carried over).** His immanent critique is real. Its cost is that it convicts us only by an ought he says binds no one.
5. **The new A4 boundary.** The shared floor should forbid every killing of the innocent that is not at his own asking. That leaves only the disputed case of a request, which A6 handles as the book's claim.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "Not a second injury beside hers, but His love refused in her." (v5)
- "A man may lay down his life, but he may not hand it over to be destroyed." (v5)
- "Not every child is wanted by those who made her; every child was wanted by the One who did." (v5)
- "Wronged no less than a strong child would have been."
- "He can correct us only with an ought he says binds no one."
- "Honesty may cost a life; it can never finally be the enemy of life."
- "His return gives her back the same. The resurrection gives her back whole."

**To verify before anything enters the book** (all paraphrased, with no direct quotations from Nietzsche): *GM* I §§7, 10; II §§8, 10, 16, 21–22; III §27; *BGE* §§4, 13, 108, 126, 168, 211, 227, 257, 258; *GS* §§107, 357; *Antichrist* §§2, 54, 57; *Twilight*, Skirmishes §36; CCC 37–38 (whether they support "spread with the Gospel"); CCC 2418; SCG III.112, III.122 (still listed as unverified in the R4 synthesis).

## 8. Score

**v5 as written: 8.5/10.** v5 handles *GM* II §22 better than any earlier version, and it took most of my Round 4 repairs. But in rewording the conclusion it dropped "because she was weak." The final step to equal worth is now invalid against the rank-theorist it addresses, and that is one Serious objection; the rest are Minor. **With the repairs above: 9.5/10.** What remains is the limit the book already confesses: it cannot compel a man who is surer of rank than of the newborn's claim. Repaired, it makes him say the costly sentence, "a lesser wrong," about her, by name.
