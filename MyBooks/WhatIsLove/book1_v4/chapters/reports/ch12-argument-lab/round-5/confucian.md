# Argument Lab, Round 5: Confucian opponent vs. v5

## 1. School and voice

The opponent speaks for the Confucian tradition at full strength: the *Analects* (Slingerland numbering), Mencius against Xunzi, the *Mean* and the *Great Learning*, the *Classic of Filial Piety*, the Neo-Confucian school of principle (Zhang Zai, the Cheng brothers, Zhu Xi), and Mou Zongsan's "immanent transcendence." He grants nearly everything the book says about love and denies only that its Source is Someone. This round he audits v5 against his Round 4 repairs R1–R8 and tests the new wording. Everything is paraphrased.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges (unchanged in substance)

1. **Justice.** It begins in repayment (*bao*): we owe first what we received, from parents, from the sages, and from Heaven and Earth. Reciprocity is its measure (15.24). *Holds, and it is close to Chapter 4.* But repayment needs a receiver.
2. **A hard case.** Shun carries his murderer father to the sea (Mencius 7A35): he does not bend the law, and he does not abandon his father. *Coherent and honest about the cost.* It strains A4 at one point: the victim's claim yields.
3. **Equal dignity.** Each person carries the whole of principle, as the one moon is whole in ten thousand rivers. *Holds on equality.* It breaks at "for her own sake": drain one river and the moon has lost nothing.
4. **Ultimate good.** Sagehood, forming one body with all things; for the world, the Great Unity in which the widow, the orphan and the disabled are all cared for. *Coherent and noble.* It stops short of communion, and of the dead.

## 3. Debate highlights

**Exchange 1: did v5 take R1–R8?**
*Audit:*
- **R1 (road two):** **taken in substance.** "The road of the great ancestral traditions" is gone. Road two now belongs to "many philosophers without God" and to Xunzi, who keeps the rites "as the human way of completing a bond, without holding that the dead hear." That is faithful to *Xunzi* ch. 19: sacrifice expresses remembrance and longing, and when the end is done as well as the beginning, the human way is complete. Confucius "would say no more": reticent, not a denier (6.22, 7.21, 11.12). Mozi now attacks those who *deny* the spirits, which matches *Mozi* 48. "Walked it honestly" is gone. Xunzi's reply to Mozi was not added. It is not needed: Xunzi's reading now comes *before* Mozi, and "Guilt does not only mourn her" grants that the rite completes the mourner, then asks for more. That answers Xunzi in his own terms.
- **R2 (the kinless dead):** **taken**, more compactly: "no one left to remember them *by name*." The Ming altars for the unattended dead and the Ghost Festival feed the forgotten without names, and so does the Mass's prayer for "those whose faith God alone has known." The contrast now rests where it belongs: on the second road, no one remembers them by name; on the third, One forgets no one (Isa 49:15). "She is not there to take up or answer" also fits Zhu Xi's *qi*-response (Round 4, Exchange 3): something is moved, but *she* does not answer.
- **R3 (the Analects gloss):** **half taken.** See Exchange 2.
- **R4 (Mou, "besides me"):** **taken.** See Exchange 3 for one scope problem in the new sentence.
- **R5 (the promise to the seeker):** **taken**, in a better form: a principle that does not steer "can promise the seeker his own completion, not that the one he lost will be kept." That credits 7.15 and 7.30 and moves the line to the right place. It is accurate. The *Mean* (25) speaks of completing oneself *and* things, but even the sage's completion of others does not keep the dead.
- **R6 (the God row):** **taken.** See Exchange 4 for a dangling modifier.
- **R7 ("a yes said to everything"):** **not taken.** See Exchange 6.
- **R8 (the call):** **taken**: "an equal call, which does not wait on what anyone can do, explains an equal worth." That answers Mencius 6B2 (anyone can become a Yao or a Shun) without having to name him.
- **R9 (optional, Neo-Confucian seekers in W3):** not taken. It was optional, and its absence is no fault.
*Result: Held.* Every Round 4 point is closed or nearly closed. What follows is the leftover and the new.

**Exchange 2: the Analects gloss still misdescribes 11.9 (and loosely 9.5).**
*Opponent.* "v5 reads: 'Confucius, in danger, in grief, and when no one understood him, spoke of Heaven as the one who gave him his virtue and who alone knew him (7.23, 9.5, 11.9, 14.35).' The two descriptions fit 7.23 (Heaven produced the virtue in me, so what can Huan Tui do to me?) and 14.35 (the one who knows me is Heaven). They do not fit 11.9, the 'grief' passage. When Yan Hui died, the Master cried that Heaven was destroying him. That is not Heaven giving virtue or knowing him; it is Heaven *bereaving* him. And 9.5 says Heaven will not let this culture perish, which is not quite 'gave him his virtue.'"
*Defense.* "Conceded. The text should say what each passage says. It is also worth seeing that 11.9 helps the book: a Heaven that bereaves a man is a Heaven he speaks to as an agent. Glossed correctly, it makes the point better."
*Result: Held with repair (Minor).* R-a.

**Exchange 3: the Mou sentence: "besides me" now scopes over every verb.**
*Opponent.* "'No one at the ground of things, besides me, knows me as myself, releases what I did to another, or keeps the one I lost.' On the first verb that is exactly right, and it quietly grants Mou's intellectual intuition: the heart at my depth does know me. But 'besides me' governs all three verbs, so the sentence implies that on Mou's account *I* release what I did to another and *I* keep the one I lost. Mou claims neither. The heart corrects itself by turning back to recognize its own ground; it does not pardon its own wrongs to others. And your own A3 says no one but the victim can pardon."
*Defense.* "Conceded. 'Besides me' belongs to knowing, not to releasing or keeping. Split the sentence: no one *besides me* knows me, and no one *at all* releases or keeps."
*Opponent rejoinder.* "With that split, I hold the summit in the same place: the infinite heart, the same in you and me."
*Defense.* "And we hold Someone besides us. That is union against communion, and it is a standoff, not a defect."
*Result: Held with repair (Minor).* R-b.

**Exchange 4: the God row's modifier.**
*Opponent.* "'It is the one that knows and loves, and knows and loves us as someone besides ourselves.' Read quickly, 'as someone besides ourselves' attaches to *us*: He loves us *as though we were* someone other than ourselves. That is the opposite of what you mean, and it is what Mou would say a transcendent God does: he estranges us from ourselves."
*Defense.* "Fair. The phrase should be the subject's: He is Someone besides us, and He knows and loves us as we are."
*Result: Held with repair (Minor).* R-c.

**Exchange 5 (new): v5 removed the ancestral world from road two but placed it nowhere, and it walks road three without a Keeper.**
*Opponent.* "In Round 4 I told you that most of the people who kept the ancestral rites believe the dead *come* to the offering and receive it. v5 fixed the misdescription by removing them from road two. Now they are on no road. Yet they are the largest company on your third road that walks it without a Creator: the named dead are present at the tablet, are told what the family has done, and are thanked and asked. v5 meets the Buddhist on the third road and answers him: the one reborn is not the same self, and does not know. That answer does not touch the ancestors. The tablet bears *her* name. So your clue weighs toward 'she is still someone,' and my ancestors had that without God."
*Defense.* "The point is fair, and the text should face it, because the answer is short and it is already in the book's own materials. On that road the dead are kept by the living. The ancestor is fed as long as her own line remembers her. After some generations her tablet leaves the hall, and Zhu Xi said her *qi* disperses. Those whom no one remembers become the wandering dead, fed once a year by a whole people, without a name. 'No one left to remember them by name' bites here too. And the rite runs through kinship. Confucius said that to sacrifice to spirits not one's own is flattery (2.24), so the stranger who wronged her has no rite of his own by which to reach her. The third road needs someone who keeps her when memory fails. That is the Catholic claim: 'she lives to God, who forgets no one.'"
*Opponent rejoinder.* "Popular religion knows the wronged ghost who will not rest until her wrongdoer makes offerings and amends. There the stranger does reach her."
*Defense.* "He reaches her by appeasing her, and the rite does not have to say whether she pardons. Even so, I grant that this is the nearest thing outside the Church to 'asks to be forgiven by her,' and it is a witness *for* A3: the debt outlives her, and it is owed to her. What it lacks is a Keeper. She lingers because the wrong holds her there, not because Someone holds her, and the kinless victim with no one to avenge her is not kept at all."
*Why it is not Serious:* our side answers it at least to parity, with the text's own premises ("by name," "who forgets no one"). The clue limits its own weight. And W1's step to a Keeper is made by the Catholic paragraph, not by the bare third road. But the text, as written, answers the Buddhist form of road three and is silent on the more common form. That is a gap the book should close.
*Result: Held with repair (Minor).* R-d.

**Exchange 6: "A yes said to everything is said to no one" (R7, carried over).**
*Opponent.* "Still there. In its place, after 'a god beyond good and evil who makes and breaks out of sheer fullness,' it hits its target. Lifted out of that place, it reads against Wisdom 11:24 (God loves all things that are and loathes nothing He made). It also reads against our Heaven, whose heart gives life to all and which, like your God, does not will the breaking."
*Defense.* "Other schools want to keep the line, and they are right that it is strong. One word keeps both its strength and its aim: a yes to whatever *happens*, the breaking with the making, is said to no one."
*Result: Held with repair (Minor).* R-e. It does not conflict with the Nietzschean's proposed addition after the same sentence.

**Exchange 7: A6's new placement, and "a man may lay down his life, but he may not hand it over to be destroyed."**
*Opponent.* "Mencius gives up life to take rightness (6A10), and Confucius says the man of *ren* gives up his life to complete *ren* (15.9). The state later honored widows who killed themselves to stay chaste. Do you count my tradition as one that 'leaves out' the line?"
*Defense.* "v5 no longer counts anyone. The consent teaching sits under A6's own words, 'this book's claims, argued in W1(2), not shared ones,' and the God row claims it only 'for those who hold it with the Church.' 6A10 and 15.9 are *laying down* life, which v5 grants. The chaste-widow suicides are what the book means by *handing it over*, and the Yongzheng emperor's edict against honoring them shows that your own tradition was divided there. Mencius 7A2 is on our side of the line: one who understands his appointed lot does not stand under a tottering wall, and dying in fetters is not a proper end. Life is not one's own to throw away."
*Result: Held.* No repair. (7A2 is noted under §7.)

**Exchange 8: smaller checks of new wording.**
(a) "Minds under threat reach for a knowing Heaven, ours as well; this shows where the account strains for its own teachers, not that it fails." *Fair:* the genealogy is applied to both sides and is denied the weight of evidence. The fairness rule is kept. Zhu Xi did have to read these passages as principle. No fault.
(b) "Confucius himself sacrificed to them as though they were present." This is the disciples' description (3.12). "As though" is a fair English rendering, and Zhu Xi's school read the phrase as sincerity, not as pretense. "Would say no more" keeps him reticent. No fault.
(c) W1(2): "A law or an order gives a nature to every member of a kind; it does not want any one of them." Against Heaven's heart to give life, this is the Round 3–4 standoff, stated fairly. No fault.
(d) W1(2): "a reader who holds it at every number has here a reason for it." Mencius 2A2 holds the line at every number: not one innocent killed, even to gain the empire. The book offers him a reason, not a correction. That is the right posture.
(e) Evil, *Third*: the Confucian hope for the Great Unity is credited accurately as an aspiration. No fault.

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: NONE.**

No Fatal or Serious objection from this school stands against v5 as written. v5 took R1, R2, R4, R5, R6 and R8, and half of R3. The new wording (Xunzi's road, "by name," "minds under threat ... ours as well," "his own completion," "besides me") is accurate, and none of it breaks a step. The nearest candidate is Exchange 5: the ancestral cult, now unplaced, walks the third road without a Keeper. It is not Serious. Our side answers it at least to parity, using premises already in the text: "no one left to remember them by name," and "she lives to God, who forgets no one." The clue limits its own weight, and the step to a Keeper is made by the Catholic paragraph, not by the bare third road. It is still a gap the book should close.

**Minor:**
1. W1(1): the ancestral rites, as most who keep them understand them, are a godless form of road three, and the text now places them nowhere. Answer them as v5 answers the Buddhist (Ex. 5). R-d.
2. Sacred-order row: "the one who gave him his virtue and who alone knew him" misglosses 11.9 (Heaven *bereaved* him) and loosely 9.5 (Heaven would not let the culture perish) (Ex. 2). R-a.
3. Sacred-order row: "besides me" scopes over "releases" and "keeps," implying that on Mou's account I release my own wrongs to others (Ex. 3). R-b.
4. God row: "knows and loves us as someone besides ourselves" can be read as modifying "us" (Ex. 4). R-c.
5. Evil, *First*: "A yes said to everything is said to no one" reads against Wis 11:24 when taken out of its place (carried over from Round 4) (Ex. 6). R-e.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- *Debts to the dead.* Xunzi's completion of the giver, or Zhu Xi's *qi*-response through the line, against a debt that waits for its creditor. Theism is ahead only where guilt asks for *her* pardon, and the clue weighs as much as the reader finds that to be the guilt he knows.
- *The ancestral third road.* The named dead who receive, kept by the memory of their kin, against the dead kept by One who forgets no one. Each is a road on which she is still someone; they part on what keeps her when memory fails.
- *Equality without capacity.* The moon in the rivers matches the theist ground on equality. Theism is ahead on "for her own sake" and "for good."
- *Life held in trust.* Trust from parents (the *Classic of Filial Piety*; Mencius 7A2) against trust from the Lord of life. Mencius's absolute (2A2) rests in the doer's rightness; the book's rests in her.
- *Union vs. communion.* Mou's infinite heart that knows and loves, mine and yours, against Someone besides us. Each side calls the other's summit a loss: the infinite made into an object, or a self with no one to be loved by.
- *The problem of evil.* A principle that intends no one carries a lighter problem and promises the seeker his own completion. A God who knows each one carries the heavier problem and promises this child.

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact wording)

**R-a. Sacred-order row: replace**
"spoke of Heaven as the one who gave him his virtue and who alone knew him (*Analects* 7.23, 9.5, 11.9, 14.35)."
**with:**
> spoke of Heaven as the one who gave him his virtue and would not let his work perish, who bereaved him when his best student died, and who alone knew him (*Analects* 7.23, 9.5, 11.9, 14.35).

**R-b. Sacred-order row: replace**
"but no one at the ground of things, besides me, knows me as myself, releases what I did to another, or keeps the one I lost."
**with:**
> but at the ground of things there is no one besides me who knows me as myself, and no one at all who can release what I did to another or keep the one I lost.

**R-c. God row: replace**
"it is the one that knows and loves, and knows and loves us as someone besides ourselves."
**with:**
> it is the one that knows and loves us, and is Someone besides ourselves.

**R-d. W1(1) para 1: insert before** "The Buddhist walks a form of the third road without a Keeper:"
> Most who have kept the ancestral rites, in China and among many peoples, walk a form of the third road too: the named dead come to the offering, and are told, thanked and asked. But on that road the dead are kept by the living. Each is fed as long as her own line remembers her, and those whom no one remembers become the wandering dead, whom a whole people feeds without a name. What keeps her there is the memory of her kin, and memory fails.

(If the Buddhist's Round 5 repair rewrites the next sentence, keep this insertion immediately before it, so that the two godless forms of the third road stand side by side.)

**R-e. Evil, *First*: replace**
"A yes said to everything is said to no one."
**with:**
> A yes said to whatever happens is said to no one.

(This is compatible with any sentence the Nietzschean adds after it.)

**R9 (optional, carried over). W3 para 2: after** "...report a peace beyond the self rather than a Person." **add:**
> So did the Neo-Confucians, who for centuries sat in stillness and searched things and their own hearts, and found a principle rather than a Person.

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

1. **Mencius 7A2: no one who knows his lot stands beneath a tottering wall.** To die having fully walked one's way is a proper end; to die in fetters is not. This is a pagan witness to A6's "not hers to throw away," and it sits beside 6A10's "give up life to take rightness." Together they draw the book's own line: lay it down, do not hand it over. (Ch. 12, A6 and W1(2); Ch. 9 on sacrifice.)
2. **Analects 2.24: to sacrifice to spirits not one's own is flattery.** The ancestral rite runs through kinship. The stranger who wronged her has no rite of his own by which to reach her, and the kinless dead have no one to reach. The Church's Keeper is not bound by blood: "who forgets no one." Use it as a datum, carefully, not as a jab. (Ch. 12, W1(1); Ch. 10 on communion.)
3. **Analects 11.9: "Heaven is destroying me!"** Confucius's grief speaks to Heaven as one who acts on him. Glossed accurately, it shows the strain the row describes better than "knew him" does. (Ch. 12, C.)
4. **The wronged ghost in Chinese popular religion.** The dead victim who will not rest until her wrongdoer makes amends is the nearest witness outside the Church to A3 and to "it asks to be forgiven by her": the debt outlives her and is owed to *her*. What it lacks is a Keeper. (Ch. 12, W1(1).)
5. **Xunzi ch. 19: when the end is done as well as the beginning, the human way is complete.** This is the exact source of v5's "completing a bond." It is worth citing if the line stays. (Ch. 12, W1(1).)
6. **Verify before anything enters the book:** *Analects* 2.24, 3.12, 6.22, 7.15, 7.21, 7.23, 7.30, 9.5, 11.9, 11.10, 11.12, 14.35, 15.9; *Mencius* 2A2, 2A6, 6A10, 6B2, 7A2, 7A35; *Xunzi* ch. 19; *Mozi* 48; *Mean* 25; *Zhuzi yulei* books 1, 3, 4; the Hongwu *li* altar ordinances; the Yongzheng edict (1728); Wis 11:24. Paraphrase only.

**Lines worth keeping (short, plain, warm):**
- "Heaven and Earth give life to things; God wanted *you*." (v5)
- "No one left to remember them by name." (v5)
- "A principle that does not steer can promise the seeker his own completion, not that the one he lost will be kept." (v5)
- "On that road the dead are kept by the living, and memory fails."
- "A man may lay down his life, but he may not hand it over to be destroyed." (v5; Mencius would sign the first half and 7A2 the second.)
- "Something can respond. Only someone can forgive." (Round 4, still true of Zhu Xi's *qi*.)

## 8. Score

**v5 as written: 9.5/10. With the repairs: 9.5/10.** v5 took nearly every repair this school asked for, and its new wording is accurate to Xunzi, Confucius, Mozi, Zhu Xi and Mou. What remains is polish: one misglossed passage, two scoping slips, one carried-over line, and the ancestral third road left unplaced. The score does not rise with the repairs, because what keeps it from 10 is not a defect but the honest standoffs (union against communion, repayment against reception, an impersonal ground against a Keeper), and the book now draws those fairly.
