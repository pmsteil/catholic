# Argument Lab, Round 5: Utilitarianism / Consequentialism vs. v5

## 1. School and voice

The opponent speaks for realist, impartialist consequentialism at full strength. That means Sidgwick's axioms, Hare's two levels, Parfit's Triple Theory, de Lazari-Radek and Singer, Railton's sophisticated consequentialism and Hooker's rule consequentialism, with threshold deontology called in as a neighbour. This round checks whether v5 closed my two Round 4 Serious objections and eight Minors, and whether its new wording broke anything. The new wording is the firefighter, "he may not hand it over to be destroyed," the widened A4, and the rewritten utilitarian row.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges (short)

1. **Justice:** each counts for one, and justice is the rules that protect the most vital interests. **Coherent, borrows nothing; breaks** where "owed" comes second to "best."
2. **Hard case:** the hijacked airliner over the stadium: shoot it down. **Holds as a decision.** It is a parity case, since Catholic moralists divide on it. The school pays its price at the secret transplant.
3. **Equal dignity:** equal *consideration*, yes. Equal *worth* whatever one's capacities, no. **Breaks at the ground, honestly.** W1(2) answers this directly.
4. **Ultimate good:** well-being seen impartially. **Coherent; thin at the summit.** Impartial reason can see that each one counts. Only a person can want each one (W3).

## 3. Debate highlights

**First: did v5 fix Round 4?**
- **S1 (the consent clause sat under "almost everyone sees"): Fixed.** "Almost everyone sees these at once" now covers only the surgeon, the riot and the unwilling prisoner. The consent and request teaching comes after "Fewer still," and it is marked: "Those are this book's claims, argued in W1(2), not shared ones: many who hold the rest of A would let a dying man ask for death, or a volunteer give his life to an experiment." That is exact and fair. The A heading names the deniers too. Closed.
- **S2 ("no account that counts only totals can say why the line is there"): Fixed**, in my wording. The dropped tail, "and none of them holds it at every number," is not missed, because A6 already says so. Closed (Exchange 1 tests it).
- **Minors 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8: all taken.** These are "even many" with *VS* 52 beside 80; Singer denying "parts of A2, A4, A6 and A7"; "her wish that it stay hidden"; "she is not there to take up or answer" (better than mine); de Lazari-Radek and Singer restored, with two-level and rule theories credited; Parfit's "no dwelling at all"; and "her strength or her mind."
- **Minor 2 (A4 → "(A6)"): not taken. In its place v5 added "not for the many and not for the few" to A4's killing clause.** That reopens the fault the lab closed in Round 3 (Exchange 5).

**Exchange 1: Does the repaired S2 sentence now misstate Hooker?**
*Utilitarian:* Rule consequentialism does not say that killing the patient merely "breaks a useful rule." It says the act is *wrong*: wrongness just is being forbidden by the code whose acceptance would do most good. That code gives her a right, so the act wrongs *her*.
*Defense:* It gives her a right *because* the code is optimal. Her claim stands only as long as the code's usefulness does. That makes the wrong to her derivative, which is what "not only the breaking of a useful rule" means. Parfit's Triple Theory has a Scanlonian element that can say "wronged her," but it does not "count only totals," and the sentence is limited to theories that do.
*Result:* **Held as written.** The sentence is now exact, and it moves the quarrel onto A2, where the book is strongest.

**Exchange 2: The firefighter and the volunteer: whose reason is "since"?**
*Utilitarian:* "Fewer still think that a man's own request or consent could never make his death right, since he may lay down his life for others, as a firefighter does." That gives one reason for two different dissents. People who accept the volunteer may reason from the firefighter. People who accept the dying man's request do not. They reason from mercy and from his own judgement of his life. And "make his death right" is loose wording: no one thinks consent makes the firefighter's *death* right. The question is whether it can make *killing him* right.
*Defense:* Both points are fair, and the honesty sentence that follows ("a dying man ask for death, or a volunteer give his life to an experiment") already names both groups correctly.
*Result:* **Held; Minor.** One clause fixes it (§6).

**Exchange 3: "A man may lay down his life, but he may not hand it over to be destroyed." What about Kolbe, and Ephesians 5:2?**
*Utilitarian:* This is the distinction v5 offers to answer my Round 4 question: why is the firefighter a hero and the volunteer forbidden? As written, it condemns your own paradigm of self-gift. St. Maximilian Kolbe stepped forward at Auschwitz and handed himself over to the guards to be starved and then killed in another prisoner's place. Your Church canonized him as a martyr of charity. Worse, the translation read at Mass in the United States says of Christ that He "handed himself over for us as a sacrificial offering to God" (Ephesians 5:2 NABRE; also 5:25). So the Church does not forbid handing oneself over to be destroyed. Kolbe was not hit by a side effect, as the firefighter is. He walked into his death. If that is permitted, your sentence does not say why my volunteer is forbidden.
*Defense:* The real distinction is not "lay down" against "hand over." It is about *right*. Kolbe gave himself up to men who killed him *wrongly*. Their act stayed murder, and he did not ask them to do right by killing him. The volunteer asks the researcher to kill him *as a right act*, which means granting a right over his life that he does not have to grant (W1(2): "not hers to dispose of"; CCC 2280, 2295; Pius XII, 1952: a man cannot give another more right over his body than he has himself). So a man may give up his life, even into the hands of those who will wrongly take it. He cannot give anyone the right to take it.
*Rejoinder:* That is a good answer, and it is the trust ground at work. It is not what the sentence says. As written, the sentence is false of Kolbe, and in the Church's own English it is false of Christ. It is also the line the Round 4 synthesis listed as "worth keeping," so it is likely to enter the book in this form.
*Result:* **Failed as written; held with repair. Serious.** Doctrine is not at issue, and the fix is one clause (§6). The fault is partly mine: my Round 4 wording carried "as a means," which v5 dropped, and even that phrase would not have cleared Kolbe cleanly.

**Exchange 4: The closeness problem: the volunteer intends the data, not his death.**
*Utilitarian:* Suppose you rest the distinction on intention, as double effect does. The volunteer in a lethal-challenge trial intends to supply the data and foresees his death, just as the firefighter intends the rescue and foresees his. Double effect's "closeness" problem (Hart, Foot's fat man in the cave) means it cannot cleanly separate them.
*Defense:* The repaired sentence does not rest on intention. It rests on authority. Whatever the volunteer intends, the researcher must *kill him* (expose him to a lethal agent as the procedure itself), and no one can give him that right. The firefighter's death is done to him by fire, and no one claims the right to do it.
*Result:* **Held at parity**, with the §6 repair. The closeness problem is a real standoff for double effect generally. The trust ground keeps A6 out of it.

**Exchange 5: A4's "not for the many and not for the few" puts the line at every number back into the shared core.**
*Utilitarian:* A4 sits under "Most people find, on reflection, that they cannot give these up," and it now says every innocent is "never … to be deliberately killed for others' sake, not for the many and not for the few." Two items later, A6 says: "Fewer hold the line when the numbers grow vast, and many who would never hang the innocent man would bomb a city to end a war. The Church holds the line at every number … Those are this book's claims … not shared ones." W1(2) agrees: "Many let the line bend when the numbers grow vast (A6)." So A4 claims as shared what A6 and W1(2) call the book's own claim. Threshold deontologists, Hooker's disaster rule, and most people asked about Hiroshima all deny "not for the many." This is exactly the Round 3 fault (S2) the lab fixed in v4. The new parenthetical in A4 sends only the "owner asks to end" case to A6, not the numbers. And the utilitarian row's charge "(A4, A6)" now reads as if this school breaks a datum everyone holds, when at vast numbers it breaks only the Church's.
*Defense:* The phrase came from the Nietzschean's Round 4 repair, which aimed at the *mere-means* clause (no one is a tool for the few, the higher men). It was meant to close the aristocratic door, not to settle the numbers.
*Rejoinder:* Then it was attached to the wrong clause. As written, A4 and A6 contradict each other on the very point the lab spent Round 3 getting honest.
*Result:* **Failed as written; held with repair. Serious.** The fix keeps the Nietzschean's "not for the few" and sends "the many" to A6 (§6).

**Exchange 6: "Held for her, never against her": then the trust permits ending a life that is bad for her.**
*Utilitarian:* W1(2) now says God "holds her life for her, never against her." A trustee who holds for the beneficiary must act in her interest. Suppose continued life is, by her own lights and on any honest reckoning, against her interest: the dying man in unrelievable pain. Then the trust you describe permits ending it, and the new wording undercuts the teaching it was written to ground.
*Defense:* "For her" means for her as a person willed for herself, and her life is not one good among her goods to be weighed against the others. It is her being, the one in whom every other good is had (GS 24; CCC 2258). A trustee may not judge that the beneficiary would be better off not existing, because there would then be no beneficiary. The care owed to the dying man is relief, and even relief that foreseeably shortens life (CCC 2279), not killing. The text also scopes this honestly: "as the Church holds."
*Result:* **Held at parity.** This is the euthanasia standoff, and v5 now marks it as the book's claim, not a datum. That is all honesty asks.

**Exchange 7: The God row: "no total may be weighed against one person, which the best rivals share."**
*Utilitarian:* Threshold deontologists, and Foot at catastrophe, do weigh totals against one person when the numbers grow vast. A6 says so. So "the best rivals share" is true of the five-for-one rule, not of "no total may be weighed." The next clause, "for those who hold it with the Church, why that line has no exceptions," shows the row means the former.
*Result:* **Held; Minor** wording (§6).

**Exchange 8: The utilitarian row: "hold them as if they had no exceptions (Hare's two levels; rule consequentialism)."**
*Utilitarian:* That is Hare. Hooker's code explicitly includes a rule to prevent disaster, so rule consequentialism holds the rules firmly but not "as if they had no exceptions."
*Result:* **Held; Minor** (§6). Everything else in the row is accurate: Sidgwick's resignation in 1869, de Lazari-Radek and Singer's defense of esoteric morality (*Ratio* 2010), CCC 1754, "in principle," and the A2 letter.

**Exchange 9 (brief): Scheffler's paradox, re-run on v5's W1(2).**
*Utilitarian:* Why not minimize killings?
*Defense:* "What protects her is not a weight that enough lives could outweigh, but a right over her life that no number of others has. No one may take what is not his to take, even to keep others from taking more."
*Result:* **Held, strongly**, and better than in v4. The phrase "a right over her life that no number of others has" now states the jurisdictional answer outright.

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**As written: TWO SERIOUS. No Fatal.** Both are one-clause fixes. Doctrine is not at issue in either; both are about what the text claims. One is a regression introduced by a repair from another school, and the other comes from my own Round 4 wording.

**S1. Serious: A4 puts the line at every number back into the shared core.** "Never, if innocent, to be deliberately killed for others' sake, not for the many and not for the few" stands under "Most people find … they cannot give these up." A6 itself ("Fewer hold the line when the numbers grow vast … The Church holds the line at every number … this book's claims … not shared ones") and W1(2) ("Many let the line bend when the numbers grow vast") contradict it. Threshold deontology, rule consequentialism's disaster rule and common judgement about bombing cities all deny it. This is the Round 3 S2 fault, reopened. It came from the Nietzschean's Round 4 repair, which was meant for the mere-means clause and landed on the killing clause.

**S2. Serious: "A man may lay down his life, but he may not hand it over to be destroyed" (A6) is false of the Church's own exemplars.** St. Maximilian Kolbe handed himself over to be killed in another man's place and was canonized as a martyr of charity. The NABRE says Christ "handed himself over for us as a sacrificial offering" (Ephesians 5:2; 5:25). As written, the sentence cannot be what separates the firefighter from the volunteer, and it leaves my Round 4 question unanswered: why the hero may die and the volunteer may not. The book's real answer is in W1(2). A man may give up his life, even into the hands of those who will wrongly take it. He cannot give anyone the *right* to take it. The sentence should say that.

**Minor**
1. A6: "since he may lay down his life for others, as a firefighter does" gives one reason for two dissents, and "make his death right" should be "make it right to kill him" (Exchange 2).
2. C, utilitarianism: "as if they had no exceptions" belongs to Hare. Rule consequentialism keeps a disaster rule (Exchange 8).
3. C, God: "no total may be weighed against one person, which the best rivals share" overstates what threshold rivals share (Exchange 7).
4. C, flourishing: add "as the Church holds" before the CCC 2280 clause, to match A6 and W1(2) (for the Aristotelian to judge).

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- Whether constraints are true or only useful (Hare, Hooker vs. A2 and W1(2)).
- The closeness problem for double effect (Exchange 4). A6, once repaired, rests on authority, not intention, and stays out of it.
- Euthanasia and "held for her" (Exchange 6): now honestly marked as the book's claim.
- The survival lottery; the hijacked airliner; evolutionary debunking of constraints (the test spares the line held at every number more readily than the shared threshold).
- Where the expanding circle leads; whether necessity needs a dwelling (Parfit vs. ST I q.15).

- The coherent dissenter who gives up A4: no argument compels him, and the book says so.

**After the repairs in §6: NONE.**

## 7. New insights for the book

- **Authority, not intention, separates the hero from the volunteer.** Kolbe, the firefighter and the lethal-trial volunteer all choose a death they foresee. What separates the first two from the third is not what each intends. It is that only the third asks another to kill him *by right*, a right no one can grant. This is W1(2)'s trust at work in A6, and it keeps the book out of the closeness problem that troubles double effect.
- **Kolbe belongs in Chapter 12, or in Chapter 9 with a cross-reference.** He is the clearest case of the whole teaching. A man gave his life; the act that took it stayed murder; and the Church calls his giving holy. One sentence about him does what three abstract clauses cannot.
- **Watch the A–W–C seam.** Each round, a repair aimed at one rival tends to slide a Church-only claim back into A. A simple check catches it: every "never," "every" and "not even" in A must be either shared or sent to A6 by name.

**Lines worth keeping**
- New: "A man may lay down his life; he cannot give anyone the right to take it."
- "What protects her is not a weight that enough lives could outweigh, but a right over her life that no number of others has." This is the plainest answer the case gives to Scheffler's paradox.
- "No one may take what is not his to take, even to keep others from taking more."
- "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent."
- "His life kept A7 better than his theory could" (Sidgwick).
- "What Christ gave was given, not taken, and it was not lost."
