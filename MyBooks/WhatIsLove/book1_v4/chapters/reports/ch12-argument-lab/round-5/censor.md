# Round 5: Doctrinal Censor's Third Read (v5, pre-imprimatur)

**Scope.** v5 only (EVOLUTION.md, "v5 — Round 5"). I checked it against my Round 4 findings (round-4/censor.md: E1, E2, N1–N8, items 9–10 and the optional minors), then read every new passage against Scripture (RSVCE), the Catechism, Vatican I and II, VS, *Spe Salvi*, *Dignitas Infinita*, *Salvifici Doloris*, *Dominus Iesus* and Aquinas. This read is not scored.

**Bottom line.** E1 is fixed. N2, N3, N5, N6, N7 and the two citation fixes are fixed. E2 is half fixed: the Aquinas sentence ("in willing that order … He wills some things to be naturally corrupted") is now right. But the new sentence just before it brings back the problem from Round 4's N8, in a sharper form, and it is **one Error**:

- **E1-5.** "the evil a free creature does is his own act, which God in no way causes." Grammatically, "which" refers to *his own act*. The sentence says God in no way causes the creature's act. That contradicts the clause just before it ("God is at work in every cause," CCC 308: "God is at work in all the actions of his creatures"). It also contradicts Aquinas: "The act of sin is both a being and an act; and in both respects it is from God"; what is not from God is the *defect* (ST I-II q.79 a.2). CCC 311 says God is in no way the cause of moral *evil*, not that He is in no way the cause of the *act*. This is a one-clause fix.

There are also twelve Needs-care items (N1–N12). The most important are these:
- A6's new line "he may not hand it over to be destroyed" does not survive Christ, who "gave himself up" (Eph 5:2), or St. Maximilian Kolbe, who asked to be killed in another man's place.
- The A6 citation for "even against … consent" is wrong. None of CCC 2261 or 2277 mentions consent. The explicit text is *Evangelium Vitae* 66.
- A4 calls a person "its owner" of her life. CCC 2280 says the opposite.
- W1(2)'s "holds her life for her" turns God into the steward, the reverse of CCC 2280.
- "the Church trusts" understates a defined teaching (Benedict XII, *Benedictus Deus*) and cites the wrong paragraph (CCC 1250).
- "*Why* death fixes the will is the Church's teaching" should be "*That*."

### Overstatements and understatements
- **Overstatements:** E1-5 (God made to cause nothing in the free act, the opposite of overclaiming His role but a misstatement nonetheless); N1 ("hand it over" made absolute); N6 (a theological "why" made into doctrine); N7 (healing not narrowed); N9 ("the very shape").
- **Understatements:** N5 ("trusts" for a defined teaching); N8 (CCC 1261's hope omitted).
- **Self-contradiction:** N3 ("its owner" vs. the book's own use of CCC 2280); N4 (God as trustee).

### Protestant-sounding or non-Catholic framing
None. E1-5 sounds occasionalist in reverse, closer to a deist or Molinist-neutral picture (the creature acts and God stands aside) than to the Thomist one. The fix restores the Catechism's wording.

> He can give the highest good as pure gift (CCC 1250), and the Church teaches that He gives it to the baptized child who dies before she can choose (Benedict XII, *Benedictus Deus*; CCC 1023).

**R-N6 (Evil, hell).**
> That death fixes the will is the Church's teaching (CCC 393, 1021), not something this case shows, and so hell stays on this side of the scale.

**R-N7 (W1(1); replaces "proposes that the fire that judges is Christ Himself, whose look burns away what is false so that the guilty too can be healed (*Spe Salvi* 47).")**
> … proposes that the fire which both burns and saves is Christ Himself: before His gaze "all falsehood melts away," and His touch heals whoever is open to Him, the guilty too (*Spe Salvi* 47).

**R-N8 (W3).**
> For the infant no one holds, there is no mother's love to carry His love to her; yet she is held by Him, and if she dies unbaptized, the Church entrusts her to His mercy and hopes that there is a way of salvation for her (CCC 1261).

**R-N9 (C, Kant).**
> And his own rule for clemency, that a ruler may remit punishment only for crimes against himself and never for what one subject did to another, has the shape of half of W1(1): no ruler can give, in the victim's place, the pardon that is hers. (God does what Kant's ruler may not: He forgives the sin whole, CCC 1441, and still does not take her place.)

**R-N10 (Conclusion).**
> … the Church does not presume that he is at fault, and leaves the judgment of every heart to God (CCC 847, 1861).

**R-N11 (W3).**
> … and it can be made before the name is known: the Spirit, in a manner known only to God, offers every person the possibility of sharing in Christ's Paschal Mystery (GS 22), and God can lead him, in ways known to Himself, to the faith without which it is impossible to please Him (CCC 848).

**R-N12 (W1).**
> … not a second injury beside hers, for nothing harms God (Job 35:6–8), but His love refused in her, and so a turning away from Him. He is offended by us, St. Thomas says, only because we act against our own good, the good He wills for us (*Summa contra Gentiles* III.122).

**Verdict for the bishop's reader:** v5 has **one Error** (E1-5), a one-clause fix that restores the Catechism's own wording. Once it is corrected, v5 has no Errors. With N1–N6 also fixed (A6's line and citation, "its owner", God as trustee, *Benedictus Deus*, "That death fixes the will"), I would expect it to pass a *nihil obstat* read. N7–N12 are refinements a careful censor would note but would probably not require.
