# Argument Lab, Round 5: Non-Naturalist Secular Moral Realism vs. v5

## 1. School and voice

Godless normative realism: Parfit (*On What Matters*), Enoch, Shafer-Landau, Wielenberg, Huemer, with Fine on essence and necessity, directed duties (Darwall, Wallace, Thompson), and Kamm and Nagel on why constraints have the shape they do. Moral truths are objective, necessary, known by reflection, and need no God. This round the school checked its Round 4 Serious item (S1) and its nine Minor repairs against v5, then tested what v5's new wording does under pressure.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

1. **Justice.** What each person is owed, much of it owed *to* her, fixed by necessary truths about persons. *Holds; it posits its ground.*
2. **A hard case.** Re A (the conjoined twins): Mary may not be killed as a means; Jodie is not bound to keep sustaining her. *Coherent; borrows justice and mercy in all but name (Rom 2:14–15).*
3. **Equal dignity.** Kind-based essentialism: worth follows the rational kind, not how far its powers are used now. *Holds, but posits the measure.* v5's "one end that is God Himself" gives a reason for it. Our side leads, narrowly.
4. **Ultimate good.** An objective list lived with integrity; death ends it. *Coherent for a life; breaks on unity, and on the child who died before any of it reached her.*

### New exchanges (v5's new wording under pressure)

**Exchange 1: A4's "not for the many." The S1 pattern in miniature. (Minor)**
*Realist:* A4 now reads "never, if innocent, to be deliberately killed for others' sake, not for the many and not for the few." Read plainly, "not for the many" is the line at every number. A6, three items on, says honestly that "fewer hold the line when the numbers grow vast" and that the every-number line is "this book's claim ... not a shared one." So the shared list asserts what A6 says is not shared. Most of my school are not absolutists at vast numbers (Huemer and Shafer-Landau allow thresholds; Parfit argued against Scanlon's refusal to aggregate).
*Defense:* The phrase came from the Nietzschean in Round 4, and its point is *whose sake*: not for the mass and not for a higher few. It was not meant as a claim about numbers. And nothing downstream treats every-number absolutism as shared: W1(2) argues it conditionally ("a reader who holds it at every number has here a reason for it"), and the God row now claims exceptionlessness only "for those who hold it with the Church."
*Realist rejoinder:* Then say so in the text. A reader who does not know where the phrase came from will read it as numbers.
*Result:* **Held with repair (N1). Minor.** In Round 4 the same kind of slip was Serious because C counted it as a win. Here C does not, and A6 corrects it two items later.

**Exchange 2: "a life that its owner asks to end." (Minor)**
*Realist:* A4's new pointer calls the person the "owner" of her life. I would agree. But the book does not: W1(2) cites CCC 2280 (stewards, not owners), and the flourishing row criticizes Foot "as though a person owned his life rather than being the one who lives it (CCC 2280)." v5 removed possession language about God ("the child is His"), and this phrase brings ownership back on the human side, against the book's own teaching.
*Defense:* Granted. It is one word, and it should borrow the flourishing row's own phrase.
*Result:* **Held with repair (N2). Minor.**

**Exchange 3: "a man may lay down his life, but he may not hand it over to be destroyed." What about Kolbe? (Minor)**
*Realist:* This is a good line, and it marks a real distinction in your tradition. But the words do not match it. St. Maximilian Kolbe asked to be put in a condemned man's place in the starvation bunker. In plain English he handed himself over to those who would destroy him, and your Church calls him a martyr. Scripture says that Christ "gave himself up" for us, which is the language of handing over. The volunteer in a lethal experiment will say that he, too, is laying down his life for the many. The distinction you need is not between laying down and handing over. It is whether his death is the thing he asks for, intended as end or means, and whether anyone kills him intending it.
*Defense:* Correct. That is the Church's line: one may accept death, even at the hands of others who do wrong, for the sake of a good; one may not intend one's death or ask another to cause it; and no one may intentionally kill the innocent. Kolbe asked to take another man's place, not to be killed. The killing was his captors' wrong alone. The line can stay if the sentence after it says this.
*Result:* **Held with repair (N3). Minor.** The claim is already flagged as the book's, so it is not Serious. But this is the first counterexample any realist will raise, and it is a case the book's own readers revere.

**Exchange 4: "held in trust ... for her, never against her." The dying woman in pain. (Parity)**
*Realist:* A trust held *for* her that forbids her to end pain that cannot be treated, when by every measure in this life death is better for her, looks like a trust held against her, by her own considered judgment of her good.
*Defense:* Three replies. (a) On this account her good is not bounded by this life. The One who holds the trust "can give it back," and she is kept (W1(1)). (b) Her good is not the same as her preference at the worst hour. (c) The Church does not require her to suffer pointless treatment or unrelieved pain. She may refuse burdensome means that only prolong dying, and she may be given relief from pain even if it shortens her life, so long as her death is not what is aimed at (CCC 2278–2279). What the trust forbids is aiming at her death, not easing her dying.
*Realist rejoinder:* (a) is your whole case, not a separate reason. (c) narrows the gap considerably, and the text should say it, because without it "even when it has grown hard" sounds harsher than your Church is.
*Result:* **Held, at parity.** The claim is flagged "as the Church holds." The optional addition N4 makes "never against her" fair to the Church as well as to the reader.

**Exchange 5: SCG III.122 and "not a second injury beside hers." Does it collapse CCC 1440's "before all else"?**
*Realist:* If God is offended *only because* we act against the good, then the wrong to her comes first in the order of explanation. So how is the offense against Him "before all else"?
*Defense:* Two orders again. In the order of knowing, the child comes first ("We see it first in what it does to the child"). In the order of being, the good she has is the good He wills for her, and His willing comes first. So it is one act, not two injuries, and "His love refused in her" says exactly that. It also keeps the directed duty my school insists on ("owed to her, truly and in her own right, and not to God alone").
*Realist rejoinder:* Then the "only because" concerns why God is *offended*, not what makes the act wrong, and "before all else" ranks its gravity. That is consistent.
*Result:* **Held.** v5's new wording breaks nothing here and repairs a Round 4 tension. One note for verification: in its context III.122 makes the point about sins that seem to harm no third party, so the "good" there is first the agent's own. The book's extension to the victim's good is sound (it is "our" good, the human good), but the citation should be checked against a critical text before use.

**Exchange 6: the realist row with Parfit. (Held)**
*Realist:* "Their necessity lives in the natures of pain, persons and promises themselves, or, as Parfit held, needs no dwelling at all, as the truths of logic need none." This is a fair paraphrase of Fine and Wielenberg, and of Parfit's quietism (normative truths with no ontological commitment, on a par with logic and mathematics). Two checks. (a) "It says the law holds of itself" fits Wielenberg better than Parfit, since Parfit would not say "holds" in any metaphysical sense. (b) Does the Parfit clause weaken the God row's "one source for three things"? It does not, because Parfit still must take the minds and the natures separately.
*Defense:* (a) is close enough for a one-line summary of the school, and the preceding sentence states Parfit's view exactly. (b) Agreed.
*Result:* **Held.** No misstatement of this school. "Every account has a first principle, ours included" keeps the parity this lab settled.

**Exchange 7: "A person it never gives away." The parasite. (Parity by concession)**
*Realist:* The order God wills gives the fawn to the wolf; you now say so plainly. But it also gives the child to the parasite, in the same causal sense. "The parasite takes from the child; God does not give her to it" seems to draw a line the child cannot feel.
*Defense:* The line is in how the order is aimed, not in the harm. Within it, animals are partly ordered to other creatures. Persons are governed for their own sake and are never ordered to another creature's life (SCG III.112). The child's harm is a side effect of the order, never its purpose. The harm is the same, and the text carries it as weight ("that too is on this side of the scale").
*Realist rejoinder:* The distinction is coherent. It is evidence only if one already grants the teleology.
*Result:* **Held, at parity by concession**, which is the right posture. v5 is more honest here than v4.

**Exchange 8: the God row's "no total may be weighed against one person, which the best rivals share and take as given." (Minor)**
*Realist:* Two small slips. (a) "No total may be weighed against one person" is itself exceptionless. If so, the next clause ("for those who hold it with the Church, why that line has no exceptions") has nothing to add. If not, the wording overstates. (b) "Take as given" sits badly beside W1(2), which says the best rivals "explain much of why she may not be killed for others" (the directed wrong, doing versus allowing, inviolable standing). What they take as given is the *standing* they explain it by. Some of my school, Parfit among them, also do not share the strong anti-aggregation rule.
*Defense:* Granted. It is wording, and W1(2) already says it correctly.
*Result:* **Held with repair (N5). Minor.**

**Exchange 9: the conclusion and McMahan once more. (Held)**
*Realist:* McMahan says the abandoned infant is wronged because she is sentient and has interests, and that her worth is still less than yours.
*Defense:* "Her worth never followed her strength or her mind." Sentience belongs to mind, and a worth grounded in sentience would make her equal to a puppy, not to you. Relations are ruled out by the case, since no one was drawn to hold her. What is left is what she is, and kind membership is all or nothing.
*Result:* **Held.** The step is a best-explanation step, not a deduction, and the text presents it that way.

**Exchange 10: two leftovers. (Minor)**
(a) A6 keeps my own Round 4 wording, "no prisoner may be experimented on to death, however many would be saved," directly before "fewer hold the line when the numbers grow vast." That was my mistake: "however many" is the every-number claim. "Though many would be saved" says what almost everyone sees. (N6)
(b) After S1, the flourishing row is the one place where the chosen-death teaching is still counted against a rival without the label the God row now carries. It gives a reason (CCC 2280) and does not pretend the point is shared, so this is not the Round 4 fault. But the label makes C consistent with itself. (N7)
*Result:* **Minor.**

## 5. Remaining sound objections

**Fatal against v5 as written:** none.
**Serious against v5 as written:** none.

**As written: NONE.**

Why: S1, this school's one Serious item in Round 4, is fixed at every place it appeared. The Church's teaching on chosen death and consent is kept in full, placed under A6's honest label, argued in W1(2) "as the Church holds," and claimed in C only "for those who hold it with the Church." The one residue of that pattern ("not for the many" in A4) is not counted as shared anywhere downstream, and A6 corrects it two items later, so it is Minor. The new wording (SCG III.122; "held in trust ... for her, never against her"; the Parfit clause; the God row) holds under pressure. Where it was pushed hardest (the dying woman, the parasite), it holds at parity, and the book says so itself.

**Minor (wording):**
- N1. A4, "not for the many": reads as the every-number claim A6 labels unshared.
- N2. A4, "its owner": against CCC 2280 and the book's own flourishing row.
- N3. A6, "hand it over to be destroyed": the words do not match the distinction, and Kolbe (and Christ's "gave himself up") are the obvious counterexamples.
- N5. God row: "no total may be weighed ... take as given" overstates both the rule and the rivals' silence.
- N6. A6, "however many would be saved": my own Round 4 slip.
- N7. Flourishing row: label the CCC 2280 criticism as the Church's claim, as the God row now does.
- Optional: N4 (CCC 2278–2279 in W1(2)); N8 ("cannot sign it away (A3)" in W1(2), so the reader knows which claim the opening means before waiving comes up).
- For the censor, not this school: the Evil section says the Church "trusts" that God gives the highest good to the baptized child who dies before she can choose (CCC 1250). The Church teaches this with more than trust, so the word may understate it. CCC 1250 itself is about the gratuitousness of infant Baptism.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- *The dead:* the tragic view (a real debt, forever unanswered) against "she is still someone." Guilt's asking fits the second better. The first is coherent and honestly held, and v5 now credits it to secular philosophers by name.
- *Where necessity lives:* in essences, nowhere at all (Parfit), or first in a Mind. Each stopping point needs nothing behind it. Our side's edge is breadth (law, natures and minds from one Source), not fewer axioms, and v5 says exactly that.
- *Reach:* reliable general reasoning plus the third factor, against the open access problem shared with mathematics. v5 presents it as shared, "it weighs, it does not settle."
- *Why the line cannot be waived:* the realist allows that it can be waived, and the Church holds that it cannot. The trust account alone explains non-waivability, and it counts only for a reader who grants the datum. v5 now claims exactly that much.
- *"Never against her":* whether a trust that forbids aiming at death can be for the good of a dying woman in pain. It rests on the hope that she is kept. That is the book's whole case, not a separate reason.
- *Whether wrongness binds of itself* or needs a Lawgiver: Anscombe's question, stated openly.
- *Equal worth:* kind-based essentialism posits the measure; the theist gives a reason. Our side leads, narrowly.
- *Natural evil:* the fawn and the parasite, judged by an independent standard. Carried as weight, with the teleological line drawn honestly. Parity only by concession.

## 7. New insights; lines worth keeping

**Insights**
1. **S1's rule worked, and it should be applied to every phrase, not only every clause.** Doctrine stated in full and labeled after A6 keeps A shared. The only residue this round was a single phrase ("not for the many") brought in by another school's repair. When a repair adds words to A, check it against A6's label before accepting it.
2. **The chosen-death line is clearest in terms of intention, not of handing over.** One may accept death, even at the hands of wrongdoers, for another's sake (Christ, Kolbe, the firefighter). One may not aim at it or ask for it, and no one may kill the innocent intending it. Said that way, the distinction survives the first counterexample any secular reader will raise, and it honors the Church's martyrs.
3. **"Held for her, never against her" is strongest when CCC 2278–2279 stands beside it.** The trust forbids aiming at her death, not easing her dying. That shrinks the realist's hardest case (the dying woman in pain) to its true size: a disagreement about whether this life bounds her good.
4. **SCG III.122 settles the order of priority without loss to either side.** It is one act, known first in the child and grounded first in God's willing of her good. It is not two injuries. The directed duty stays whole, so the realist and the Catholic agree on more here than either expected.
5. **The animal and person line is now drawn by teleology, not by "means vs side effect."** That is more honest and harder to attack: the order does give the fawn to the wolf; it never orders a person to another creature's life.
6. **Chapter text (open since Round 2):** chapter_12_current.md still has "something only a person can do" and "Only a Someone can." Directed-duty realism grants that persons lay claims, namely the victim. Rework both along v5's W1 frame when the chapter is redlined.

**Lines worth keeping**
- From v5: "Not a second injury beside hers, but His love refused in her." "We agree she is owed. We part at the grave, and over what gives her the claim at all." "Holds her life for her, never against her." "A person it never gives away." "If she was wronged, her worth never followed her strength or her mind. Then it follows what she is, and she is that as fully as you are." "Every account has a first principle, ours included."
- New: "He may give his life for another; he may not make his death the thing he asks for."
- New: "The trust forbids aiming at her death, not easing her dying."

**To verify before use:** SCG III.122 (the line usually given in Latin as *non enim Deus a nobis offenditur nisi ex eo quod contra nostrum bonum agimus*; check paragraph number and context against a critical text — I could not reach one this round); CCC 2277, 2278, 2279 (vatican.va); Kolbe's substitution (Auschwitz, 1941; canonized 1982 as a martyr); Parfit, *On What Matters* vol. 2–3, on non-metaphysical cognitivism; Parfit, "Justifiability to Each Person" (2003), against anti-aggregation. All non-magisterial material is paraphrased.
