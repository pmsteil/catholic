## The Error (censor)
Evil section: "she lets her theologians differ over it, holding *only* that no one is lost except by his own willful turning away from God, held to the end (CCC 1037)." "Only" understates defined doctrine. Also defined: grace is a free gift that leaves the will free to refuse (Trent VI ch. 5, can. 4); God commands nothing impossible (ch. 11); He predestines no one to evil (can. 17). Theologians may differ within those limits (Paul V, 1607). Fix: replace "only" with those points; keep CCC 1037.

## The three Serious items
1. **Realist — the conclusion.** "The best explanation … is a Lawgiver who is Love" outruns the text's own ledger against the godless realist: moral side at parity with a modest edge (W1(2)); evil heavy and no evidence against realism; the realist missing from the evil section's list of rivals who bear evil easily. Fix: scope the claim — name the realist; say this chapter alone does not settle the question against him; add him to the list.
2. **Evil — the conclusion's claim for Love.** A4 is shared with the nearest rivals; W2's Source is shared with Spinoza; W1(1)/W3 are clues a dark god would leave alike; evil, hiddenness and hell fall on Love only. Fix: the conclusion should say where the scale is thinnest.
3. **Evil — the Mary sentence against hell.** Read universally ("God can bring a free creature to say yes without overriding her"), with "willed for her own sake" and "permitting a refusal one could have won isn't for her good," it entails certain universal salvation, which the Church excludes; the other exit is ST I q.23 a.5 ad 3 (justice shown in some for the order of the whole). Fix: hedge the Mary sentence within the *de auxiliis* limits (the Molinist option stays open) and name q.23 a.5 ad 3 honestly. (Same place as the censor's Error.)
