# Blind review: the Evolutionary Debunker / Error Theorist

## 1. School and voice

I speak for the school that joins J. L. Mackie's error theory (moral judgments claim objective, categorically binding prescriptions; there are none; so the positive ones are untrue) to the evolutionary debunking arguments of Sharon Street (the Darwinian dilemma), Richard Joyce (moral belief explained without moral truth, so unjustified; moral fictionalism as a way to keep the practice) and Michael Ruse (morality as an adaptation that presents itself as objective), and to the cognitive science of religion (agency detection, theory of mind, attachment, supernatural-punishment and "big gods" accounts: Barrett, Bering, D. D. P. Johnson, Norenzayan). My claim is epistemic first (the truth of moral beliefs plays no part in the best explanation of why we hold them, so they are unjustified on a realist reading) and metaphysical second (there are no objective prescriptions). I do not counsel cruelty. I counsel lucidity.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

1. **Starting point for justice.** Justice is a human device, what Hume called an artificial virtue: rules that limited sympathy and scarcity make worth adopting. What anyone is "owed" is what the rules of a practice we have reason to keep (given our shared wants: security, trust, cooperation) assign. With Street I can add: owed *from within* the standpoint of beings who value as we do. **Holds** as coherent; it **breaks** the categorical sense of "owed" by design, and keeps the word only in a deflated or fictional sense (Joyce). That is the honest cost, and the text states it accurately.
2. **A hard dilemma, without the book's definitions.** The sealed transplant case (one healthy patient, five dying, perfect secrecy). I refuse it, and I can say why without "truth, justice, mercy, self-gift" as the book defines them. My reasons: (a) the policy-level reason (a medical order that kills when the books balance destroys the trust that makes medicine work, Mackie's device rationale); (b) the reason that survives perfect secrecy: on reflection I and nearly everyone I can reason with endorse being people with a firm rule against using a person this way, and that endorsement settles what I will do. **Holds** practically; **breaks** at the limit. A reflectively coherent agent who started from other attitudes (Street's ideally coherent Caligula) could have reasons, on my view, to do what we call cruel, and I cannot call him mistaken in any further sense. I borrow ordinary moral vocabulary under that confession, not the book's definitions.
3. **Equal, unconditional dignity.** I deny it as an objective fact. I recommend it, strongly, as a commitment: a bright-line rule of equal standing is far safer than any graded-worth regime, whose gradings are always drawn by the strong in their own favor. **Breaks** "unconditional": the newborn's protection is conditional on our holding the commitment, and the infant no one is drawn to hold is protected only by the rule, not by anything in her. The text's line (A4, C) that on my view she "has no worth except what we are built and taught to give" is fair.
4. **Ending point for flourishing.** There is no ultimate good. "Good for humans" can be read descriptively (what creatures like us need and, fully informed, would want: health, attachment, knowledge, cooperation, freedom from needless pain), and I grant those as facts, not as prescriptions. Guiding principles: lucidity (no comforting illusions), reduction of suffering, reciprocity, keeping faith. **Holds** without "ultimately"; it **borrows** a commitment to lucidity whose authority over the fearful I cannot show (the text's A7 point, below).

## 3. Debate highlights

**Exchange 1. The Darwinian dilemma against A1.**
*Attack.* Selection shaped our evaluative starting points for fitness. Either moral truths have no causal or explanatory link to those starting points, and agreement between them and our beliefs would be a coincidence, or the realist says selection tracked moral truth, which is bad science (selection tracks fitness). So by the text's own rule in A (trust what seems true after reflection "including honest reflection on how we came to believe it," unless "its truth plays no part in the best account of why we hold it"), A1 is defeated.
*Defense (from the text, W2 and A).* The rule is the right rule, and the text adopts it openly; but the second disjunct is governed by "something more certain." The realist and theist answer with a third factor: the features that make a deed wrong (pain, trust, betrayal) are the very features survival taught us to notice, so selection could push us toward true moral beliefs without tracking moral truth as such (W2). The reader may be more certain of A1 than of the debunker's premise that truth plays no part.
*Rejoinder.* The third-factor reply assumes the conclusion it needs: that the features survival made salient are the wrong-making ones. That is the coincidence, restated.
*Result: Held.* The text does not claim to defeat the dilemma; it states my rejoinder (W2: "a judgment about where explanation may stop, and the rivals do not share it"). Parity, stated as parity.

**Exchange 2. Companions in guilt: A7 against me.**
*Attack.* The text says I ask people to give up beliefs "because the evidence requires it," and that requirement binds only those who want truth. Fine. Epistemic norms are hypothetical or constitutive of belief (Olson, Joyce, verify). My argument is addressed to inquirers; it need not bind the fearful.
*Defense (Cuneo-style, verify; CCC 2104).* Then you cannot say anyone *ought* to accept your conclusion, nor that the man who believes against the evidence because it comforts him is irrational in any sense beyond failing an aim he could drop. But your case trades on exactly that charge against the realist: "your belief is unjustified." If epistemic "ought" is as queer as moral "ought," error theory about the one is error theory about the other, and your conclusion becomes advice.
*Rejoinder.* Advice is all I need, and all the text claims for itself against the man who will not look (its conclusion: it "cannot compel"). Olson accepts epistemic error theory about irreducible epistemic reasons and reduces the rest to evidential relations plus the aim of belief. The cost is real but not self-refuting.
*Result: Held.* The text says "neither settles it," which is the truth. A standoff at parity.

**Exchange 3. A4 and the Moorean reply.**
*Attack.* The text's weight against me "lies in how much surer the reader is that the infant no one held was wronged than of any premise in his argument." But Moorean confidence is the output of the faculty under suspicion; using it to reject the debunking is bootstrapping.
*Defense.* Two points, both strong. First, every argument ends in premises held by the same mind; the debunker's confidence in his own explanatory premises is also an output of evolved cognition. Comparing confidences is not bootstrapping; it is the only way anyone weighs anything. Second, and sharper: A4 is the belief selection *least* predicts. The text says so itself (A4: instinct fails "for the infant no one is drawn to hold"; Seneca; the hillside). Kin selection and discriminative parental investment predict the opposite of A4. So the debunker cannot debunk A4 by selection; he must debunk the *reasoning* that extended regard to her, which is reason holding itself to consistency, and that brings him close to debunking reason.
*Rejoinder.* Consistency carries only the attitudes it starts with (Street): from evolved sympathy plus the descriptive fact "she is one of us," consistency yields A4 without A4 being true. And the history of A4 is not neutral: a truth "fully recognizable by reason alone" that most reasoners failed to see for most of history, and that arose where one religion spread, fits my account more exactly than the realist's.
*Result: Held with repair.* The Moorean comparison is legitimate and the "selection least predicts A4" point is the text's best card against me. But W2's line that a world with no one behind it "predicts it too" understates my side: I predict the specific pattern (conscience fails just where fitness and the law part), while the Church's account must add the wound of sin to absorb it. The text already puts this on the scale in the evil section ("So, roughly, does conscience"); W2 should say so too (Repair 2).

**Exchange 4. Does theism do better than godless realism against the dilemma?**
*Attack (Wielenberg, *Robust Ethics*, verify).* God, on Catholic teaching, worked through evolution. Then the correlation between our moral beliefs and moral truth still runs through the third factor; theism adds a guarantor, but the evidence of a guarantee would be moral cognition that departs from fitness where fitness and truth part, and we find the opposite.
*Defense.* The theist does not claim a detectable departure; he claims that the same Mind made the natures the law is about, the minds that read it, and the process that formed them, so the third-factor correlation is not brute but intended (ST I q.15; I-II q.91 a.2; *Veritatis Splendor* 43). The failures are expected on Catholic teaching: our nature is wounded and the intellect darkened (CCC 405, 1960), while the first principles are known to all (ST I-II q.94 a.4, a.6). And reason's self-correction (the slave, the stranger) is the law of truth at work.
*Rejoinder.* "Wounded nature" can absorb any pattern of failure; an account that would fit every distribution of conscience is not confirmed by this one.
*Result: Held.* The text claims only that one Source "explains it more fully" and calls that a judgment the rivals do not share. That is not an overclaim. I would not let the text claim more, and it does not.

**Exchange 5. The cognitive science of religion against W1(1) and W3.**
*Attack.* The longing to be known wholly, the inner judge appealing to an all-seeing Judge (Smith), guilt that reaches for the dead: these come from attachment, theory of mind, and reputation psychology, the same systems that produce belief in watching gods. The "fit" between the longing and a God who answers it is manufactured: the concept of God was shaped to meet the longing. Fit between a desire and a concept tailored to it is no evidence. Worse, the "converging" witnesses share a common cause, so their convergence is double counting.
*Defense.* The text already grants most of this: "the longing alone settles nothing"; "a mind made for living bonds would ask whether or not she hears"; W1(1) "does not reach the error theorist"; and in C and the evil section the two witnesses are "clues of fit, not evidence," which "a dark god or an indifferent world would leave just as we find them." Further, the case is an argument, not a belief held without argument; the cognitive science of religion explains spontaneous theistic belief, and an inferential case is judged on its premises.
*Rejoinder.* Agreed on both. But then the conclusion's appeal to Newman's "many signs converging in one mind" must not count these clues as independent signs. And the text should make its CSR defense explicit: at present CSR appears only as a half-clause ("often joined to an evolutionary account of conscience and of religion").
*Result: Held with repair* (Repairs 3, 5, 6).

**Exchange 6. Is error theory self-undermining? (the text's C row)**
*Attack on the text.* C says that on my view no one "ought to keep looking when he fears what he will find, or to care whether its own case is true." That reads as a refutation.
*Defense.* It is offered as a price, not a refutation; C ends: "Its ablest defenders accept that cost or argue that it is none."
*Rejoinder.* Then it is fair.
*Result: Held.*

**Exchange 7. "The case ends in a choice."**
*Attack.* The A preamble says that against the one who says nothing binds, "the case ends in a choice, not a proof." But A7 forbids believing what comforts. If A4 is chosen, it is held by will, and the error theorist's suspicion (moral belief is wanted, not found) is confirmed by the text's own wording.
*Defense.* What is meant is not a choice of belief but the comparative judgment of the conclusion ("which are you surer of"), which is epistemic.
*Rejoinder.* Then say that.
*Result: Held with repair* (Repair 1).

**Exchange 8. The Moorean weapon used selectively (1 Samuel 15).**
*Attack.* The text's main weapon against me is: be surer the abandoned infant was wronged than of any theory saying otherwise. Yet with St. Thomas it overrides the same seeming about the infants of 1 Samuel 15:3 by a theory (God's right as Lord of life). If theory may override the seeming there, why may my evolutionary explanation not override it here?
*Defense.* The book keeps the seeming whole for every human agent acting on his own right (CCC 2258) and overrides only its extension to the Creator, on the distinction between the One who gives being and can restore it and creatures who can do neither; that distinction is principled, not ad hoc. And the book does not hide the cost: "a reader weighing by reason alone may be surer that those infants were wronged than of any argument that they were not ... for him it is a cost against the case."
*Rejoinder.* The distinction is intelligible, and the cost is booked where it belongs. I press nothing further.
*Result: Held.*

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**As written: NONE.**

Against my school the text claims only what it can hold: that error theory is consistent and pays a price (A1, A4, A7), that the debunking argument and its own replies (A7 and A4) end at parity, and that the case cannot compel the one who gives up A4. Nothing it asserts against me is unsound.

**Minor items**
1. "there the case ends in a choice, not a proof" (A preamble) suggests belief by will, against A7. Repair 1.
2. W2: "a world with no one behind it predicts it too" understates my side: I predict the *pattern* of conscience's failures more exactly. Repair 2.
3. The cognitive science of religion is mentioned only in passing; the text's good answer (it explains belief held without argument, not an argument) is never stated. Repair 3.
4. Convergence and common cause: the conclusion's Newman appeal does not say that signs one cause could produce together count as one. Repair 6, with Repair 5.
5. W3: "it shows what answer would fit" could add that a concept shaped by the longing fits it either way. Repair 4 (optional).

**Misstatements of my school** (none serious)
- "Its price: torturing a child for fun is not really wrong": accurate, but readers may hear "so it is permitted." Mackie's and Olson's error theories deny "permitted" as firmly as "wrong." Repair 3.
- The heading "Nothing really binds" and "for the one who says nothing binds": error theorists (Joyce, Olson) accept hypothetical and instrumental reasons; what they deny is binding *whatever one wants*. The C row's body makes this clear for A7; the heading can stand if Repair 3 is made.
- Joyce's moral fictionalism, the school's main answer to "then why go on?", is absent; Mackie's "device" stands in for it adequately, but naming Joyce is fairer (Repair 3).

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- The Darwinian dilemma versus the third-factor reply: each side's stopping point is "a judgment about where explanation may stop."
- Companions in guilt (A7): epistemic error theory versus categorical epistemic norms.
- Moorean confidence in A4 versus the explanatory premise of the debunking.
- Theistic versus godless realism on the fit: neither gains on me; the text says so.

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

**Insights**
- **The error theorist shares the text's conditional.** Mackie, in *The Miracle of Theism* (ch. 6), granted that if there were objective, intrinsically prescriptive values, they would make a god's existence more probable than it would otherwise be (paraphrase, verify). Joined to his acceptance of Anscombe's diagnosis (verify), this means my school and the book agree that the full "moral ought" needs something like a Lawgiver; we part only over whether anything is morally binding. The C row could say this in one sentence: it shows that, against my school, the whole case turns on A1, A4 and A7, which is where the text already places it.
- **Selection did not give us A4; reason did, against selection.** This is the text's best ground against me, and it is now spread across A4, W2 and the conclusion. One sentence in W2, at the point where it says the case rests on A7 and A4, would gather it: the belief the debunker most needs to explain away is the one selection least predicts, so he must debunk the reasoning that carried regard to the infant, not the instinct that withheld it.
- **The rule in A is the debunker's own test.** The book could say so openly: it takes the strongest form of the genealogical challenge (truth playing no part in the best account) as its own rule, and binds itself by it in the Nietzsche row. Few theistic cases do that.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "What still seems true to us after honest reflection, including honest reflection on how we came to believe it, we have reason to trust until something more certain shows it false, or shows that its truth plays no part in the best account of why we hold it."
- "to call the alarm false one must already know that the difference it tracks does not matter, which is the very question"
- "A history settles neither."
- "Its ablest defenders accept that cost or argue that it is none."
- "Against him the case rests on two things, and neither settles it"
- "a mind made for living bonds would ask whether or not she hears"
- "No rival's origins are used against it that we would not let it use against ours."
- "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."

## 8. Score

**8 / 10.** Against my school the text claims exactly what it can hold, a costly but consistent rival and a standoff over A4 and A7, states my argument in its strongest form, and leaves only small repairs (the "choice" wording, the "predicts it too" understatement, and convergence without common causes).

**Honest ceiling for any theistic case against my school:** parity. Error theory is consistent, so no moral argument can refute it. The best any case can do is make it costly (A1, A4, A7) and show that its own premises are held with more confidence than the debunking premise. With the six repairs this text reaches that ceiling (9/10 for robustness); no theistic case can reach 10 against me, and this one honestly does not claim to.
