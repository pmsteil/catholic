# Censor's report: "A moral argument for God" (Ch. 12, *What Is Love?*)

## Scope

A pre-imprimatur doctrinal read of /tmp/claude-0/lab11/ARGUMENT.md in full (Form, A intro, A1–A7, W1 frame, W1(1), W1(2), W1 closing, W2, W3 both paragraphs, every C row, the section on evil, the Conclusion). Read sentence by sentence against Scripture (RSVCE), the Catechism, the councils (Trent, Vatican I, Vatican II), the encyclicals and other magisterial documents, and St. Thomas. The brief was read for frame only. Philosophical accuracy about non-Catholic schools is outside a censor's office and is not judged here, except where a description of them carries a claim about Catholic teaching. Citations were checked on vatican.va where possible (authoritative on its own); the rest are listed in section 2 by how they were checked.

Special attention went to: God as author of the natural law; 1 Samuel 15, divine commands and St. Thomas; forgiveness, Baptism, Reconciliation, temporal punishment and satisfaction; hell, the Church's hope and prayer, grace and freedom; euthanasia, suicide, legitimate defense, CCC 2258, the vital-conflict case; the unbaptized infant; other religions (*Nostra Aetate*); overclaiming against CCC 31–36; culpability of unbelievers; polemic; uncited claims about what the Church has or has not declared.

## Bottom line

One Error:

- **E1 (evil section, on grace and freedom).** "...she lets her theologians differ over it, holding *only* that no one is lost except by his own willful turning away from God, held to the end (CCC 1037)." This misstates how far the Church's teaching goes. On this exact question she also holds, as defined doctrine, that grace is a free gift that does not destroy freedom, so that one moved by it can still refuse (Trent, Session VI, ch. 5 and can. 4); that God commands nothing impossible (Session VI, ch. 11; can. 18); and that He predestines no one to evil (Session VI, can. 17; CCC 1037). The licence for theologians to differ (Paul V, 1607, ending the *De auxiliis* dispute) applies only inside those limits. "Only" has to go. The fix is in section 3.

Everything else is either sound or needs only care: missing citations, or wording that should be tightened so it cannot be misread. None of it touches faith or morals as written.

## 3. Replacement wording

**R1 (E1, required).** Old:
> why one will is won by His grace and another refuses it to the end, the Church does not explain, and she lets her theologians differ over it, holding only that no one is lost except by his own willful turning away from God, held to the end (CCC 1037).

New:
> why one will is won by His grace and another refuses it to the end, the Church does not explain, and she lets her theologians differ over it. She holds that grace is a free gift that does not destroy freedom, so that one moved by it can still refuse (Trent, Session VI, ch. 5, can. 4); that God commands nothing impossible (Session VI, ch. 11); and that He predestines no one to hell, for no one is lost except by his own willful turning away from God, held to the end (CCC 1037).

**R2 (recommended).** Old:
> How permitting a refusal held to the end is for the sake of the one who refuses, reason cannot show, nor does the Church explain it.

New:
> The Church teaches that God permits such a refusal because He respects the freedom He gave, and that the one who is lost is not spent for others but excludes himself (CCC 311, 1033); how that permission is still for the sake of the one who refuses, reason cannot show, and the Church does not explain further.

**R3 (recommended).** Old:
> she has never declared of anyone that he is lost.

New:
> she has never declared of any human being that he is lost, and it is not granted to us, without special divine revelation, to know who is (John Paul II, General Audience, 28 July 1999, 4).

**R4 (recommended).** Old:
> and there the case ends in a choice, not a proof.

New:
> and there the case ends in a choice, not in a proof that compels him (CCC 31).

**R5 (recommended).** Old:
> never in what they are worth.

New:
> never in the dignity that is theirs by being human (DI 7).

**R6 (recommended).** Old:
> a nature made in His image, which reason can see (DI 1) in every human being, whatever her power to understand or choose (DI 24, 53),

New:
> a rational nature, whose dignity reason can see (DI 1) in every human being, whatever her power to understand or choose (DI 24, 53), and which faith knows is made in His image (Genesis 1:27; CCC 1700),

## Verdict

**Nihil obstat as it stands: no, on one point only.** Once R1 corrects E1, nothing in the text is contrary to faith or morals, and a censor could give the nihil obstat. R2–R10 are recommended so that a reader cannot take a correct teaching for an incomplete or uncited one. They are not conditions. On the matters singled out for this review, the text is accurate and carefully fenced: the natural law with God as its author and no voluntarism; 1 Samuel 15 held with St. Thomas inside DV 12 and VD 42; the distinction between guilt, satisfaction and restitution; hell affirmed alongside the Church's prayer and hope; the absolutes on euthanasia, suicide, the innocent and the vital-conflict case; the unbaptized infant; *Nostra Aetate*; the non-presumption of fault in unbelievers; and the modest claims made under CCC 31–36.
