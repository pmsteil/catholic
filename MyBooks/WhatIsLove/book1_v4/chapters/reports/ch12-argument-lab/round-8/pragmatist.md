# Blind review, Round 8: the pragmatist / anti-representationalist and relativist school

## 1. School and voice

I speak for the pragmatists and the relativists. That means Dewey and James. It means above all Richard Rorty, for whom no vocabulary, whether in ethics, physics or mathematics, mirrors a world beyond our practices of justification, and for whom moral progress is the widening of "we," won more by sentimental education than by argument. It also means Gilbert Harman (moral judgments relative to implicit agreements; moral facts do no explanatory work) and David Wong (pluralistic relativism: more than one true morality, constrained by human nature and by what morality is for). One correction about the label: Rorty disowned "relativism." He called himself "ethnocentric," meaning we can justify only from where we stand (verify). Harman and Wong own the label. What we share is that we refuse the demand the text rests on: that A1–A7 be true of something beyond our best practice, and that their "reach" and "necessity" need a Source. I argue as a non-theist who asserts most of A at full strength and declines only the metaphysical gloss.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

1. **Starting point for justice.** Rorty: justice is loyalty to a larger group (paraphrase of "Justice as a Larger Loyalty," verify). What anyone is owed is what our widest community of loyalty has come to owe its members. Principles are summaries of practices that worked, not the source of what is owed. Harman: justice comes from implicit bargaining, which explains why the duty not to harm is stronger than the duty to help (verify). **Verdict: coherent, but it breaks at the edge.** Those outside every bargain and every "we" (Hume's "weak creatures") are owed only what we choose to give. It borrows its direction, *wider is better*, from a universalism it cannot itself justify.

2. **A hard dilemma, resolved.** The case: a lucid patient with ALS, where assisted death is legal, asks his physician to end his life. Dewey's method: treat it as a problematic situation, and "rehearse" each option in imagination with every stakeholder. Weigh his suffering and his authorship of his own life against the risk that the option itself pressures the disabled and the poor. Rorty's public test is to avoid cruelty and humiliation. We would probably permit it with strict safeguards. **Verdict: coherent.** It uses thin truth (honest disclosure), fairness and compassion. It does not use self-gift or the covenantal sense the book gives these words, and it openly denies the second half of A6, which the text already names as the book's own claim.

3. **Equal, unconditional dignity.** Rorty: nothing grounds it, and asking for a ground is asking for a skyhook. Equal dignity is our best idea. We hold it unconditionally in the sense that we refuse to let anything condition it, and we spread it by stories (Rorty's example is *Uncle Tom's Cabin*), not by proofs (verify). Wong: it belongs to some adequate moralities, not to all. **Verdict: coherent, but it gives up a clause.** It keeps "equal" and "unconditional" as commitments. It denies "prior to anyone's recognition" in any sense beyond what we now say. Worth is extended, not found. This is the price the text's conclusion asks the reader to weigh, and I pay it knowingly.

4. **Ultimate flourishing.** Dewey: growth itself is the only moral end (paraphrase, *Reconstruction in Philosophy* ch. 7, verify). Rorty: in private, self-creation; in public, a liberal utopia that has stopped humiliating anyone. James: meliorism, a world that *may* be saved by our effort, with no guarantee. **Verdict: it breaks on "ultimately".** We deny there is an ultimate good. "Growth" needs a direction: a swindler grows in skill. Dewey's answer, "growth that opens further growth," is thin and partly borrowed.

## 3. Debate highlights

**E1. The A heading's choice between truths and shared feelings.**
*Attack.* "These are offered as truths, not as feelings people happen to share" offers a false choice. Between truths "out there" and feelings we "happen" to share lies the pragmatist's third option: commitments warranted within a practice, held with full seriousness, open to revision, and not "happened upon," because they have a history and reasons. The heading's rule for trusting beliefs ("what still seems true... until... what produced the belief had nothing to do with its truth") is a fine Peircean rule in practice. But its last clause assumes beliefs can be measured against a truth beyond all justification.
*Defense.* The heading's sense of "truth" is minimal: it asks only that the reader trust what survives honest reflection. The pragmatist asserts A1, A3 and A7 at first order himself, so he is not attacking the ledger, only the gloss. And "feelings people happen to share" is close to Rorty's "ethnocentrism": justification to *us*.
*Rejoinder.* Agreed on first order. But the heading is where the case sets the terms, and it lists the deniers. Pragmatists and relativists are missing from that list, so the reader never learns there is a denial that keeps A and refuses the gloss.
**Held with repair** (R1).

**E2. "Every account that keeps these words agrees: the wrong is wrong of necessity" (W1 frame).**
*Attack.* As written, this is false. Rorty, Harman, Wong and Dewey all keep "obligation," "duty" and "wrong" in live, unembarrassed use, and they deny necessity. For Rorty, moral obligation differs from prudence only in that it concerns a wider "we" (verify). For Harman, it is relative to agreements. W1 then uses this "agreement" to rule that "no one can release us" "does not decide between the accounts," and narrows the contest to realist rivals. Against the school that attacks starting points, the frame assumes the point in dispute.
*Defense.* Anscombe's question was whether the words keep *their sense*, the sense of a binding law. Those who keep the sounds but reduce the sense have, by her test, not kept them. So "keeps these words" can fairly be read as "keeps them in that sense."
*Rejoinder.* Then the text should say so. The pragmatist's whole claim is that the reduced sense is all the sense the words ever had in use. If the sentence is true only by definition, the reader should see the definition.
**Held with repair** (R2). As written, this is the misstatement listed in §5.

**E3. Grounding is idle: Rorty against W1(2) and the reach claim in the "A God who is Love" row.**
*Attack.* A ground has never made anyone more just. Kant's arguments did not end slavery; Stowe's novel did more. The text admits as much itself: "Christians too were slow to see what slavery denied," with the grounding in hand. The demand for a ground for equal worth answers a question nobody needs answered in order to protect the newborn. What protects her is a culture that has learned to see her as one of us.
*Defense.* (a) The case never claims to work better in practice. It claims to explain and to be true, and A7 forbids choosing a view because it works. (b) The text has the best reply in its own words: "where instinct fails, for the infant no one is drawn to hold, we learn whether what we meant was worth or only attraction." Sentimental education works through identification, and it fails exactly where identification fails. (c) The Church agrees that equal worth spread through persons and stories (CCC 37–38; W3: "a mother's love before a creed"). The theist does not need to deny the sentimental mechanism, only that it is the source.
*Rejoinder.* (b) is answered by *commitment*: we decide to include the infant no one holds. But a decided inclusion is conferred worth, which is what A4 denies. So the exchange ends where it should, at whether her worth is found or extended.
**Held.** The text claims explanation, not usefulness, and it names the cost honestly.

**E4. Reach and necessity are idle wheels (W2).**
*Attack.* "How we can see that cruelty would be wrong for any mind in any world" uses the mirror metaphor, the mind *seeing* a modal reality. The pragmatist reads the universality as grammar. Given what we mean by "cruelty" and "wrong," anything we would call cruelty is wrong. Nothing has been seen across worlds, so there is no reach to explain. The comparison with mathematics ("he does not distrust the proof") does not bind a global pragmatist, who deflates mathematical necessity the same way. Harman adds an asymmetry: we trust mathematics because it is indispensable to explaining what we observe, and moral facts are not (verify).
*Defense.* (a) Harman's asymmetry concerns first-order content. W2's point concerns *modal* reach, and selection explains modal knowledge in mathematics no better than in ethics, so on reach the asymmetry fails. (b) The stronger comparison is the text's own A7: the norm that we *ought* to follow the evidence is no more indispensable to physics in Harman's sense than the moral ought, yet every debunker relies on it in making his argument. (c) If the necessity is only grammar, ask why our grammar tracks what cruelty does to the child, and what we say of a people whose grammar does not.
*Rejoinder.* Rorty takes the global horn: epistemic norms too are social. That is consistent, and it is the price the "Nothing really binds" row names for A7. But it is Rorty's price, not Mackie's, and W2's note on non-realists does not list him.
**Held with repair** (R3, R4). W2 already says "it weighs, it does not settle," and that modesty is correct.

**E5. Moral progress, and "slow to see" (A4).**
*Attack.* "Slow to see" assumes there was something to see. On Rorty's account, the abolitionist and the feminist were prophets building a new language, not uncovering a hidden truth about slaves or women (paraphrase of "Feminism and Pragmatism," verify). Progress is the widening of "we," and "we" have come to call that widening better.
*Defense.* Then before anyone extended it, the slave in 1600 was not wronged in any way a truth could record. If the other side had won and taught everyone otherwise, nothing would have been there to say they were wrong. Rorty was candid about this. Reading Orwell, he granted that if O'Brien breaks Winston, no truth in Winston was there to protect him (paraphrase, *Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity* ch. 8, verify). The conclusion's test then applies: which is the reader surer of, that the exposed infant was wronged before anyone recognized it, or this theory?
*Rejoinder.* "It was wrong in 1600" is a sentence we assert *now*, and we assert it of 1600. Defense: yes, but what makes it so waits on the winners' vocabulary, which is exactly what "prior to anyone's recognition" denies.
**Held.** This is the decisive cost for my school, and the text states it only for Humeans ("equal worth wins only by being ours"). It needs its own row (R6).

**E6. Wong: persistent reasonable disagreement explains better than one Lawgiver (against the conclusion).**
*Attack.* If one Lawgiver wrote the law in every heart and reason can read it, why do informed, coherent, reflective traditions settle so differently: the Confucian ordering of community and role, the liberal ordering of rights, and the hard cases in A6? Wong's pluralism, a few universal limits set by human nature and the job morality does, with several adequate moralities above them, predicts exactly this.
*Defense.* Aquinas predicted it first. The first common principles are the same for all and known to all. The further conclusions can fail through passion, bad custom or bad habit; his example is the Germans who did not think robbery wrong (ST I-II q.94 a.4, a.6). And the Church teaches that the natural law's precepts "are not perceived by everyone clearly and immediately" (CCC 1960). Wong also grants a universal core, which is A1, and that core's reach is W2's question.
*Rejoinder.* Wong explains the core by the job morality does for creatures like us, which is the "flourishing of our kind" answer, and he claims nothing for every possible mind. That leaves a standoff at parity on reach. The Thomist reply on disagreement is sound, but it does not appear in the text.
**Held with repair** (R5).

**E7. Harman: "wrong even if every culture approved" (A1).**
*Attack.* An "inner" judgment ("it was wrong of him," "he ought not") says the agent had reasons. If the agent shares none of our attitudes, those judgments misfire. We can call him evil and our enemy, but not "wrong of him" (paraphrase of "Moral Relativism Defended," verify).
*Defense.* A2 is about the victim, not the agent's reasons. Harman's split leaves the child of the approving culture harmed, and her harm deplored, but does she have a claim against the one who harmed her? If his split erases the difference between wronging and worsening for every outsider, he pays A2's price there.
*Rejoinder.* Harman can say she was harmed and that the act was terrible by our morality. He grants that he cannot say *he* violated her claim in his own terms.
**Held at parity.** This is a real cost for Harman and should be named in the new row.

**E8. James: the pragmatist who is a witness for the other side.**
*Attack.* James's "The Moral Philosopher and the Moral Life" grounds obligation in the claims of sentient beings, with no God needed: even two people alone on a rock have a moral universe (verify).
*Defense.* The same essay, as I understand it, holds that the stable, complete moral order a philosopher seeks is possible only if there is a divine thinker whose demands cover all the rest, and that belief in such a demander calls out the "strenuous mood" (paraphrase, verify). More to the point, if obligation is the claim of a claimant, as James says and as A2 says, then when the claimant dies the claim has no one left to press it. That is exactly W1(1)'s choice of roads. Of the pragmatists, James sides with the third.
*Rejoinder.* James's God was finite, and his theory of truth would call theism true if it "works." The text must not take that theory on, because A7 and W3 forbid believing a thing because it comforts.
**Held, as a clue only.** See §7.

## 4. Ledger

- **A heading:** Holds with repair. The rule for trusting beliefs is acceptable to a pragmatist in practice. The choice between truths and shared feelings, and the list of deniers, leave out the denial that keeps A and refuses the gloss (R1).
- **A1:** Holds. Rorty asserts it at first order. Harman's limit on judging outsiders is a denial with a cost (E7). Wong grants it as part of every adequate morality.
- **A2:** Holds. The letter-in-the-street case is sharp, and the pragmatist's thin "cruelty and humiliation" vocabulary does not erase the difference between wronging and worsening. Harman's split pays for it.
- **A3:** Holds. Rorty accepts "no vote, no consent makes it right" as a rule of our practice. Only Harman's deepest-agreement view could in principle shift it, and the heading's "none is beyond denial" covers that.
- **A4:** Holds. "Prior to anyone's recognition" is the clause my school denies, openly and at a cost (E3, E5). "Slow to see" is correct and has its price.
- **A5:** Holds. Dewey dissolves the dichotomy between fact and value, but agrees that what is desired is not thereby desirable. A5 is modest enough for him.
- **A6:** Holds. The absolute at every number and against a man's own consent is honestly labeled as the book's own claim. The point against accounts that count only totals stands against a pragmatism of cruelty-avoidance too, though less sharply.
- **A7:** Holds. "Against the evidence" is narrower than Clifford, so James's "right to believe" does not reach it. Rorty denies that truth is a goal of inquiry. That cost is real, but it is stated only in the error-theory row (see R6).
- **W1 frame:** Holds with repair. "Every account that keeps these words agrees... of necessity" is false of pragmatists and relativists (E2, R2). The rest, including the reading of Anscombe, the agreement on owed guilt and the parting "at the grave," holds.
- **W1(1):** Holds as a clue. The second road is the pragmatist's road (loyalty kept by memory and story), and "many of the dead have no one left to remember them by name" answers it fairly. James's account of claims sharpens the point (E8).
- **W1(2):** Holds. "A law or an order... does not want any one of them" also holds against a vocabulary or a "we": we too fail to want every child. Rorty's "no further why" is a stopping point, and the text treats it as one.
- **W1 closing:** Holds. "A will that invents values can command, but it cannot bind" is aimed at voluntarism, and it holds as well against a "we" that invents values. Rorty would reply that we bind ourselves the way a promise does. That is a standoff, not a defect.
- **W2:** Holds with repair. The modal reach claim survives Harman's indispensability point (E4). The comparison should lean on A7's epistemic ought as well as on the proof (R4). The note sorting non-realists leaves out the global pragmatist (R3), and the passage needs Aquinas on disagreement (R5).
- **W3 (longing):** Holds. "The longing alone settles nothing" and "that is not evidence" are exactly right. Rorty's hope that we grow out of the wish for a non-human authority (verify) is a prediction, not a refutation, and the text does not need to answer it.
- **W3 (hiddenness):** Holds. Nothing in my school bears on it beyond what the text already grants.
- **C intro:** Holds with repair. "Each rival below" is true but leaves out the school that refuses the demand to explain (R6).
- **C, Nothing really binds:** Holds. Do not take it as covering Rorty: he rejects Mackie's error theory as still a theory of representation (verify).
- **C, Feeling, refined:** Holds. It is the nearest row to my school, and its key lines ("which one sees truly only from within the heart we share"; "equal worth wins only by being ours"; "no further why is needed") apply to Rorty exactly. But it describes our words as *voicing attitudes*, which Rorty rejects as one more dualism.
- **C, Reason's own law:** Holds.
- **C, What we owe each other:** Holds.
- **C, Well-being, counted impartially:** Holds.
- **C, The flourishing of our kind:** Holds. The "no place to stand outside" answer is close to Dewey's and is fairly stated.
- **C, Godless realism:** Holds.
- **C, An impersonal sacred order:** Holds.
- **C, The Buddha's Dhamma:** Holds.
- **C, Power and rank:** Holds. A Brandom-style pragmatist takes the first horn (discursive norms bind every reasoner, because reasoning is made of them), which sends the question back to W2. Rorty takes the second and bites the bullet. Both are covered.
- **C, A God who is Love:** Holds. "Reach, not count" is the right claim. The pragmatist offers a rival unity, law, beings and minds as aspects of one practice, but it buys that unity by making her personhood depend on our descriptions, and that cost belongs in R6.
- **C, missing row:** Fails as written. There is no row for pragmatism or relativism (§5, R6).
- **Evil, "On the other side of the scale":** Holds. It is honest, and it carries the weight where it belongs.
- **Evil, First:** Holds.
- **Evil, Second:** Holds.
- **Evil, Third:** Holds with an optional repair. Its list of hopes without a Creator could add James's meliorism (R7).
- **Conclusion:** Holds. The Moorean test of the exposed infant is the best line in the case against my school, and a Rortian cannot honestly refuse it. He prizes first-order conviction over theory. He can only refuse its last step ("it follows what she is"), and the text puts that step as "this book answers," not as proven.

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**Serious: the case does not confront the denial of its starting points, and W1 misstates who agrees.**
The A heading promises that "the comparison (C) shows what each denial costs." The pragmatist and relativist denial, which keeps A1–A7 as our best vocabulary and declines to call them true beyond it, appears nowhere in that list, in W2's sorting of non-realists, or in C. Worse, the W1 frame asserts as common ground ("every account that keeps these words agrees") the very necessity my school denies while keeping the words. A Rortian reader finds himself filed as an error theorist (he is not one) or an expressivist (he rejects that too), and never sees the costs that belong to him: worth that is extended, not found; prophets with nothing yet to see; nothing in Winston for truth to protect; widening with no reason to keep widening. The objection is valid. Its premises (the text's own promise, and the plain existence of the school) are certain. As written, the text cannot answer it at parity, because the answer is not on the page.
It is not Fatal. The substance of the answer is already in the text ("equal worth wins only by being ours," "slow to see," "where instinct fails," the conclusion's test of the infant), so R1, R2 and R6 repair it at low cost.

**Minor**
- W2's mathematical comparison is open to Harman's indispensability reply unless it is joined to A7's epistemic ought (R4).
- W2 needs Aquinas's own account of moral disagreement against Wong (R5).
- The "Feeling, refined" row's wording ("moral words voice our attitudes") does not cover anti-representationalists, though its arguments transfer.
- The evil section's list of hopes without a Creator omits pragmatist meliorism (R7).

**Misstatements of my school**
- None by name: no pragmatist or relativist is named anywhere.
- By implication, the W1 frame's "every account that keeps these words agrees" misstates us.
- So does the A heading's choice between "truths" and "feelings people happen to share," which suggests the only alternative to truth is shared feeling.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- Whether a ground for equal worth is needed, or whether a commitment held unconditionally is enough (E3).
- Whether universality is seen or is grammar (E4).
- Whether reach beyond our kind is a datum at all, against Wong (E6).
- Whether "it was wrong in 1600," said now, is enough (E5).
- Neither side can compel the other. The conclusion says so ("it cannot compel the one who gives up A4"), and Rorty says the same of himself.

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact old → new)

**R1 (A heading).**
Old: "These are offered as truths, not as feelings people happen to share."
New: "These are offered as truths, not as feelings people happen to share, nor as the best vocabulary one community has so far, true for us until we learn to talk otherwise."

Old: "None is beyond denial: Mackie denies that any of them is objectively true;"
New: "None is beyond denial: Mackie denies that any of them is objectively true; the pragmatist Richard Rorty asserts most of them, but denies that any is true of anything beyond our widening 'we'; the relativists Gilbert Harman and David Wong hold them true only relative to a morality, and Wong holds that more than one can be true;"

**R2 (W1 frame).**
Old: "On one point every account that keeps these words agrees: the wrong is wrong of necessity, not up to anyone."
New: "On one point every account that keeps these words in that sense agrees: the wrong is wrong of necessity, not up to anyone. (Some keep the words and let that sense go: for the pragmatist the wrong is not up to any one of us, but it is ours; for the relativist it is wrong relative to a morality. C weighs what that costs.)"

**R3 (W2, closing note).**
Old: "Those who say our moral words voice refined feeling need no meeting of law and mind; for them the question is the one in C.)"
New: "Those who say our moral words voice refined feeling, and the pragmatists, who say that no words of ours, in ethics or in mathematics, mirror anything beyond our practices, need no meeting of law and mind; for them the question is the one in C.)"

**R4 (W2, the debunker).**
Old: "But survival no more selected for seeing what holds in every possible world when we read a proof, and he does not distrust the proof."
New: "But survival no more selected for seeing what holds in every possible world when we read a proof, and he does not distrust the proof; nor did it select for seeing that every reasoning mind ought to follow the evidence, and he relies on that ought in the very argument he makes (A7)."

**R5 (W2, after "...disordered self-love is the root of sin (ST I-II q.77 a.4; CCC 1850, 405).").**
Insert: "Nor does one Source require that every people agree. Aquinas held that the first principles of the law are the same for all and known to all, while its further conclusions can be darkened by passion, bad custom or bad habit (ST I-II q.94 a.4, a.6), and the Church teaches that its precepts are not perceived by everyone clearly and immediately (CCC 1960)."

**R6 (C, new row, after "Feeling, refined").**
Insert:
"- **Our best vocabulary, widening** (pragmatism: Dewey, and above all Richard Rorty; and the relativists Gilbert Harman and David Wong) does not deny A; it declines to ask what makes A true. It can say that torturing a child is wrong, and would be even if every culture approved; it adds only that this is said from within the vocabulary we have come to, and that no vocabulary, in ethics or in physics, mirrors a world beyond it. Moral progress, on this account, is the widening of 'we,' won less by argument than by stories that teach us to see the stranger, the slave and the disabled as one of us, and such stories, it says, have done more than any ground. Much of that the Church can grant: equal worth spread where the Gospel was told (CCC 37–38), and for most of us God's love comes first through persons (W3). Harman holds that to say a man ought not to have done a thing applies only to one who shares the understanding it rests on; Wong, that more than one morality can be true, within limits our nature sets. Its price is the one the infant on the hillside shows. If worth is what our widest 'we' extends, then before anyone extended it the slave was not yet wronged in a way any truth could record; the abolitionist was making a new language, not seeing what was there; and the infant no one is drawn to hold is safe exactly as far as our stories reach her. Rorty was honest about this: reading Orwell, he granted that if torture breaks a man until he believes what he is told, no truth within him was there to protect him. When two peoples part over the newborn, it can say which sees truly only by our lights, and equal worth wins only by being ours. It cannot say why 'we' should go on widening and not shrink; widening is simply what we now count as better. And when the one we wronged is gone, it can keep her memory, but no one is left to forgive."
(Verify the Orwell paraphrase against *Contingency, Irony, and Solidarity*, ch. 8, and the Harman paraphrase against "Moral Relativism Defended," 1975, before the row enters the book.)

**R7 (Evil, Third; optional).**
Old: "and the Confucian hopes for a world in which the widow, the orphan and the disabled are all cared for."
New: "the Confucian hopes for a world in which the widow, the orphan and the disabled are all cared for; and the pragmatist hopes, with William James, that the world can be made better by our effort, though nothing promises that it will be."

## 7. New insights; lines worth keeping

**New insights**
- **James as a witness, used with care.** James founded my school, held that obligation is the claim of a claimant, and (verify) held that a complete moral order calls for a divine demander. That makes him a pragmatist witness for W1(1): if obligation is a claimant's claim, the debt to the dead needs a claimant who is still someone. *Do not* adopt James's theory of truth ("true if it works," verify). It contradicts A7 and W3's "that is not evidence."
- **The sharpest wedge against Rorty is in the text already:** "where instinct fails... we learn whether what we meant was worth or only attraction." Sentimental education runs on identification. Its one honest repair, *deciding* to include her, turns worth into something conferred. The chapter can say this in one sentence.
- **Rorty's Orwell concession** (verify) is the most candid statement of the cost of anti-representationalism by its own best defender. It belongs in the row, because it is his own admission and not a genealogy used against him.
- **The Church can grant the pragmatist's history.** Equal worth did spread through persons and stories (CCC 37–38; W3). The dispute is not over how worth came to be seen, but over whether there was something to see. Saying this disarms Rorty's strongest rhetorical move.
- **Aquinas's account of disagreement** (ST I-II q.94 a.4, a.6) is the right reply to Wong and to the argument from diversity, and the text does not yet use it.
- **The text already obeys the fairness rule against my school.** It uses no genealogy against us, and the Nietzsche row's "neither ancestry settles anything" is a principle Rorty applies to himself.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "Where instinct fails, for the infant no one is drawn to hold, we learn whether what we meant was worth or only attraction."
- "Two refined hearts can still part over whether the newborn who will never think is worth as much as you, and then equal worth wins only by being ours."
- "A law or an order gives a nature to every member of a kind; it does not want any one of them."
- "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."
- "A truth can be true; only a person can forgive."
- "This is a question of fit, and the rivals contest it; it weighs, it does not settle."

## 8. Score

**7/10 as written; 8/10 with R1–R6.** The substance needed to meet the pragmatist is already in the text: the Moorean test of the infant, "equal worth wins only by being ours," "slow to see," and the honest "it cannot compel." But as written, the case never names the school that keeps A and refuses the gloss, and its W1 frame claims as agreed the necessity that school denies.

**Honest ceiling for any theistic case against my school: 8/10.** A consistent pragmatist can always decline the question of explanation and assert everything at first order, so no case compels him. The best a theistic case can do is what this one, repaired, does: make the decline costly and plainly priced, chiefly at "prior to anyone's recognition," for any reader who is surer that the exposed infant was wronged than of any theory that says the wrong waited on us.
