# Blind review, Round 8 — the Marxist school

## 1. School and voice

I speak for historical materialism and its moral heirs: Marx and Engels; Allen Wood's reading, on which Marx refused to condemn capitalism as "unjust" because justice is a juridical standard that answers to each mode of production, and criticized it instead for what it does to freedom, community and self-realization; the analytic Marxists who answer Wood (G. A. Cohen, Norman Geras), for whom exploitation is a real injustice; Steven Lukes, who writes as a sympathetic critic and diagnoses the tradition's split between a morality of emancipation and a scorn for the morality of rights; Terry Eagleton's Aristotelian, half-theological Marx; and the Frankfurt Marxists (Benjamin, Horkheimer, Adorno), who felt the claim of history's dead. My three charges against the text: its "truths" are offered as timeless, and they are products of social relations; its guilt and forgiveness are interpersonal and private, while the gravest wrongs of history are structural; and its hope consoles where it should transform ("the heart of a heartless world," "the opium of the people," Marx 1844; the point is to change the world, *Theses on Feuerbach* XI, paraphrase). I am a non-theist, and I keep the fairness rule: I will not use a genealogy of the text that I would not let it use on me.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Starting point for justice.** My school divides honestly here. Wood's Marx: there is no trans-historical justice; a transaction is just when it fits the prevailing mode of production (Capital III, ch. 21, paraphrase, verify), so the critical standard is not justice but freedom, community and the full development of human powers. Cohen and Geras: workers are owed what exploitation takes from them, and in the end what each is owed follows need, "from each according to his ability, to each according to his needs" (*Critique of the Gotha Programme*). Cohen later held that the ultimate principles of justice are fact-insensitive (*Rescuing Justice and Equality*, paraphrase, verify). → **Holds as coherent, but borrows or breaks.** Wood's version breaks A1 at the level of justice (ancient slavery was "bad," not "unjust"); Cohen's version keeps A1 only by becoming godless realism, which C already answers.

**2. A hard dilemma.** The Paris Commune, 1871: Versailles is shooting captured Communards; the Commune holds hostages, the Archbishop of Paris among them, and must decide whether to shoot them to stop the executions. My principles: solidarity with the oppressed, the free development of each as the condition of the free development of all, and responsibility traced to the structure (Marx in *The Civil War in France* laid the Archbishop's death at Thiers's door, paraphrase, verify). Classical Marxism (Trotsky, *Their Morals and Ours*, paraphrase, verify) answers that means are justified if they truly serve the liberation of humanity. → **Breaks** at A6: it spends innocent hostages for an outcome. The humanist answer (Geras's criticism of Trotsky, verify), never shoot the hostages, **borrows** a rights constraint that historical materialism does not by itself supply.

**3. Equal, unconditional dignity.** Every human being is a member of the species and a bearer of needs; the needs principle reaches the newborn and the severely disabled more surely than any contribution principle, and Marx criticized "equal right" exactly because it ignores the worker who cannot work (Gotha, paraphrase). Communism was to free the bourgeois as well as the proletarian. → **Borrows, and breaks at its edges.** Need explains what the newborn is owed, not why she is *worth as much as* you (needs are unequal); "prior to recognition," "never lost by crimes," and "the enemy" are affirmed by the humanists but taken as given, and were denied in practice by the classical tradition's treatment of class enemies.

**4. Ultimate flourishing.** The realm of freedom beyond necessity (Capital III, ch. 48, paraphrase); many-sided, non-alienated activity; a community in which love can be exchanged only for love and trust only for trust (1844 Manuscripts, paraphrase, verify); the free development of each as the condition of all. → **Holds** as a this-worldly end, coherent and humane. **Breaks** at death and at the past: Marx's own answer to death is that the individual is a determinate species-being and as such mortal while the species goes on (1844, paraphrase, verify), which is exactly the "for the species" answer the text rejects (GS 24); and for the victims already spent the classless future gives memory, not pardon or repair.

## 3. Debate highlights

**Exchange 1. Timeless truths or class morality (A heading, A1).**
*Attack.* Engels, in the chapter of *Anti-Dühring* on "eternal truths" in morality, argued that every morality so far has been a class morality, answering to the economic life of its age; a human morality above class becomes possible only when class antagonism is overcome (paraphrase, verify). The human essence is the ensemble of social relations (*Theses* VI). Your A1, "wrong even if every culture approved," claims a standpoint outside history that no one has.
*Reply.* The text does not claim a view from nowhere; it claims that what survives honest reflection, including reflection on how we came to believe it, may be trusted until shown false or shown to be produced by something unrelated to its truth (A heading). Engels himself granted moral progress (same chapter, verify), and progress needs a standard it moves toward; that is W2's "conscience corrects itself." Marx condemned slavery and wage-slavery as dehumanizing in every age; Geras showed that he held a constant human nature (*Marx and Human Nature*, 1983, verify). So the Marxist keeps trans-historical goods, and either keeps trans-historical wrongs (Cohen, Geras) or, with Wood, sets the word "unjust" aside, which is Anscombe's move in W1, and pays A2's price: the ancient slaveholder did not wrong the slave in justice. And by the fairness rule a genealogy of the text's realism settles nothing, as a genealogy of Marx's eschatology settles nothing about him (the text already says so of Nietzsche).
*Rejoinder.* I cannot pay A2's price; no Marxist says the slave was not wronged. My school's own humanists stand with A1.
**Held.**

**Exchange 2. Guilt is structural, not only personal (A2, W1, W1(1)).**
*Attack.* Marx treats persons in *Capital* as personifications of economic categories and holds the individual less responsible than anyone for relations whose creature he remains (Preface to the first edition, paraphrase, verify). The worker is robbed (Cohen) though no single capitalist need intend it. A2 draws every example from one friend and one secret; W1 says "guilt faces a person." The great wrongs of history (the slave trade, the colonial famines) are done by systems, and an account that privatizes guilt into a pardon between two people, sealed by absolution, lets the system go untouched.
*Reply.* The text limits its scope: W1 rests on "the wrongs that do" wrong someone. Structural wrongs do wrong someone: the slave is wronged, and the wrong is done by many hands. Catholic teaching names it: sins give rise to "structures of sin," which are the expression and effect of personal sins (CCC 1869; *Sollicitudo Rei Socialis* 36), and we share responsibility for others' sins when we cooperate in them, approve them, or fail to hinder them when bound to (CCC 1868). Absolution does not cancel repair (CCC 1459, already in the text). And the structural case strengthens W1(1): the victims of the slave trade were wronged by thousands, nearly all now dead; the debt is owed to millions who have no one on earth to receive it and, often, no one to remember their names. That is the text's "wandering dead" on a world scale.
*Rejoinder.* The doctrine answers me, but the text as written does not say it. A Marxist reading A2 and W1 meets only interpersonal cases and concludes the book's moral world is the confessional's.
**Held with repair** (Section 6, R2).

**Exchange 3. Double effect as the morality of the propertied (A6).**
*Attack.* "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent." In the colonial famines, grain left starving districts under the rule of the market; no one *aimed* at a death. The distinctions you rely on (intending and foreseeing, doing and allowing; W1(2) cites Foot's doing/allowing approvingly) are exactly the ones that protect an existing distribution of property. A system can spend millions as side effects while your absolute guards only against the surgeon and the hangman.
*Reply.* The Church refuses that laundering. Those whose usury and avarice lead to the hunger and death of others indirectly commit homicide, imputable to them; the acceptance by society of murderous famines without efforts to remedy them is a scandalous injustice and a grave offense (CCC 2269, which the text already cites in the section on evil). A theory that makes profit the exclusive norm and end of economic activity is morally unacceptable (CCC 2424); labor has priority over capital (*Laborem Exercens* 12); in urgent necessity, using another's goods to stay alive is not theft (CCC 2408); and a worker who accepts harder terms because need leaves him no choice is a victim of force and injustice (*Rerum Novarum* 45). Weighing side effects under double effect requires proportion; it is not a license.
*Rejoinder.* Then A6 should say so where the weighing is introduced; as it stands, the sentence can be read as the license I described.
**Held with repair** (R3).

**Exchange 4. The text turns A6 on my school.**
*Attack (mine, first).* Your absolute is a luxury of those whom the order already protects. No slavery was ended by people who refused all force: not in Haiti, not in the American Civil War.
*Reply.* A6 forbids deliberately killing the *innocent*, not just force. The Church allows legitimate defense (CCC 2263–2265) and even armed resistance to grave, prolonged tyranny under strict conditions (CCC 2243). What it forbids is spending the hostage, the prisoner, the innocent for the outcome, and the classical Marxist tradition did exactly that: the Commune's hostages, Trotsky's justification of means by the end of human liberation, Lukács's early argument that the revolutionary must take on the guilt of killing (*Tactics and Ethics*, 1919, paraphrase, verify). Lukes's diagnosis is that a movement which scorned the morality of rights had nothing left to resist its own atrocities with (*Marxism and Morality*, paraphrase, verify). A6's "Without it, no innocent is safe from being spent" describes the record.
*Rejoinder.* I concede it. My humane heirs restored the line, but they took it from outside historical materialism. Here my school does not object; it learns.
**Failed** (my attack); the text holds.

**Exchange 5. The opium charge (W3, W1(1), the section on evil).**
*Attack.* Religious suffering is both the expression of real suffering and a protest against it; to call on people to give up illusions about their condition is to call on them to give up a condition that needs illusions (Marx 1844, paraphrase). "The last word over the suffering child is not the suffering" is the sentence of a world that will not change her conditions. Your hope consoles.
*Reply.* First, epistemically the text has already bound itself: we may not believe a thing because it comforts (A7); the longing "is not evidence" (W3); the speaking to the dead "settles nothing" (W1(1)). The opium charge as a debunking is a genealogy, and the text answers genealogies at parity. Second, practically: "His permission never excuses ours" (CCC 2269); the hands that pull the child from the fire are part of what He made real; the absence of the knowing Lover is "a summons to mission." Third, the charge turns. A hope only for those who come after offers nothing to the children already spent; it makes them, in effect, the price of the future, which is A6's "spent for better totals" on the scale of history. Benedict XVI answered this school directly: a world that must create its own justice is a world without hope; Adorno saw that true justice would require even past suffering to be undone, which would mean a resurrection of the dead; the question of justice is the strongest argument for eternal life (*Spe Salvi* 42–43).
*Rejoinder.* Benjamin offered a godless answer: remembrance can reopen what history has closed; each generation has a weak messianic power to which the past has a claim (*Theses on the Philosophy of History* II, paraphrase, verify). But this is the text's second road, and the text's answer to it applies: what the living pay, the dead are not there to receive, and remembrance fails the nameless. Benjamin himself called his reply theology (Arcades Project, N8,1, paraphrase, verify).
**Held**, with the repair that the text should name this hope and use *Spe Salvi* 42–43 (R4, R5).

**Exchange 6. Projection and the species (W1 frame, W1(2), W3).**
*Attack.* Feuerbach showed, and Marx took it over, that God is the human species-essence projected and estranged; "every wrong is also, and before all else, an offense against Him" is the projection at work, moving the victim's claim to heaven. And the longing to be received whole is the longing of the isolated individual of bourgeois society; in a free association we would be confirmed in one another's love. As for death, the species goes on.
*Reply.* The projection theory is a genealogy; the text grants that science can trace how we long and insists that this "tells us how we long, not whether there is Someone" (W3), and by the fairness rule it cannot be pressed unless the text may press a genealogy of atheism, which it refuses to do. The displacement charge is answered in the text itself: the offense against God is "not a second injury beside hers ... but His love refused in her," and "God does not take her place in giving the pardon that is hers." The relational self is anticipated: "even if we are made of our relationships, the longing remains." And the species answer is named and rejected: the person is "the only creature on earth God willed for itself ... not for the species" (GS 24). The species does not know her, cannot forgive, and does not keep her.
*Rejoinder.* Whether a person is for herself or for the species is a standoff at parity on first principles. But I grant the text has met each of my claims in its own words.
**Held.**

**Exchange 7. Whose ancestry is equal worth? (A4, the Nietzsche row).**
*Attack.* Marx argued that Aristotle could not decipher the equality hidden in the exchange of goods because Greek society rested on slave labor; the idea of human equality became a fixed popular conviction only in a society of generalized commodity exchange; and Christianity, with its cult of abstract man, is the religion that fits commodity producers (Capital I, ch. 1, §§3–4, paraphrase, verify). Equal worth spread with the market, not the Gospel.
*Reply.* Neither ancestry settles anything, and the text already says so: "His own account gives the will to truth the same Christian ancestry he gives equal worth, so neither ancestry settles anything." Marx's Aristotle example even serves A4 and W2: interest and the order of a slave society blinded a great mind, as "conscience fails where self-interest pulls."
*Rejoinder.* Agreed on parity. But the text's parenthesis in the Nietzsche row makes the Church "agree that equal worth ... spread with the Gospel" and cites CCC 37–38, which teach the difficulty of knowing God and the moral law by reason as we are, and say nothing about how equal worth spread. That is a historical claim given a magisterial citation it does not have.
**Held with repair** (R7).

## 4. Ledger

- **A2** — Holds with repair. True as far as it goes; its cases are all interpersonal, and structural wrong (done by many hands) needs one sentence (R2).
- **A3** — Holds. Marxism agrees that no one can consent himself into slavery; the point has an economic application (R8, optional).
- **A4** — Holds. Marx's commodity genealogy of equality is at parity; the needs principle reaches the newborn but does not ground *equal worth*.
- **A5** — Holds. *Theses* VI does not touch it: listing what social relations make us does not tell us which to choose.
- **A6** — Holds with repair. The absolute stands, and my school's record shows its necessity; "Side effects may be weighed" needs the CCC 2269 clause so it cannot be read as a license for structural killing (R3).
- **A7** — Holds. Marx's own condemnation of "vulgar economists" as hired prize-fighters who traded inquiry for apologetics (Afterword to the second German edition of Capital, paraphrase, verify) presupposes that truthfulness binds every inquirer.
- **W1 frame** — Holds. The Feuerbachian displacement charge is answered by "not a second injury beside hers" and "God does not take her place."
- **W1(1), the three roads** — Holds with repair (optional). The Frankfurt Marxists walked these same roads, Horkheimer near the first, Benjamin on the second reaching toward the third; naming them strengthens it (R4).
- **W1(1), the Catholic answer** — Holds. The CCC 1459 point (absolution does not remedy all the disorders) blocks the "private absolution" charge.
- **W1(2)** — Holds. Marx's species answer to death is exactly what "not for the species" (GS 24) rejects; Cohen's rejection of self-ownership, on very different grounds, converges with "held in trust, not owned" (verify).
- **W1 closing** — Holds. "A will that invents values can command, but it cannot bind" is a point a Marxist can grant against voluntarism.

- **W3, hiddenness** — Holds. "A summons to mission" partly answers *Theses* XI; not my school's main front.
- **C introduction** — Holds with repair. "No rival's origins are used against it that we would not let it use against ours" is kept; but no row names my school (R6).

- **C: What we owe each other** — Holds. Not my school.
- **C: Well-being, counted impartially** — Holds. The Sidgwick secrecy point is one a Marxist welcomes (esoteric morality is ideology in the strict sense).
- **C: Flourishing of our kind** — Holds. Its content answers Wood's and Eagleton's Aristotelian Marx (no one left to receive the debt; accident or order), but it does not say so.
- **C: Necessary truths that belong to no one** — Holds. Its content answers Cohen's fact-insensitive principles.
- **C: Impersonal sacred order** — Holds. Not my school.
- **C: The Buddha's Dhamma** — Holds. Not my school.

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**"As written: NONE."**

No Marxist objection is both valid and resting on premises at least as plausible as the ones it attacks while the text cannot answer it at parity. The historicist charge is a genealogy and falls under the fairness rule, which the text already applies; the structural-guilt and double-effect charges are answered by Catholic teaching that the text either already cites (CCC 1459, 2269) or can add in a sentence; the opium charge is met by A7 and W3 epistemically and by CCC 2269 and the summons to mission practically; and where the text turns A6 on my school, my school concedes.

**Minor items.**
1. *C has no row for this school.* Its content is answered, scattered across the flourishing, realism and power-and-rank rows and W1(1), but a Marxist reader will not find himself named, and the school's three distinctive charges (ideology, structural wrong, consolation) are nowhere answered as charges. Given that the Church herself answers Marx by name (*Spe Salvi* 20–21, 42–43), the silence is conspicuous. (R6)
2. *A6, "Side effects may be weighed,"* without CCC 2269's clause on avarice and famine, invites the reading of double effect as a morality of the propertied. (R3)
3. *A2 and W1 are wholly interpersonal;* structures of sin (CCC 1868–1869) are absent. (R2)
4. *The section on evil's list of hopes without a Creator* omits the Marxist hope, and with it the text's strongest card against that hope (*Spe Salvi* 42–43). (R5)
5. *The Nietzsche row's parenthesis* attributes to CCC 37–38 the claim that equal worth "spread with the Gospel"; those paragraphs do not say it. (R7)
6. *The A heading's list of deniers* omits Engels. (R1)

**Misstatements of my school.** None; the school is absent, not misstated.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects).**
- Whether the person is for herself (GS 24) or a determinate member of a species that outlives her (Marx 1844): a clash of first principles.
- The competing genealogies of equal worth (Gospel vs. commodity exchange) and of belief (projection vs. any genealogy of atheism): neither settles, as the text says.
- Whether the mind's reach is explained by social practice (*Theses* II) or by one Source of law and mind (W2): the text rightly says it weighs and does not settle.
- Inside my school, Wood against Cohen and Geras on whether Marx had a theory of justice at all; either horn lands on a row the text already has.

**R5 (section on evil, "Third").**

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

**Insights.**
- *Spe Salvi* 42–43 is the Church's own answer to this school, and it is W1(1) in a papal voice: atheism as a protest against injustice, a world that must make its own justice is without hope, justice for the dead would require the resurrection, and justice is the strongest argument for eternal life. The text already cites *Spe Salvi* 39, 44, 47, 48; adding 42–43 lets the book meet the most influential modern atheism with its own magisterial source.
- The Frankfurt Marxists are a witness from inside the opposing camp. Horkheimer's late "longing for the totally Other" (cited in SS 42) and Benjamin's admission that his answer to the dead was theological show the debt to the dead pressing on honest atheists. This is the strongest single argument against my school, and the book has nearly made it already.
- W2's "conscience fails where self-interest pulls" is a partial ideology critique. The book can own the true part of Marxism here: moral blindness is socially patterned, and the Church names that pattern (structures of sin). Marx's own example, Aristotle blinded to human equality by a slave society, illustrates A4's "history shows how easily it is lost."
- Need is not worth. The needs principle is the best Marxist answer to the newborn and the disabled, and the book can grant that it reaches them while saying precisely what it does not do: need explains what she is owed; it does not explain why she is worth as much as you.
- A3's consent principle has an economic application (RN 45) that a Marxist reader will recognize as his own concern taken seriously.
- The Church's absolute is not pacifism: CCC 2243 allows armed resistance to grave tyranny under strict conditions. Saying so, if ever needed, removes the charge that A6 is a counsel of submission.

**Lines worth keeping.**
- "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent." (with R3)
- "Without it, no innocent is safe from being spent." (my school's history confirms it)
- "even if we are made of our relationships, the longing remains"
- "The hands that pull a child from the fire are part of what He made real, so His permission never excuses ours."
- "A yes said to whatever happens is said to no one."
- "A truth can be true; only a person can forgive."
- "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."

## 8. Score

**8/10** as written: no Fatal or Serious Marxist objection stands, because every distinctive charge is answered by the text's fairness rule, A7, its absolute and its doctrine, but the comparison leaves my school unnamed and leaves structural wrong and double effect open to the class-ideology reading that three short sentences would close (with R2, R3, R5 and R6, 9/10).
**Honest ceiling for any theistic case against my school: 9/10.** The theist's best card, justice for the dead, wins against Marxist hope, and my own heirs half-conceded it; but whether the person exists for herself or as a member of the species, and whether solidarity and remembrance are enough, remain standoffs of first principle that no argument from reason alone can close.
