# Round 8 blind review: the Evolutionary Debunker / Error Theorist

## 1. School and voice

I speak for the school that runs from Darwin's hive-bee passage through J. L. Mackie's error theory (no objective prescriptive values; our moral talk projects attitudes onto the world), Michael Ruse (morality as an adaptation whose sense of objectivity is part of how it works), Richard Joyce (moral judgment presupposes categorical reasons that do not exist; evolution explains moral belief without any appeal to its truth), and Sharon Street's Darwinian dilemma (a realist must either claim that selection tracked evaluative truth, which is bad science, or admit that our evaluative starting points match the truth only by coincidence). Street herself is not an error theorist but a Humean constructivist; I use her argument and say where the two come apart. I add the cognitive science of religion: agency detection, theory of mind, supernatural-watcher accounts of cooperation, and attachment accounts of the longing for God (Barrett, Boyer, Bering, Norenzayan, Kirkpatrick: all paraphrased, verify). My aim is to show that everything the text calls a witness has a causal explanation that does not depend on its truth.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Starting point for justice.** Mackie: morality is something we invent. It is a social device that makes up for our limited sympathies and lets strangers cooperate. "What is owed" is what our conventions of reciprocity and impartial review assign, and Street adds: whatever follows from the values we already hold, once we make them consistent (verify). **Holds** as a consistent position. It **breaks** where the book uses "owed" in the categorical sense, but it does not claim that sense, so it does not borrow it.

**2. A hard dilemma.** One ventilator, two patients: a 30-year-old and an 80-year-old with equal short-term prognosis. I resolve it by (a) a public rule that every affected party could accept in advance (expected life-years, with a lottery to break ties), (b) consistency with commitments we will not give up, such as no deciding on wealth or race, and (c) refined sympathy for the one who loses. I use truthfulness as the aim built into belief, fairness as convention, and compassion as sympathy, but not truth, justice, mercy and self-gift as the book defines them. **Holds** procedurally. It **breaks** at one point: I cannot say the 80-year-old is *wronged* if the rule goes the other way, only that the rule changed.

**3. Equal, unconditional dignity.** As an objective fact, I deny it (Mackie, Joyce). As a commitment I keep it, and Joyce allows keeping it as a useful fiction (verify). Street's version: equal dignity binds anyone whose considered values, made consistent, extend to the newborn and the enemy, and most of ours do. But she concedes that a Caligula whose values hang together perfectly makes no mistake (verify). **Breaks** on "unconditional." It is honest about that and borrows nothing.

**4. Ultimate flourishing.** I hold there is no ultimate good beyond what creatures like us value on full reflection. My guiding principles are truthfulness, less suffering, fair cooperation, and love of particular persons for as long as they last. **Coherent.** It denies "ultimately" rather than borrowing it. Its weak point is A7 (see Exchange 3).

## 3. Debate highlights

**Exchange 1: the Darwinian dilemma against A's own rule of trust.**
*Attack.* A's preamble says a seeming is trusted "until something ... shows that what produced the belief had nothing to do with its truth." That is exactly Street's and Joyce's claim. Kin care, reciprocity, punishing cheats and aversion to cruelty within the group were all adaptive whether or not any objective moral truth exists. On realism, then, what produced A1–A6 had nothing to do with their truth, and by the text's own rule they are defeated.
*Defense.* The rule is the right one, and the text states it accurately: it does not confuse debunking with the genetic fallacy. Theism supplies the link the dilemma says is missing. The Creator who made the natures that moral truths are about also made the minds that read them, through secondary causes including selection (CCC 306, 308; *Veritatis Splendor* 41–43). This is a "third factor" reply of the kind Enoch and Wielenberg offer, except that this third factor is a Mind (verify). And the reply is not circular: in an inference to the best explanation, the hypothesis that explains both the data and why the data are reliable gains support from both.
*Rejoinder.* As an inference to the best explanation this is legitimate. But the rival package is not godless realism. It is naturalism plus anti-realism, which explains the seemings with one cause and has no reliability gap to fill, because it never claims the seemings are true. So the contest becomes priors plus Moorean confidence in A1. That is a fair standoff, but the text should say so rather than send the reader to W2 as though W2 removed the defeater.
**Result: Held with repair.**

**Exchange 2: proof parity and "reach" (W2).**
*Attack.* W2 argues that survival did not select for seeing truths that hold in every possible world any more than it did when we read a proof, so the same question hangs over both. It does not.
(a) Arithmetic and logic helped us survive because they were true: two lions plus one lion really is three lions, and an inference that does not preserve truth gets you eaten. A belief that kin must be cared for helped us survive whether or not it was true.
(b) Necessity costs nothing extra. A necessary truth holds in every world, so a mind reliable about it here is reliable about it everywhere. "Reach" is not a second puzzle.
(c) The text's own engine closes the gap. W2 says conscience corrects itself through "reason holding itself to consistency." Evolved aversion to cruelty toward us, plus the consistency we already trust from logic, gives the judgment "cruelty would be wrong for any mind that can suffer." That is the expanding circle (Singer, verify), and it needs no moral truth.
*Defense.* Clarke-Doane argues that mathematical realism faces the same evolutionary challenge, since selection paid only for applied counting (verify). And knowing that something *must* hold is modal knowledge, which selection did not pay for directly. The question of how a mind made of matter sees necessity hangs over logic too.
*Rejoinder.* Modal knowledge grows out of counterfactual reasoning, which was adaptive and reliable about nearby possibilities. Necessity is its limiting case (Williamson, verify). More decisively, the debunker can drop mathematical platonism if he must, but no one can drop logic, the debunker's own argument included. Morality has no such indispensability. And against an anti-realist, "we can see that cruelty is wrong for any mind" assumes the very realism in dispute: what consistency extends is our attitudes, and the text needs it to extend truth.
**Result: Failed as written against my school.** It holds only as a question put to godless realism, and that is where it has real force.

**Exchange 3: A7 and the companions-in-guilt argument.**
*Attack.* "Follow the evidence" does not need categorical, non-natural force. Belief aims at truth by its nature (the "transparency" literature, verify), so anyone who believes is already under the norm, simply as a believer. That gives A7 its universality without Mackie's queer prescriptivity, so the C row is wrong to say it reduces to "advice for those who happen to want truth."
*Defense.* That covers *belief*, not *inquiry*. A7 also says "we may not stop looking because we fear what we will find." Choosing to stop looking is a practical choice, and the aim built into belief does not govern it. Further, the error theorist blames the believer who thinks wishfully, and that blame is categorical in form. If he withdraws it, he must say the theist who refuses to look makes no error, and that the error theorist's own argument gives no one a reason to accept it beyond what he already happens to want.
*Rejoinder.* Joyce and Olson accept this (verify). The argument can be sound without anyone being categorically obliged to heed it. Still, the cost is real: my school cannot fault the man who looks away, and this cuts against its own practice of unmasking.
**Result: Held.** The cost stands, and a minor repair would credit the reply that the norm is built into believing.

**Exchange 4: guilt toward the dead (W1(1)).**
*Attack.* The text concedes that "science can show why minds like ours would speak to their dead whether or not anyone hears, so the speaking alone settles nothing." Guilt asking the dead for pardon *is* speaking to the dead. The systems that track relationships and repair broken bonds do not switch off when the partner dies (Bering on afterlife intuitions as a by-product; McCullough on forgiveness as an evolved system for repairing relationships, verify). So guilt's request is predicted whether or not she still exists, and its evidential weight is about zero. By A's own rule it is defeated. Yet W1(1) still rests on it ("guilt asks for what it could have only if she is still someone"), and the final C row counts "forgiveness when no one on earth is left to give it" and "guilt's request to be forgiven" as things theism *explains*.
*Defense.* The witness does not rest on the psychology. It rests on A3, a moral truth: we still owe her. Theism explains how a debt still owed can be received, and the text hedges the point openly ("a clue ... weighs as much as the reader finds the third road ... truer").
*Rejoinder.* A3 establishes that the debt survives, and the second road keeps that. Nothing in A says a debt truly owed must be something *she* can receive or release. That step comes only from guilt's asking, the datum already conceded. And "explains" is doing double duty: theism does not explain the request better than attachment and by-product accounts do; it *answers* it. Answering a longing is fit, not evidence, as W3 itself says.
**Result: Held with repair.** The text must keep "explains" separate from "answers."

**Exchange 5: equal dignity (A4), the hardest case for my school.**
*Attack.* Equal worth spread where Christianity spread, so it is believed because it was taught. That is a cultural genealogy.
*Defense.* The text already answers this with parity: Nietzsche gives the will to truth the same Christian ancestry he gives equal worth, so ancestry settles neither. And A4 is *counter-selected*: the text concedes that "our instincts favor the healthy, the beautiful and our own." Evolutionary debunking works only on beliefs that selection would produce whether or not they are true. Selection would *not* produce equal worth for the infant no one is drawn to hold. Of all the text's moral claims, A4 is the one the Darwinian dilemma reaches least.
*Rejoinder.* My fallback is consistency applied to evolved sympathy. But then A4 is reached by reason working against instinct, not by instinct, and my school's best remaining account is Street's constructivism, which concedes the coherent Caligula. That brings me back to the C row "Feeling, refined": equal worth wins only by being ours.
**Result: Held.** This is the text's strongest ground against my school.

**Exchange 6: the shape of conscience as evidence (W2 and the evil section).**
*Attack.* The text admits that pain in nature "falls roughly where survival would put it, not where justice would," and puts that on the scale against itself. Conscience has the same shape. It is warmest toward kin, friends and those who can repay. It was slow to reach the slave, the stranger and the infant. It carries disgust-based and in-group norms. That is selection's signature, yet W2 counts "a law and a mind fitted to each other" as a witness for theism and puts the misfit on the other side of the scale only as sin.
*Defense.* The Church *expects* conscience to fail where self-interest pulls, because disordered self-love is the root of sin (ST I-II q.77 a.4; CCC 1850, 405). Conscience also corrects itself against interest, toward those who can never repay.
*Rejoinder.* That fits, but it accommodates the pattern with an auxiliary doctrine that a non-Christian has no independent reason to grant. Naturalism predicts the same pattern with no auxiliary, and predicts slow, partial correction by consistency as well. By the text's own measure for pain, the shape of conscience is a weight against it, not only a fit.
**Result: Held with repair.** Put the pattern on the scale.

**Exchange 7: debunking theism itself, under the fairness rule.**
*Attack.* The cognitive science of religion explains belief in a watching moral Lawgiver through agency detection, theory of mind, and the cooperative payoff of supernatural watchers (Norenzayan, Johnson, verify).
*Defense.* The case does not argue from religious intuition. It argues abductively from moral data and treats every feeling of being watched, heard or answered as settling nothing (W1(1), W3, and the C row that credits error theory with explaining "why we feel bound, guilty and watched"). The fairness rule cuts both ways, and the text keeps it.
*Rejoinder.* Agreed. The cognitive science of religion hits only the items in Exchange 4, not the inference from A.
**Result: Held.**

**Exchange 8: the Moorean close (the Conclusion).**
*Attack.* Your confidence that the abandoned infant was wronged is exactly the confidence evolved moral emotion, extended by culture, would produce whether or not it is true. A Moorean appeal to it begs the question.
*Defense.* Your confidence in the debunking premises, and in the logic you run them with, is also produced by evolved faculties. Any debunking has to stop somewhere. The Conclusion does not compel; it asks which you are surer of, and it lists "or that you cannot know it," which is my agnostic version.
*Rejoinder.* A fair standoff. My school's honest answer is that it is surer of the debunking than of the felt wrongness, and few readers will share that.
**Result: Held.**

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**Serious 1: W2's proof parity does not answer the debunker, though A's preamble relies on it to.** A sends the evolutionary defeater to "W2 and the conclusion." W2's reply ("survival no more selected for seeing what holds in every possible world when we read a proof ... The same question hangs over both") overlooks three points:
- Logic and arithmetic helped us survive because they were true; moral beliefs helped whether or not they were true.
- Necessary truths make reliability here into reliability everywhere, so "reach" is not a separate puzzle.
- The text's own engine, consistency applied to evolved concern, produces the "any mind" judgment with no moral truth.

Against the anti-realist the reach argument assumes realism. It is a fair and strong question for godless realism only. The parenthetical ("This speaks most strongly to those who say conscience is only what survival built") is defensible only for W2's A7 point. Repairs R1–R3.

**Serious 2: "explains" is used for items the text elsewhere concedes have a full naturalistic explanation.** W1(1) concedes that science explains why we would speak to the dead whether or not anyone hears. W3 concedes the same for the longing. Yet W1(1) still rests the third road on "guilt asks for what it could have only if she is still someone," and the final C row lists "forgiveness when no one on earth is left to give it" and "a longing no one on earth can answer" among what theism *explains*, with "guilt's request to be forgiven" as something that suggests Someone. When naturalism predicts a datum about as well as theism does, that datum is fit, not evidence. The text says this about W3 and should say it here too. Repairs R3 and R4.

**Minor**
- W3's Smith sentence overlooks attachment science, which explains the wish to be told, before earning anything, that it is good that we exist (R5).
- The evil section weighs the selection-shaped pattern of pain but not the selection-shaped pattern of conscience. The Church's explanation by sin is an auxiliary a non-Christian need not grant (R6).
- W2's "a law and a mind fitted to each other" slightly overstates the fit. It is partly offset by "it weighs, it does not settle."

**Misstatements of my school**
- The C row "Nothing really binds" files Street's constructivism under error theory. Her debunking argument is used *for* the view that values bind from within, which belongs under "Feeling, refined" (R7).
- The same row says the error theorist reduces A7 to "advice for those who happen to want truth." His strongest reply makes it the aim built into believing, which every believer is under. What he truly loses is the norm of *inquiry* (R7).
- W2 states the debunker's answer accurately ("survival would give us the sense of seeing it, true or not") but leaves out his principled asymmetry with proof.

Otherwise the text represents my school fairly, and in places generously: "consistent and borrows nothing," "with one cause," "why we feel ... watched."

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- The Moorean question: A1 against the premises of the debunking argument.
- Companions in guilt over epistemic norms. The inquiry norm is a real cost to my school, and the error theorist can bite it.
- A theistic third factor against naturalism plus anti-realism. Each explains the seemings; they differ on priors and on whether the seemings are true.
- The cognitive science of religion against theism, under the fairness rule. The case does not argue from religious intuition, so the inference from A is untouched.
- A4. Evolutionary debunking cannot reach it, and cultural debunking is neutralized by the Nietzsche parity. My school's fallback, constructivism, keeps it only "by being ours."

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

**Insights**
1. **A4 is the belief evolution cannot debunk.** The text already concedes that instinct favors the healthy, the beautiful and our own. Evolutionary debunking works only on beliefs that selection would produce whether or not they are true, and selection did not produce equal worth for the infant no one is drawn to hold. So the debunker must fall back on cultural ancestry, which the Nietzsche row's parity already neutralizes, or on reason extending sympathy, which concedes that reason is doing the work. The Conclusion's newborn is aimed at exactly the spot my school reaches least. The book could say so in one sentence in A4 or the Conclusion.
2. **Use Street against godless realism.** Her dilemma is this book's ally under "Necessary truths that belong to no one": a realist with no Mind must explain why minds built for survival track truths that owe nothing to survival. Theism is a third factor that does that work. Aim W2's reach question there, not at the error theorist.
3. **Mackie shares the book's conditional.** "A will that invents values can command, but it cannot bind" is a premise Mackie accepts. He reasons that if anything bound categorically it would be strange in a world without God, and he reportedly granted that objective values would make a god more probable (*The Miracle of Theism*, ch. 6, verify before use). The Catholic and the error theorist agree on the conditional and part only over whether the child's torture is really wrong. That is a clean, fair way to frame the C row.
4. **The coherent Caligula** (Street's concession, verify) is the sharpest illustration of "equal worth wins only by being ours." The "Feeling, refined" row could use it in place of a longer exposition.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "No rival's origins are used against it that we would not let it use against ours."
- "His own account gives the will to truth the same Christian ancestry he gives equal worth, so neither ancestry settles anything."
- "Science can show why minds like ours would speak to their dead whether or not anyone hears, so the speaking alone settles nothing; it cannot show that no one does."
- "A will that invents values can command, but it cannot bind."
- "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent."
- "A world with no one behind it would put pain where survival puts it, which is where we find it."
- "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."

## 8. Score

**7 / 10 as written; 8 after repairs R1–R7.** The text states my school fairly, admits its consistency, makes the A7 inquiry cost bite, and puts its weight on the Moorean question and the counter-selected A4, where my school is weakest. But W2's proof parity does not answer the Darwinian dilemma, and the final tally counts as "explained" two items the text itself concedes science explains just as well.

**Honest ceiling for any theistic case against my school: 8.** A consistent error theorist can always pay the price. The best a theistic case can do is show him the bill (A1, A4, the norm of inquiry) and leave him to choose, and this case, once repaired, does that.
