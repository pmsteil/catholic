# Blind review, Round 8: the Daoist school

## 1. School and voice

I speak for the classical Daoist school: the *Daodejing* (Laozi) and the *Zhuangzi*, read with A. C. Graham, Chad Hansen, P. J. Ivanhoe, Livia Kohn and Brook Ziporyn, and with an eye on religious Daoism where Kohn shows its ethics. On this view the Way that can be spoken is not the constant Way. Heaven and Earth are not humane (*bu ren*): they treat the ten thousand things as straw dogs. Benevolence and righteousness show up when the great Way has been abandoned (DDJ 18, 38). The sage acts by not forcing (*wu wei*) and lets things be "so of themselves" (*ziran*). Zhuangzi adds that "this" and "that", right and wrong, depend on where one stands (*Qiwulun*), and he drums on a pot when his wife dies. I am not an error theorist, a sentimentalist or a nihilist. There is a Way, it is the one Source of all things, and it guides. What I deny is that it is a Someone who wants anyone, keeps anyone, or is owed anything. Paraphrases of the Daoist texts and of modern scholars below are mine (verify).

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Starting point for justice.** Heaven's Way works like stringing a bow. It lowers what is high, raises what is low, takes from surplus and gives to lack, whereas the human way takes from those who lack to serve those who have plenty (DDJ 77). The ruler who taxes until people starve, and calls that order, is a robber boasting (DDJ 53, 75). Justice here means balance and not dominating others, not paying debts. The sage holds the left half of the tally and does not press his claim (DDJ 79). **Holds** as a principle of balance. **Borrows** at one point: DDJ 79's sage, who declines to collect, presupposes that there is something owed which he could collect. Daoism has the creditor's virtue without a theory of credit.

**2. A hard dilemma.** The surgeon with one healthy patient and five dying ones should refuse. The world is a sacred vessel that cannot be worked on, and whoever works on it ruins it (DDJ 29). To kill in place of the Executioner is to cut wood in place of the master carpenter, and few who do so keep their hands whole (DDJ 74). A dying father who asks for death gets the same answer: the true man does not seek his end and does not cling, and does not "use the human to help Heaven" (*Zhuangzi* 6). On both cases the answer **holds**, and it reaches the Church's lines without an absolute rule. It **breaks** at St. Maximilian Kolbe. Zhuangzi ranks Bo Yi, who starved for righteousness, alongside the robber who died for gain, since both harmed their nature (*Zhuangzi* 8). He prizes living out one's Heaven-given years. Daoism can respect courage that comes from compassion (DDJ 67), but it cannot praise taking a condemned man's place without strain.

**3. Equal, unconditional worth.** Seen from the Way, nothing is noble and nothing is base (*Zhuangzi* 17). The sage is always good at saving people, so he abandons no one (DDJ 27). The newborn is the image of complete virtue (DDJ 55). The maimed, the criminal with his foot cut off and the ugliest man in Wei are whole in virtue, and others forget their form (*Zhuangzi* 5). The enemy dead are mourned, and a victory is kept with funeral rites (DDJ 31). This **holds** for equal and unconditional regard, enemy included. It **breaks** on human dignity as an inviolable rank. The same Way that makes me equal to the newborn also makes me equal to a rat's liver and a bug's arm (*Zhuangzi* 6). Where religious Daoism forbids killing, it took that precept over from Buddhist vows (Kohn, verify).

**4. Ultimate good.** The ultimate good is to return to the root and the uncarved block (DDJ 16, 28), live out one's natural years, wander free and easy (*xiaoyao you*), and accept transformation with an even mind. The guiding principles are *wu wei*, *ziran*, and the three treasures: compassion, frugality, and not daring to be first in the world (DDJ 67). It **holds** on its own terms. It **strains** in two places. Compassion (*ci*) treats each being as mattering for itself, yet nothing in the account says why. And the joy of the "ten thousand transformations" is spoken by an "I" that will not be there to enjoy them.

## 3. Debate highlights

**Exchange 1. Does C silently file me under someone else?**
*Attack.* Daoism has no row in C. The "impersonal sacred order" row puts Zhu Xi and the Stoics together, but its central charge is Laozi's thesis, not Zhu Xi's: the order "gives life to each thing and intends none", and "Heaven and Earth give life to things". Zhu Xi held that the mind of Heaven and Earth, giving life to things, *is* humaneness (*ren*) (his *Treatise on Ren*, verify). Laozi says the opposite. So the row has absorbed the Daoist, but none of the Daoist's own replies to it are on the page. Worse, the historian Sima Qian's question was put to a Daoist saying: "the Way of Heaven has no favorites and is always with the good" is the last line of DDJ 79, quoted at the start of his biography of Bo Yi. And the robber who lived long, Robber Zhi, is a figure out of the *Zhuangzi*.
*Defense.* The row's charges are that an order can neither be wronged nor forgive, that it cannot know or love, and that it gives a nature but wants no one. Each applies at least as strongly to a Way that is avowedly not humane. Adding a row would add a name, not an argument. The text never claims C is complete. It claims each rival is stated fairly, and it states none falsely.
*Rejoinder.* The charges carry over. The Daoist's distinctive answers do not appear anywhere: that the highest giving does not possess, that guilt heals by forgetting, that being transformed is good, and that every thing has its own "so" rather than only a kind-nature. And the Sima Qian line is misfiled as it stands.
*Verdict:* **Held with repair** (Repairs 1 and 2).

**Exchange 2. "Offered as truths, not feelings" against "when all know the good as good, the not-good arises" (DDJ 2).**
*Attack.* A treats truth and feeling as the only two options, and C's rows are sorted by that split. The Daoist is on neither side. There is a constant Way, but to name it is to lose it (DDJ 1), and moral words are fish-traps you forget once you have the fish (*Zhuangzi* 26). Worse for a book on love: its four goods (truth, justice, mercy, sacrifice) are, by DDJ 18 and 38, the list of what appears once the Way has been lost. Lose the Way and virtue appears; lose virtue and humaneness appears; then righteousness, then ritual, which is loyalty and trust worn thin.
*Defense.* (i) A1 concerns the act, not the word. The Daoist calls the starving of the people "not the Way" (*fei dao*) everywhere and for everyone, so he grants A1 in substance. (ii) The Church also holds that our words fall short of what they name (CCC 42). Between Creator and creature, no likeness can be stated without an even greater unlikeness (CCC 43, Lateran IV). (iii) The Church half agrees with DDJ 18. The law was "added because of transgressions" (Galatians 3:19). Moses allowed some things "for your hardness of heart" (Matthew 19:8). The written list exists because a harmony was lost (CCC 374–379, 405), which the text itself says in W1's closing paragraph. But the Church holds that love is not one more rule added after the loss. Love is the root, "the fulfilling of the law" (Romans 13:10). (iv) Fairness rule: to say these words exist because the Way was lost is a story about where they came from. The text could reply that the Daoist's lament is what Warring States carnage produced. Neither origin story settles anything.
*Rejoinder.* Granted. The real disagreement narrows to this: was the lost harmony a harmony *with Someone*? That is W1 and W3's question, not A's.
*Verdict:* **Held.**

**Exchange 3. Guilt as debt: the left half of the tally.**
*Attack.* The sage holds the left tally and does not exact payment; those with virtue keep the tally, those without it collect (DDJ 79). Repay resentment with virtue (DDJ 63). When the springs dry up, fish on the bank keep each other alive with their spittle, and it would be better for them to forget each other in the rivers and lakes. Better to forget both Yao and Jie than to praise one and blame the other (*Zhuangzi* 6). Debt-to-the-dead is clinging. Healing comes from forgetting and transformation, not pardon. W1(1)'s first road ("what is left is remorse, a wound that will never be healed") leaves this road out.
*Defense.* DDJ 79 supports A3. A sage who holds the left tally *is* owed and chooses not to collect, so releasing the debt is the creditor's gift. The text says exactly this: "A friend can release a promise as a gift", and "the pardon ... is hers to give." The debtor cannot hold the creditor's tally. If a man forgets what he did to another, he is not the sage of DDJ 79. He is a debtor who tore up his own IOU. Zhuangzi's drumming is about his wife's death, not about a wrong he did her.
*Rejoinder.* "Forget Yao and Jie" carries forgetting into blame, and Yan Hui's sitting-in-forgetfulness forgets humaneness and righteousness themselves (*Zhuangzi* 6). The Daoist really does let the moral ledger dissolve. Which of us is truer to what guilt asks is exactly what the text leaves the reader to weigh ("it weighs as much as the reader finds the third road ... truer").
*Verdict:* **Held with repair.** The argument stands at parity, but the first road should name the Daoist's healing by forgetting (Repair 3).

**Exchange 4. "God wanted *you*" against "it gives life and does not possess" (DDJ 51).**
*Attack.* The highest virtue gives life and does not possess, acts and does not presume, leads and does not dominate (DDJ 51, 10). "Wanting" someone is the carpenter's way of looking at a tree, and it is why trees get cut down. The useless tree lives out its years because no one wants it (*Zhuangzi* 1, 4). Shu and Hu loved Hundun and drilled him seven holes, one a day, and on the seventh day he died (*Zhuangzi* 7). The text ranks a possessing love above a giving that does not grasp. And W1(2)'s contrast does not fit me: "A law or an order gives a nature to every member of a kind." The Way gives each thing its own "so", not just its kind (Guo Xiang's "self-so" and "solitary transformation"; Ziporyn's omnicentrism, verify).
*Defense.* The God of the text does not possess either. He "needs nothing outside Himself" (C, last row). He creates not to increase His glory but to share His goodness (CCC 293–295). He willed her "for itself" (GS 24), "not for the species, not for the total, not for us". He gave her life "to her keeping", and He "never spends her as a mere means". That is DDJ 51 with one addition: He knows her. And the useless tree is GS 24's ally against every account that spends the one for the many. Hundun's friends did not fail by giving too much. They failed in truth about him, which is what the book means when it says mercy needs truth to be mercy.
*Rejoinder.* The Way also meets each thing particularly. The perfected person uses his mind like a mirror: he responds and does not store (*Zhuangzi* 7). What remains between us is *storing*, keeping her. The Catholic calls it fidelity (Isaiah 49:15). I call it the last grasp.
*Verdict:* **Held** at the level of argument. The "kind" sentence in W1(2) needs a clause (Repair 5).

**Exchange 5. "It is good that we exist as ourselves forever" against the great smith.**
*Attack.* If molten metal leapt up in the forge and demanded to be made a famous sword, the smith would call it bad metal. If I demand to be a man and only a man, the Fashioner of Things would think me ill-omened. Having human form is a joy, but it has ten thousand transformations with no end (*Zhuangzi* 6). W3 answers only the Buddhist ("birth is suffering") and the "friends of nature" ("a life must end to be human"). The Daoist says neither. Existence is good, and *so is being transformed*, which makes "forever as herself" a shrinking of the "yes", not its fullness. The Great Clod makes my life good, and by the same act makes my death good.
*Defense.* (i) The Christian hope includes change: "we shall all be changed" (1 Corinthians 15:51); what is sown perishable is raised imperishable (15:42–44); the lowly body is changed into a glorious one (CCC 999). Christians say yes to transformation, but transformation *of her*, not her replacement by a rooster's leg. (ii) Master Yu, imagining his arm becoming a rooster, says *he* will then call out the dawn. The "I" survives in his own picture of it, which is the same strain the text points out in Confucius's knowing Heaven. (iii) W3 offers this as a clue, not a premise.
*Rejoinder.* The playful "I" is real and is the Daoist's strain, but it mirrors the puzzles of identity across resurrection. W3 should still name the Daoist view so that it does not look as if only two alternatives exist.
*Verdict:* **Held with repair** (Repair 7).

**Exchange 6. The absolutes of A6 as "ritual, the thinning of loyalty and trust".**
*Attack.* A6 is exceptionless rule-making, which DDJ 38 calls the start of disorder. The Daoist reaches the same conduct without absolutes: the sacred vessel (DDJ 29), the Executioner (DDJ 74), not hastening one's end (*Zhuangzi* 6). So the absolute is not what protects the innocent. Zhuangzi also condemns dying for righteousness (Bo Yi) as he condemns dying for gain, so the text's praise of the man who lays down his life is not a shared premise.
*Defense.* (i) The convergence counts *for* the text. DDJ 74 has the same logic as "He who gives life, and can give it back, may call it home; we, who can do neither, may not take it." Zhuangzi's "neither seek the end nor cling" matches CCC 2278–2279: refuse burdensome treatment, but do not aim at death. So the "trust" structure is not Christian-only. (ii) Zhuangzi's target is dying for a *name* (reputation) or for gain, which are both ways of serving oneself. Kolbe did not aim at death or at a name. The Church shares Zhuangzi's doubt about any death that is aimed at (CCC 2280–2281) and parts from him only where love accepts a death it does not seek (John 15:13). (iii) The text already labels the Kolbe line and the no-exceptions line "this book's claims ... not shared ones".
*Rejoinder.* Then where the text needs an absolute, it is right that the Daoist does not supply one, and the text never pretends otherwise. The Daoist still asks why a rule beats a sage. Answer: because most of us are not sages (CCC 37), which DDJ 65's own policy of keeping the people simple tacitly admits.
*Verdict:* **Held.**

**Exchange 7. Evil: straw dogs fit the evidence.**
*Attack.* The text admits that pain falls "where survival would put it". A Way that is not humane predicts that with no theodicy and no nihilism. It is still a generous Source, so the text cannot dismiss it as "no one behind it". The evil section names two rivals who bear this weight more easily, and I am a third, and the strongest. Its first point then says an indifferent source, or a god beyond good and evil, does not explain "a worth that does not follow ability". The Way does: nothing is noble or base, the maimed are whole, the sage abandons no one. The text's own sacred-order and Buddhist rows grant the same to those accounts ("worth follows nature, not capacity").
*Defense.* The section concedes the weight in full ("it is heavy"). It claims only that evil does not overturn a case built on W1–W3. The Daoist's own maxim, "always with the good" (DDJ 79), and "Heaven's Way benefits and does not harm" (DDJ 81) run into Sima Qian's evidence just as the text's God does.
*Rejoinder.* DDJ 79 and 81 strain against DDJ 5 the same way Confucius's knowing Heaven strains against Zhu Xi's principle, and fairness says the text may use that strain only as it uses Confucius's: to show where the account strains, not that it fails. The "worth that does not follow ability" sentence is simply wrong about at least one impersonal source, and it contradicts the text's own C rows. What W1(2) actually argues is narrower and sound: worth that is *wanted* and *not to be spent*.
*Verdict:* **Held with repair** (Repair 6).

**Exchange 8. Nameless Way or named Someone.**
*Attack.* W2 concludes there is "one Source" of law and mind. Granted: the Way gives birth to one, one to two, two to three, three to the ten thousand things (DDJ 42), and the empty heart that waits on things is the Way's own (*Zhuangzi* 4). But "Someone" is a name, and "Revelation gives the Lover's name" claims the very thing DDJ 1 says cannot be had.
*Defense.* The divine name "I AM" is "at once a name revealed and something like the refusal of a name" (CCC 206). The text says "not less than personal". The Daoist's "not humane" says less than humane; the Church says more than humane, never less. So the question is whether "beyond the personal" includes knowing and loving in a higher way (the text) or leaves them out (DDJ 5). The text answers with W1 and W3, as clues.
*Rejoinder.* Agreed that this is the real fork, and agreed that reason alone does not settle it. Religious Daoism, as Kohn shows, kept reaching for persons: the deified Laozi, the Three Officials who pardon sins (verify). That is a strain on my side, to the same degree, and no more.
*Verdict:* **Held**, as an honest standoff at parity.

## 4. Ledger

- **Form paragraph**: Holds. A converging case; overclaiming disclaimed.
- **A heading (method and deniers)**: Holds with repair. Phenomenal conservatism is at least as plausible as Zhuangzi's global "how would I know?", which Zhuangzi turns on himself and then sets aside in practice (Cook Ding; "I know it from here above the Hao"). So the method does not beg the question. The list of deniers could name the Daoist (Repair 8).
- **A1**: Holds. The Daoist grants it in substance as "not the Way", everywhere.
- **A2**: Holds. The secret case separates wronging from worsening. The Daoist's *xin* (trustworthiness) is a virtue owed toward someone, and DDJ 79 presupposes claims. The Daoist would file guilt under "flawed character" (lost virtue), but the case does not let him stay there.
- **A3**: Holds. DDJ 79 confirms that release is the creditor's gift. The Daoist denies the last sentence (the debt outlives her), which A openly offers as deniable.
- **A4**: Holds. The Daoist grants equal, unconditional regard (DDJ 27, 31, 55; *Zhuangzi* 5, 17) and denies only a human-specific rank. A4's claim is about humans, and nothing in Daoism refutes it.
- **A5**: Holds. The Daoist erases the line between what we are and what we are for (*ziran*). But A5's "by itself" is about listing capacities and desires, which the Daoist also says settles nothing ("return to the root" is a norm).
- **A6**: Holds. The first half is granted (the sage puts himself last). The prohibitions converge with DDJ 29 and 74. The Kolbe and no-exceptions lines are labeled as the book's own claims.
- **A7**: Holds. "Knowing where to stop" (*Zhuangzi* 3; DDJ 44) is not stopping *out of fear*, so the qualifier saves A7. DDJ 65's policy of keeping the people simple is a real Daoist cost under A7, parallel to Sidgwick.
- **W1 frame**: Holds. "Not even God makes cruelty right" agrees with the Daoist's own anti-voluntarism. The Daoist sits in the "order of cause and fruit" slot ("what is not the Way soon ends", DDJ 30, 55).
- **W1(1)**: Holds with repair. The first road omits healing by forgetting (Repair 3). The ancestral road underplays religious Daoist rites that deliver the forgotten dead (Repair 4). The argument itself is a clue, weighed at parity.
- **W1(2)**: Holds with repair. "A law or an order gives a nature to every member of a kind" does not fit a Way that gives each thing its own "so" (Repair 5). "Few say why the line is not even hers to waive" stays true, though the Daoists come near saying it.
- **W1 closing**: Holds. Anti-voluntarism is shared. "He is Himself the Good" against "the Way is not humane" is the core standoff, stated honestly.
- **W2**: Holds. Its conclusion (one Source of law and mind) is shared by the Daoist, which shows W2 does not by itself reach a person, and the text says so ("W1 and W3 show why"). The question about reach stands. Zhuangzi's eel-and-monkey perspectivism contests it, but it does not touch gratuitous cruelty.
- **W3 (longing)**: Holds with repair. The Daoist yes to transformation is a third alternative left unstated (Repair 7). The claim stays a clue, honestly framed.
- **W3 (hiddenness)**: Holds. It could add the Daoist adept who sat in forgetting and found a nameless Way, but the paragraph's logic covers him.
- **C preamble**: Holds.
- **C: Nothing really binds**: Holds. The Daoist is not this.
- **C: Feeling, refined**: Holds. The Daoist is not this either; the truth/feeling split leaves him out but does not misstate him.
- **C: Kant**: Holds.
- **C: Contractualism**: Holds.
- **C: Utilitarianism**: Holds. The Daoist agrees that persons are not to be spent for totals (DDJ 29).
- **C: Secular Aristotelian**: Holds.
- **C: Godless realism**: Holds.
- **C: Impersonal sacred order**: Holds with repair. Its central charge is the Daoist thesis attributed to Zhu Xi, and the Sima Qian question is misfiled (Repairs 1, 2).
- **C: Buddha's Dhamma**: Holds.
- **C: Nietzsche**: Holds.
- **C: A God who is Love**: Holds with repair. "Against the flourishing view and the sacred order" should take in the nameless Way, and CCC 206 meets DDJ 1 (Repair 9).
- **Evil, first paragraph (the weight)**: Holds with repair. Honest and heavy. Its list of rivals who bear the weight more easily leaves out the Way that is not humane but does give life (folded into Repair 6).
- **Evil, "First"**: Holds with repair. "a worth that does not follow ability" is false of Daoism and in tension with the text's own C rows. "A yes said to whatever happens" does not fit Zhuangzi, whose yes is to transformation, not to cruelty (Repair 6).
- **Evil, "Second"**: Holds. "The parasite takes from the child; God does not give her to it" stands. The Daoist's straw dogs give her to it, which is the honest difference.
- **Evil, "Third"**: Holds. It names Buddhist and Confucian hope fairly. The Daoist hope is to return to the root, not to be raised, and the paragraph's "that is not evidence, but it is where the accounts part" covers it.
- **Conclusion**: Holds. The Daoist joins the newborn test wholeheartedly (DDJ 55, 27). Its inference ("it follows what she is") is shared, and the step to God is properly left to W1(2) and W3.

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**"As written: NONE."**

No Fatal or Serious objection from the Daoist stands. On every point where the Daoist really parts from the text (whether guilt outlives the creditor, whether the Source knows and keeps, whether "forever as herself" is the fullness of "it is good that you exist"), the text already presents its side as a clue the reader must weigh, not as a premise. On those terms the text answers the Daoist at parity.

**Minor items:**
1. *Evil section, "First"*: "nor a worth that does not follow ability" is false of the Daoist Way, which grounds capacity-independent equal regard (*Zhuangzi* 5, 17; DDJ 27). It also contradicts the text's own sacred-order and Buddhist rows. The argument W1(2) actually makes (wanted, and not to be spent) is sound; the summary sentence misstates it. This is a real error, but easily repaired.
2. *C, impersonal sacred order*: the row's central charge is Laozi's thesis (DDJ 5) assigned to Zhu Xi, whose Heaven has humaneness as its mind. The Daoist is absorbed without his own answers being recorded.
3. *W1(2)*: the "every member of a kind" contrast does not fit a Way that gives each thing its own "so".
4. *W1(1)*: the first road leaves out healing by forgetting (*Zhuangzi* 6). The ancestral road says the forgotten dead are only fed, but religious Daoist rites of universal deliverance aim to rescue them (Kohn, verify). The point survives (they are sent on, not kept as themselves for the one who wronged them), but the description is thin.
5. *W3*: the transformation view (good to be, good to be changed) is a third reply to "forever" that the text does not name.

**Misstatements of my school:**
- Sima Qian's "is the Way of Heaven just?" was put against DDJ 79's "Heaven's Way has no favorites and is always with the good" and the long life of *Zhuangzi*'s Robber Zhi. The text assigns it to the Neo-Confucian "order that cares" (verify the text of the Bo Yi biography).
- "A yes said to whatever happens" (evil section) misreads Zhuangzi if it is meant to cover him. Daoist acceptance is of *ming* (what cannot be otherwise: life, death, deformity), not of the tyrant's cruelty, which the *Daodejing* condemns (DDJ 30, 31, 53, 74, 75).
- No other misstatement. The text never names Laozi or Zhuangzi, so the defect is omission, not distortion.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects):**
- Whether the Source is beyond the personal in a way that includes knowing and loving (text) or leaves them out (DDJ 5). CCC 206 against DDJ 1; religious Daoism's reach for persons against Confucius's knowing Heaven.
- Whether guilt is healed by her pardon or by forgetting.
- Whether "it is good that you exist" is fullest as "forever as herself" or as "good in every transformation".
- Whether self-gift unto death (Kolbe) is the summit of love or a harm to one's nature. The text labels it the book's own claim.
- Whether the moral words (humaneness, righteousness, justice) are love's own form (text) or what is left once the Way is lost (DDJ 18, 38). The Church grants half of DDJ's point (Galatians 3:19; CCC 405).

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact old → new)

**Repair 1 (C: a Daoist row, inserted after the sacred-order row).** Old: `- **The Buddha's Dhamma** (karma,` → New:
`- **The Way that gives and does not claim** (Laozi and Zhuangzi) refuses our starting point more gently than Nietzsche and more deeply than Zhu Xi. Heaven and Earth, it says, are not humane; the Way gives life to each thing, nourishes it, and does not possess it; benevolence and righteousness appear when the Way is lost, as fish on a dried bank keep each other wet, when they might have forgotten each other in the lakes. It sees much of A: the cruelty of rulers, the grief of war (a victory, Laozi says, should be kept with funeral rites), the newborn as the image of whole virtue, the maimed and the ugly whole in virtue, and a sage who abandons no one (A1, A4). It even holds that life and death are not ours to hasten: to kill in the Executioner's place is to cut in the master carpenter's place, and the true man neither seeks his end nor clings (A6). But it heals guilt by forgetting, not by pardon (A2, A3); it lets the dead transform, and counts the wish to remain oneself forever as the metal that leaps up in the forge demanding to be a famous sword (W3). Where we say God wanted *you*, it says the highest giving does not want: it gives life and does not possess. We answer that God does not possess either: He willed her for her own sake and gave her life to her keeping (GS 24). We part over whether the Giver knows her, and whether, when the bank dries, she is kept or let go.`
`- **The Buddha's Dhamma** (karma,`

**Repair 2 (sacred-order row, Sima Qian).** Old: `as the historian Sima Qian did when he set the good men who starved beside the robber who lived long and asked whether the Way of Heaven is just.` → New: `as the historian Sima Qian did when he set the good men who starved beside the robber who lived long, against the old saying that the Way of Heaven has no favorites and is always with the good (*Daodejing* 79), and asked whether the Way of Heaven is just.`

**Repair 3 (W1(1), first road).** Old: `Some deny it: the debt died with her, and what is left is remorse, a wound that will never be healed by the one it was done to.` → New: `Some deny it: the debt died with her, and what is left is remorse, a wound that will never be healed by the one it was done to; or, as Zhuangzi would have it, a wound that heals by forgetting, as fish that kept each other alive on a dry bank forget each other in the lakes.`

**Repair 4 (W1(1), ancestral road).** Old: `What keeps her there is the memory of her kin, and memory fails.` → New: `What keeps her there is the memory of her kin, and memory fails; where the Daoist and Buddhist rites of universal deliverance reach the forgotten, they free her from hunger and send her on, not keep her as herself for the one who wronged her.` (verify the liturgical description with Kohn or Schipper.)

**Repair 5 (W1(2)).** Old: `A law or an order gives a nature to every member of a kind; it does not want any one of them.` → New: `A law or an order gives a nature to every member of a kind; it does not want any one of them. Even a Way that gives each thing its own self-so, and not only its kind, as Zhuangzi's does, gives without wanting: it lets each one be, and lets each one go.`

**Repair 6 (evil section, "First").** Old: `neither explains a debt that outlives every creditor on earth, nor a worth that does not follow ability, nor a longing to hear from Someone that it is good that *you* exist. A yes said to whatever happens is said to no one; it must say yes to what was done to her as well as to her, and so it cannot take her side.` → New: `neither explains a debt that outlives every creditor on earth, nor a worth that is each one's own, wanted for her own sake and not to be spent for others (an impersonal source can make all things equal, as Zhuangzi's Way does, but it wants none of them), nor a longing to hear from Someone that it is good that *you* exist. A yes said to whatever happens is said to no one; it must say yes to what was done to her as well as to her, and so it cannot take her side. (Zhuangzi's yes is narrower: it is said to the changes of life and death, not to the tyrant's cruelty, which his Way condemns; but when she dies, it, too, lets her go.)`

**Repair 7 (W3).** Old: `Some wise friends of nature answer that a human life must end to be human, and that to want more is to want to stop being human; but the Christian hope is not more of the same years.` → New: `Some wise friends of nature answer that a human life must end to be human, and that to want more is to want to stop being human; Zhuangzi answers more gaily that it is good to be human now and good to be changed into whatever comes next, and that to demand to stay oneself is to be the metal that leaps up in the forge. But the Christian hope is not more of the same years, nor a refusal of change, for "we shall all be changed" (1 Corinthians 15:51).`

**Repair 8 (A heading, deniers).** Old: `Singer denies parts of A2, A4, A6 and A7;` → New: `Singer denies parts of A2, A4, A6 and A7; Zhuangzi would grant much of A1 and A4 in his own terms but deny the end of A3, that we still owe the one who is gone;`

**Repair 9 (C, God row).** Old: `Against the flourishing view and the sacred order, which also stop at one, it is the one that knows and loves us, and is Someone besides ourselves.` → New: `Against the flourishing view, the sacred order and the nameless Way, which also stop at one, it is the one that knows and loves us, and is Someone besides ourselves; and the Church, too, says that His name is "at once a name revealed and something like the refusal of a name" (CCC 206).`

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

**Insights.**
- **DDJ 38 and Matthew 6:3.** The highest virtue does not regard itself as virtue; do not let your left hand know what your right is doing. Chapter 12 (or Chapter 3) can absorb Laozi's critique of humaneness and righteousness rather than fight it. The written law is what was added "because of transgressions" (Galatians 3:19) after a harmony was lost (CCC 374–379, 405). Love is not one more rule after the loss; it is the root, "the fulfilling of the law" (Romans 13:10).
- **DDJ 79, the left tally**, is a non-Christian witness for A3. Only the one owed can release the debt, and the sage's not collecting presupposes the debt.
- **DDJ 74 and *Zhuangzi* 6 ("not using the human to help Heaven")** show that the structure of holding life in trust, not seeking death and not clinging (CCC 2278–2279), was seen outside the Church. They strengthen W1(2)'s claim that reason can see the line, while the no-exceptions form stays the book's own.
- **DDJ 55, DDJ 27, *Zhuangzi* 5.** The infant as the image of whole virtue, the sage who abandons no one, and the maimed who are whole in virtue are strong non-Christian witnesses for A4 and for the conclusion's newborn on the hillside.
- **The useless tree (*Zhuangzi* 1, 4)** is GS 24's ally: a being not "for" anyone's use is safe from being spent. It sharpens the book's point that being "made for an end" means made for communion, never for use.
- **Hundun (*Zhuangzi* 7)** illustrates "mercy needs truth to be mercy". Benevolence that does not know its object kills it.
- **CCC 206** is the book's best single answer to DDJ 1. **Philippians 2:7** (He emptied himself) together with Laozi's water, which takes the low place and benefits all without contending (DDJ 8, 78), is a bridge worth one sentence in W3 or the evil section's "Third".
- **Fairness tools for the book:** DDJ 81 ("Heaven's Way benefits and does not harm") strains against DDJ 5 the same way Confucius's knowing Heaven strains against Zhu Xi. Zhuangzi's "Fashioner of Things" is a quasi-personal placeholder. Use them only as the text uses Confucius: to show strain, not failure.

**Lines worth keeping.**
- "A truth can be true; only a person can forgive."
- "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent."
- "A will that invents values can command, but it cannot bind."
- "The parasite takes from the child; God does not give her to it."
- "God says no to what was done to her, and yes to her."
- "Heaven and Earth give life to things; God wanted *you*." Keep it, knowing it answers Laozi as much as Zhu Xi, and pair it with GS 24 so "wanted" cannot be heard as "possessed".
- "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."

## 8. Score

**8 / 10.** The text never begs the question against the Daoist: its three contested starting points (moral words name truths, guilt is owed, it is good that we exist as ourselves forever) are either argued (A2's secret case, which DDJ 79 itself supports) or explicitly offered as clues to weigh (W1(1), W3), and its only real faults against my school are an absorbed-but-unnamed C row, a misfiled Sima Qian, and one false summary clause in the evil section, all repairable in a sentence each.

**Honest ceiling for any theistic case against the Daoist: 8.5 / 10.** The decisive fork, whether the nameless Source is beyond the personal in a way that includes knowing and keeping, or in a way that leaves them out, can be weighed by reason but not closed by it. With Repairs 1–9 this text reaches that ceiling.
