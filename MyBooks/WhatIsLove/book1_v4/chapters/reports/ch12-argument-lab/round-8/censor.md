# Censor's report: "A moral argument for God" (Chapter 12)

## Scope

A pre-imprimatur doctrinal read of /tmp/claude-0/lab8/ARGUMENT.md, read cold and in full, sentence by sentence (the Form paragraph, A and A1 to A7, W1 with (1), (2) and its closing, W2, W3 and its second paragraph, every row of C, the two paragraphs on evil, and the Conclusion). BRIEF.md was read for its frame only. The text was checked against Scripture (RSVCE), the Catechism, Vatican I (*Dei Filius*), Vatican II (GS, LG, NA, DV), the encyclicals and other documents it cites (VS, EV, SS, DCE, SD, DI, SB), and St. Thomas. Magisterial texts were checked on vatican.va in this session wherever the page could be read; St. Thomas on newadvent.org and isidore.co. Nothing under /tmp/claude-0/lab/ or any earlier review was opened.

The question asked of each sentence: is it contrary to faith or morals; would a bishop's reader stop at it; is each citation accurate and correctly attributed; does it claim more than CCC 31–36 allow; does it presume that unbelievers are culpable; is it polemical; could a non-Catholic reader fairly take it as contrary to the faith?

## Bottom line

**One Error.**

**E1. C, "Reason's own law" (Kant):** "He did not make that Ruler the author of the law, and neither does this book (Chapter 3)." Read plainly, this says the book denies that God is the author of the moral law. The Church teaches the opposite. *Veritatis Splendor* 36 rejects, as contrary "to Sacred Scripture … and the Church's constant teaching," the denial "of the fact that the natural moral law has God as its author" (verified on vatican.va). VS 40 says "The moral law has its origin in God and always finds its source in him" and that "God gave this light and this law to man at creation." The sentence also contradicts the chapter's own title ("The Lawgiver Is Love"), the Conclusion ("a Lawgiver who is Love"), W1's closing, W2 ("The Church places such truths in God's own wisdom") and the realist row ("it holds first in a Mind"). What the author means is sound: God does not *invent* the law by an arbitrary choice (Chapter 3's answer to Euthyphro). But the words say something else, and they line the book up with the very autonomy thesis VS 36 condemns. A censor would be bound to require an amendment. The fix is in §3.

Everything else is either sound or needs only care. The text is strong on CCC 31–36: it claims convergence and "best explanation", and it affirms that certainty is attainable (CCC 36; *Dei Filius* ch. 2) without claiming that this case compels it. It is careful never to presume that unbelievers are culpable (CCC 847, 1861; LG 16). It states the Church's absolutes on the innocent, euthanasia, suicide and experimentation precisely. It treats other religions respectfully, in the manner of NA 2.

## 3. Replacement wording

**R1 (Error 1, Kant row).**
Old: "He did not make that Ruler the author of the law, and neither does this book (Chapter 3)."
New: "He did not make that Ruler the author of the law. This book does, but not as a will that invents it: the law is God's own wisdom, which He did not choose because He is the Good, and whose light He gave to man at creation (Chapter 3; *Veritatis Splendor* 36, 40)."

**R2 (A4).**
Old: "never, if innocent, to be deliberately killed for others' sake, nor "for his own good" without his asking;"
New: "never, if innocent, to be deliberately killed for others' sake, nor "for his own good" (and, the Church adds, not even at his asking);"
(Keep the parenthesis that follows, which A6 already marks as the book's claim.)

**R3 (W1(1)).**
Old: "He does so through His Church, ordinarily in the Sacrament of Reconciliation (CCC 1442, 1484), and His forgiveness is whole."
New: "He does so through His Church: first in Baptism, and for sins after Baptism ordinarily in the Sacrament of Reconciliation (CCC 977, 1263, 1442, 1484); and His forgiveness is whole."

**R4 (W1(2)).**
Old: "No one may take what is not his to take, even to keep others from taking more. He who gives life, and can give it back, may call it home; we, who can do neither, may not take it. That right is never ours, and no one, whatever voice he says he heard in His name, may claim it for himself (CCC 2258)."
New: "No one may take what is not his to take, even to keep others from taking more. He who gives life, and can give it back, may call it home; we, who can do neither, may not take an innocent life. That right is never ours: no one can under any circumstance claim for himself the right directly to destroy an innocent human being (CCC 2258), whatever voice he says he heard in God's name, for no private voice can correct what God has revealed (CCC 67). (Defending the innocent against an unjust aggressor is another matter, CCC 2263–2265.)"

**R5 (Neo-Confucian row).**
Old: "Zhu Xi's answer, that principle is wholly good and the unevenness lies in a material force it does not steer, is close to the Church's own (CCC 310); but a principle that does not steer…"
New: "Zhu Xi's answer, that principle is wholly good and the unevenness lies in a material force it does not steer, is close to the Church's in one respect: the source is wholly good, and the world's unevenness belongs to a creation still on its way (CCC 310). It parts from hers in another, for the Church holds that God governs all things, 'from the least things to the great events of the world' (CCC 303). And a principle that does not steer…"

## Verdict

**As it stands: no nihil obstat.** The one sentence in E1 would have to be amended first, because it says in plain words that the book does not hold God to be the author of the moral law, against VS 36 and 40. The sentence also contradicts the book's own thesis, so the amendment costs nothing.

With R1 made, and preferably R2 to R5 as well, I would expect a nihil obstat. The rest of the text is doctrinally careful to an unusual degree:
- It keeps the claim of reason within CCC 31–36 and *Dei Filius*.
- It states the absolutes on innocent life, euthanasia, suicide and experimentation exactly.
- It handles evil, providence, hell and imputability correctly.
- It never presumes that unbelievers are culpable.
- It speaks of other religions in the spirit of *Nostra Aetate*, without polemic.
