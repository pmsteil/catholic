# Argument Lab — standing brief for every debate session

## What this is
A multi-round lab to build the most robust "argument from love" for God's existence for Chapter 12 ("The Lawgiver Is Love") of Patrick Steil's Catholic book *What Is Love? God's Perfect Love*. Each round, a panel of the strongest non-theist opponents attacks the current version. Goal, in Patrick's words: FIND THE TRUTH. Honesty over winning. The lab ends when no opponent can log a logically sound argument against the current version that our side cannot answer at least to parity, or after 7 rounds.

The current chapter text is in /tmp/claude-0/lab/chapter_12_current.md (read it). The version under test is in /tmp/claude-0/lab/EVOLUTION.md — read the LATEST version in full, and skim earlier ones and their failure notes so you do not re-raise objections already answered unless the answer is actually bad.

## The book, in brief
Canonical definition: "God's Perfect Love is the sacred gift of covenant which binds truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice into life-giving communion." Ch. 1 evil (sin = choosing a lesser good over the greatest Good; free will); Ch. 3 love is sacred; Euthyphro answered: goodness is God's own nature, neither above Him nor invented by Him; Ch. 4 love is a gift of covenant, a gift forced is no gift, love presupposes freedom; Ch. 5 love binds the facets into one; Chs. 6–9 truth, justice, mercy, sacrifice; Ch. 10 communion; Ch. 11 prudence.

## Patrick's side must hold to these (for this lab)
- Catholic teaching is the frame. Catholic sources first (Scripture, Catechism, councils, popes, saints, Catholic philosophers: Aquinas, Suárez, Newman, Anscombe, Marcel, MacIntyre, Kreeft, etc.). FOR THIS LAB ONLY, a Protestant thinker may be cited if his argument builds on the Catholic tradition and does not contradict it.
- Reason's witness, not a proof that replaces faith: arguments are "converging and convincing" (CCC 31) and "can predispose one to faith" (CCC 35). No overclaiming ("God must exist", "far more faith").
- God's authority is NOT mere power to dictate. It is the authority of the Creator: His power to create, to establish what we are for, and to define what love must be submitted to — and He can do this because He IS the Good (no voluntarism; Euthyphro answered in Ch. 3).
- Grace first: God loves first (1 John 4:19). Free will is real.
- Divine simplicity: God's truth, justice, mercy are one with His being, not parts. Self-gift within the Trinity is total and without loss; "sacrifice" is what self-gift becomes in a fallen world.
- Unbelievers are not presumed culpable (CCC 847, LG 16).
- Human dignity is "fully recognizable even by reason alone" (Dignitas Infinita 1); the challenge is not that reason cannot see it, but whether a non-theist can GROUND equal, unconditional dignity.
- Human nature vs natural law (Patrick): human nature is what we ARE — capable of truth and lies, justice and injustice, mercy and cruelty, giving and grasping, with good and disordered desires. Describing it never by itself yields an "ought" (is/ought). The natural law, in the Church's sense, is what we are FOR: our share in God's eternal law (ST I-II q.91 a.2), knowable by reason even without knowing its Author (Rom 2:14–15), fully realized only by grace (CCC 1960). The "ought" lives there.
- Don't put Christian-only demands (obligatory self-sacrifice unto death, love of enemies) into premises a non-Christian must grant. Patrick: "to do good for someone you must sacrifice; that is how reality works."
- Plain words; no "pillars"/"threads"; avoid "marks" and "strand" (Patrick's rejected terms).

## Standing challenges every opponent MUST answer honestly, in their own school's best voice
1. What is your starting point for justice? From what principle do you derive what anyone is owed?
2. By what principles would you actually resolve a hard moral dilemma (give one concrete case) — without borrowing truth, justice, mercy and self-gift as the book defines them?
3. What grounds the EQUAL, unconditional dignity of every human being — the newborn, the dying, the severely disabled, the enemy — in your view? Or do you deny it?
4. What is your ending point for human flourishing: what is ULTIMATELY good for humans, and what are your guiding principles?
Record where the opponent has a coherent answer and where it borrows or breaks. Be fair: if a school answers well, say so.

## Rules of the debate
- Argue the opponent at full professional strength (the real literature, not straw men). Paraphrase positions; NEVER invent direct quotations from real thinkers. Keep any quotation from a copyrighted source to a short phrase (long copied passages trigger a content block).
- At least 6 escalating exchanges: opponent attack → Patrick's-side defense → opponent rejoinder where warranted.
- Be honest when a defense fails. A "sound" objection = valid, with premises at least as plausible as the ones it attacks.
- Modify no files except your own output file.

1. **School and voice** (who; 2–3 sentences).
2. **Answers to the four standing challenges** (each: their answer → does it hold, borrow, or break?).
3. **Debate highlights** (condensed exchanges: objection → reply → Held / Held with repair / Failed).
4. **Ledger for the version under test**: one line per numbered item in the latest version → Holds / Holds with repair / Fails + reason.

6. **Proposed repairs**: exact replacement wording for any item that failed or needed repair.
7. **New insights** for the book; **lines worth keeping** (short, plain, warm).
8. **Score**: robustness of the version against this school, 1–10, with one sentence.
