# Brief for the doctrinal review

## What you are reading
/tmp/claude-0/lab12/ARGUMENT.md is a moral argument for God written for Chapter 12 ("The Lawgiver Is Love") of Patrick Steil's Catholic book *What Is Love? God's Perfect Love*, a book meant to help Christians understand the Catholic faith and to stand before a bishop's censor for a *nihil obstat* and *imprimatur*. It is long by design (it will be condensed later). It argues from reason, compares the Catholic account with non-theist rivals, and weighs evil honestly.

You read it cold. Do NOT open, read, list or search anything under /tmp/claude-0/ except this brief, ARGUMENT.md and your own output file. Earlier versions and reviews exist; do not look for them. Web sources for checking texts are expected.

## Your task
You are a Catholic doctrinal censor of the strictest kind, the reader a bishop would appoint for a *nihil obstat*. The author asks: be VERY THOROUGH; DO NOT MISS ANYTHING. Read every sentence of your assigned part, slowly, against:
- Sacred Scripture (RSVCE wording where quoted or paraphrased);
- the Catechism of the Catholic Church;
- the councils (especially Trent, Vatican I *Dei Filius*, Vatican II), and defined dogma;
- papal and curial documents (encyclicals, *Veritatis Splendor*, *Evangelium Vitae*, *Spe Salvi*, *Dignitas Infinita*, *Samaritanus Bonus*, *Dei Verbum*, *Nostra Aetate*, *Lumen Gentium*, *Gaudium et Spes*, etc.);
- St. Thomas Aquinas where he is cited or where the text states a Thomist position;
- approved theological opinion (note where the text adopts a permitted opinion and whether it says so).

Look for, at minimum:
1. Any statement contrary to faith or morals, or that a careful reader could fairly take as such (Error).
2. Any statement that is ambiguous, overstated, understated, or that presents an opinion as doctrine or doctrine as opinion (Needs care).
3. Every citation: does the cited paragraph, canon or article actually say what the text uses it for? Wrong number? Paraphrase that shifts the meaning? A quotation not in the source?
4. Overclaiming against CCC 31–36 and *Dei Filius* (reason's certainty vs the text's "weighing"), and underclaiming (saying less than the Church teaches about what reason can know).
5. Anything that presumes the culpability of unbelievers (CCC 847, 1861; LG 16), or is polemical, or misdescribes another religion in a way contrary to *Nostra Aetate*.
6. Moral teaching: the inviolability of innocent life (CCC 2258–2283; EV 57, 58, 62, 65–66), legitimate defense (2263–2265), the death penalty (2267, as revised 2018), euthanasia and suicide, ordinary care, nutrition and hydration (*Samaritanus Bonus*), double effect and intrinsically evil acts (VS 50–52, 80).
7. Grace and freedom, predestination, hell (Trent VI; CCC 1033–1037, 1058, 1821), the de auxiliis limits; Mary's sinlessness (CCC 490–493).
8. Forgiveness, Baptism, Reconciliation, temporal punishment, satisfaction, restitution (CCC 977, 1263, 1440–1460, 1472–1473, 2412, 2487; Trent XIV).
9. God's attributes: impassibility, simplicity, God as author of the natural law without voluntarism (VS 36, 40–43), creation as free (*Dei Filius* can. 1.5), providence and evil (CCC 309–314, 324).
10. Scripture: inspiration and inerrancy (DV 11–12) in the handling of hard passages (1 Samuel 15), and any paraphrase of a verse.

Severity: **Error** = a censor would be bound to strike it or withhold the *nihil obstat*. **Needs care** = should be corrected before print but is not contrary to faith. **OK** = sound.
