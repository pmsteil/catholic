# Censor E: the section on evil and the Conclusion (ARGUMENT.md, lines 43–46)

## Scope

**What I read.** The brief in full. ARGUMENT.md in full for context (lines 1–47). My assigned part sentence by sentence: line 43 ("**On the other side of the scale is real weight, and it is heavy.**" through "...only at the end (CCC 314)"), line 44 ("Three things bear on that weight...", through "...and to ask what makes it true."), and line 46 ("**Conclusion.**" to the end).

## Bottom line

No Errors.

Nothing in the part is contrary to faith or morals. Its handling of these points is sound:
- providence and evil: God is in no way the cause of moral evil; evil never becomes good; a good is drawn from that very evil;
- hell: real and eternal; self-exclusion; no predestination to hell; the Church's prayer and hope;
- the Trent limits on grace and freedom;
- Mary's sinlessness;
- *Benedictus Deus* on the baptized infant;
- impassibility (*Spe Salvi* 39);
- the resurrection;
- the Church's teaching that reason can know God with certainty, which the Conclusion states accurately before claiming less for this one case;
- the culpability of unbelievers, which it does not presume.

Twelve points need care before print. Most are fixes to how a citation fits the claim. Two are theological:
- **F7:** "God does not give her to it" (the parasite and the child) is overstated against St. Thomas on the evil of penalty.
- **F3:** "no one is lost except by his own willful turning away" is broader than CCC 1037, and could be read as settling the question of the unbaptized infant.

## 3 Verdict

**As it stands:** I would not withhold the *nihil obstat* for any Error, because there are none. A strict censor would still return the section with F3, F4, F5, F7 and F11 marked for correction before print. F3 and F7 are the two that a less charitable reader could fairly take in a sense contrary to Catholic teaching: the first on the unbaptized infant, the second on God's will regarding the evil of penalty. F4, F5 and F11 make the text say or credit more than its sources do.

**With the fixes:** Yes, I would grant the *nihil obstat* to this part. With F1–F12 applied it is accurate, it attributes its sources correctly, and its weighing of evil is honest without conceding anything the Church does not allow. It states what reason can know exactly as CCC 31–36 and *Dei Filius* teach before claiming less for this one argument. And it presumes the bad faith of no unbeliever.
