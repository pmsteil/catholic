# Censor's report: Section W ("Witnesses")

## Scope

**Read:** the whole of section W of ARGUMENT.md (lines 14–22): the W1 frame; W1(1) *The debt still owed*, both paragraphs; W1(2) *A worth no deed confers and no order wants*; the W1 closing paragraph ("This Lawgiver is not a commander…"); W2; W3, including the hiddenness paragraph. I read every sentence. The rest of the file (A, C, the section on evil, Conclusion) was read only for context and to check that W agrees with it.

**Checked against:** Sacred Scripture (RSVCE); the Catechism; Trent, Session XIV; Vatican II (*Lumen Gentium*, *Gaudium et Spes*, *Dei Verbum*, *Nostra Aetate*); *Humani Generis*; *Veritatis Splendor*; *Evangelium Vitae*; *Deus Caritas Est*; *Spe Salvi*; *Verbum Domini*; *Samaritanus Bonus*; *Dignitas Infinita*; Pius XII, Address of 27 November 1951; the Holy Office responses on craniotomy (1884, 1889); St. Thomas (ST, SCG). Source checks are listed in §2.

**Special attention, as assigned:** forgiveness, Baptism, Reconciliation, temporal punishment, satisfaction and restitution; the state of the dead, the communion of saints and prayer for the dead; 1 Samuel 15 and St. Thomas on divine commands (against DV 11–12, VD 42, CCC 67, 2258); inviolability of life, euthanasia, ordinary care, the vital-conflict case (EV 58, 62; Pius XII 1951); the image of God, the soul's creation, dignity (DI; GS 19, 24); the unbaptized infant (CCC 1257, 1260, 1261); salvation of non-Christians (LG 16, GS 22, CCC 847–848).

## Bottom line

**No Errors.**

Nothing in section W is contrary to faith or morals. Twenty points need care before print. Most are about precision: a paraphrase that drifts from its source, a citation that does not carry the exact claim, or a phrase a careless reader could take the wrong way. The ones that matter most are these:

- **W-3.** The relation between "God alone forgives sins" and "the pardon that is hers to give."
- **W-4.** The grounding of reparation after Baptism.
- **W-11.** The authorities for the vital-conflict case.
- **W-12.** Stating St. Thomas on the commanded killings exactly, with the original-sin ground he gives.
- **W-13.** Quoting VD 42 exactly.
- **W-17.** The Catholic "all of that is real" after the Buddhist "wisdom that no longer clings to a self."
- **W-20.** Adding CCC 848's "that faith without which it is impossible to please him" to the salvation of non-Christians.

They are fairly stated and not polemical. W-17 is the one place where the Catholic side is stated too loosely.

## 3 Verdict

**As it stands:** I would not withhold the *nihil obstat* for any statement contrary to faith or morals, because I found none. The section is doctrinally careful throughout. It keeps the line on innocent life at every number and against the person's own request. It keeps the Thomist position on divine commands as a stated, permitted opinion within DV 11–12, CCC 67 and CCC 2258. It handles the unbaptized infant and the salvation of non-Christians in the Church's own words. It presumes no one's guilt.

As a strict censor I would still return it for correction before granting the *nihil obstat*, because of W-1, W-3, W-4, W-11, W-12, W-13, W-17 and W-20. Each of these is a place where a careful reader could fairly take the text to say something other than what the Church teaches: about the primacy of the offense against God, about whether God's forgiveness is whole, about the authorities for the vital-conflict case, about St. Thomas's exact ground, about VD 42's wording, about the self, and about the faith needed for salvation.

**With the fixes:** yes. With the exact replacements in §1 (the optional ones in W-14 and W-25 may be left out), I would grant the *nihil obstat* to section W.
