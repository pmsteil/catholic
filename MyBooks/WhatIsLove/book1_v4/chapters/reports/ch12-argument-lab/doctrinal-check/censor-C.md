## Bottom line
One Error:
- **"Reason's own law" (Kant), "On one point Kant draws the line more absolutely than this book does. He held that not even God may use a rational being merely as a means … parts from him about God"**. A careful reader can fairly take this to mean that the book holds God *may* use a rational being merely as a means. That would be contrary to GS 24 ("the only creature on earth which God willed for itself"), to SCG III.112, and to the book's own claims in W1(2) and in the section on evil. Kant and the book really part over something narrower: whether the Lord of life may call a life home by a human hand (Abraham, 1 Samuel 15). The wording has to say that. Fix in row 4 of the Findings table.

## 3 Verdict
As it stands: no. Finding 4 leaves open a reading on which the book holds that God may use a person merely as a means, and a censor would be bound to require that it be corrected. With that fix, and preferably the Needs-care fixes (above all 7, 12, 13, 17, 31, 33 and 41), I would grant the *nihil obstat* to section C. The section's descriptions of Confucian, Daoist, Buddhist and Spinozist thought are accurate, charitable and free of relativism, consistent with *Nostra Aetate* 2 and *Dominus Iesus*. Its account of God as author of the natural law is faithful to *Veritatis Splendor* 36–43 and free of voluntarism. Its claims about reason stay within CCC 31–36 and *Dei Filius*, apart from the underclaim in Finding 41.
