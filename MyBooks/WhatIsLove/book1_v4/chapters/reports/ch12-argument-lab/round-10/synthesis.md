## The two Serious items
1. **Evil — the lost.** The text holds together: God governs each person for her own sake and never spends anyone against her own good; dignity cannot be lost; grace can bring a free creature to say yes without overriding her (Mary); hell is eternal. For one lost forever, existence is an infinite evil to him (Mt 26:24); the only reasons left serve the whole, the logic the book forbids for persons elsewhere. The text never says that hell is where its own "for her own sake" claim is most strained, and omits the hope the Church holds out (CCC 1058; also CCC 1037, 1821). Repair: name it plainly as the place the book's own principle is under most strain; add the Church's hope and prayer; leave it as weight, not answer. This is the oldest standoff in theology; do not try to solve it.
2. **Aristotelian — the flourishing row.** "Her equal worth must come from justice, taken as given, and not from the end" misstates the school: neo-Aristotelians ground equal standing in the form, had whole (*Categories* 5); falling short bears on the life she can live, not on what she is; the text gives the realist this same credit. Real difference: whether her being was given and willed, and whether the end is offered to her — a difference of hope, a fair standoff.
