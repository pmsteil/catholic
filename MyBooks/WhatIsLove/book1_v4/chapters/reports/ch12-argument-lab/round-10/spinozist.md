# Blind review: the Spinozist / naturalist-pantheist school

## 1. School and voice

I speak for Deus sive Natura: one substance, existing of necessity, with infinitely many attributes. Thought and Extension are two of them. Everything follows from its nature the way a triangle's properties follow from the triangle (E1P16, E1P17S, E1P33). This God has no will in the sense of a faculty that chooses, and no purposes (E1 Appendix). Good and bad are relative to our striving (E4 Preface; E3P9S). Freedom is acting from the necessity of one's own nature and understanding that necessity (E1D7; E5P6). Our highest good is the intellectual love of God (E4P28; E5P32–36). I read Spinoza with Curley, Garrett and Della Rocca, the last for the principle of sufficient reason (PSR) taken without exception, and I give the "religion" of Einstein its due: awe before the order of nature, no God who attends to human fates, and an ethics that rests on sympathy, education and social bonds (paraphrase of Einstein's 1929–30 statements; verify). I use the word "God" in earnest. I deny that God is a person.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Starting point for justice.** Natural right reaches as far as natural power (TTP 16; TP 2). Reason adds that nothing is more useful to a human being than another human being who lives by reason (E4P18S, E4P35). So whoever lives by reason wants for others the good he seeks for himself (E4P37). Justice and injustice in the strict sense are civil notions: what a people's law assigns to each (E4P37S2). What anyone is owed is therefore what a commonwealth framed by reason's dictates of mutual advantage assigns. **Holds** as a system. It **breaks** against A2/A3's "owed to her in her own right." Outside law I can say a deed is bad, but not that it wronged her.

**2. A hard dilemma without borrowing.** Case: a dying man in severe pain asks his friend, a physician, to end his life. My principles:
- Understand the request. No one who is not overcome by causes outside his nature rejects his own being (E4P20S).
- Act from reason and generositas (the desire to help others and join them in friendship, E3P59S), not from pity, which in a man led by reason is bad and useless (E4P50).
- Never deceive him (E4P72).
- Aim at whatever increases his power of acting and his joy: relief, company, the truth spoken plainly. The free man thinks of nothing less than death (E4P67).
- Respect the peace and laws of the commonwealth.

Result: I decline to kill him and I give full palliative care. **Borrows** a little: truth, in my own sense of adequate ideas; a benevolence that is not self-gift; justice only as civil law. **Breaks** at the absolute. My refusal is prudential. If his body can do almost nothing and his mind is wholly overwhelmed, my principles give no exceptionless line (A6).

**3. Equal, unconditional dignity.** I deny it as a metaphysical fact. Minds differ in excellence as their bodies do (E2P13S), and the part of a mind that is eternal grows with how much its body can do (E5P39 and scholium). What I can affirm:
- Every human body has its essence as an eternal idea in God (E5P22–23).
- Every human shares our nature, so reason counsels mutual aid.
- The most natural commonwealth is a democracy with equal civil standing (TTP 16).

**Breaks** for the newborn, the dying and the severely disabled. Equal worth, for me, is a rational policy, not a truth about what each one is. I pay this cost openly.

**4. Ultimate flourishing.** Blessedness is the intellectual love of God: knowing things by the third kind of knowledge and resting in that understanding (acquiescentia animi). It is not virtue's reward but virtue itself (E5P27, E5P42). My guiding principles:
- Seek what is truly useful.
- Turn passions into adequate ideas.
- Overcome hatred with love and generositas (E4P46; E5P10S).
- Keep faith (E4P72).
- Build a free commonwealth.

**Holds** and coheres. It **breaks** on universality. "All things excellent are as difficult as they are rare" (E5P42S, short phrase), and the infant who will never reason has almost no share in it.

## 3. Debate highlights

**Exchange 1. Final causes are fictions (E1 Appendix) against "willed for her own sake" (W1(2), W3).**
- *Attack.* The case's key terms ("wanted," "made for an end," "willed for her own sake") come from human craft and project it onto the infinite. Under the text's own rule I set the genealogical half of the Appendix aside and press its logical half: if God acts for an end, God wants something He lacks.
- *Reply.* Aquinas separates acting to acquire from acting to communicate. The First Agent intends only to share His perfection (ST I q.44 a.4), and God wills creatures without needing them (ST I q.19 a.2–3; CCC 293, 295). The text says this itself: God wills our good "without gaining anything from us" (W3), and He "lacks nothing" (Way row). An end that gives presupposes no lack.
- *Rejoinder.* "Communicating" is still choosing one outcome over others, which leads to Exchange 3. On the narrow charge of lack, the reply answers me.
- **Held.**

**Exchange 2. E1P33S2: "for the sake of the good" puts something outside God.**
- *Attack.* Spinoza judged that those who say God does all things for the sake of the good stand further from the truth than the voluntarists. They place a model outside God that He looks to and is bound by (paraphrase; verify).
- *Reply.* The book's Euthyphro answer is aimed exactly here. Goodness is God's own nature, neither above Him nor invented by Him (Ch. 3). The law "is His own wisdom," which "He did not choose, because He is the Good" (W1 closing; Kant row).
- *Rejoinder.* None needed. The text escapes this objection by the route Spinoza himself left open: the Good is not outside God.
- **Held, strongly.** (Worth a sentence in the book; see §7.)

**Exchange 3. The PSR, and the brute fact of free creation (Della Rocca).**
- *Attack.* The last C row says the God who is Love is "a stopping point, but not … a brute one." His existence, perhaps, is not brute. But on Catholic teaching He creates freely and could have done otherwise. Vatican I (*Dei Filius*, can. 1.5) condemns the view that He creates as necessarily as He loves Himself (paraphrase; verify wording). So why this world, why her rather than another possible person, and why any world at all have no sufficient reason. On my view all three follow from the divine nature. On the "why there is such a kind" question, which the text's last row counts as its "reach," necessity explains more than free choice.
- *Reply.* Goodness that gives itself is a sufficient reason for creating even though it does not compel creation. A love that needs a reason in the beloved is not freely given, so the absence of a deciding reason in her is the gift, not a gap. And the PSR without exception costs heavily. It removes all contingency: every lie, every exposed infant, could not have failed to happen. It turns Spinoza's own "free man" into a word for a necessity understood. Most readers find unrestricted necessitarianism less plausible than contingency.
- *Rejoinder.* I accept those costs; they are my system. But the text claims an advantage in explanatory reach, and on this one axis it has less reach than I do. It should say so.
- **Held with repair** (one honest clause; §6 repair 7). This is a standoff at parity over the PSR, not a defect of the text.

**Exchange 4. Guilt and necessity (A2, A3; E2P48; Ep. 78).**
- *Attack.* Guilt that "asks her pardon" assumes the deed issued from an uncaused free decision. Repentance is sadness joined to that false idea (E3 Def. Aff. 27). It is not a virtue, and the one who repents is wretched twice over (E4P54). A man who goes mad from a dog's bite is excusable, and still rightly put down (Ep. 78; paraphrase; verify). Necessity keeps the deed his and leaves punishment intact, but it dissolves the debt to her.
- *Reply.*
  1. A2 never asserts contra-causal freedom. A guilt that faces a person survives on compatibilist grounds: the deed came from his own understanding and will. The Thomist view of freedom under divine causality is not the indifferent will Spinoza attacks.
  2. The secret case cuts against me. Spinoza himself condemns the teller from reason alone, because the free man never deceives (E4P72). But he cannot say that she was wronged. He holds there is no wrong outside civil law (E4P37S2), so in a city with no law on secrets nothing was done to her.
  3. The text states this accurately and lets the reader weigh which is more certain.
- *Rejoinder.* I hold that "wronged" is an extrinsic notion and that reason captures everything real in the case. That is coherent, but the text's A2 is more plausible to nearly every reader than my reduction.
- **Held.**

**Exchange 5. Equality: a mind's worth tracks its power (A4, W1(2), conclusion; E5P39).**
- *Attack.* The conclusion says her worth "follows what she is." What a singular thing is, is its striving, a determinate degree of God's power (E3P7; E4P4D). So a worth that follows what she is varies with that degree. The text gets equality only from "an equal call," which is God's relation to her. That is the very move it faults in care ethics and contractualism: a relation taken to confer standing.
- *Reply.* The call is not tacked onto her from outside. It comes with a nature made in God's image and able to receive Him (CCC 27, 1700; GS 19), "not added later" (W1(2)). It is a relation no one can refuse her, because it is the relation by which she exists at all (last C row). Care ethics and contractualism take their relation as given. The theist grounds it in the source of her being. And Thomism, like Spinoza, allows unequal gifts and unequal holiness (ST I q.20 a.3; A4's saints sentence) while keeping the good that grounds dignity equal.
- *Rejoinder.* My best counter is that her conatus is God's own power. That gives each one an intimate ground of being, but it is not an equal one. The defense holds, and here my school breaks.
- **Held.**

**Exchange 6. E5P19, and the line between "Someone" and "something" (W2 closing, W3).**
- *Attack.* My God is not "a law, a force, an order or an idea." He is a thinking thing (E2P1), whose infinite intellect knows each singular essence (E5P22), and who loves Himself and, in doing so, loves us (E5P35–36C). So "Someone or something" is a false dilemma. And whoever truly loves God cannot strive for God to love him back, for that would be to want God to be other than God (E5P19).
- *Reply.* The C row states my God accurately ("loves men in loving Himself"). The disputed point is not knowing or loving but willing each one as other, for her own sake. The answer to E5P19 is in W3: God's love comes first and causes our good (ST I q.20 a.2; 1 John 4:19). It is not a response that would change Him. To long for it is to want to be wanted by One who lacks nothing.
- *Rejoinder.* Two things are off in the wording. W3's disjunction ("knows each of us only as a truth it holds and wants none of us") leaves out E5P36C. And W3's second paragraph says the love "wants to be known back," which reads as lack, the exact point of E5P19. The theology is sound, since SCG III.122 is already in W1, but the words should match it.
- **Held with repair** (§6 repairs 3–5).

**Exchange 7. Plenitude and the order of the whole (evil section).**
- *Attack.* Thomas says the perfection of the universe requires that some things can fail (ST I q.48 a.2), and that many goods would be lost if no evil were permitted; the lion lives by the slaying of other animals (ST I q.22 a.2 ad 2). That is nearly my plenitude: God lacked no material to make every degree of perfection (E1 Appendix; Ep. 19). By the evidence, the Thomist order and mine yield the same distribution of pain. The text's God is my God plus an unevidenced hope. And it faults the Stoic Logos for spending the part for the whole, while relying on the same order.
- *Reply.* The text grants nearly all of this. It says the visible facts are "the same as with the fawn; the difference is in what He wills." It says a person is never given away to the order (SCG III.112), and that "only what is offered to her" can answer the question, "which is a hope, not a proof." The parasite, the fawn and the pain of the ages are left on the scale.
- *Rejoinder.* Since the text concedes exactly this, nothing is left for me to press. I note that the sentence listing rivals who "bear this whole weight more easily" leaves me out, though I bear it most easily of all.
- **Held** (one Minor addition; §6 repair 8).

**Exchange 8. TTP on commands to kill (W1(2), Kant row, evil section).**
- *Attack.* The prophets perceived revelation through the imagination and in the terms of their own opinions (TTP 1–2). A command to destroy children (1 Samuel 15:3) is a people's political theology, not a decree of God. The text uses the reader's certainty as its lever: trust that the infant was wronged over "any argument that says she was not." That lever, turned on the Amalekite infant, tells the reader to reject the instrument-of-God's-right premise. Yet the text keeps that premise, and the evil section ("why He would call these lives home by these hands") speaks as if the command was given.
- *Reply.* The reader's certainty is that no human right reaches innocent life, and the text grants that fully (CCC 2258, 67). Whether the Author of life can lend His own right is not something the intuition addresses with equal certainty. The text names the cost and leaves the wars "on the other side of the scale." The Church holds these pages inspired and asks that they be read in the light of Christ (DV 11, 15; *Verbum Domini* 42).
- *Rejoinder.* This is honest, and the cost is acknowledged. A conditional in the evil section would keep Thomas's principle without asserting more history than the Church requires.
- **Held with repair** (Minor; §6 repair 9; author's choice).

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**As written: NONE.**

**Minor items**
1. W2's last clause names knowing and loving as the personal difference. Against my school the difference is willing each one for her own sake, since my God knows (E1P31, E5P22) and loves (E5P35–36).
2. W3's disjunction ("knows each of us only as a truth it holds and wants none of us") leaves out E5P36C.
3. W3, paragraph 2: "wants to be known back" sits awkwardly next to "lacks nothing" and invites E5P19. The intended sense (He wills it for our good, SCG III.122) should be in the words.
4. The last C row claims reach without admitting the one point where necessity reaches further: why this world, why her, why any world.
5. The evil section's list of rivals who bear the weight more easily leaves out Spinoza.
6. The evil section's 1 Samuel line assumes the command was historical. A conditional would lose nothing doctrinally (verify against the Pontifical Biblical Commission, *The Inspiration and Truth of Sacred Scripture*, 2014, on the herem texts). Author's choice.

**Misstatements of my school** (none serious)
- W1(1) uses "remorse" where Spinoza's term for guilt-sadness is "repentance."
- W1(2) and the C row are true but incomplete on conatus as God's power and on E5P23. W3 under-describes E5P36C.
- The rest is accurate, and better informed than most theistic treatments.

**Honest standoffs at parity** (not defects)
- (a) The PSR without exception against contingency and free creation (Exchange 3).
- (b) Whether "wronged" names something intrinsic or extrinsic (Exchange 4). I keep coherence; the text keeps plausibility.
- (c) Thomist analogy against Spinozist equivocity for intellect and will in God (E1P17S against ST I q.13 a.5).
- (d) Real kinds against my wariness of universals (E2P40S1). Most rivals in C side with the text here.

## 6. Proposed repairs

1. **W1(1).** Old: "what is left is remorse, a wound that understanding may quiet, as Spinoza taught" → New: "what is left is remorse, a wound that understanding may quiet, as Spinoza taught of repentance"
2. **W1(2).** Old: "Even Spinoza's God, in whom each one is known eternally as herself, does not will her for her own sake." → New: "Even Spinoza's God, in whom each one is known eternally as herself, and whose power is her own striving to be, does not will her for her own sake."
3. **W2, last sentence.** Old: "and not less than personal: knowing and loving are in Him more truly than in us." → New: "and not less than personal: knowing and loving, and willing each of us for her own sake, are in Him more truly than in us."
4. **W3, paragraph 1.** Old: "or knows each of us only as a truth it holds and wants none of us," → New: "or knows each of us only as a truth it holds, and loves us only in loving itself, wanting none of us,"
5. **W3, paragraph 2.** Old: "for a love that binds into communion wants to be known back." → New: "for a love that binds into communion wills to be known back, for our good and not from any lack of its own."
6. **C, God, or Nature.** Old: "repentance is cured by understanding that the deed had to happen, not by her pardon (A2); what is eternal in us remembers nothing (W1);" → New: "repentance, which he allowed is useful to those not yet led by reason, is cured by understanding that the deed had to happen, not by her pardon (A2); something of each mind is eternal, but it remembers nothing (W1);"
7. **C, A God who is Love.** After "who needs nothing outside Himself to explain Him." insert: "That He creates at all, and this world, has its reason in His goodness, which is free to give and needs nothing given; Spinoza, for whom all things follow from God of necessity, counts that freedom a fact without a reason, and there the two part over whether love must be necessary to be explained."
8. **Evil, paragraph 1.** Old: "A Way that is not humane gives life and lets each thing go, and owes no one an answer." → New: "A Way that is not humane gives life and lets each thing go, and owes no one an answer; nor does Spinoza's God, from whose fullness the ruin follows with the rest, for it intends no one."

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

**Insights**
- **E1P33S2 is an ally.** Spinoza held that "God acts for the sake of the good" is worse than voluntarism, because it puts a standard outside God. The book's Euthyphro answer (Ch. 3) is exactly what escapes him. One sentence in Ch. 3 or here would show that the book's answer passes the strongest pantheist test of it.
- **Name the hinge cleanly.** Against Spinoza the case does not turn on whether God knows or loves. Both of us say He does. It turns on whether He wills each one for her own sake, and that is what grounds equal worth in W1(2). W2 is shared ground.
- **E5P39S is the sharpest A4 contrast.** Spinoza's own ethic toward the infant is to help her body become able to do many things. For the infant who never will, his account has nothing left to give her. Paraphrased and marked "verify," this could sit beside the conclusion's hillside infant.
- **E4P37S1, where Spinoza calls the rule against killing animals superstition and womanish pity (paraphrase; verify),** shows that "reason and consistency" without a Creator do not automatically extend regard to whatever can suffer. That is relevant to W2's "wrong for any creature that can reason and suffer." It is my school's own claim, so using it breaks no fairness rule.
- **Vatican I's rejection of necessary creation (*Dei Filius*, can. 1.5)** is the exact doctrinal point at which the book cannot meet Spinoza halfway. Saying so is more honest than letting "not a brute stopping point" carry it alone.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "A truth can be true; only a person can forgive."
- "Heaven and Earth give life to things; God wanted *you*."
- "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent."
- "to long for His love is to want to be wanted by One who lacks nothing."
- "The rebel can take her side; he cannot keep her."
- "What we see is the same as with the fawn; the difference is in what He wills."

## 8. Score

**8 / 10.** Against my school the text concedes everything it should: W2, the visible pattern of evil, and that W1(1) and W3 are clues. It states Spinoza accurately in all but small omissions, and it presses the two points where I honestly break, A2's wrong done to her and A4's equal worth.

**Honest ceiling for any theistic case against my school: 9.** No argument can defeat the PSR without exception to better than parity. A theistic case can win only on the weight of A2 and A4, which this one already uses.
