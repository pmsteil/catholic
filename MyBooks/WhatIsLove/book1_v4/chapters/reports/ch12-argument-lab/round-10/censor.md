# Censor's report: "A moral argument for God" (Ch. 12, *What Is Love?*)

## Scope

A pre-imprimatur doctrinal read of /tmp/claude-0/lab10/ARGUMENT.md in full: the Form note, A (with A1–A7), W1 (frame, (1), (2), closing), W2, W3 (both paragraphs), every row of C, the section on evil (both paragraphs) and the Conclusion. I read it sentence by sentence against Scripture (RSVCE), the Catechism, Trent, Vatican I and II, the encyclicals and other magisterial documents, and St. Thomas. I read only the brief and the argument. I checked citations on vatican.va where the site would serve them this session, and against standard texts otherwise (see §2). The philosophical reports about non-Catholic thinkers (Kant, Spinoza, the Buddhist and Confucian sources and so on) fall outside a censor's competence. I checked them only where they touch doctrine.

## Bottom line

**No Errors.**

Nothing here is contrary to faith or morals, and nothing here is a censor bound to strike. The places that need care are precision and citation points. One of them, the sentence "His forgiveness is whole" in W1(1), is close enough to a condemned proposition (Trent, Sess. XIV, can. 12) that I ask for the precision before printing (Finding 6).

## 3. Replacement wording

**R1 (W1(1)), required.**
- Old: "and His forgiveness is whole. Yet it does not cancel what we owe her: absolution "does not remedy all the disorders sin has caused" (CCC 1459), so we must still repair what we can, and God does not take her place in giving the pardon that is hers to give."
- New: "and the guilt He forgives, He forgives whole. Yet it does not cancel what we owe her: absolution "does not remedy all the disorders sin has caused" (CCC 1459), so we must still repair what we can, and for sins after Baptism still make satisfaction (CCC 1459–1460, 1472–1473); and God does not take her place in giving the pardon that is hers to give."

**R2 (A4).**
- Old: "the Church teaches, CCC 2277; *Evangelium Vitae* 65–66;"
- New: "the Church teaches, CCC 2277; *Evangelium Vitae* 57, 65–66; *Veritatis Splendor* 52, 80;"

**R3 (A6), optional.**
- Old: "a man may lay down his life for another, or for the truth, and accept the death that comes,"
- New: "a man may lay down his life for another, or for the truth (*Veritatis Splendor* 50), and accept the death that comes,"

**R4 (W1(2) and the Kant row).**
- Old (W1(2)): "This has a cost: Kant protects her here more firmly than this book does (C, Reason's own law)."
- New: "This has a cost: Kant draws the line more absolutely than this book does, for he binds even God (C, Reason's own law)."
- Old (Kant row): "On one point Kant protects her more firmly than this book does."
- New: "On one point Kant draws the line more absolutely than this book does."

**R5 (W1(2)).**
- Old: "The Church holds those pages inspired (*Dei Verbum* 11), and asks that they be read in the light of Christ,"
- New: "The Church holds those pages inspired (*Dei Verbum* 11), asks that what their human authors meant be sought through the literary forms of their age (*Dei Verbum* 12), and asks that they be read in the light of Christ,"

**R6 (W3).**
- Old: "when such a person turns from a wrong out of love for the good, the Church hopes he is already turning toward the One who forgives."
- New: "one who seeks the truth and does God's will as he understands it can be saved (CCC 1260), and when such a person turns from a wrong out of love for the good, this book hopes he is already turning toward the One who forgives."

**R7 (W3).**
- Old: "yet she is held by Him."
- New: "yet she is not forgotten by Him (Isaiah 49:15)."

**R8 (W2).**
- Old: "Evolution explains well why our senses and reasoning mostly get the world right:"
- New: "Evolution explains well why our senses, and the bodily powers our reasoning works through, mostly get the world right:"

**R9 (evil).**
- Old: "That is a good, not a necessity; whether it is worth this world's cost is part of the weighing, not settled by it,"
- New: "That is a good, not a necessity; whether it is worth this world's cost is, for reason, part of the weighing, not settled by it (faith holds that God permits no evil from which He does not bring good, CCC 324),"

**R10 (evil).**
- Old: "a person can refuse God forever by his own free choice (CCC 1033)."
- New: "a person can refuse God forever by his own free choice (CCC 1033); and the Church gives this teaching as an urgent call to conversion (CCC 1036)."

**R11 (W1 frame).**
- Old: "though it is wrong because of what it does to her;"
- New: "though it is wrong because of what it does to her, and so against the law that is His own wisdom;"

**R13 (C, Aristotelian row).**
- Old: "the Church answers that his life does not stop being a good to him,"
- New: "the Church answers that his life does not stop being a good to him (*Evangelium Vitae* 34),"

## Verdict

**Nihil obstat: yes, as it stands.**

Nothing in the text is contrary to faith or morals. I ask the author to adopt R1 before printing, for theological precision: as written, "His forgiveness is whole" is patient of an orthodox reading, but it sits next to a proposition Trent condemns. R2 through R13 are recommended improvements, not conditions.

The text handles natural law, 1 Samuel 15, the unbaptized infant, unbelievers' culpability and *Nostra Aetate* with unusual accuracy and restraint. It handles CCC 2258 and the absolutes on euthanasia and suicide just as carefully. It overclaims nowhere.
