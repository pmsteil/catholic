# Blind review: the Marxist school

## 1. School and voice

I argue for historical materialism and the Marxist moral tradition at its strongest. That means Marx and Engels themselves: the critique of religion as the inverted consciousness of an inverted world, the claim that morality and right answer to the mode of production, and the condemnation of capitalism for unfreedom, alienation and exploitation. It also means the best of their heirs: the Tucker–Wood reading, on which Marx does not condemn capitalism as *unjust*; Husami and Geras, who argue against it; G. A. Cohen's fact-insensitive principles of equality and community; Steven Lukes's diagnosis (*Marxism and Morality*, 1985) that rejecting the morality of rights left Marxism defenceless against means–end reasoning; and Terry Eagleton's reading of Marx as a moralist of human flourishing. I attack the starting points as well as the steps. Under the fairness rule, any genealogy I aim at the text I must also let the text aim at me. The rule binds me hard, because Marxism is above all a genealogical school.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**(1) Starting point for justice.** Orthodox answer (Marx, Engels, Wood): no transhistorical starting point. "Justice" is a juridical category that fits each mode of production (*Capital* III, ch. 21, verify). The critical standard lies elsewhere, in human emancipation: free, conscious, communal self-activity and the meeting of needs. What anyone is "owed" is then not a question I answer in my own terms. The heirs' answer (Cohen, Geras): there are fact-insensitive principles of equality and community, held as basic. **Verdict:** the orthodox answer is coherent but drops "owed" for the past, which breaks A1–A2 for the ancient slave. The heirs' answer holds, but it stops where the realist stops, taking the principle as given. So it does not break; it reaches parity with godless realism and no further.

**(2) A hard dilemma.** A nurses' union faces chronic understaffing that is quietly killing patients. Management will not move without a strike, and a strike endangers the patients in the wards today. My principles: (i) the standpoint of the exploited and of those who depend on them; (ii) solidarity, since the patients are working people too; (iii) the real movement, so act in the way that builds collective power and does not divide workers from patients; (iv) need first. Resolution: strike, keep emergency and critical-care crews, publish the staffing figures, and build an alliance with patients. **Verdict:** this holds in its own terms. But the line I would not cross (abandoning the critical patient) I hold as solidarity and judgment, not as inviolability. In principle it bends if the struggle requires it. There I either borrow the book's justice, mercy and self-gift under other names or let A6 go.

**(3) Equal, unconditional dignity.** Marx refused to measure persons by one standard, made the free development of each the condition for the free development of all (*Manifesto*), and gave the most to those who can give least ("according to his needs," *Critique of the Gotha Programme*). The newborn, the dying and the severely disabled count whole, by need. The class enemy is to be freed with everyone else: abolishing classes frees the bourgeois from his own estrangement too (*Holy Family*, verify). But Engels openly denies that equality is eternal. He judged the real content of the proletarian demand for equality to be the abolition of classes, and said any demand beyond that "passes into absurdity" (*Anti-Dühring* I.10). **Verdict:** the orthodox position breaks on *unconditional*. My heirs affirm equal concern only by taking it as given. Where I ground it in species-being (free, conscious activity), it must stretch to reach the child who will never act, just as Kant's ground must. Historically, I also borrow: Engels himself traces the idea of equality partly to Christianity.

**(4) Ending point for flourishing.** The realm of freedom beyond necessity (*Capital* III, ch. 48, verify). The rich individuality of all-round development. Labour become "life's prime want" (*Gotha*). A community in which producing for another affirms both (*Comments on James Mill*, 1844). Guiding principles: abolish exploitation and domination, need first, solidarity, ruthless criticism of all that exists, and truth tested in practice (*Theses on Feuerbach* II). **Verdict:** as an account of earthly flourishing it is coherent, and the Church shares much of it (*Laborem Exercens*; *Centesimus Annus* 41 on alienation). It breaks at the grave: it promises those who come after and offers the slain nothing. It also stretches for the never-capable, since "development of capacities" is measured by powers.

## 3. Debate highlights

**E1. Against the starting points (A heading).**
*Attack.* "What still seems true after honest reflection" is a formed consciousness certifying itself. The ruling ideas of each age are the ideas of its ruling class (*German Ideology*). Seneca reflected too, and was calm about drowning weak newborns. A rule that trusts reflective seemings trusts whatever social being has deposited in us.
*Defense.* The rule is not bare intuitionism. It includes reflection *on how we came to believe*, and it states a defeat condition: a belief is defeated if the best account of what produced it would have produced it whether or not it was true. Ideology critique, done properly, is functional: a belief is ideological when it presents a particular interest as universal. A1 (torturing a child for fun) and A4 (the equal worth of the infant and the disabled) serve no ruling class. They cost the powerful, since they forbid exposure, forbid measuring persons by output, and forbid killing the burdensome. Marx too starts from a seeming he will not give up. His 1844 Introduction ends the critique of religion in a "categorical imperative" to overthrow every relation in which man is debased and enslaved.
*Rejoinder.* Then take Marx's own genealogy of equality. Aristotle could not decipher the equivalent form of value because Greek society rested on "the inequality of men." The secret can be read only once "the notion of human equality has already acquired the fixity of a popular prejudice," and that happens only where the commodity form is general (*Capital* I, ch. 1 §3). Commodity exchange really does equate labours. So it would produce the belief in equality whether or not any metaphysical equal dignity exists. By your own rule, A4 is defeated.
*Second defense.* The genealogy explains the equality of *exchangers and labourers*. The text's test case is the newborn on the hillside, and she exchanges nothing. Marx himself says commodity society tends to resolve personal worth into exchange value (*Manifesto*, verify). If his account predicts anything about the infant, it predicts worth tied to productive capacity, the opposite of A4. Worth that follows nature and not capacity was also taught where there was no general commodity form (Mencius, the Buddhist texts, Genesis 1:27; the text's A4 and the sacred-order row). And under the fairness rule, the same genealogy covers the proletarian demand for equality, which Engels admits.
**Held.** The A heading's rule survives the strongest Marxist genealogy of equality, and does so on the very case (the infant) the conclusion rests on.

**E2. Against A2: guilt as moralism.**
*Attack.* A2 builds morality around a guilty individual asking pardon. Marx deliberately refused this: the capitalist is a personification of economic categories, and his standpoint can less than any other make the individual responsible for relations whose creature he remains (*Capital* I, Preface, verify). Personalizing structural harm as "sin" is the classic ideological move.
*Defense.* A2 is not only personal. It names structures of sin (CCC 1869). It names shares in a wrong by willing participation, approval or failure to hinder (CCC 1868). Elsewhere it grades imputability by knowledge and freedom (CCC 1735, cited in the evil section), which is exactly where Marx's structural point properly belongs. And Marx cannot do without person-directed reproach: *Capital* is full of robbery, vampirism and theft. Geras's verdict stands: Marx thought capitalism unjust without thinking he thought so (verify).
*Rejoinder.* Granted. But the text's "Much of this the Church grants" papers over a real difference. The Church holds structures to be the *expression and effect* of personal sins (CCC 1869; *Sollicitudo Rei Socialis* 36). Marx holds that the structure produces the motives. The explanation runs in opposite directions, and the C row should say so.
**Held with repair** (R2).

**E3. Against the "History and class" dilemma.**
*Attack.* The row offers two horns: justice fits each age (Wood), or the deepest principles hold in every age (the analytic heirs), which meets the realist's question. It misses a third: Marx's condemnation in the name of *human nature*, a species whose good is free, shared activity (Geras, *Marx and Human Nature*; Eagleton). It also misreads the *Gotha* critique. Marx refused "equal right" because it applies one standard (labour) to unequal individuals, regarding them "from one definite side only," as workers (verify). Refusing to measure persons is not refusing their equal standing. The text's own Kant row says dignity "admits no equivalent," which is also a refusal to measure.
*Defense.* The third horn lands in the secular-Aristotelian row: whether our nature is an accident or an order, and how the child who will never reason fares under an end each must achieve. Freedom and self-realization are transhistorical goods in any case, so even the Woodian Marx is a realist about goods and meets W2 there. On "owed," Wood's Marx really does decline to say what the slave was owed.
*Rejoinder.* Accepted on the horns. The *Gotha* sentence is still a misstatement. Its inference ("refused to measure … so … not that she is worth as much as you") does not follow. The accurate reason Marx will not say it is that he treats "worth" and "right" as the language of an age, and Engels caps equality at the abolition of classes.
**Held with repair** (R1, R3). After repair the row's verdict is unchanged: the never-capable are reached by need, but equal standing is either stretched from capacities or taken as given.

**E4. Feuerbach's and Marx's critique of religion, against W1(2) and W3.**
*Attack.* "Every child was wanted by the One who made her" is human love alienated: our own species-powers to want, keep and forgive, set over us as another's. Religion is the sigh of the oppressed creature, the heart of a heartless world. W3's longing to be received whole fits its answer because the answer was made to fit it.
*Defense.* Projection is a genealogy, and the text has already conceded its force where it applies. W1(1) and W3 are offered only as "clues of fit, not evidence," because science can explain the longing "whether or not anyone hears." The load-bearing witnesses (W1(2), W2) infer from moral facts, not from longing. Fairness cuts both ways: the communist hope has an equally available projection genealogy as secularized eschatology (Löwith, verify), and I would not let that settle anything against me. Substantively, the species cannot do what the text needs. Feuerbach's infinite species, like Mou Zongsan's heart or the moon in ten thousand rivers, wants no *one*, and keeps no one lost.
*Rejoinder.* The species is not an abstraction. It is living persons in solidarity, and they *can* want each one.
*Reply.* They can want the living. By hypothesis no one wanted the infant on the hillside, and solidarity cannot want the dead. Marx's own image of a community in which each affirms the other describes the living.
**Held.** W3's honest scope is what saves it.

**E5. Against A6: clean hands and structural violence (Trotsky, *Their Morals and Ours*; Merleau-Ponty, *Humanism and Terror*).**
*Attack.* The choice is never between violence and non-violence, only between kinds of violence. An absolute against "spending" the innocent while "weighing" side effects shelters those whose violence runs through systems and never aims at anyone. The absolute keeps the absolutist's hands clean and costs the poor their lives.
*Defense.* The text concedes that the absolute "can cost lives, even many." It refuses the shelter: usurious dealings that lead others to hunger and death are indirect homicide, imputable to the dealers (CCC 2269). Catholic teaching also does not forbid all resistance: armed resistance to grave, prolonged oppression is permitted under strict conditions (CCC 2243), and the starving who take what they urgently need do not steal (CCC 2408). The line forbids only *targeting the innocent*. And Marx's own structural non-blame (E2) makes class terror against persons the spending of the non-culpable for history's goal. That is Lukes's diagnosis.
*Rejoinder.* The book has its own instrument clause. With St. Thomas it holds that the Lord of life could command the killing of Amalek's children, and that the executor acts by God's right and not his own (W1(2)). Trotsky's executor acts as history's instrument. The structure is the same.
*Reply.* The text pays that cost openly against Kant, and the parity is not exact. The holder of that right gave the life, wills her for her own sake and keeps her past death. History's goal gave her nothing and loses her. And the text shuts the door on any present claim to such a command (CCC 67), whereas history's goal always claims present executors.
**Held** (an honest standoff already priced in the text).

**E6. Against W2's account of moral progress.**
*Attack.* Conscience did not correct itself by "reason holding itself to consistency." Slavery fell when its economics and its enemies' power changed (Eric Williams, *Capitalism and Slavery*), and through Saint-Domingue's revolt. Progress is class struggle.
*Defense.* The text already says the push came "from the wronged themselves, who would not be ignored." Williams's decline thesis is contested by Drescher's *Econocide*, which argues that Britain abolished against its interest (verify). The decisive cases are the corrections toward those who can neither struggle nor repay: infants and the profoundly cognitively disabled. Power realignment cannot explain those.
*Rejoinder.* Fair. But "what moves those who hear them is reason …" is stated as if interest and force did nothing.
**Held with repair** (R4, minor).

**E7. Against W1(1) and the evil section: the debt to the dead.**
*Attack.* Marx is forward-looking: the revolution must let the dead bury their dead (*Eighteenth Brumaire*). The debt to the slain is paid by completing their struggle, which is Benjamin's "tradition of the oppressed." Calling this "memory, not repair" understates it.
*Defense.* The text honours that road ("the second road is noble, and the Church walks much of it") and says exactly what it cannot do: what the living pay, she is not there to receive. My own heirs concede this. Horkheimer wrote to Benjamin that past injustice is complete and the slain are really slain. Benjamin answered that remembrance can make the completed incomplete, and admitted that this is theology (Arcades N8,1, verify). Adorno's line, which the text cites through *Spe Salvi* 42, belongs to the same debate.
*Rejoinder.* None at strength. I accept the cost. The clue weighs as much as the reader finds a debt owed *to her* must be receivable by her, and the text claims no more.
**Held.**

**E8. Against the conclusion's Moorean appeal.**
*Attack.* "Which are you surer of" measures formation, not truth. Seneca's certainty ran the other way.
*Defense.* The appeal is made after the A rule's genealogical scrutiny (E1), not in place of it. And my school runs on the same move: no argument shakes Marx's certainty that the debasement of man is to be overthrown. If I disqualify the reader's certainty about the infant, I disqualify my own about the factory child.
**Held** (parity).

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**As written: NONE.**

No Fatal or Serious Marxist objection survives. The strongest candidates were the ideology critique of the starting points, the commodity-form genealogy of equality, the projection critique, and the clean-hands critique of A6. Each fails against the text's own rules and admissions, or lands on a cost the text already states openly.

**Minor items**
1. The W2 sentence "what moves those who hear them is reason holding itself to consistency …" overstates its point by leaving out interest and force (E6; R4).
2. The Marx row does not name the critique of religion, which is the school's best-known challenge to the whole project. The answer is already in the text (W1(1), W3, the evil section, the sacred-order row), so this is a matter of naming, not of substance (R2).

**Misstatements of my school**
1. "Marx refused to measure persons by any one standard, so it can say her needs are to be met, not that she is worth as much as you." The premise is true, but the inference is wrong. Refusing to measure persons is not denying their equal standing; the text's own Kant row treats "admits no equivalent" as a strength. The accurate reasons Marx does not say it are that he treats "worth" and "right" as the language of an age, and that Engels caps equality at the abolition of classes. The row's verdict is unchanged by the fix (R1).
2. "The gravest wrongs are done by systems as well as by persons. Much of this the Church grants." Marx speaks of systemic harms whose agents he refuses to hold responsible. The Church holds structures to be the effect of personal sins. That is a real parting, not a shared view (R2).
3. The justice dilemma omits the human-nature horn (Geras, Eagleton). It is answerable through the Aristotelian row (R3).

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- Where explanation may stop. Cohen's fact-insensitive principles and Engels's fit between thought and nature stop where the realist stops; the text's one Source stops elsewhere (W2).
- Truth's bindingness. "Truth tested in practice" as a constitutive aim, against A7's "binds every reasoning mind."
- The instrument clause. 1 Samuel 15 under St. Thomas's principle is a cost the text pays openly, against my humane heirs as well as against Kant.
- Functional critique of consolation. Whether faith demobilizes or mobilizes the struggle against suffering is an empirical question, and the history runs both ways.

## 6. Proposed repairs

**R1 (History and class: the needs sentence).**
Old: "Need reaches the newborn, and asks most for those who can give least; but Marx refused to measure persons by any one standard, so it can say her needs are to be met, not that she is worth as much as you (A4)."
New: "Need reaches the newborn, and asks most for those who can give least; and Marx, who refused to measure persons by any one standard and held the free development of each to be the condition of the free development of all, counts her whole and not as a worker. What he would not say is why she counts as much as you: "worth" and "right" he held to be the language of an age, and Engels judged any demand for equality beyond the abolition of classes absurd. Those of his heirs who say it take it as given, as the contractualist does, or ground it in a nature whose good is free, shared activity, a ground that, like Kant's, must stretch to reach the child who will never act (A4)."

**R2 (History and class: opening).**
Old: "holds that moral codes answer to how a society produces its life and often serve those who rule, and that the gravest wrongs are done by systems as well as by persons. Much of this the Church grants (A2, W2)."
New: "holds that moral codes answer to how a society produces its life and often serve those who rule; that the gravest harms are done by systems, whose agents Marx would not hold responsible for relations whose creatures they remain; and that religion is our own powers set over us as another's, the heart of a heartless world. Much of this the Church grants (A2, W2), though she parts over which way the explanation runs, for she holds such structures to be the expression and effect of personal sins, in which whoever willingly takes part shares (CCC 1868–1869); and the longings religion answers this book weighs as clues, not proofs (W1(1), W3)."

**R3 (History and class: the dilemma).**
Old: "if, with its analytic heirs, the deepest principles of justice hold in every age, it meets the realist's question (W2)."
New: "if, with its analytic heirs, the deepest principles of justice hold in every age, it meets the realist's question (W2); and if, with others of them, its measure is a human nature whose good is free, shared activity, it meets the questions put to the flourishing of our kind."

**R4 (W2: moral progress).**
Old: "The push has often come from the wronged themselves, who would not be ignored; what moves those who hear them is reason holding itself to consistency with goods it already sees: the law of truth again."
New: "The push has often come from the wronged themselves, who would not be ignored, and interest and force have often done their part; but what moves those who hear them against their own interest, and what reaches those who can neither struggle nor repay, is reason holding itself to consistency with goods it already sees: the law of truth again."

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

**Insights**
1. **Marx's own imperative.** In the 1844 Introduction to the critique of Hegel's *Philosophy of Right*, the critique of religion ends in the teaching that man is the highest being for man, and so in a categorical imperative to overthrow every relation in which man is debased, enslaved, forsaken or despised. The fiercest critic of religion-as-ideology founds his own project on an unconditional imperative of human dignity. This is Lukes's paradox and the text's closing invitation in one. The book could cite it at the protester paragraph: the protest ends exactly where the invitation begins.
2. **The commodity-form genealogy fails for the infant.** Marx's most serious genealogy of equality (*Capital* I, ch. 1 §3) explains the equality of those who exchange. The child on the hillside exchanges nothing. This is the strongest available answer to a Marxist debunking of A4, and it uses the conclusion's own case.
3. **Structural non-blame plus class violence.** Marx refuses to make the individual capitalist responsible for relations whose creature he remains (*Capital* I, Preface). Revolutionary terror against persons is then, by his own account, the spending of the non-culpable for history's goal. This sharpens the row's A6 sentence without resting on any genealogy of Marxism's origins.
4. **Rerum Novarum 45** (vatican.va): a bargain accepted "through necessity or fear of a worse evil" makes the worker "the victim of force and injustice," under "a dictate of natural justice more imperious and ancient than any bargain." This is the Church's ally for A3 (consent does not make it right), and it shows the Church already holds the Marxist point about coerced consent.
5. **Centesimus Annus 41** (vatican.va) takes up Marx's concept of alienation and leads it back to the Christian vision as "a reversal of means and ends." It would serve W3 and communion: the Church accepts the diagnosis and relocates its root.
6. **Horkheimer to Benjamin, 1937** (verify): the slain are really slain. Benjamin answered that remembrance can unclose what is closed, and that this is theology. This confirms W1(1) from inside the Marxist tradition, and it is a stronger witness than Adorno alone.
7. **CCC 2243 and 2408** belong in any reply to the clean-hands objection. The line forbids only the deliberate killing of the innocent: armed resistance to grave oppression is permitted under strict conditions, and the starving who take what they urgently need do not steal.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent."
- "A truth can be true; only a person can forgive."
- "The rebel can take her side; he cannot keep her."
- "to the children already spent they can offer memory, not repair."
- "The hands that pull a child from the fire are part of what He made real, so His permission never excuses ours."
- "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."

## 8. Score

**8.5 / 10.** Against my school the text is robust. It anticipates the structural objection, the clean-hands objection and the hope-for-the-future objection, and it prices its own costs openly. What remains is a misstated *Gotha* inference and a missing horn, both repairable in a sentence without changing any verdict.

**Honest ceiling for any theistic case against Marxism: about 9.** At the bottom, the parting is over where explanation may stop and whether the debt to the dead must be receivable. The text treats these, rightly, as convergence and clue, not compulsion. A Marxist who accepts that the slain are really slain, and pays that price, is not refuted. He is shown what he gives up.
