# Blind review: the Daoist school (Daodejing and Zhuangzi)

## 1. School and voice

I speak for the classical Daoist texts, the *Daodejing* (DDJ) and the *Zhuangzi* (ZZ), read with A. C. Graham, Chad Hansen, P. J. Ivanhoe, Livia Kohn and Brook Ziporyn. The Way (dao) cannot be named or fixed as a code. It gives life and does not own, rears and does not rule (DDJ 10, 51). Heaven and Earth are not humane (DDJ 5). Benevolence and righteousness (ren, yi) show up when the Way has been lost (DDJ 18, 38). The sage acts without forcing (wu wei) and lets things be so of themselves (ziran). He sees that "this" and "that" depend on where one stands (ZZ 2), and he meets death as one more change (ZZ 6, 18). I am not a theist. I am also not a nihilist: compassion is the first of the sage's treasures (DDJ 67), and the sage abandons no one (DDJ 27). In this debate I refuse the court the case sets up (debts, claims, dignities) more than I deny what that court is trying to protect.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Starting point for justice.** Justice is not where I start. Yi is a sign that the Way has already been lost (DDJ 38). What I do have is a pattern: Heaven's Way takes from what has too much and adds to what has too little, while the human way does the opposite (DDJ 77). The sage also holds the creditor's tally and does not press for payment (DDJ 79). So what anyone is "owed" is room to complete their own course to their natural span without being cut off. Ruling is mostly not interfering (DDJ 57). → **Holds** as an account of balance and prevention. **Breaks** on repair: my school cannot say what a victim already harmed is owed. Laozi himself admits that when a great resentment is reconciled, some resentment remains (DDJ 79).

**2. A hard case, resolved without the book's four terms.** A dying father in pain he cannot bear asks his son to end his life. My principles:
- Wu wei: force the change neither way.
- Nourish life to its Heaven-given span (ZZ 3, 4).
- Never "cut in place of the master carpenter," that is, never kill in the Executioner's place (DDJ 74).
- Compassion (ci, DDJ 67).

The resolution: ease the pain, stop treatment that only strains against the change, stay with him, and do not kill him. When Zilai lay dying, his friend told the weeping family not to disturb the change (ZZ 6; verify). For the surgeon with five patients waiting, the "machine heart" that calculates (ZZ 12) is the error itself, so the sage refuses. → **Holds**, and borrows nothing; ci belongs to my own tradition. **Breaks at the edge:** my "no" is a disposition of the sage's heart, not a rule. Rules come after the Way is lost. So against pain that truly cannot be relieved, I cannot say *never*. Making a man endure can also be forcing.

**3. Equal, unconditional dignity.** Seen from the Way, no thing is noble or base; each thing ranks itself high and others low only from its own standpoint (ZZ 17). The soft and weak go with life, the hard and strong with death (DDJ 76). The maimed and misshapen can be fuller in virtue than the whole (ZZ 5). The not-good are not cast away (DDJ 62). This covers the newborn, the dying, the disabled and the enemy, without conditions. → **Holds** for equality. **Breaks, or declines,** on dignity in the book's sense. My equality is shared with the ox and the rat's liver (ZZ 6). I do not treat "dignity" as a final category. It gives me no exceptionless bar on killing. And my leveling means I can say both "she was harmed" and "no one lives longer than a child who dies in infancy" (ZZ 2). Holding both requires "walking two roads" (ZZ 2), and whether that is coherent is honestly disputed.

**4. Ultimate good and guiding principles.** The ultimate good is free and easy wandering in accord with the Way (ZZ 1), and returning to the root, which is stillness and the recovery of one's destiny (DDJ 16). It is the true person, in whom Heaven and the human do not defeat each other, who neither delights in life nor hates death (ZZ 6). My principles are wu wei, ziran, simplicity, and the three treasures: compassion, frugality, and not daring to be first (DDJ 67). → **Holds** and is coherent. It **does not keep** the individual: the good is to be changed without resisting. It **borrows partly**. The practice depends on illumination (ming, ZZ 2), on seeing truly, and that works much like the book's A7. Graham conceded that "be aware" is the one imperative reason itself supplies (Graham, *Reason and Spontaneity*; verify).

## 3. Debate highlights

**E1. The vocabulary.**
- **Attack.** The whole case speaks the language of a lost Way: owed, guilt, claim, dignity, debt. When the springs dry up, the fish left on dry land keep each other wet with spit; they would do better to forget each other in the rivers and lakes (ZZ 6). A case built on spit-and-moisture words mistakes the symptom for evidence about the deepest reality. Even "covenant which binds" falls under Zhuangzi's point that what is truly joined holds without glue, and what is truly bound holds without cords (ZZ 8; verify).
- **Defense.** The book does not make law the highest thing either. Love is the fulfilling of the law (Rom 13:10). The New Law is the grace of the Holy Spirit written on the heart (Jer 31:33; CCC 1965–1966, 1972). Catholic teaching also tells of a lost original harmony (CCC 374–379, 400), a story that runs close to DDJ 38. The A items describe what love must answer to *in the drought we actually live in*. The infant is on the hillside now. And the Daoist keeps the substance while refusing the words: he condemns rulers' cruelty, abandons no one, and holds compassion first.
- **Rejoinder.** Granted: the drought is real, and the two schools differ only in the remedy, a return to the Way or a covenant. That is a standoff at parity, not a defect.
- **Held.**

**E2. Equality without a Wanter.**
- **Attack.** I ground A4 more cheaply: from the Way nothing is noble or base (ZZ 17), and no Wanter is needed. Your Wanter is also a Lord, and that Lord once ordered children killed (1 Sam 15:3), with a man as His instrument. The Way never sends anyone to kill in the Executioner's place (DDJ 74). On this point the child is safer under the Way than under the Lord.
- **Defense.** An equality the newborn shares with the rat's liver is not A4's equality of persons. The Way's equality does not stop killing at any number: the sage takes up weapons when he cannot avoid it (DDJ 31). He treats the people as straw dogs (DDJ 5, second half), objects used in a rite and then thrown away. The book pays the 1 Samuel cost openly (W1(2), the Kant row, the evil section) and forbids anyone now to claim such a command (CCC 67). And DDJ 74's Executioner kills everyone in his season. That is the book's own principle: the One who gives life does no wrong in calling it home, and we could only take it.
- **Rejoinder.** "Straw dogs" means impartiality, not use; Wang Bi reads it as Heaven and Earth simply not intervening (verify). Even so, I concede the Way gives no exceptionless line. But the book records openly that Kant protects her more firmly on divine command, and it does not record the same of the Way, which also never deputizes anyone to kill. That is a fairness gap in the Daoist row.
- **Held with repair** (R3).

**E3. The debt to the dead (W1(1)).**
- **Attack.** Laozi saw A3 and drew the opposite conclusion. A great resentment, once reconciled, still leaves resentment (DDJ 79), so prevent it and do not keep the ledger. Wanting *her* pardon is the self wanting its standing back, which is grasping again. And between those nearest, no apology is needed at all (ZZ 23).
- **Defense.** The text already calls W1(1) a clue, weighed only as much as the reader finds that a debt truly owed must be receivable. More importantly, DDJ 79 helps the book. The sage holds the *creditor's* tally and does not press, so the virtue of releasing a debt belongs to the one who is owed. That is A2's claim that the pardon is hers to give. When the creditor is dead, who holds the tally? Forgetting quiets the debtor and does nothing for her.
- **Rejoinder.** In the end both tallies are forgotten in the change; nothing is left asking. I accept this is parity: the two roads part over whether she is still there.
- **Held** (and DDJ 79 should be put to the book's use: R2, insights).

**E4. "Go on being herself" (W3).**
- **Attack.** The book's line between refusing change and keeping one's identity collapses. "Let her go on being herself, body and all" is exactly the metal leaping up in the forge and demanding to be made into a famous sword, the cry "only a man!" (ZZ 6). Zhuangzi welcomes becoming a rooster's leg or a rat's liver.
- **Defense.** The Christian hope is received, not demanded. It is the opposite of grasping (Gen 3:5; CCC 398). The text claims only "where that account stops," not proof. And Zhuangzi himself, when his wife died, first admits he could not help being moved, and only after reflecting drums on the basin (ZZ 18; verify). His grief is the love the Way does not answer.
- **Rejoinder.** The Daoist calls passing through grief to the drum maturity; the Christian calls it a loss. This is a difference in what is ultimately good. It is a standoff, and the text treats it as one.
- **Held.**

**E5. Ought from awareness (A5, W2).**
- **Attack.** Graham's "quasi-syllogism" closes the is–ought gap without a Lawgiver: be aware of everything relevant, and you will be moved one way rather than another (verify). Mind and world fit because both are the Way being so of itself. The Way models itself on ziran (DDJ 25). A Mind behind it is an extra posit.
- **Defense.** A5 says only that ability and desire do not settle the ought. Awareness is a third thing, and it already contains a norm, "be aware," which is A7. Graham treated that imperative as given by reason itself (verify). W2's question then returns: why does awareness track necessary truths at all? W2 says openly that where explanation stops is a judgment the rivals do not share.
- **Rejoinder.** Illumination is the Way being so of itself, not an obligation. But I concede it works as a norm. Parity.
- **Held.**

**E6. Perspectives and the conclusion's test.**
- **Attack.** Who can judge between us (ZZ 2)? "Which are you surer of?" is asked from the human standpoint. That standpoint would produce certainty that the infant was wronged whether or not it is true from the Way, so it fails A's own debunking test. From the Way, no one lives longer than a child who dies in infancy.
- **Defense.** A's test applies to every standpoint, the Way-standpoint included. Zhuangzi calls his own words wild and asks to be heard wildly (verify). If he takes back the human standpoint by "walking two roads," he grants the human judgment its rightness, and the conclusion's question stands. He also diagnoses the exposure of the deformed infant as the work of a heart that ranks useful above useless, so he concedes that something happened which ought not to have. The conclusion's last step, that her worth "follows what she is," is one I accept. It was never meant to separate us from theism.
- **Rejoinder.** My "ought not" is not "wronged." Accepted as parity.
- **Held.**

**E7. Heaven's machine heart (the evil section).**
- **Attack.** "God permits no evil unless He draws a greater good from it" (CCC 324) is the calculating heart moved up to Heaven (ZZ 12). And the One who could have kept her, and did not, now says "no" to what was done to her.
- **Defense.** The book's claim is narrower: she is not spent as a means, and the good is offered to her. Permission is not willing the loss for its own sake (ST I q.19 a.9). The text puts the whole weight on the other side of the scale and admits my school bears it more easily.
- **Rejoinder.** It bears it more easily at the price the text names: no one keeps her. That is honest parity.
- **Held.**

**E8. A7 against the Way's "daily loss."**
- **Attack.** Life has a limit and knowing has none; to chase the limitless with the limited is dangerous (ZZ 3). In pursuing the Way, something is dropped every day (DDJ 48). Duty to the truth without end is a fever.
- **Defense.** A7 forbids stopping *because we fear* what we will find. The sage stops because he sees limits, and A7 does not forbid that. And the Laozi has its own cost here: the ruler empties the people's hearts and keeps them without knowledge (DDJ 3, 65). The text could fairly press that.
- **Rejoinder.** In DDJ 65, yu means simple and unaffected, not deceived (Ivanhoe; verify). The point stands for Laozi's politics, though not for Zhuangzi.
- **Held.**

## 4. Ledger

**The A section**
- **A heading:** **Holds with repair.** The epistemic rule fits Zhuangzi's "going by the present 'this'" (yinshi) and survives E6. The list of deniers leaves out the Daoist denial of A3's last sentence; the text itself records that denial in W1(1) (R1).
- **A1:** **Holds.** My school condemns gratuitous cruelty whatever a culture approves; cultures' approvals are what corrupt. I decline the word "wrong," which is a vocabulary standoff.
- **A2:** **Holds.** Breaking a dying friend's trust is a breach of xin (trust), not only a flaw of character. Zhuangzi's claim that the nearest need no apology (ZZ 23) is a different ideal, not a refutation.
- **A3:** **Holds.** DDJ 79's opening supports the first half. Daoism denies the last sentence, the debt that outlives her, and that denial is answered at parity in W1(1).
- **A4:** **Holds.** Daoism accommodates the equality (ZZ 17, ZZ 5, DDJ 62, 76). It lacks the exceptionless bar and declines "dignity." The text labels the Church-only claims honestly.
- **A5:** **Holds.** Graham's awareness is neither ability nor desire, so A5 as stated stands (E5).
- **A6:** **Holds.** The first half fits DDJ 81, where giving makes the giver richer. My school agrees with refusing the surgeon (the machine heart, DDJ 74) but keeps no rule at every number. The Church-only claims are labelled.
- **A7:** **Holds.** It is compatible with the sage's "daily loss" because of its clause about fear (E8).

**The W section**
- **W1 frame:** **Holds.** Aquinas's point that God is offended only because we act against our own good (SCG III.122) is congenial to my school.
- **W1(1):** **Holds** at parity as a clue. The use of Zhuangzi (forgetting praise and blame) is accurate (ZZ 6). It would gain from DDJ 79 (E3).
- **W1(2):** **Holds** as the book's own account. The 1 Samuel cost is paid openly. It does not note that the Way also never deputizes a killing (R3, placed in the C row).
- **W1 closing ("This Lawgiver is not a commander"):** **Holds.** It fits ZZ 8's binding without cords, read as a law on the heart.
- **W2:** **Holds with repair.** It honestly says where explanation stops. "Laozi … denies that it is a law" misses DDJ 25, where the Way is a model that things follow; the precise point is that it cannot be named or laid down (R4).
- **W3, first paragraph:** **Holds.** The forge passage is accurate. It is a standoff about value (E4).
- **W3, second paragraph (why honest seekers do not find Him):** **Holds.** Daoism is not named. The admission that this is "part of an answer" is honest.

**The C rows**
- **Nothing really binds:** **Holds.** There is no Daoist stake.
- **Feeling, refined:** **Holds.** There is no Daoist stake.
- **Our best words, widening:** **Holds.** It is close to Hansen's reading of Zhuangzi, and the treatment is fair.
- **Reason's own law:** **Holds.** It pays its cost openly, which is the model for R3.
- **What we owe each other:** **Holds.** There is no Daoist stake.
- **Care between persons:** **Holds.** The "mother's child" point has a Daoist counterpart in the Way as mother (DDJ 52).
- **Well-being, counted impartially:** **Holds.** Daoism agrees against calculating totals (ZZ 12).
- **The flourishing of our kind:** **Holds.**
- **Necessary truths that belong to no one:** **Holds.**
- **An impersonal sacred order:** **Holds with repair.** Sima Qian quotes both halves of the saying, that Heaven's Way has no favorites and is always with the good. The text omits "has no favorites," the Daoist half (R5).
- **The Way that gives and does not claim:** **Holds with repair.** The quotations are accurate (DDJ 5, 10, 18/38, 27, 49, 51, 63, 67, 74, 79; ZZ 6). Its faults:
  - DDJ 55 alone, the newborn as an image of virtue's power, is a thin basis for A4.
  - "no one asks pardon and no one gives it" leaves out ZZ 23's distinction: one begs a stranger's pardon in the market, and the nearest need none.
  - It omits the Way's real strengths on A4 (ZZ 17, ZZ 5, DDJ 62, 76).
  - It omits the Way's real costs: DDJ 5's second half, the sage treating people as straw dogs, and DDJ 3/65 against A7.
  - It omits the cost the book pays the Way on divine command (R2, R3).
- **God, or Nature:** **Holds.**
- **The Buddha's Dhamma:** **Holds.**
- **History and class:** **Holds.** Zhuangzi's line that the thief of a hook is executed while the thief of a state becomes a lord with benevolence at his gate (ZZ 10) is a Daoist parallel.
- **Power and rank:** **Holds.**
- **Freedom that makes its values:** **Holds.**
- **A God who is Love:** **Holds.** "The nameless Way … stop[s] at one" is true of Laozi and contestable for Zhuangzi, who argues that "one" cannot even be said without making two (ZZ 2). This is minor (R6).

**The evil section**
- **First paragraph ("On the other side of the scale…"):** **Holds.** "A Way that is not humane … owes no one an answer" is fair. It could add Zhuangzi's answer to the child who dies young (R7).
- **"First" (evil has nothing of its own):** **Holds.** The aside on Zhuangzi, that his yes is to the changes of life and death, not to the tyrant, is fair. His stance toward tyrants in ZZ 4 is more evasion than condemnation, which is minor.
- **"Second" (permission is not a trade):** **Holds** (E7).
- **"Third" (God suffers with us; the hopes of other accounts):** **Holds.** The list of hopes omits the Daoist hope, the return to the root, which is minor. The invitation to the one who protests is fair to my school, which also condemns the cruelty.

**The conclusion**
- **Holds.** It claims less than CCC 31. Its final step is one my school accepts, and it does not pretend that step favors theism.

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**"As written: NONE."**

Every Daoist attack I could press at full strength ends at parity, and the text has already declared each of those points a judgment, a clue or a weight:
- the personal versus the impersonal source;
- whether being "wanted" adds anything to equal worth;
- whether keeping one's identity is grasping;
- the ledger as a symptom of the lost Way.

The strongest Daoist point is that the Lord who wants each one is also the Lord who once commanded a slaughter, while the Way never deputizes anyone. That cost is already paid openly in W1(2), in the Kant row and in the evil section. What is missing is only that the Daoist row should record it, which is a fairness gap, not unsoundness.

**Minor**
- **M1.** The Way row does not record that the Way, like Kant, never deputizes a killing (E2; R3).
- **M2.** The Way row rests its A4 support on DDJ 55 and misses the stronger texts (ZZ 17, ZZ 5, DDJ 62, 76) (R1-row).
- **M3.** "no one asks pardon and no one gives it" flattens ZZ 23: the Daoist outgrows the ledger rather than simply abolishing it (R2).
- **M4.** The A heading's list of deniers omits Zhuangzi's denial of A3's last sentence (R1).
- **M5.** The book leaves unused weapons against my school: DDJ 5's second half (the sage treats people as straw dogs, a cost on A4) and DDJ 3/65 (keeping people without knowledge, a cost on A7). This is a missed strength, not a defect.

**Misstatements of my school**
- **S1.** "Laozi grants one Source and denies that it is a law" (W2). DDJ 25 makes the Way the model that Heaven follows; what Laozi denies is that it can be named or laid down (DDJ 1, 38) (R4).
- **S2.** Sima Qian's citation of DDJ 79 drops "has no favorites," the half of the saying that is most Daoist (R5).
- **S3.** "The nameless Way … stop[s] at one" fits Laozi but not Zhuangzi, who doubts "one" can be said (ZZ 2) (R6).
- **S4.** "Zhuangzi's yes … not to the tyrant's cruelty, which his Way condemns." This slightly overstates ZZ 4, where the counsel is to fast the heart and avoid the tyrant rather than oppose him. It can be tolerated.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- Whether personal wanting adds to the explanation of equal worth.
- Whether the Way's being so of itself, or a Mind, best explains the fit between mind and law (W2).
- Whether a debt that cannot be received is still a debt (W1(1)).
- Whether keeping one's self through change is fulfilment or grasping (W3).
- Whether the ledger words are ultimate or symptoms (E1).
- The evil weighting: my school bears it more easily and keeps no one.

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact old → new)

**R1 (A heading).**
- Old: "and McMahan, no utilitarian, that killing a newborn wrongs her as gravely as killing a grown man;"
- New: "and McMahan, no utilitarian, that killing a newborn wrongs her as gravely as killing a grown man; Zhuangzi, that the debt outlives her (A3);"

**R1-row (Way row, A4 support).**
- Old: "It sees much of A: the cruelty of rulers, the newborn as the image of whole virtue, a sage who abandons no one, is good to the good and to the not-good alike, and holds compassion as his first treasure (A1, A4; *Daodejing* 27, 49, 67), and that to kill in the Executioner's place is to cut in the master carpenter's place (A6)."
- New: "It sees much of A: the cruelty of rulers; that the soft and weak are the companions of life and the hard and strong the companions of death, so that the newborn is its image of whole virtue (*Daodejing* 55, 76); that, seen from the Way, no thing is noble or base (*Zhuangzi* 17), and the maimed and misshapen may be fuller in virtue than the whole (*Zhuangzi* 5); a sage who abandons no one, not even the one who is not good, who is good to the good and to the not-good alike, and holds compassion as his first treasure (A1, A4; *Daodejing* 27, 49, 62, 67); and that to kill in the Executioner's place is to cut in the master carpenter's place (A6). It pays for this on A4 too: Heaven and Earth treat all things as straw dogs, and so, it says, does the sage treat the people (*Daodejing* 5)."

**R2 (Way row, ledger).**
- Old: "It meets a grievance by not keeping the ledger: the sage holds the creditor's tally and does not press for payment, and answers resentment with virtue (*Daodejing* 79, 63), and Zhuangzi would forget both praise and blame; so in the end no one asks pardon and no one gives it (A2, A3), and it lets the dead be transformed (W3)."
- New: "Laozi grants that when a great resentment is reconciled, some resentment remains (*Daodejing* 79; A3). So it meets a grievance by not keeping the ledger: the sage holds the creditor's tally and does not press for payment, and answers resentment with virtue (*Daodejing* 79, 63), and Zhuangzi would forget both praise and blame. One begs pardon for treading on a stranger's foot in the market, Zhuangzi says, but between those nearest nothing need be said (*Zhuangzi* 23): the ledger is not settled but outgrown, as fish left together on dry land keep each other wet, and would do better to forget each other in the rivers and lakes (*Zhuangzi* 6). So in the end no one asks pardon and no one gives it (A2, A3), and it lets the dead be transformed (W3). This book agrees that the tally is the creditor's to hold, hers and no one else's (A2); it parts over whether she is still there to hold it (W1(1))."

**R3 (Way row, cost paid openly).**
- Old: "But He remains its Lord (CCC 2280), and there the Way parts from Him most plainly: it rears and does not rule (*Daodejing* 10, 51)."
- New: "But He remains its Lord (CCC 2280), and there the Way parts from Him most plainly: it rears and does not rule (*Daodejing* 10, 51). On one point the Way, like Kant, protects her more firmly than this book does: it never sends anyone to kill in the Executioner's place, while this book holds with St. Thomas that the Lord of life once could (W1(2)). On the line itself it is weaker: it keeps no rule, for rules, it says, come when the Way is lost (*Daodejing* 38), and its sage takes up weapons when he cannot avoid it (*Daodejing* 31), so it holds the line only as a sage's heart holds it."

**R4 (W2 parenthesis).**
- Old: "Laozi grants one Source and denies that it is a law."
- New: "Laozi grants one Source, which Heaven follows as it follows what is so of itself (*Daodejing* 25), and denies that it can be named or laid down as a law (*Daodejing* 1, 38)."

**R5 (sacred-order row).**
- Old: "against the old saying that the Way of Heaven is always with the good (*Daodejing* 79)"
- New: "against the old saying that the Way of Heaven has no favorites and is always with the good (*Daodejing* 79)"

**R6 (A God who is Love).**
- Old: "Against the sacred order, the nameless Way and the God of Spinoza, which also stop at one,"
- New: "Against the sacred order, the nameless Way and the God of Spinoza, which also stop at one (though Zhuangzi doubts that even 'one' can be said, *Zhuangzi* 2),"

**R7 (evil section, first paragraph).**
- Old: "A Way that is not humane gives life and lets each thing go, and owes no one an answer."
- New: "A Way that is not humane gives life and lets each thing go, and owes no one an answer; Zhuangzi can even say that no one lives longer than the child who dies in infancy (*Zhuangzi* 2)."

## 7. New insights for the book; lines worth keeping

**Insights**
1. **"Bound without cords" (ZZ 8).** The canonical definition says covenant "binds." Zhuangzi's challenge is that what is truly united holds without glue or cords. The Catholic answer is near his own ideal: the New Law is written on the heart, not imposed from outside (Jer 31:33; CCC 1965–1966, 1972). Chapter 4 or 10 could meet him by name. This is a convergence, and it answers the charge that "binding" means compulsion.
2. **DDJ 79 is a non-theist witness for A3 and A2.** Reconciliation after a great wrong always leaves something behind, and the release belongs to the one who holds the creditor's tally. That is exactly W1(1)'s claim that the pardon is hers to give.
3. **DDJ 74 parallels W1(2)'s principle** that the One who gives life may call it home and we could only take it. The parallel has two edges, because the Way never deputizes, and that strengthens the case for R3.
4. **ZZ 12, the "machine heart."** It is a witness, more than two thousand years old, against the calculation of totals in A6, and it lies outside both the Church and Kant.
5. **ZZ 18, the sea bird of Lu.** The marquis fed the bird what would feed a man, and it died. Care must fit the nature of the one cared for. This serves the treatment of proportionate care in W1(2) (CCC 2278).
6. **ZZ 5 and ZZ 4.** The maimed sages and Shu the deformed, who survives by being useless, show that worth does not follow strength. They join the sacred order and the Dhamma as witnesses from outside Christianity (the Nietzsche row).
7. **Zhuangzi grieved before he drummed (ZZ 18).** W3 would be fairer, and stronger, if it said that Zhuangzi first admitted grief. The grief is what the book weighs.
8. **Religious Daoism.** Kohn and Terry Kleeman describe how the Celestial Masters revered the deified Laozi (Lord Lao) and had the sick write confessions of their faults to the Three Officials (verify). A tradition grown from the impersonal Way turned to personal powers to confess. Under the book's genealogy rule this can be only a clue, like the ancestral rites in W1(1).
9. **Costs the book could press but does not:** the second half of DDJ 5, and DDJ 3/65 against A7.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "Heaven and Earth give life to things; God wanted *you*."
- "Where we say God wanted *you*, it says the highest giving does not want."
- "Side effects may be weighed; persons may not be spent."
- "A truth can be true; only a person can forgive."
- "The rebel can take her side; he cannot keep her."
- "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."

## 8. Score

**8 / 10.** The text gets the Daodejing and Zhuangzi substantially right and concedes every real standoff as a judgment or a clue. Its faults against my school are omissions:
- it undersells the Way's strengths on A4;
- it does not record the cost it pays the Way on divine command, as it does for Kant;
- it misses the Way's own costs.

None of these is unsound. **Honest ceiling for any theistic case against Daoism: about 9.** A Daoist can always deny, without contradiction, that being personally wanted and kept is the highest good. That disagreement can be argued to parity but not to a win.
