# Blind review: the evolutionary debunker / error theorist

## 1. School and voice

I argue from the evolutionary debunking tradition and its metaethical conclusions: J. L. Mackie's error theory (moral claims presuppose objective, categorically binding values, and there are none), Richard Joyce's genealogical undermining (an adequate explanation of moral belief that never mentions moral facts leaves those beliefs unjustified unless there is independent support), Michael Ruse's "morality as an adaptation" view, Sharon Street's Darwinian dilemma (the realist must either cut moral truth off from the forces that shaped our values and accept skepticism, or claim those forces tracked the truth and lose to the simpler adaptive explanation), and the cognitive science of religion (agency detection, intuitive dualism, teleological bias, belief in moralizing high gods, the sense of being watched). I am not a nihilist about living well. I hold that nothing is *objectively* owed, and that the confidence the text leans on is what our history would have produced whether or not anyone is behind it.

## 2. Answers to the four standing challenges

**1. Starting point for justice.** Mackie: justice is a human device, conventions of mutual forbearance and fair dealing that make cooperation possible among creatures whose sympathy is limited. Street: what anyone is owed is what follows, under full reflection and consistency, from the standpoint of beings who already value fairness, reciprocity and one another. *Verdict:* coherent, and it borrows no objective "owed." It **breaks** at the edge: it can say nothing binding to a fully consistent agent whose starting values differ (Street accepts the "ideally coherent Caligula"). I accept that cost.

**2. A hard dilemma, resolved.** Case: a competent man, dying in pain, asks his doctor to end his life. Method: list the attitudes on the table (relief of his suffering, respect for his own valuing of his life, protection of vulnerable people from pressure, the doctor's aversion to killing). Then discount any attitude that, on scrutiny, tracks a feature we ourselves count irrelevant, such as Joshua Greene's "personal force" alarm (verify), and ask what the remaining, mutually consistent attitudes endorse: legal assisted death under strict safeguards. *Verdict:* **holds** as a method. It uses honesty, fairness and relief of suffering as human commitments, not as the book's truth, justice, mercy and self-gift, which are grounded in God. It **borrows** in one place: "should discount" and "may not hide the evidence" carry a pull I can only treat as hypothetical (if you want to see clearly). The text's A7 bites exactly here.

**3. Equal, unconditional dignity.** I deny it in the text's sense: no one, newborn or genius, has a worth that would hold if no valuer existed. What I can give is a status: equal standing that we confer, defend and build into institutions, and which consistency extends to every human being once we admit that the features we care about in ourselves are shared. *Verdict:* that **holds** for the healthy and the wanted. It **breaks** for the infant no one is drawn to hold and for the permanently unconscious. Consistency carries regard only as far as the attitudes it starts from, and many coherent standpoints (Seneca's, and Singer's and McMahan's today) give such beings less. I admit it.

**4. Ultimate flourishing.** Nothing is *ultimately* good in the text's sense. The best available end is lives that go well by the reflective lights of those who live them, in communities of trust, honesty and solidarity that let people stand to one another as equals. Guiding principles: reduce cruelty, keep faith, tell the truth, widen the circle. *Verdict:* **coherent** as a project, and it **breaks** only if "ultimately" is required. It promises the dead nothing, and I do not pretend it does.

## 3. Debate highlights

**Exchange 1: the A heading's test for defeating a belief.**
*Attack.* A sets its test as: trust a reflective belief until the best account of what produced it "would have produced it whether or not it was true." The text holds that its moral truths are necessary (W1: "wrong of necessity"). For a necessary truth, "had it been false" is a counterpossible. On the standard semantics every such conditional comes out vacuously true, so every necessary belief, logic included, is debunked. On non-standard semantics none is. Either way the test does no work. The literature's live tests are explanatory ones: does the belief's truth figure in the best explanation of why we hold it (Harman, Joyce)? Or does our method stay reliable across the starting values we might easily have had (Street)?
*Reply.* The problem is shared. Every account that holds necessary truths of logic or number faces it, the debunker included, since he trusts modus ponens. The text's intent is plainly the explanatory reading, and nothing later depends on the vacuous one.
*Rejoinder.* Agreed. But the test should be stated in the form that does the work, because the Nietzsche row relies on it (Exchange 7).
**Held with repair** (R1).

**Exchange 2: A6 and the "alarm."**
*Attack.* Greene's work suggests that the certainty against pushing the one to save five is set off by personal force and contact, features nobody thinks matter morally. The text answers that "an alarm that tracks that difference would sound just so if the difference were real." That is the wrong conditional. By the text's own test the question is whether the alarm would sound *if the difference were not real*, and the answer is yes.
*Reply.* The next clause carries the weight: "the regard for the many that would spend the one has a history too, in sympathy learned among kin." A selective debunking of deontology needs a normative premise saying which features matter. That is Selim Berker's point against Greene (verify), and Guy Kahane's point is that debunking cuts against consequentialist intuitions too (verify). "A history settles neither" is correct.
*Rejoinder.* Yes. The parity point and the Berker point stand. The first clause is a misstep that a trained reader will notice.
**Held with repair** (R2).

**Exchange 3: A2, guilt as a repair emotion.**
*Attack.* Robert Trivers and Robert Frank explain guilt as an adaptation that moves us to repair cooperative bonds and makes our commitments credible (verify). An emotion built for dyadic repair would face a person ("I wronged *her*") and seek her pardon, whether or not any wronging in the realist's sense exists. Guilt toward the dead friend misfires the way grief does.
*Reply.* A2 is not a claim about the feeling. It is about a distinction reflection finds: making things worse versus wronging, the secret blown into the street versus the secret told. Realists, Kantians, contractualists and Darwall's second-person theorists all draw that distinction from argument, not from twinges. And W1(1) already concedes the genealogy: "a mind made for living bonds would ask whether or not she hears."
*Rejoinder.* The second-person structure of moral concepts is itself what a dyadic, cooperation-tracking psychology would produce. Still, the reply is right that A2 is offered as a reflective seeming, and its fate is the fate of A as a whole.
**Held.** It reduces to the general standoff (Exchange 6).

**Exchange 4: the Darwinian dilemma against W2's "one Source."**
*Attack.* W2 explains the fit between our moral minds and moral truth by one Source. That is a pre-established harmony, and the data do not favor it. Where conscience fits survival, the text credits wisdom. Where conscience fails (kin first, the slave last), it credits sin. The evil section admits "a world with no one behind it predicts it without help." A hypothesis that only accommodates is beaten by one that predicts. Self-correction "toward those who can never repay" is also predicted: consistency reasoning is a general capacity that paid its way in logic, and applied to attitudes it carries "only our attitude." Darwin's bees, who would reason consistently toward a sacred duty of fratricide, are the text's own example.
*Reply.* For a theist the harmony is not ad hoc. A good Creator has an independent motive to make creatures who can know the good. Original sin is a teaching older than Darwin, not a patch made for this argument. The text claims no more than "a judgment about where explanation may stop," and grants that neither of its two answers to the debunker settles the matter. On fit, the theist does better than the secular realist, who must call the harmony a brute coincidence or appeal to a "third factor." The fit to be explained also covers logic and number, and Justin Clarke-Doane argues that our grasp of mathematical necessity is in the same position as moral grasp (verify): survival explains why we believe what holds of lions and cliffs, not why we grasp what holds of necessity.
*Rejoinder.* Clarke-Doane's parity is the theist's best tool here, and the text could use it more (Section 7). The theistic harmony is only as credible as theism on other grounds, so W2 cannot do much independent lifting against me. The text says as much.
**Held** as an honest standoff. The text does not overclaim.

**Exchange 5: A7, the companion-in-guilt argument.**
*Attack.* "Follow the evidence" needs no categorical reason. Belief aims at truth by its nature (Shah and Velleman on transparency, verify), so anyone deliberating about whether *p* is already held to the evidence. The text's cost is no cost.
*Reply.* The constitutive aim governs *beliefs*. A7 is about *inquiry*: "we may not stop looking because we fear what we will find." Inquiry is an action, governed by practical reasons. The aim of belief tells you which belief is correct. It does not tell the frightened man that he ought to open the next file. On error theory, the man who stops looking does nothing he ought not do in the full sense. The error theorist also asks the reader to give up beliefs "because the evidence requires it."
*Rejoinder.* Correct, and I accept it. Jonas Olson accepts an error theory of categorical epistemic reasons. Bart Streumer holds that the full error theory is true but cannot be believed (both verify). My argument to the reader is hypothetical in force: if you want to believe truly, here is what follows. That is a real cost, and the text names it fairly as "a cost some error theorists accept." No theistic argument makes the frightened man look either. What remains disputed is whether he *fails* at something.
**Held.** Standoff at parity, fairly stated.

**Exchange 6: the Moorean turn on A4 against historical contingency.**
*Attack.* The conclusion asks which you are surer of: that the unheld infant was wronged, or any argument that says otherwise. But the text itself records that Seneca, who wrote that slaves are men, wrote calmly that weak newborns are drowned. Confidence in A4 is local to a history. Street's contingency point is that a reader raised as Seneca would be as sure the other way. A confidence the debunker can explain cannot count as independent evidence against him.
*Reply.* Three points. (i) Seneca's view was inconsistent with his own premise, and reflective revision has run one way, toward the infant. (ii) The debunking argument also runs on confidence: in specific adaptive and cultural stories, many of them speculative, and in an asymmetry claim (that logic was selected for truth but evaluation was not) which Clarke-Doane contests. A7 asks the reader to weigh honestly, and the honest weighing is the one the conclusion describes. (iii) The infant "no one is drawn to hold" is the hardest case for the debunker, because the belief runs *against* the adaptive instinct (the text: "where instinct fails"). So his genealogy must lean on culture plus consistency, which is a weaker and more disputed story than "selection installed it."
*Rejoinder.* (i) is answered by Darwin's bees: consistency from a different base converges elsewhere. (iii) is real. And I cannot honestly say that I, or most readers, am surer of the premises of any debunking argument than that the infant was wronged. Joyce's argument needs only that *some* truth-free explanation is adequate, but its premise that the explanation is adequate is itself a judgment with less certainty than A4 for most people.
**Held.** This is the deep standoff, and the text names it as a choice, not a proof.

**Exchange 7: the Nietzsche row's "assuming the answer."**
*Attack.* The row says a genealogy defeats a belief only if it "would have produced it whether or not it was true," and since, if God exists, a belief in equal worth that grew from belief in His love "was answering to what is there," "the genealogy can defeat A4 only by assuming the answer." That protects A4 with God's existence inside an argument *for* God. My argument does not assume naturalism. It assumes parsimony: the truth-free explanation is adequate, and the theistic one adds nothing we can detect (the evil section grants that conscience falls where survival puts it). A genealogy does not show A4 false. It shows that A4's history gives it no support, so A4 must stand on something else.
*Reply.* Read charitably, the sentence is a parity claim: a genealogy alone cannot decide between the accounts. A4 already says so, and A4 locates the remaining weight in how sure we are.
*Rejoinder.* Then the sentence should say that, and not imply that the debunker begs the question.
**Held with repair** (R1b).

**Exchange 8: cognitive science of religion against the converging case.**
*Attack.* The case calls itself "converging" (CCC 31; Newman in the conclusion). But W1(1) (persons persist after death, the dead hear), W3 (an all-knowing Other who sees us whole, Smith's "all-seeing Judge"), and W2's framing (accident or *wisdom*) map onto three documented biases: intuitive dualism and afterlife intuitions (Bering, Bloom), the sense of supernatural watchers, and teleological bias (Kelemen) (all verify). Witnesses with a common cause in one cognitive profile do not converge. They count once.
*Reply.* The text already demotes W1(1) and W3 to "clues of fit, not evidence," concedes the scientific explanation of each, and does not stack them. W1(2) and the necessity part of W2 do not rest on these biases. Theism also predicts a built-in disposition to seek God, so the correlation is expected on both hypotheses and does not favor naturalism.
*Rejoinder.* Agreed on all three points. One sentence saying the clues may share a source and are counted once would block the double-counting charge before it is made.
**Held with repair** (R4).

**Exchange 9: evil and the pattern of pain.**
*Attack.* Pain in nature, and the warmth of conscience, fall where survival puts them.
*Reply.* The text says exactly this, unprompted, and adds that the pattern "still weighs" and that the hope for the fawn "is thinner."
**Held.** I have nothing to add that the text has not conceded.

## 5. Remaining sound objections against the text AS WRITTEN

**As written: NONE.**

Every claim the text makes against my school is either conceded where I am strong (the scientific explanation of guilt, of longing, of speaking to the dead, of the pattern of pain and of conscience) or pressed only to parity (A4's Moorean weight, A7's cost, W2's judgment about where explanation stops). In each case the text states plainly that it does not settle the question.

**Minor items**
1. *A heading:* the test for defeating a belief is a counterfactual, vacuous or useless for the necessary truths the text claims (Ex. 1).
2. *A6:* "would sound just so if the difference were real" is the wrong conditional by the text's own test (Ex. 2).
3. *Power and rank:* "the genealogy can defeat A4 only by assuming the answer" implies the debunker begs the question. His premise is parsimony (Ex. 7).
4. *Converging form:* W1(1), W3 and W2's teleological framing may share one source in our cognition. That should be said, so the clues are not counted twice (Ex. 8).

**Misstatements of my school**
1. *W1(1) parenthetical:* "The error theorist shares this book's premise that a debt owed in the full sense needs someone to receive it." He does not. His error lies in the presupposition of objective, categorical authority, and "owed" is in error of the living as much as the dead. Whether a receiver exists plays no part.
2. *C, Nothing really binds:* "the newborn has no worth except what we are built and taught to give her" describes Street's constructivism. For Mackie and Joyce, no one has worth in the objective sense, and the newborn's lack is not special to her. The text's real point, that projected regard is weakest for the infant no one holds, is correct and should stay.

**Honest standoffs at parity (not defects)**
- How sure the reader is of A4 versus the debunker's premises (adequacy of a truth-free explanation, parsimony). The text calls this a choice, and it is.
- Whether giving up categorical epistemic reasons, above all a duty to keep inquiring, is a cost (A7).
- Where explanation of the mind–law fit may stop: a theistic harmony, a secular third factor, or "there is no fit to explain."
- Whether the religious dispositions found by cognitive science are designed or byproducts. Both hypotheses predict them.

## 6. Proposed repairs (exact old → new)

**R1 (A heading).**
Old: "or shows that the best account of what produced the belief would have produced it whether or not it was true."
New: "or shows that its truth plays no part in the best account of why we hold it."

**R1b (C, Power and rank).**
Old: "the story of how a belief arose defeats it only if that story would have produced it whether or not it was true; and if there is a God who loves each one, a belief in equal worth that grew from belief in His love was answering to what is there. So the genealogy can defeat A4 only by assuming the answer to the question this case asks;"
New: "the story of how a belief arose defeats it only if its truth plays no part in the best account of why we hold it; and if there is a God who loves each one, a belief in equal worth that grew from belief in His love was answering to what is there. So the genealogy alone cannot show A4 false; at most it shows that A4's history lends it no support, and then its weight lies where A4 and the conclusion put it, in how sure we are of it;"

## 7. New insights; lines worth keeping

**Insights for the book**
- **A7 is stronger as a claim about inquiry than about belief.** The error theorist's best escape is that belief aims at truth by its nature. That governs which belief is correct, not whether one must keep looking. A7's wording ("we may not stop looking because we fear what we will find") already gets this right. One clause in W2 or the C row could say why: looking is something we *do*, and the aim of belief does not command it.
- **The theist's best ally against Street is parity with mathematics.** Clarke-Doane argues (verify) that the debunker's asymmetry (logic paid its way by being true, morality either way) is weakest exactly where the text puts its weight: the grasp of *necessary* truth, in number as in right. W2 already names "logic and number as well as of right." Saying so explicitly would turn a concession ("logic paid its way") into a challenge.
- **The infant no one is drawn to hold is the debunker's hardest case, and the text should know why.** The belief runs against the adaptive instinct, so the genealogy has to rely on culture plus consistency, which is a more speculative story than "selection installed it." The text has found this case. It could say in one line why the case matters.
- **Against Greene,** the standard reply is Berker's: a debunking of the means/side-effect line needs a normative premise saying which features matter (R2 builds it in).
- **On fit, the theist beats the secular realist, not the debunker.** The text's honesty here ("a judgment about where explanation may stop") is the right posture. It should not be strengthened into a claim of victory.

**Lines worth keeping**
- "That guilt asks proves nothing, for a mind made for living bonds would ask whether or not she hears."
- "Against him the case rests on two things, and neither settles it."
- "though a world with no one behind it predicts it too."
- "Darwin's bees are the reminder that minds could have been tuned otherwise."
- "So the belief's history does not decide between the accounts; its weight lies in how sure we are of it."
- "What rises in you then is not the source of her worth. It is your answer to it."

## 8. Score

**8 / 10.** Against my school the text concedes everything the science actually shows, presses only A4's Moorean weight and A7's cost on inquiry, and calls the result a choice, not a proof. Its defects are four local phrasings and two small misstatements, all repairable in a line.

**Honest ceiling for any theistic case against my school: about 8.** Our disagreement ends at parity: confidence that the unheld infant was wronged against confidence that a truth-free explanation suffices. No argument can push it past that. A theist can win the fit question against the secular realist, but not against me. This text, with the repairs, reaches that ceiling.
