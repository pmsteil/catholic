### Points of greatest weight, reviewed and found sound

- **Voluntarism**: none. The text states repeatedly that God does not make a wrong right by willing it (W1, W1(2)), and grounds the law in God's wisdom and goodness (VS 36, 40–43). The only wording risk is finding 8.
- **God using persons as means**: the text expressly denies it (GS 24; SCG III.112). It honestly faces the tension with ST I q.23 a.5 ad 3 (see finding 4).
- **St. Thomas** is quoted and paraphrased accurately (I-II q.94 a.5 ad 2; q.100 a.8 body and ad 3; II-II q.64 a.6 ad 1). The Thomist conclusion is correctly bounded: no one may now claim such a command (CCC 67).
- **Forgiveness, satisfaction, reparation**: sound in substance (CCC 1441, 1459–1460, 1472–1473, 2412, 2487; Trent XIV can. 12). Finding 2 is about wording only.
- **Hell, grace, freedom**: sound. Hell is real and eternal; God predestines no one to hell (CCC 1037); grace can be resisted (Trent VI ch. 5, can. 4); God commands nothing impossible (ch. 11); the de auxiliis question is left open (Paul V, 1607); Mary is free of every sin by grace (CCC 490–494); there is no repentance after death (CCC 393, 1021); no human being is declared lost (John Paul II, 1999).
- **The unbaptized and non-Christians**: sound. The text follows LG 16, CCC 847–848, 1257, 1260–1261 and GS 22. It does not presume the culpability of unbelievers (Conclusion: LG 16; CCC 847, 1861). Its descriptions of Buddhism, Confucianism and Daoism are respectful and consistent with *Nostra Aetate* 2.
- **Inviolability of life, the vital-conflict case, euthanasia, the death penalty**: sound, subject to finding 3.
  - Direct killing of the innocent is excluded at every number and even at the person's request (EV 57, 62, 65–66; CCC 2258, 2277, 2280–2282, 2295; SB V.1).
  - Direct abortion is excluded even in the vital-conflict case. The treatment Pius XII allows (27 Nov 1951) is correctly distinguished.
  - Legitimate defense is correctly set apart (CCC 2263–2265).
  - Ordinary care, including food and water, is stated exactly as in SB V.3 ("provided that it does not cause harm or intolerable suffering").
  - Pain relief that may shorten life is stated correctly (CCC 2279).
  - The death penalty is stated as inadmissible (CCC 2267, as revised in 2018).
  - The paragraphs on suicide are pastorally correct (CCC 2282–2283).
- **Opinion and doctrine**:
  - *Spe Salvi* 47 is correctly marked as an opinion of "some recent theologians."
  - "This book hopes…" passages are marked as hope.
  - The Thomist reading of 1 Samuel 15 should be marked as permitted opinion (finding 1b).
  - Natural-law norms should not be marked as mere premises (finding 3).
- **Reason's reach**: the Form paragraph and the Conclusion correctly affirm CCC 31, 35–36 and *Dei Filius* ch. 2 and can. 2.1. "This case claims less" is a legitimate modesty about this particular argument, because the text also affirms that reason can reach certainty. Findings 6 and 7 tighten two places where the wording could be read as underclaiming.
- **God's attributes**:
  - Impassibility is kept: "nothing harms God" (Job 35:6–8; SCG III.122); "God cannot suffer, but He can suffer with" (*Spe Salvi* 39).
  - Creation is affirmed as free, from no need (GS 24; ST I q.19 a.3; q.44 a.4; CCC 293, 295).
  - On providence and evil, God is not the cause of moral evil (CCC 311; I-II q.79 a.2), and evil never becomes good (CCC 312, 324).

## 3 Verdict

**As it stands:** I would not yet sign. This is not because of any Error: I found nothing contrary to faith or morals. It is because findings 1–4 concern exactly the points a bishop's office reads most closely: Scripture's inerrancy and the commanded killings, the sufficiency of sacramental absolution, the natural-law status of the prohibitions on killing the innocent and on euthanasia, and the permission of damnation. In each of them, the current wording lets a careful reader take a sound position as something else.

**With the fixes:** yes. With findings 1–9 applied, or with equivalent wording, I would grant the *nihil obstat* to the whole text.
