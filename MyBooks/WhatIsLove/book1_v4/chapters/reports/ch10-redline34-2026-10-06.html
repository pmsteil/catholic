Each paragraph now opens by saying which three pillars are present, so its examples read as that one pillar missing. Truth: fair, forgiving and devoted, but won’t face the truth. Justice: honest, affectionate and forgiving, but the cost falls on a few. Mercy: honest, fair and hardworking, but never forgiving. Sacrifice: honest, fair and forgiving, but giving only what is convenient. I checked every example against that test, and they hold. The justice paragraph’s old framing (“conspiracy of the strong,” “absorbed by the powerless,” “devouring its own weaker members,” “some people using others”) described malice. That contradicted a family that has truth and mercy, so it now describes a burden left on one person. “Fair pay” and “honest dealing” become respect, fairness, gratitude and care.

Unity without justice

Section: Unity Requires Submission to the Authority of His Perfect Love · line 296

A unity without justice is a conspiracy of the strong. Where some members are treated unfairly—denied the fair pay, honest dealing, respect lives at someone else's expense. A family can be honest, affectionate and quick to forgive, and still not give each member the respect, fairness, gratitude or care that justice says is theirs—what looks like communion is really some people using others . Then the cost falls quietly on a few: the mother who cooks, cleans, drives and remembers every birthday while the family treats it as simply her job; the one sibling who cares for an aging parent alone while the others come for holidays; the child whose gifts are barely noticed beside a favored brother's; the marriage in which one spouse's needs are always sacrificed to the other's comfort. Such arrangements feel stable precisely because the cost is being silently absorbed by the powerless a home can feel warm and stable precisely because someone is silently absorbing the cost. But a body cannot be one while quietly devouring its own weaker members; the moment they can leave, they often do stay one while some members carry what all should share; sooner or later they give out, or they leave. …
