## Chapter 6 · Truth: God Is Truth

### Chapter assessment

The edited chapter is stronger than the earlier whole-book audit reflected. It now correctly calls truth the epistemological floor of love rather than the highest divine attribute; identifies Christ as the eternal Word while preserving Scripture as inspired; describes the Church as receiving rather than creating truth; preserves the Mass-before-written-Gospels insight; and no longer says legitimate authority becomes real only when voluntarily accepted. Its strongest passages unite natural reason, revelation, Christ and ecclesial communion without reducing truth to information.

The remaining stop-ship problems are concentrated rather than pervasive. Lying is still made the foundational sin behind every sin; inspiration is tied to the human author’s spiritual surrender; the Passion language needs the Church’s Christological and anti-Judaism safeguards; later paragraphs oppose personal truth to propositions, natural reason to revelation and physical truths to moral truths. The long apostolic-authority section contains several historically unequal founder/date claims, makes Matthias prove more than Acts states, accidentally says apostles wrote after their deaths, overstates uniform succession across ancient sees, and describes the whole Bible as written and authenticated by the Church without distinguishing the Old Testament or God’s authorship. The chapter’s Catholic claim can remain confident while becoming more exact, more ecumenically Catholic and more historically credible.

### CH6-01 · Do not make lying the root or necessary condition of every sin

**Severity:** Stop-ship / doctrine of sin · **Control:** DC-10, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_06.md:39–50, 164–166`

**Issue.** Lying is a specific sin against truth. Every sin involves some disordered judgment of an apparent good, but Catholic tradition identifies disobedience and pride in the first sin and does not teach that the act of lying makes every other sin possible.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 397–398; CCC 1849–1851; CCC 2482–2487.

**Source passages with proposed inline redline:**

Every corruption of love downstream from here begins with ~~a lie~~ ++some refusal or distortion of truth++ — a lie about God, a lie about the human person, or a lie about love itself.

Satan is called <em>"the father of lies"</em> (John 8:44) because ~~every sin traces back to some falsehood believed or spoken. Lying is not merely one sin among many — it is the foundational sin that makes every other sin possible~~ ++his temptation draws us away from God’s truth. Lying is a grave and fertile offense against truth, while pride and disobedience are the deeper root dynamics by which the sinner prefers self to God++.

The Eighth Commandment prohibits not only direct lying but "every kind of act contrary to justice and truth" (CCC 2476). **Lying is ~~the~~ ++a++ foundational destroyer of love because love cannot ~~exist~~ ++remain rightly ordered++ without truth.**

**Editorial consequence.** The chapter can emphasize the generative power of lies without replacing the Church’s account of sin with a one-sin taxonomy.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

### CH6-05 · Do not derive the Church’s governing authority from an overstated etymology

**Severity:** Major / ecclesiology, language and reasoning · **Control:** DC-14, DC-20, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_06.md:87`

**Issue.** Greek *ekklesia* means an assembly or those called together; particular civic assemblies could exercise public authority, but “governing assembly” is not the word’s universal lexical definition. **Controlling sources:** CCC 751–752; CCC 857–865; Matthew 16:18–19; 18:18; 28:18–20.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

And to ensure that His truth would reach every generation intact, He established His Church. What is the Church? The word itself ~~tells us~~ ++helps us see part of the answer++. The Greek *ekklesia* did not first mean a building or a loose gathering of the like-minded. In the Greek world it was ~~the ordinary word for a city's **governing assembly**—the body of citizens *called out* to gather, deliberate, and decide~~ ++a word for an assembly, including the civic assembly of citizens called together for public business++. And in the Greek Bible the apostles carried, it was the word chosen to translate the Hebrew *qahal*: the covenant **assembly of Israel**, the people solemnly summoned *by God* to stand under His authority and receive His law—"the day of the assembly" at Sinai (Deut 9:10; cf. 4:10–13). Hold the two meanings together and they ~~say exactly what the Gospel means~~ ++illuminate the people Christ gathers around Himself++: in founding His *ekklesia*, Christ was reconstituting God's own covenant assembly around Himself—a holy ~~governing~~ assembly ~~with real authority to teach in His name~~ ++whose real teaching authority comes from Christ’s explicit commission++, charged with one sacred task: to receive, guard, and hand on everything He had commanded (cf. Matt 28:18–20; 2 Tim 1:14). An assembly summoned *by God* already stands under His authority—~~the word itself hints at what~~ Christ's own acts, as we are about to see, confirm ++the authority He entrusted to the apostles and their successors++. She is the pillar and foundation of truth (1 Tim 3:15), and Christ promised the Holy Spirit to guide her into all truth (John 16:13).

**Editorial consequence.** The etymology supports the image, while Scripture and apostolic institution bear the doctrinal claim.

### CH6-06 · State canon reception and the one deposit with historical precision

**Severity:** Major / canon and revelation · **Control:** DC-20, DC-28, DC-30 · **Location:** `chapter_06.md:89`

**Issue.** Hippo and Carthage were regional councils that witnessed and transmitted canonical lists; Trent dogmatically defined the canon amid a later controversy. “Two streams” is serviceable only if their unity is immediately controlling, but Scripture is not merely revelation written down: it is the inspired Word of God in writing, while Tradition transmits the Word entrusted to the apostles.

**Controlling sources:** *Dei Verbum* 8–10; CCC 80–83, 101–141; Council of Trent, Decree Concerning the Canonical Scriptures.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

And it was the Church, guided by that same Spirit, who received, recognized, and handed down the Scriptures — ~~gathering the 73 books Catholics still read today from the apostolic witness, ratifying them at the regional councils of Hippo (393) and Carthage (397), and defining the canon for the whole Church at Trent (1546)~~ ++receiving the forty-six books of the Old Testament and the twenty-seven of the New as inspired and canonical. Regional councils including Hippo and Carthage witnessed fourth-century canonical lists; the ecumenical Council of Trent later defined that canon dogmatically++. But a careful distinction matters: the Word of God is not first a book — He is a Person. "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14). Christ Himself is the eternal Word, and everything God has revealed reaches us through ~~two inseparable streams flowing from Him: **Sacred Scripture** (revelation written down) and **Sacred Tradition** (revelation handed on in the Church's teaching, sacraments, and life)~~ ++Sacred Scripture, the Word of God consigned to writing under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, and Sacred Tradition, by which the Word entrusted to the apostles is handed on in the Church’s doctrine, life and worship++ — *"one sacred deposit of the Word of God"* (*Dei Verbum* 10; cf. CCC 80–82).

**Editorial consequence.** The canon argument becomes historically and theologically exact without weakening the Church’s indispensable role.

### CH6-09 · Add Christological and anti-Judaism safeguards to the Passion passage

**Editorial consequence.** The rhetoric retains its force while becoming Chalcedonian, non-collectivizing and faithful to CCC 598.

### CH6-11 · Relate truth and authority without saying every definer of reality commands worship

**Severity:** Stop-ship / authority and idolatry · **Control:** DC-14, DC-27 · **Location:** `chapter_06.md:184–192`

**Issue.** Authorities make judgments within limited competence; they do not thereby “define reality” or command worship. Legitimate authority derives from God and is morally bounded by truth, right reason, human dignity and the common good. The current absolute formulation can make any institutional pronouncement sound divinely binding.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1897–1904; CCC 2104–2109; Acts 5:29.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

Once we see that every love bows to *something* as its ultimate reference point, an unavoidable question follows: *~~who gets to say what is true?~~ ++how do we recognize truth, and which authorities may teach or command within their proper competence?++* Truth and authority ~~cannot be separated — whoever defines reality commands our worship~~ ++are related but not identical. No human authority creates reality, and worship belongs to God alone++. If there is no authority higher than the self, then "my truth" becomes the final word, and we are back to idolatry by another name. So before we can speak rightly about truth, we have to speak rightly about authority — what it is, where it comes from, and why submitting to it is not the opposite of freedom but its foundation.

This means that any authority ~~claimed~~ ++rightly exercised++ by a person or institution is *borrowed*. Governments hold authority because God is the Author of social order (cf. Romans 13:1). Parents hold authority because God is the Author of the family. The Church holds authority because Christ—the Author made flesh—entrusted it to her. ++Each human authority is limited by its proper competence, the moral law, human dignity and the common good; an unjust command does not bind the conscience.++ Strip God away, and all authority collapses into raw power. Power can compel. It cannot bind. It cannot command love. Only truth can do that.

**Editorial consequence.** The section gains the Catholic limits needed to protect obedience from coercive misuse.

### CH6-12 · Keep Paul’s slavery metaphor while avoiding an unrestricted spirituality of enslavement

"For you were called to freedom, brethren; only do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh, but through love be servants of one another" (Galatians 5:13). ~~We are always slaves to something~~ ++Paul contrasts slavery to sin with willing obedience to God++—either to sin, which leads to death, or to God, which leads to life.

When we voluntarily submit to God's authority—grounded in truth and expressed through love—we become ~~slaves to Love itself. This divine service is freedom’s perfection++, because Love wills our good, knows our nature, and leads us to our true fulfillment. **Editorial consequence.** Paul’s paradox remains powerful without becoming a transferable warrant for domination.

### CH6-15 · State the hierarchy’s development and sacramental visibility more carefully

**Severity:** Major / Holy Orders and ecclesiology · **Control:** DC-14, DC-20, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_06.md:220`

**Issue.** The three degrees are divinely instituted and ancient, but New Testament terminology for *episkopoi* and *presbyteroi* is not yet used with complete later precision in every passage. **Controlling sources:** CCC 861–862; CCC 1536–1600, especially 1550, 1554–1556.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

From the apostolic age forward, the Church has been governed by a threefold hierarchy instituted by Christ through the apostles: **bishops** as successors of the apostles, **priests** as their co-workers in word and sacrament, and **deacons** as servants of the Church's charity and proclamation (cf. **Editorial consequence.** The passage still makes a confident Catholic claim without compressing historical development or sacramental mediation into identity.
