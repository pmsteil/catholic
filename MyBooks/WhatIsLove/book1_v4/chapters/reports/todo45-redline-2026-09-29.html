Section: Why Love Is So Hard to Define · line 103

Throughout history, philosophers and saints have offered profound insights—four of the Greek words for love—eros, philia, storge, and agape—each naming a different facet; Augustine's teaching that love is the ordering principle of all virtue; Aquinas's definition that "to love is to will the good of for another" and that love gives "form" to all other virtues. (Aquinas's Latin is precise: amare est velle alicui bonum. Alicui means for someone; the good is not only admired in another but willed for them. Chapter 5 shows how much that small word carries.) Every one of these is true, and we see by their light; everything that follows in this book is in their debt. …
