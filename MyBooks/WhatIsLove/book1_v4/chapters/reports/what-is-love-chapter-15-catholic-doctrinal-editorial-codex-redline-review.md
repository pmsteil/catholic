## Chapter 15 · Counterfeit Love in the Modern World

### Chapter assessment

Chapter 15 has a legitimate Catholic purpose: test cultural claims, defend objective truth and human dignity, oppose abortion and pornography, uphold sexual difference and marriage, reject atheistic collectivism, affirm Scripture–Tradition–Magisterium, insist that faith must live through charity, and call Christians to perseverance. It also occasionally acknowledges the genuine good that makes an error attractive.

In its current form, however, this chapter is a major obstacle to episcopal review. It treats amorphous labels (“postmodernism,” “wokeism,” “self-actualization”), distinct moral acts, schools of thought and separated Christian traditions as twelve unitary systems; assigns each motives and inevitable outcomes; and uses the author’s seven boxes as both definition and verdict. Catholic doctrine recognizes real natural virtue, affection, sacrifice, marriage-like commitment and truth outside explicit faith while locating their ultimate fulfillment in Christ. Catholic social doctrine teaches both personal sin and “structures of sin,” so “only individual hearts” is not Catholic. Gender distress, identity claims, social transition, puberty blockers, hormones and surgeries need separate moral and factual treatment; changing medical claims require current primary sources. The abortion section is especially unsafe: “easy way out,” universal guilt and shame, inability to love afterward, “post-abortion syndrome,” dismissal of therapists and mapping all seven deadly sins violate *Evangelium Vitae* 99’s pastoral standard. The religious sections caricature diverse Protestant teachings and ignore Vatican II’s acknowledgement of shared gifts and shared guilt for division; Benedict XVI explicitly describes a Catholic sense in which “faith alone” is true when faith is not opposed to charity. This chapter needs exact propositions, Catholic sources, distinctions, evidence and remedies—not softer convictions, but better Catholic reasoning.

### CH15-01 · Withdraw the claim that one missing component proves no love exists

**Severity:** Stop-ship / natural love and framework authority · **Control:** DC-01, DC-07 · **Location:** `chapter_15.md:17–27`

**Issue.** Fallen people can show real natural affection, sacrifice and virtue while love remains incomplete or disordered. A missing facet can reveal a defect in a claim without proving “incomplete love isn’t love at all” or that the author’s definition exhausts every act.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1804–1811, 1812–1829.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

We can test claims about love against Scripture, natural law and Church teaching, using the book’s definition as one subordinate lens. ~~If any component is missing or distorted, it's counterfeit—and counterfeits always cause harm. Incomplete love isn't love at all~~ ++A missing or disordered good may expose a counterfeit claim, but real though imperfect natural love can remain. The task is to identify the precise truth retained, the precise error introduced, the actual moral object and the harm supported by evidence++.

**Editorial consequence.** The chapter can expose counterfeits without denying every natural good in imperfect people and communities.
