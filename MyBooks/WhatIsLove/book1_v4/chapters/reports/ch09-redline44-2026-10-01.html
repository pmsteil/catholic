Anselm and Aquinas.

Further Study · Christian Thinkers

Yes, fully Catholic. Anselm is a canonized saint and a Doctor of the Church. He was a Benedictine monk and Archbishop of Canterbury in the 11th century, more than 400 years before the Church of England existed. Readers may hear “Canterbury” and think Anglican, so the entry now says who he was. While here: (1) Anselm and Aquinas both ended with the same phrase, “satisfying justice while offering mercy”; now each says something different. (2) “Expiatory and oblative” were hard words. Aquinas's entry now gives his actual teaching in plain words: the Passion was a true sacrifice and atonement (III q.48 aa.2–3), and the most fitting way to save us, first of all because it shows God's love (III q.46 a.3). That is also the Catholic nuance on Anselm, whose “necessity” Aquinas softened to “most fitting.”

- St. Anselm of Canterbury (1033-1109): Benedictine monk, archbishop and Doctor of the Church, writing centuries before the Reformation. His Cur Deus Homo (Why God Became Man) explains the necessity of Christ's sacrifice to satisfy justice while offering mercy why only the God-Man could make satisfaction for sin, so that justice and mercy meet in Christ

- St. Thomas Aquinas (1225-1274): His treatment of sacrifice in the Summa Theologiae shows how Christ's sacrifice is both expiatory and oblative, satisfying justice while offering mercy (III, qq. 46–48) shows that Christ's Passion was a true sacrifice and atonement, and that it was the most fitting way to save us because it shows us how much God loves us
