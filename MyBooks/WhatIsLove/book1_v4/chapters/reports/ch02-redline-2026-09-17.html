    1. Reason and revelation — the UPHOLD you marked highest priority

    Audit row: Major · 51, 75–86 · Claude · Uphold — highest priority in this chapter

    The error is not in the argument, it is in two labels. Natural reason reaches God's existence and certain of His attributes; that God is love in its full sense is Trinitarian, and revealed (CCC 237). Line 51 already has this right — "revelation confirms who that God is: Love itself" — so the chapter contradicts itself, and the wrong version is sitting in the two places a censor reads first: a section heading and a callout.

    Three touchpoints. The body prose at line 77 is correct as written ("The Bible reveals that God is love") and is left alone.

    Section heading, line 75

    > ## Scripture Confirms What Reason DiscoversWhat Reason Reaches, Revelation Completes

    Callout, line 86 — the whole callout, so the second sentence is shown unchanged for context.

    > What reason discovers, Scripture confirms: God is love.Reason can reach the highest good; only revelation names Him: God is love.

    > Love is the path to eternal life and the answer to evil.

    *Alternative heading if you want the turn harder: "Scripture Reveals What Reason Cannot Reach." I prefer the one above because the section really does argue continuity — reason gets to the highest good, revelation supplies who He is — and a heading that denies reason its reach overcorrects.*

    4. Nature and grace — lines 169 and 119

    Audit row: Major · 119, 155–169 · Claude · Uphold, narrowed to one sentence — narrowed by one line too many; see below

    The ruling this now applies

    Your 17 September ruling, in two stages:

    1. Nature. God writes the orientation toward the good into every soul, and it cannot be escaped. The atheist who loves his child, disciplines him in justice, forgives him in mercy and sacrifices to provide for him is really doing those things — aimed at goods that are perceived, limited and incomplete, but really doing them. Even the man who argues against God is orienting himself toward something he takes to be good and against something he takes to be evil.
    2. Grace. When the Source is recognised, God draws the person into His own love, reorienting them toward what is actually good — true justice, true mercy, true sacrifice — rather than the partial goods reason alone arrives at.

    This is already the book's position. Chapter 1 line 43 has "the natural law written on every human heart," with Romans 2:14-15 in its Further Study; Chapter 4 lines 105 and 217, Chapter 6 lines 61 and 73, and Chapter 7 line 39 all say it again. Chapter 7 line 67 states it almost exactly: not because non-believers can't recognize justice (natural law allows this), but because they can't ground it. So the two edits below are not a theological correction — they bring Chapter 2 into line with what four other chapters already say.

    4a. Line 169

    "Love must be sacred—or it is nothing at all" denies stage one outright: it makes love depend on the lover's recognition of its source, so the atheist's love for his child is not love. Stage two is where recognition changes things, and it changes them by drawing the person in, not by conferring reality on what was already there.

    Line 169

    > Every facet of love rests on a single foundation: love must be sacred—or it is nothing at all.is sacred—it comes from God, whether or not the one who loves knows it.

    4b. Line 119 — reinstated

    In the first build I flagged this as a departure from the triage and then advised holding it, because Chapter 3 carries the same unstaged language in eight places and a lone fix here would have made the two chapters disagree. Your ruling supplies the rule that sorts all of them, so it goes back in.

    The triage clears lines 119 and 155 together and justifies that by quoting *"if love doesn't come from God … everything else collapses"* — which is line 155's text, not line 119's. Line 119 reads:

    > Sacred: Love participates in the divine nature (1 John 4:8). Because every person is created in God's image, love is never merely emotion but participation in God's life.

    "Participation in God's life" is the vocabulary of sanctifying grace — stage two. The reason given for it is creation in God's image — stage one, and true of everyone. So the sentence rests a stage-two claim on a stage-one ground, which is the step that makes grace look redundant.

    Line 119

    > Sacred: Love participates in the divine nature (1 John 4:8).comes from God, who is Love (1 John 4:8).

    > Because every person is created in God's image, love is never merely emotion but participation in God's life.—the pull toward it is written into the soul and cannot be escaped.

    > And when we turn to its Source, He draws us into His own life, where our loving becomes a real sharing in His.

    *Three sentences where the other glosses run one or two. Cut the third if it reads heavy — lines 155 and 169 already carry the source claim, and Chapter 3 is where stage two gets unpacked.*

    What this sets up for Chapter 3

    The rule sorts Chapter 3's participation sentences mechanically. Stage-two sentences keep participates and shares in His life — lines 135 and 149 read correctly as they stand. Stage-one sentences need the other verb: reflects, images, is written into, comes from. Lines 128, 169 and 173 are in that group, as is the chapter_03.md:128 callout the audit upholds, which becomes something on the order of "All authentic human love reflects this eternal divine exchange, and by grace is drawn to share in it." I have not written any of that; it is the next pass, and §18 below records the rule so it is not re-argued cold.

    5. Line 57 — privation is of a due good

    Audit row: Argument · Claude · Split — the buried half is the good one (privation upheld, circularity downgraded)

    Privation is the absence of a good that is owed to that nature: a broken bone lacks soundness, blindness lacks sight. "The absent good is love" is true of the three spheres of moral evil Chapter 1 examined, and false as a general law of privation — which is what "this follows from the very logic of privation" claims. The fix keeps the chapter's real claim and shows it knows the distinction, which is what a Thomist reader is checking for.

    Line 57

    > And if evil is, at its core, the absence of a good that ought to be there, then the cure for evil is the restoration of the very good that is missing.

    > Not every privation is a privation of love—a broken bone lacks soundness, blindness lacks sight.

    > Chapter 1 showed what is missing in every sphere of evil—personal, relational, social: each one is a communion broken.But Chapter 1 showed what is missing in every sphere of moral evil—personal, relational, social: each one is a communion broken.

    > The absent good is love. This follows from the very logic of privation: where love is lacking, evil takes root; where love is given, evil is undone.There the absent good is love: where love is owed and withheld, evil takes root; where love is given, evil is undone.

    This also strengthens the chapter's coherence — restricting the claim to moral evil is exactly what "A Note on Natural Evil" already says the book is doing.

    12. Line 115 — "Holy" (wholly) — your call, and Codex's reason was wrong

    Audit row: Copy · Claude · CODEX WRONG

    Into §1, Standing Rules — the "Never use" list

    > Never use: performed, earned, transaction, payment plan, debt-payment, unmercy, destiny (for sacrifice/faith context), imputed / imputation

    *Reason recorded with it: the term is the Reformation's forensic category — righteousness reckoned extrinsically rather than infused — and Trent's sixth session anathematizes justification by it. It is also wrong in the other direction, since original sin is contracted rather than reckoned to us (CCC 404-405), a distinction the September Chapter 1 revision already navigated. The book's own phrase for what is meant is written on the heart. The word appears nowhere in the manuscript today; this keeps it out.*
