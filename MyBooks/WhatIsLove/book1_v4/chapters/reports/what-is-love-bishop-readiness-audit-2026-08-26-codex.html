Pre-censor manuscript audit

What Is Love? God’s Perfect Love

Catholic doctrinal, scriptural, sacramental, pastoral, logical, editorial, and production-readiness report
Manuscript state reviewed: August 26, 2026 · 17 chapters plus front and back matter · RSV project standard

Verdict: not ready yet for final episcopal review

The manuscript is unmistakably Catholic in intention, strongly Christ-centered, sacramental, pro-life, pro-marriage, and committed to the teaching authority of the Church. Its governing insight—that love binds truth, justice, mercy, and sacrificial self-gift into communion—has genuine catechetical promise. But the present text repeatedly presents that private synthesis as the exhaustive content of the Gospel and as a universal theological test. It then uses the synthesis to make claims that are inaccurate or materially incomplete in Trinitarian theology, Christology, grace and justification, sacramental theology, moral theology, ecclesiology, and interreligious or ecumenical judgment.

Several passages are also likely to cause pastoral harm, especially to women after abortion, children of divorce, single parents, people experiencing same-sex attraction or gender distress, infertile couples, abuse victims, and persons with scrupulosity. Chapters 15 and 16 require reconstruction, not a light copyedit. The book also contains unresolved permissions, sourcing, cross-reference, and markup problems.

Submission recommendation. Do not send this version as a “final” draft. Resolve the stop-ship items below, obtain review by a qualified dogmatic/sacramental theologian and a moral/pastoral theologian, complete source and permissions verification, then submit the revised text through the diocese’s preferred process. Canon 827 §3 recommends that writings especially concerning religion or morals be submitted to the local ordinary; canon 830 describes review by a censor and the ordinary’s judgment.

Scope, method, and limits

This audit read the current reader-facing manuscript from cover through the Author’s Note and checked its controlling claims against primary Catholic sources, chiefly the Catechism of the Catholic Church, conciliar documents, papal texts, and the Code of Canon Law. It also assessed internal logic, Scripture use, factual and historical overclaims, pastoral consequences, prose, formatting, purposeful progression, unnecessary repetition, citations, and production readiness.

This is an editorial and theological risk audit, not a canonical nihil obstat, imprimatur, or judgment in the name of the Church. Final doctrinal judgment belongs to the competent ecclesiastical authority and the censor or experts he appoints. Line numbers refer to the files as reviewed on August 26, 2026 and will drift after revision.

What is already strong

  • The intention is ecclesial rather than revisionist. The author wants to receive, explain, and defend Catholic teaching, and explicitly seeks correction from the Church.
  • The manuscript keeps Christ, covenant, the Cross, sacraments, virtue, and communion together. That integrated instinct is more Catholic than a merely sentimental definition of love.
  • Truth and mercy are repeatedly held together. The book rightly rejects both truthless permissiveness and loveless condemnation.
  • The Eucharistic and ecclesial vision is ambitious. Even where formulas need correction, the text aims at real presence, sacrificial worship, communion of saints, and sacramental transformation rather than individualistic spirituality.
  • The work takes mortal sin, confession, human dignity, protection of life, marriage, children, and the works of mercy seriously.
  • The prose often has memorable images and strong pastoral momentum. The best portions translate doctrine into lived decisions without flattening it.
  • The exact project definition is consistently recognizable: “God’s Perfect Love is the sacred gift of covenant which binds truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice into life-giving communion.” The problem is not the sentence as a proposed synthesis; it is the authority and universal explanatory burden later assigned to it.

Stop-ship doctrinal themes

These are the recurring issues most likely to concern a diocesan reviewer. They should be corrected globally before sentence-level polishing.

Stop-ship 1 A private synthesis is repeatedly treated as the Church’s exhaustive definition of love and as the Gospel itself.

The four-facet formula may be offered as a fallible catechetical lens. It cannot be presented as “the interpretive key we’ve been missing,” the test that proves every virtue and sacrament, the complete answer to evil, or the full content of revelation. Christ and the apostolic kerygma—not an author-created matrix—are the norm. Explicitly subordinate the framework to Scripture, Tradition, and the Magisterium, and allow it to be corrected by the Church.

Stop-ship 2 “Sacrifice” is treated as an eternal co-constitutive attribute of the divine essence.

The manuscript regularly moves without distinction among eternal Trinitarian self-gift, the Son’s Incarnation, suffering, loss, payment, and death. God’s divine nature is impassible; Christ suffers and dies in his assumed human nature. Eternal divine self-gift can be named as the source and analogue of sacrificial love, but sacrifice in the strict sense of cost, privation, suffering, or death belongs to the economy of creation and redemption. “Sacrifice incarnate” and claims that sacrifice eternally “costs God” should be removed or carefully reformulated.

Stop-ship 3 Grace, justification, sanctification, charity, and natural virtue are repeatedly collapsed.

Initial justification is a gratuitous real transition—ordinarily in Baptism—followed by lifelong growth in grace and holiness. Sanctifying grace is a created habitual gift and participation in divine life; actual grace is divine help; the Holy Spirit is the uncreated Gift. Authentic natural love and acquired virtue can exist without infused charity. Faith without charity is dead or unformed but remains real faith unless faith itself is lost. The current formulas blur or deny these distinctions.

Stop-ship 4 Eucharistic formulas contain material errors and omit necessary safeguards.

Correct “source and summit of our faith” to “source and summit of the Christian life.” God does not become bread; the substance of bread is changed into Christ’s Body while the appearances remain. The Eucharist is not merely symbolic, but it is truly a sacramental sign. A Host is not “divided infinitely.” Baptism incorporates a person into the Church; Eucharist deepens and builds ecclesial communion. Extended invitations to receive must state the requirement of sacramental confession before Communion when conscious of mortal sin, as well as faith, ecclesial communion, and the Eucharistic fast.

Stop-ship 5 Several moral-theology rules are inaccurate and may form consciences badly.

Rejected temptation is not venial sin. If voluntariness is absent, there may be no personal sin rather than automatically a venial sin. The number of boxes in a private vice matrix does not determine gravity. Sin is not always a perceived good taken at another person’s expense, nor is lying the foundational dynamic of every sin. Legitimate authority can impose retributive punishment ordered to justice and the common good; punishment is not exclusively therapeutic. Mercy is never “owed” in strict justice.

Stop-ship 6 Authority, freedom, covenant, and baptismal identity are overstated or reduced.

Authority does not exist only when voluntarily accepted; legitimate authority participates in God’s ordering of the common good and may bind a resisting subject. Freedom is perfected by adherence to the good; the ability to choose evil is not constitutive of perfect freedom. Mortal sin destroys charity and sanctifying grace but does not erase Baptism’s indelible character or simply terminate ecclesial incorporation. Covenant is graciously initiated by God and is not always a negotiated, consciously accepted relation—infant Baptism is an obvious counterexample.

Stop-ship 7 The Gospel and Church are reduced to the framework.

The Gospel is the good news of Jesus Christ and the Kingdom—his Incarnation, life, death for sins, burial, Resurrection, exaltation, gift of the Spirit, Church, judgment, and promised consummation. Love is its heart and fulfillment, but the four facets cannot replace the apostolic kerygma. The Church is the seed, beginning, and sacrament of the Kingdom, not simply the Kingdom in its eschatological fullness.

Stop-ship 8 Chapters 15–16 use categorical culture-war rhetoric where precise doctrine and pastoral care are required.

The defense of life, marriage, sexual integrity, and embodied personhood should remain unequivocal. But the current text often attributes one selfish motive to entire groups, denies authentic natural goods outside explicit Catholic faith, generalizes disputed medical or social claims, caricatures separated Christians, and predicts deterministic damage to children. These chapters would be high-risk in a bishop’s hands even if every underlying moral conclusion were retained.

Stop-ship 9 Claims of divine “revelation” or “inspiration” to the author are unsafe.

The Preface and Author’s Note can sound as though the Holy Spirit authored or guaranteed the manuscript. Reserve “inspired” in the technical sense for Sacred Scripture. Describe prayer, grace, study, and fallible discernment; explicitly submit every original synthesis to the Church. AI tools cannot “ensure faithfulness.”

Stop-ship 10 Source, permissions, and production status are not submission-ready.

The front matter asserts “Used with Permission” for RSV-2CE and Catechism material, but no supporting license or permission documentation was located in the project area checked. It also says some quotations were “adapted,” which means they should not be presented as exact quotations from a named edition. The manuscript has an obsolete chapter bridge, a malformed HTML block, extensive raw TeX, inconsistent metadata, and incomplete source support for historical, medical, statistical, and patristic claims.

Robustness and repetition audit

Chapter scale is not itself a defect. The governing editorial question is functional: does each passage perform necessary work, or does it repeat a conclusion the reader has already received without adding doctrinal precision, a new step in the argument, a necessary example, pastoral application, or deliberate rhetorical force?

Recurring patternRiskRevision test
Full recaps of the four-facet frameworkLater chapters can pause to re-prove the framework instead of using it to reach a new conclusion.Keep the shortest callback that orients the reader; retain a longer recap only when it changes the application or resolves a new objection.
Sacrificial cost, payment, debt, and transaction imageryRepeated metaphors can flatten Catholic atonement, grace, and sacramental distinctions while making adjacent sections sound interchangeable.Assign each image one doctrinal job, integrate complementary Catholic models, and remove repetitions that do not add a distinct truth.
Culture-war catalogues and universal claimsMultiple examples may increase heat without increasing precision, evidence, or pastoral usefulness.Use the fewest examples needed to establish the moral principle; define the act or proposition precisely and source empirical claims.
Repeated altar-call conclusions and rhetorical questionsFrequent climaxes can compete with the book’s intended Gospel culmination.Preserve deliberate invitations, but let each chapter own a distinct response rather than repeating the same emotional cadence.
Scripture or doctrine revisited across chaptersRepetition can be either cumulative theology or needless restatement.Retain it when the later use develops a new dimension; otherwise use a concise cross-reference to the chapter that owns the full treatment.

Chapter-by-chapter findings

The entries below consolidate recurring instances; they are not proposed redlines. “Major” means the claim can materially mislead a reader or a reviewer and should be revised before submission. “Review” means it requires clarification, evidence, or specialist verification.

Front matter, Preface, and Introduction

2_preface.md · 3_introduction.md
  • Major Introduction:90–106 calls the private definition the missing interpretive key that “unlocks everything.” Present it instead as a proposed pastoral synthesis under the deposit of faith. Christ is the definitive Word; the Church’s rule of faith is not generated by the framework.
  • Major Introduction:104–106 calls divine childhood humanity’s “birthright.” Every person has created dignity and is called to communion; supernatural adoptive filiation is a gift of grace, ordinarily conferred in Baptism.
  • Major Introduction:146–150 says justification is ongoing “not a single moment,” every virtue contains all four facets, and incomplete love is not love. Distinguish initial justification from growth in sanctification; align the virtue claim with Chapter 13’s contrary matrix; acknowledge real but imperfect natural love.
  • Pastoral The opening catalogue of BLM, “wokeism,” gender transition, abortion, Marxism, and “modern Christianity” is loaded, under-sourced, and front-loads partisan conflict before the positive theological case. Name precise propositions and distinguish persons, movements, moral objects, and empirical claims.
  • Voice Preface:44–50 says whatever is true has the Holy Spirit as “author” and that God “revealed” the insight. Together with the Author’s Note this can imply inspiration or private revelation. Recast as prayerful, fallible reflection submitted to the Church.
  • Sources The permissions page says RSV-2CE and Catechism material are “Used with Permission,” yet also says quotations were adapted. Verify every license and quotation; paraphrases and shortened composites must not appear as exact quotations from a named edition.
  • Copy Title capitalization varies; “Denton TX” needs punctuation; several sentences are ungrammatical (“what God is Love means”), fragmented, or burdened by double spaces and catalogue-length syntax. “We specifically chose” also obscures authorship in a single-author book. The August 30 date is intentional and is not an audit issue.

Chapter 1 — The Problem of Evil

chapter_01.md
  • Major 69–73 makes the capacity for evil necessary to freedom. Replace the good/evil symmetry with the Catholic account that finite freedom can be abused and becomes more perfect as it adheres to God and the good.
  • Major 91–101 defines every sin as taking a perceived good for oneself at another’s expense. Sin is first offense against God and failure in genuine love; interior sins, omissions, sins against oneself, and directly Godward sins exceed this formula.
  • Argument 75–81 offers a false atheist dilemma and declares protest against evil incoherent without answering natural evil or particular divine permissions. It risks turning a sufferer’s lament into an accusation against the sufferer. Use a modest free-will defense and the Cross/resurrection/providence account.
  • Precision “Each generation inherits brokenness as birthright” can imply inherited personal guilt or determinism. Distinguish original sin’s wounded condition and social consequences from personal culpability.
  • Evidence Claims about school secrecy, abortion totals, father absence, pornography, anthropological universals, and policy incentives need current primary data and correlation/causation discipline. Do not turn a Black organizer vignette into a racialized single-cause diagnosis.
  • Logic/copy Law can educate; science can study proximate causes; “we refuse to use the tools” neglects wounded nature, ignorance, trauma, constraints, concupiscence, and grace. Correct subject–verb errors, comma splices, repeated Romans 2:15, and inconsistent heading style.

Chapter 2 — The Answer Is Love

chapter_02.md
  • Major 47–57 identifies the summum bonum simply as love and asserts that every culture and person reaches that answer. The objective supreme good and final end is the Triune God/beatific communion; charity is the greatest virtue uniting us to Him.
  • Major 51, 75–86 says reason discovers and Scripture confirms that “God is love.” Natural reason can know God and attributes; the Trinitarian fullness of “God is love” is revealed.
  • Argument Five author-selected criteria are used to prove that love is the only reality satisfying them. Privation theory concerns a due good, not necessarily an immediate absence of “love.” The present reasoning is circular unless charity’s integrating role is independently demonstrated.
  • Major 119, 155–169 makes every authentic act of love supernatural participation in divine life and says love must be explicitly sacred or be nothing. Preserve natural moral knowledge, natural virtue, and genuine natural love while locating their ultimate source and supernatural elevation in God.
  • Precision Covenant is not every permanent human relation; justice renders God as well as neighbor his due; mercy and justice cannot be pictured as rival forces; “sacrifice proves love” is too absolute. “The West conquered much evil” is undefined and triumphalist.
  • Cleared “Infinite dignity” should not be flagged merely because the adjective is strong: the Dicastery’s 2024 declaration is titled Dignitas Infinita and deliberately applies the expression to human dignity. The surrounding metaphysical claims still require precision.
  • Copy The “four Greek words for love” account and holy/whole wordplay are popular simplifications, not etymological or theological proof. Fix agreement, punctuation, capitalization, and spacing.

Chapter 3 — Sacred: God Is the Source of Love

chapter_03.md
  • Major 123–135 is the main systematic fault line: it makes “sacrifice” eternal in God without distinguishing divine self-gift from suffering, privation, cost, and death in the economy of salvation.
  • Major 130–183 repeatedly says every authentic human love participates in the divine nature. Natural love images or reflects divine goodness; infused charity in sanctifying grace supernaturally participates in divine life.
  • Trinity “Eternal exchange,” each Person giving to “the others,” and the Spirit as “fruit” need the guardrails of one essence, intellect, will, and operation, with real relations of origin. The Spirit is a consubstantial Person proceeding as Love/Gift, not the product of a prior communal process.
  • Argument “A law requires a lawgiver” is asserted as a decisive refutation of non-theistic moral realism. A Thomistic argument must connect being, goodness, finality, rational nature, eternal law, and providence; the selected four categories cannot prove their own divine source.
  • Precision Human dignity is ontologically grounded in rational nature, creation in God’s image, and divine vocation—not simply in being valued by another. “Sacrilege” is also too broad for every exploitation of a person; reserve its technical meaning.
  • Evidence/pastoral Childhood attachment is not uniformly secure or unconditional, solitude is not itself a violation of nature, and evolutionary biology need not explain why the universe exists. Do not straw-man scientific or secular explanations when making the metaphysical case.
  • Copy Holy does not derive from whole; a broken “pinky promise” is not automatically an offense against humanity. Long paragraphs and universal statements obscure the strongest theology.

Chapter 4 — Gift of Covenant: Love’s Willing Submission

chapter_04.md
  • Major 115–149 says authority inheres only in an author and is active only when freely accepted. Parents, Church, and civil rulers exercise participated authority ordered to the common good; legitimate commands bind even when resisted.
  • Major Contracts are caricatured as temporary/selfish and covenants as unconditional and unbreakable even though Scripture speaks of covenant violation. Divine fidelity does not erase covenant commands, conditions, sanctions, or human breach.
  • Safety “I’m yours, no matter what,” “no escape clause,” and the broken-vow discussion need explicit teaching on separation for grave danger and permissible civil divorce to secure legal protection while a valid bond endures.
  • Vocation Marriage does not most fully fulfill every baptismal covenant and is not a “sacred necessity.” Consecrated virginity/celibacy for the Kingdom has a distinct evangelical preeminence; widowhood, singleness, and infertility do not frustrate personal fulfillment in God.
  • Creation Parents procreate; God alone immediately creates each spiritual soul. Do not call parents authors/creators in a way that confuses creaturely cooperation with divine creation.
  • History/logic Human law is not necessarily fabricated morality enforced by raw power. Rome, medieval kingdoms, slavery, Nazism, and communism require precise historical causation and sources, not a single inevitable “morality without God” sequence.
  • Marriage Clarify validity, sacramentality, ratification/consummation, nullity, and the fact that earthly marriage ends at death. Avoid equating submission to spouse or children with submission owed to God.

Chapter 5 — Binding: What Love Unites

chapter_05.md
  • Major Genuine truth, justice, and mercy do not become cruelty, vengeance, or enabling. Counterfeits and partial claims masquerading as virtues can do so. Preserve the reality of acquired natural virtue and the supernatural informing role of charity.
  • Major Aquinas’s account of bonum is not exhausted by truth + justice + mercy, and alicui does not semantically mean sacrifice/cost. Label this as the author’s analogy rather than Thomas’s hidden anatomy.
  • Atonement “Debt only God can pay” and “paid in full” reduce Catholic soteriology if isolated. Integrate loving obedience, expiation, satisfaction, merit, victory, reconciliation, and participation.
  • Scripture John 8 does not show true Mosaic justice demanding the woman’s execution until mercy interrupts. The missing man, witness/procedure requirements, trap, and Christ’s call from sin matter. Manipulation of law is not “truth without mercy.”
  • Judgment “You are going to hell” is never technically accurate as a judgment about a living person’s final destiny. Warn of grave danger without presuming damnation.
  • Sacraments Distinguish Christ’s glorified Body, Eucharistic Body, and Mystical Body; Baptismal incorporation and Eucharistic communion; venial wounds and mortal loss of charity; forgiveness, reconciliation, trust, and safety.
  • Pastoral Divorce does not deterministically deform every future relationship. Justified separation exists, and catechesis is not a substitute for trauma-informed clinical help.

Chapter 6 — Truth: God Is Truth

chapter_06.md
  • Major Lying is not “the foundational sin that makes every other sin possible.” A false practical judgment accompanies sin, but lying is a specific offense; pride/disobedience are the traditional root dynamics.
  • Inspiration Prophets and apostles did not become inspired because they surrendered so completely that truth flowed through them. God is Scripture’s author while true human authors used their faculties; holiness is not the criterion of inspiration.
  • Christology “The sin that killed God” and “the priesthood failed Him” need Chalcedonian and anti-Judaism safeguards. The divine Person truly died in his human nature; the divine nature is impassible. Responsibility for the Passion rests on sinners universally, not a people or priesthood as such.
  • Authority The chapter repeats the error that authority becomes real only through acceptance. Obedience remains rational and bounded by moral law, but refusal does not extinguish legitimate authority.
  • Church/history Apostolic-see founder/date/succession claims, canon formation, the relative chronology of liturgy and Gospels, and “Bible born at the altar” language need historical qualification. The Church discerned the canon; God authored inspired Scripture.
  • Anthropology Biological sex is bodily and significant, but “every cell” and “half the organs” are scientifically crude. State the whole-person sexual differentiation without relying on false cellular absolutes.
  • Truth A personal encounter with Christ does not make propositional truth dispensable; willingness to suffer authenticates witness but does not constitute truth. Avoid “human truth” versus “God’s truth” as rival species.
  • Sources/copy Mark/John dates are estimates; the “Two Books” schema conflicts with the project’s recorded decision; one sentence implies apostles wrote after their own deaths. The chapter also has long paragraphs and extensive dash/quotation inconsistency.

Chapter 7 — Justice: God Is Justice

chapter_07.md
  • Major An offender has rights to dignity, due process, and humane treatment, but not a strict justice-right to mercy precisely as mercy. Mercy exceeds strict due even when Christians are commanded to show it.
  • Justice Punishment is not just only when it restores a person or relationship. Catholic teaching includes retributive redress, protection, deterrence, and medicinal aims. Restitution restores due equality, not automatically reconciliation.
  • Marriage Betrayal can justify separation and protection but does not make the valid bond bind the innocent “less.” Avoid any implication that grave fault frees a spouse to remarry.
  • Life ethics Abortion/euthanasia are intrinsically evil direct killings of innocents. The death penalty has a different moral history; the current CCC calls it inadmissible. Do not present all three as though the Church always classified them identically.
  • Taxonomy The injustice/idolatry/exploitation scheme and use of dulia as generic reciprocal honor are not received Catholic classifications. Aquinas’s parts of justice and annexed virtues require exact scholarly sourcing.
  • Rights/politics Religious freedom is immunity from coercion within just limits, not an unlimited right to act on conscience. Voting/cooperation with evil cannot be reduced to a single candidate test without intention, kind of cooperation, proportionate reasons, and prudence.
  • Pastoral Precisely distinguish dysphoria, pornography, prostitution, surrogacy, worship, idolatry, prejudice, and particular “equity” or “anti-racism” proposals; do not group unlike persons or moral objects under elastic labels.
  • Production Fix the unclosed HTML callout at 133–135, Hebrew transliteration, CCC cross-references, quotation punctuation, and raw TeX/dash inconsistencies.

Chapter 8 — Mercy: God Is Mercy

chapter_08.md
  • Major “Mercy has no purchase where there is no repentance” contradicts the later correct distinction: victims may forgive unilaterally, renounce revenge, pray, and keep merciful boundaries; repentance is required for absolution, fruitful reception, restored trust, and reconciliation.
  • Grace A hardened heart can resist grace, but “no grace can pass through it” is false; converting grace can soften it. Later grave refusal does not retroactively “revoke” previously absolved sins.
  • Justice Mercy does not maintain what true justice would destroy, and no one may “sacrifice” justice owed to victims or the common good. One may relinquish a personal claim while preserving public duties and protection.
  • Penance Contrition, confession, and satisfaction are the penitent’s acts; priestly absolution is essential. “Restoration” is an effect, not a fifth component. Do not promise total instantaneous psychological or relational healing.
  • Atonement “Substitute taking what we deserved,” “paid debt,” and “mercy absorbs sin” need the fuller Catholic synthesis of free loving obedience, representation, sacrifice, expiation, satisfaction, merit, reconciliation, and victory.
  • Culpability Christ’s “they know not” prayer cannot be universalized so that every sinner lacks full knowledge. Mortal guilt can include grave matter, full knowledge, and deliberate consent.
  • Safety Forgiveness is not a guaranteed technique that makes trauma lift. Explicitly reject self-harm in the abortion-doctor vignette and direct distressed readers toward confession, emergency help, and competent clinical care.
  • Anointing/private revelation Anointing, Penance, Apostolic Pardon, Viaticum, and conditions for forgiveness must be distinguished. “A Catholic does not die alone” is not a factual promise. Add CCC 67’s status of private revelation to Faustina material and explain “mercy as greatest attribute” as greatest in effects toward creatures.
  • Verified The “Every person therefore is invited to repent…” quotation and note are correctly attributed to St. John Paul II, Redemptoris Missio 13. Do not change it to Pope Leo XIV.

Chapter 9 — Sacrifice: Love’s Eternal Self-Gift

chapter_09.md
  • Major The chapter’s title and controlling claim conflate eternal Trinitarian self-gift with strict sacrifice. Cost, suffering, blood, privation, and death do not belong to the divine nature; Christ’s sacrifice is of infinite value because of the divine Person who offers in his humanity.
  • Faith Faith and obedience are not identical, and unformed/dead faith is not “no faith.” Faith is a supernatural gift and intellectual assent moved by the will under grace; charity gives it living form and salvific union.
  • Love Love need not always be outward in a way that excludes ordered self-love, contemplation, rest, or duties to self. Christian hope for one’s own salvation is virtuous, not selfish. Sacrifice is not the measure of every love in every moment.
  • Atonement Dense price, debt, payment, punishment, exchange, and tribunal language narrows the Paschal mystery. “Not a tribunal but a Father” is also a false dichotomy: Scripture and Tradition use filial, judicial, cultic, covenantal, victorious, and medicinal images in an integrated way.
  • Grace Images in which grace waits until the sinner opens a hand by doing can imply semi-Pelagian initiative. God’s prevenient grace awakens, enables, and accompanies the very opening and every meritorious response.
  • Mass The Mass is the sacramental re-presentation of the one sacrifice, not a repeated sacrifice or literal “time travel.” The offertory, water/wine symbolism, *missa*, and scriptural offering taxonomy need to follow the rite and Catholic sources rather than invented meanings.
  • Sacraments Personal conscious consent is not a universal condition of sacramental covenant: infant Baptism is decisive. Sacramental fruit also depends on disposition; grace is not mechanical.
  • Scripture/history The Old Testament offering system, James 2, sloth/lukewarmness, *teleios*, Peter’s denial, martyrs’ biographies, Tertullian, and the attribution of incurvatus in se to Augustine all need source correction or qualification.
  • Family Contraception frustrates the procreative meaning of intercourse but does not “sacrifice the child.” A marriage already forms a family; infertile spouses can be fruitful. Do not make visible cost a test that shames exhausted parents or caregivers.
  • Structure The chapter repeats themes, quotations, and examples often enough that important distinctions can blur. Preserve its depth, but organize it around one doctrinally controlled thesis and consolidate historical typology or applications when they repeat work already completed.

Chapter 10 — Life-Giving Communion: Love’s Goal

chapter_10.md
  • Trinity 40–56 first denies divine need, then says love “must create, must multiply.” God creates freely, not by necessity. The Spirit is a consubstantial Person proceeding as Love/Gift, not simply the mutual relationship of Father and Son.
  • Trinity 282–304 uses the Son’s human/economic obedience to support eternal “submission” within God. The three Persons possess one divine will; distinguish relations of origin, mission, and the Son’s assumed human obedience.
  • Eucharist Correct “source and summit of our faith” and “God becomes bread.” Use transubstantiation’s received language and acknowledge the Eucharist as effective sacramental sign—not merely symbol—while affirming real, true, substantial presence.
  • Reception Add confession before Communion for conscious grave sin, Eucharistic faith, ecclesial communion, and fast. The Penitential Act does not remit mortal sin. Do not promise automatic perfect communion or transformed desires irrespective of disposition.
  • Eucharistic detail Seeing the Host is not the Beatific Vision; a Host is not infinitely divisible; the water is not humanity “lost” in divinity; Baptism first incorporates into the Church; Christ’s Eucharistic Body and Mystical Body are related but not identical.
  • Marriage/family Marriage has both inseparable goods of spouses and procreation/education; it does not exist only for children. A birth scene cannot be made a universal Trinity replica, and family unity is analogical rather than “the same” as divine consubstantial unity.
  • Liturgy Creed/readings differ by day; *missa* denotes dismissal and supports a sending interpretation but does not literally mean “sent”; offertory, consecration, intercessions, and mission claims need rite-based wording.
  • Ecumenism/eschatology Division is a wound, but separated Christians possess real gifts and Catholics share responsibility. Saints intercede; Scripture does not show them as spectators, and reunion cannot be promised indiscriminately with every deceased acquaintance.
  • Production 354–366 points to the Lawgiver as the next chapter, but Chapter 11 is Prudence. Replace the obsolete bridge.

Chapter 11 — The Mind of Love: Prudence

chapter_11.md
  • Method The father/sons parable and four facets are made a definitive answer for every prudential case. Catholic moral discernment also requires moral object, intention, circumstances, precepts, virtues, gifts, vocation, counsel, competence, and consequences. Keep the framework heuristic, not exhaustive.
  • Sacrifice God’s “infinite sacrifice” and pouring out without depletion repeat the eternal-sacrifice category problem.
  • Providence The claim that God assigns each person a family/community set so “the whole Body is covered” is an unsupported system and can excuse needs that fall outside kinship networks.
  • Dignity/soul God is a greater good than any created soul, and murder destroys bodily life—not the immortal soul. “Your soul, which is to say, the truth” is a category error.
  • Pastoral The gender/same-sex/cohabitation scenario needs respect, compassion, no unjust discrimination, no rash judgment, and case-specific counsel while retaining moral truth.
  • Happiness “Happiness is the purpose of life” is not simply a lie. Distinguish passing satisfaction from beatitude, the human final end in God.
  • Life/end of life Abortion is gravely evil but not safely labeled “the worst thing a person can do.” “Never end life” must mean never intentionally kill; withdrawing disproportionate treatment and proportionate analgesia require CCC 2278–2279 precision.
  • Soteriology A self-written “book that saves you and the world” makes praxis sound salvific in itself. Christ alone saves; grace-enabled prudence and charity are our cooperation.

Chapter 12 — The Lawgiver Is Love

chapter_12.md
  • Argument “Laws require lawgivers” equivocates among physical regularities, abstract moral norms, and promulgated civil laws. It is one contested cumulative argument, not a self-evident proof that refutes all secular moral realism.
  • Tone Claims that atheist reasoning self-destructs, unbelievers merely suppress sufficient evidence, and all non-theistic accounts collapse diagnose motives and culpability the author cannot know.
  • Revelation Natural theology can know God exists and infer perfections; it does not deduce the revealed Triune mystery or the full Johannine affirmation “God is love” merely from the four-part framework.
  • Law/grace Natural law written on hearts is not the New Law, which is principally the grace of the Holy Spirit. Human response is moved by prevenient grace, not unaided voluntary recognition.
  • Fear/salvation Servile fear is imperfect, but fear of the Lord is a gift and fear of punishment can begin conversion. For salvation outside visible Church boundaries, state all Lumen Gentium 16 conditions and that every salvation comes through Christ and is related to the Church.
  • Exegesis The Beatitudes-to-four-facets map and heart/soul/mind/strength maps are devotional applications, not lexical or Magisterial anatomy. Label them as such.
  • Internal logic The chapter again says every virtue contains all four pillars, contradicting the matrices that follow. The framework cannot serve as evidence for intelligent design when its categories were selected by the author.

Chapter 13 — Faith Works Through Love

chapter_13.md
  • Virtue Charity as the “form of the virtues” does not mean every natural/acquired virtue is made of charity. Infused charity orders the Christian’s infused virtues and acts to supernatural life and merit.
  • Grace/habit Repeated practice acquires moral habits but does not infuse faith, hope, or charity. The theological virtues are God-given with sanctifying grace and then exercised/cooperated with.
  • Matrix The tables define virtues to fit selected boxes and then use the fit to prove the framework. Sacrifice/cost is not definitional of every virtue, and stable virtue can act promptly and joyfully. Present, at most, as a tentative devotional aid after expert review.
  • Definitions Hope resides in the will as confident desire/expectation; dulia is technical veneration, not generic honor; penance is not paying an unpaid divine bill; zeal can be disordered; the “capital virtues” list is not a fixed Magisterial taxonomy.
  • Humility Humility is not uniquely “created,” purely vertical, prior to faith, or the definition of Catholic identity. It concerns truthful self-estimation under God and has direct neighbor-facing effects.
  • Justification Initial justification remits sin and gives sanctifying grace and the infused virtues; it is not merely God forming one virtue at a time. Subsequent growth is lifelong. State of grace is not simply a fully ordered lifestyle.
  • Faith Faith without charity is unformed/dead but remains faith. Avoid saying it becomes inauthentic or nonexistent.
  • History/pastoral Avoid a blanket Pharisee caricature. Correct Augustine’s chronology, garden account, concubinage context, and inflated titles; Reconciliation forgives/reconciles and is not merely a lens scrubber.

Chapter 14 — Sin and Vice: The Failure to Love

chapter_14.md
  • Matrix The star/cross system is not a Catholic classification of sin, and the number of facets violated does not determine gravity. Use moral object, intention, circumstances, grave matter, full knowledge, and deliberate consent.
  • Root sin Every sin need not involve believing or telling a lie. Pride/disobedience are root dynamics; a person can knowingly choose a recognized evil from weakness or malice.
  • Definitions Unlearned unbelief is not automatically infidelity; despair and presumption are distinct; anger can be righteous; legitimate force is not inherently “violence”; disobedience to unjust commands may be required; mercy is not owed.
  • Punishment Private revenge is forbidden, but public authority may impose proportionate punishment including retributive redress. Forgiveness does not cancel public justice.
  • Culpability If voluntariness is absent there may be no personal sin, not automatically venial sin. Habits may diminish freedom but deliberately cultivated habits can increase responsibility.
  • Baptism Mortal sin destroys charity and sanctifying grace but not the baptismal character or incorporation. Reconciliation restores grace; it is not literally a second Baptism.
  • Scrupulosity 208–216 labels immediately rejected impure thoughts/glances venial sin. Rejected temptation is not sin. This is one of the most pastorally urgent corrections.
  • Confession An examination should begin with God’s grace and objective commandments, not raw emotional “pain.” Perfect contrition includes firm resolution to confess as soon as possible. No one has ordinary certainty from introspection alone that the heart is perfectly ordered.

Chapter 15 — Counterfeit Love in the Modern World

chapter_15.md
  • Structure This chapter is a major submission risk. It piles loosely defined “isms,” moral objects, social movements, disputed empirical claims, and separated Christian communities into one prosecution. Rebuild around precise propositions, Catholic sources, distinctions, and remedies.
  • Natural good Atheists and non-Catholics can know and perform real natural goods; secular persons are not thereby incapable of love, sacrifice, marriage, or virtue. Their ultimate good is fulfilled in God, but natural law and actual grace are not erased.
  • Social doctrine The Church rejects atheistic collectivism and class hatred, but also teaches that sinful social structures and “structures of sin” require institutional reform. “Only hearts, never structures” is not Catholic social doctrine.
  • Gender/sexuality Retain the created unity of body and soul and sexual difference, but distinguish persons, inclinations, acts, diagnoses, and interventions. State respect, compassion, and prohibition of unjust discrimination. Replace undefined “wokeism” and current medical claims with exact sourced claims.
  • Abortion/pastoral Remove “easy way out,” “cannot love,” the seven-deadly-sins mapping, “no therapist can heal,” and categorical motive assignment. State objective gravity while following Evangelium Vitae 99’s acknowledgment of pressure, coercion, fear, wounds, forgiveness, hope, sacramental reconciliation, and expert help.
  • Mental health “Post-abortion syndrome” is not a universally recognized diagnosis; grief and mental-health outcomes vary. Do not displace licensed care or imply therapy competes with Christ. Spiritual and clinical care can cooperate.
  • Ecumenism The “faith alone,” “Bible alone,” denomination-count, and once-saved-always-saved passages caricature diverse traditions. Vatican II requires acknowledging shared guilt for division and real sanctifying elements outside visible Catholic boundaries. Benedict XVI explicitly explained a Catholic sense in which “faith alone” can be true when not opposed to charity.
  • History Holocaust, eugenics, totalitarianism, racism, and modern ideology are multicausal. Do not present them as the automatic consequence of one philosophical premise or claim the Church never used force or always led every justice reform.
  • Copy Correct “object truth” to “objective truth”; define every technical or political label; remove repeated catalogues and unsupported superlatives.

Chapter 16 — What Counterfeit Love Costs the Children

chapter_16.md
  • Thesis Children are not the “ultimate test” of every adult moral choice, and the four facets do not mechanically predict child outcomes. Children are persons to love, not diagnostic instruments for proving the framework.
  • Determinism Stable marriage/family is a profound good and major protective factor, not a guarantee. Wounded families can form saints; devout intact families can fail. Grace, freedom, temperament, abuse, community, and many conditions intervene.
  • Medicine “Gender ideology sterilizes children” collapses social identification, blockers, hormones, and surgeries. Effects, availability, age, jurisdiction, and evidence differ. State Catholic anthropology and ethical objections with current primary medical data.
  • Abortion/divorce Do not make every abortion a convenience sacrifice or every divorce selfish abandonment. Distinguish objective acts, motives, coercion, culpability, separation, nullity, civil protection, and valid bond.
  • Families Same-sex households do not all arise through purchased surrogacy; cohabiting homes do not all disappear tomorrow; pornography users are not all addicted fathers. Name moral concerns without denying natural affection, real stability, dignity, conversion, or varied facts.
  • Dignity Remove the single-mother stereotype and the two-child chart that makes one child confused/prone to evil and the other secure/virtuous. Origins do not determine moral worth or destiny.
  • Evidence Education, anxiety, secular upbringing, family outcomes, pornography, and multi-generation formulas require reliable studies and probabilistic language. Proverbs 22:6 is wisdom, not an unconditional outcome guarantee.

Chapter 17 — The Gospel: Perfect Love Revealed in Christ

chapter_17.md
  • Gospel The chapter identifies the Gospel with the author’s definition. Restore the apostolic kerygma—Christ’s person, Kingdom, Incarnation, death for sins, burial, Resurrection, exaltation, Spirit, Church, judgment, and consummation—and present the framework as one meditation on its love.
  • Grace/faith Grace is not identical with the definition. Preserve created sanctifying grace, actual grace, and the uncreated Gift; initial justification and growth; adult cooperation moved by grace and infant Baptism; dead/unformed faith that remains faith.
  • Incarnation God does not “become love”; God eternally is Love and the Son becomes flesh without change in divinity. Mind is a faculty of the rational soul, not a third substance beside body and soul.
  • Gift/justice Humanity is not owed Incarnation, forgiveness, adoption, or salvation. They are gratuitous mercy. Children owe appropriate honor/obedience even though dignity is not earned and they are not financial debts.
  • Covenant/Church Infant Baptism disproves a universally negotiated covenant. The Adam–Noah–Abraham–Moses–David social-expansion scheme is a popular synthesis requiring attribution. The Church is seed and beginning of the Kingdom, not the Kingdom’s full eschatological identity.
  • Paschal mystery Label the “Fourth Cup” as a debated typology, not established biblical fact. At death Christ’s human soul separated from his body while both remained united to the divine Person. Psalm 85’s four-term Calvary map is devotional, not an explicit prophecy.
  • Eucharist Repeat the Chapter 10 corrections: not merely symbolic; Baptism incorporates; Eucharist deepens unity; Host not infinitely divisible; confession before Communion if conscious of mortal sin; faith, ecclesial communion, and fast.
  • Salvation/pastoral Concern for one’s salvation is the virtue of hope, not selfishness. Do not deny rays of truth and holiness in other religions or assign culpability to all who “reject Christ.” State that all salvation is through Christ and related to his Church while respecting invincible ignorance.
  • Initiation “State of perfect love” is not the Communion criterion. Validly baptized candidates in OCIA are not rebaptized; initiation depends on canonical status and pastoral discernment.
  • Language Remove “sacrifice incarnate,” infertility as “sterility,” deterministic child-wholeness promises, every-sin-kills language, and total temporal-healing promises.

Epilogue and Author’s Note

epilogue.md · z_authors_note.md
  • Development “We know His love more deeply today than any generation” is false and triumphalist. Doctrine develops without replacing the apostolic deposit; no age can rank itself above Mary, apostles, Fathers, Doctors, and saints in lived knowledge of divine love.
  • Authority “Everything this book teaches” comes from the Church’s well and “now you understand what love actually is” overstate a private synthesis that still contains errors. End with explicit submission to ecclesial judgment.
  • Salvation Jesus does not save merely “one loving soul at a time.” He saves by the once-for-all Paschal mystery and grace; transformed witnesses cooperate in his ecclesial mission.
  • Terminology “Holy Communion” normally means the Eucharist, not generic harmonious life. Use communion with God and neighbor or explicitly connect the phrase to Eucharistic communion and disposition.
  • Author’s Note Replace “revealed to me,” “Holy Spirit’s inspiration,” and AI “ensured faithfulness” with prayer, grace, study, fallible discernment, tool assistance, independent source checking, and the author’s full responsibility.
  • Church/order Christ founded the Church and entrusted it to the apostles; bishops succeed the apostles, priests are coworkers, and deacons share ordained ministry. Use “apostles,” “disciples,” and “offices” precisely.
  • Copy Standardize 2,000/2000, Cross/cross, age formats, and “version that I have written”; remove the double space and unsupported “atheist’s strongest argument always” superlative.

Grammar, clarity, logic, citations, and production

1. Source and quotation control

  • Translation conflict: the project instruction says Scripture must always be RSV, while the front matter identifies RSV-2CE. Choose the actual licensed edition, then make every quotation and permissions line match it. RSV and RSV-2CE are not interchangeable labels.
  • “Adapted quotations”: an adapted or composite sentence is a paraphrase, not an exact quotation. Use quotation marks only for verified text; show ellipses and brackets honestly; identify verse numbering differences.
  • Permissions: no supporting permission/license file was found in the nearby project folders searched. That does not prove permission is absent, but the claim “Used with Permission” should not remain without documentary support and the exact licensor-required notice.
  • Primary sources: replace popular summaries and secondary quotations with exact CCC, council, papal, liturgical, biblical, patristic, Thomistic, and canon-law references. Technical claims about Aquinas, Greek/Hebrew, covenant typology, martyrs, canon history, and the Mass need page/article/section-level citations.
  • Empirical sources: medical, psychological, sociological, crime, divorce, family, abortion, education, race, pornography, and historical death-toll claims need dated, methodologically appropriate sources. Do not treat anecdotes or correlation as universal causal proof.
  • Bibliography: create one authoritative source ledger that records exact edition, URL/publication data, page or paragraph, quote status, permission status, and manuscript locations. Current endnotes are not sufficient for a bishop-facing scholarly audit trail.

2. Argument and exposition

  • Reduce universal quantifiers. “Always,” “only,” “every,” “no one,” “nothing,” “must,” “guarantees,” and “cannot” cause many of the book’s errors. Use them only when doctrine or evidence actually supports universality.
  • Separate levels of claim. Mark whether a statement is dogma, authoritative doctrine, common theological opinion, disputed hypothesis, devotional meditation, prudential judgment, empirical generalization, or personal testimony.
  • Do not prove the framework with categories created by the framework. The virtue/vice matrices and five-criterion argument repeatedly assume what they claim to demonstrate.
  • Avoid false binaries. Justice/mercy, tribunal/Father, symbol/reality, reason/revelation, spiritual/clinical help, hearts/structures, marriage/celibacy, and truth/person are Catholic both-and distinctions, not mutually exclusive alternatives.
  • Distinguish analogy from identity. Human family, marriage, covenant, social unity, body imagery, and sacrificial love can image divine mysteries without being the same kind of unity, causality, procession, or gift as the Trinity and sacraments.
  • Use the conclusion the argument earns. Several chapters claim decisive proofs against atheism or a complete answer to evil after offering only one plausible cumulative consideration.

3. Prose and house style

  • The manuscript relies heavily on sentence fragments, all-caps emphasis, one-sentence paragraphs, rhetorical questions, repeated callouts, em-dash chains, and altar-call conclusions. These are effective in moderation but presently make portions of the book feel more repetitive and polemical than their content requires.
  • Automated preflight found at least 37 double-space occurrences across six reader files and 63 spaced-hyphen patterns across eleven. Some occur in tables or deliberate typography, but all need normalization under one house style.
  • Recurring copy defects include subject–verb agreement, missing articles, comma splices, quotation punctuation, title capitalization, straight/curly quote mixing, inconsistent capitalization of Church/Cross/Gospel, singular/plural drift, ungrammatical “what God is Love means,” “object truth,” and “privates” where “deprives” appears intended.
  • False or misleading wordplay should be removed: holy does not derive from whole; missa is not literally “sent”; ekklesia etymology cannot prove ecclesiology; alicui does not contain sacrifice.
  • The core definition should remain word-for-word when quoted as the project’s definition. Paraphrases should be identified as paraphrases rather than silently altering the canonical line.

4. File and layout preflight

  • Malformed HTML: chapter_07.md:133–135 opens a paragraph inside a Div but closes only the Div. Pandoc reports the Div as unclosed until much later.
  • Raw TeX: approximately 131 raw TeX lines occur across 20 manuscript files, often \hfill\small inside Markdown/HTML. These require the actual print/EPUB build pipeline; they will not port reliably to ordinary HTML or EPUB.
  • Cross-reference: chapter_10.md:354–366 points to the Lawgiver as the next chapter, but Chapter 11 is Prudence and Chapter 12 is the Lawgiver.
  • Metadata: standardize title casing, author line, location, version/date, Scripture edition, copyright notices, and front/back matter order.
  • Build gate: render the entire final manuscript to the exact PDF and EPUB formats intended for submission, inspect every page, table, callout, note, hyperlink, orphan/widow, heading, and quotation, then run spelling and link checks on the rendered output.

Items verified or cleared

  • Verified Chapter 6’s “sacred authority is participation in the authority of Christ…” quotation is authentic Pope Leo XIV. The manuscript’s source—his November 21, 2025 address to participants in the international course promoted by the Tribunal of the Roman Rota—is correct. Calling it generically an “Address to the Roman Curia” would be less accurate.
  • Verified Chapter 8’s “Every person therefore is invited to ‘repent’ and to ‘believe’ in God’s merciful love” is correctly attributed to St. John Paul II, Redemptoris Missio 13. It is not a Pope Leo XIV sentence.
  • Verified The St. John Paul II wording that the body “and it alone” can make visible the invisible is authentic from the February 20, 1980 general audience. Its context must still be respected: it supports the sacramental meaning of the body, not a body–mind–soul three-substance anthropology.
  • Cleared “Infinite dignity” is legitimate Catholic wording in light of the 2024 declaration Dignitas Infinita. Surrounding claims may need explanation, but the adjective is not itself heterodox.
  • Strong The exact core definition is internally stable and can remain as the author’s proposed synthesis, provided the book consistently identifies it as fallible, analogical, non-exhaustive, and subordinate to the Church’s received faith.

Recommended revision sequence

  1. Freeze cosmetic copyediting. Sentence polish will be wasted while central theological structures are changing.
  2. Write a one-page doctrinal control sheet. It should define the framework’s limited status and set binding distinctions for divine self-gift/sacrifice, divine nature/human suffering, natural virtue/infused charity, grace types, initial justification/growth, faith/formed faith, Baptismal incorporation/Eucharistic communion, forgiveness/reconciliation, and justice/mercy.
  3. Correct global doctrinal language. Start with Chapters 3, 9, 10, 13, 14, and 17; then harmonize Introduction, Epilogue, and Author’s Note.
  4. Rebuild Chapters 15 and 16. Use primary Church teaching, precise moral objects, current evidence, pastoral safeguards, and concrete paths of repentance/healing. Remove deterministic child profiles and caricatures.
  5. Perform a source-and-permissions pass. Verify every direct quotation, Scripture edition, CCC paragraph, papal/council document, historical claim, medical statistic, etymology, and license notice.
  6. Perform a robustness-and-repetition pass while preserving the one-volume design. Give each chapter the space its argument requires, but consolidate any passage that merely restates an established conclusion without adding doctrinal precision, a new argumentative step, a necessary example, pastoral application, or deliberate rhetorical force.
  7. Commission two human reviews. At minimum: (a) a dogmatic/sacramental theologian for Trinity, Christology, grace, Church, Eucharist, and atonement; (b) a moral/pastoral theologian, preferably with trauma-informed clinical consultation, for sin, culpability, marriage, abortion, sexuality, abuse, and vulnerable readers. A Scripture scholar should review recurring exegesis and Jewish-Christian language.
  8. Copyedit and production-preflight only after content approval. Repair grammar, markup, citations, cross-references, typography, metadata, PDF, and EPUB.
  9. Submit as a revised review draft, not as presumptively final. Ask the diocese what form, supporting source ledger, permissions proof, and censor workflow it prefers under canons 827–830.
