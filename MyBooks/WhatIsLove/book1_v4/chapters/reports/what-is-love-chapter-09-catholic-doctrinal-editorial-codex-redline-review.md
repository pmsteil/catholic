## Chapter 9 · Sacrifice: Love's Eternal Self-Gift

### Chapter assessment

Chapter 9 has a compelling pastoral center: Christ freely offers Himself in love; Christian discipleship has a bodily and costly form; grace-enabled self-gift can be joined to Christ’s one sacrifice; mercy, justice and truth must govern what is offered; self-destruction and coerced suffering are not Christian sacrifice. The chapter also gives the Eucharist its proper centrality and repeatedly denies that our works add to Calvary.

The governing language nevertheless makes “cost” the test by which every love becomes real and places “sacrifice” inside God’s eternal life without distinguishing eternal Trinitarian self-gift from the incarnate Son’s historical offering in His humanity. Dense debt, price, punishment and payment language narrows the Paschal mystery. The open-hand account assigns the first operative movement to human deeds instead of prevenient grace; faith, obedience and charity are repeatedly treated as identical; dead faith is called no faith; ordered self-love, hope for one’s own salvation, contemplation and rest disappear. Several Mass explanations, sacramental-consent claims, Old Testament classifications, historical attributions, family applications and promised outcomes also need correction. The following redlines keep the chapter one robust, substantial unit while removing repetition that currently magnifies the errors.

### CH9-01 · Distinguish eternal Trinitarian self-gift from sacrifice in the strict sense

**Severity:** Stop-ship / Trinity and Christology · **Control:** DC-02, DC-03, DC-04 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:1`

**Issue.** The title can imply that cost, suffering, privation or death belongs eternally to the divine nature. The Son’s sacrifice is of infinite value because the divine Person freely offers Himself in His assumed humanity; God’s eternal communion is perfect self-gift without loss.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 202, 232–267, 456–483, 606–618.

**Source heading with proposed inline redline:**

# Sacrifice: ~~Love's Eternal Self-Gift~~ ++Christ’s Perfect Self-Gift++

**Editorial consequence.** The chapter can still relate Calvary to Trinitarian love without locating strict sacrifice inside the impassible divine nature.

### CH9-02 · Do not make visible cost the universal condition that turns every love into love

**Severity:** Stop-ship / definition of love · **Control:** DC-01, DC-07, DC-26 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:39–45`

**Issue.** Love normally bears cost when fidelity requires it, but loving contemplation, receiving another’s gift, prudent rest, gratitude and ordered self-care are not unreal merely because no present deprivation is visible. Sacrifice manifests and perfects love in the face of cost; it does not create every act of love from an otherwise loveless opinion.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1766, 1809, 1822–1829, 2099–2100, 2288.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

Sacrifice is where love ~~stops being theory and becomes reality~~ ++proves its fidelity when the beloved’s true good carries a cost++. This is where the rubber meets the road, where faith becomes works of love, where words become flesh.

Truth, justice, and mercy specify *the good*; sacrifice ~~is what makes it *for another* — willed at cost to me, rather than merely wished for from a safe distance. Without sacrifice, the *for another* never lands. You have an opinion about someone's good; you do not yet have love~~ ++is the freely accepted cost of willing that good when fidelity requires it. Love also receives, contemplates, rests and rightly cares for the self God created; but it does not abandon the good merely because the cost becomes real++.

Love is **sacrificial** ~~. Not in ideas. Not in theory. Not in good intentions. In action. In cost. In putting another's good before our own~~ ++when the true good requires costly self-gift. It must become action and cannot remain a good intention that refuses every real demand++.

**Editorial consequence.** The central thesis remains demanding without defining all love by felt deprivation.

### CH9-03 · Correct the identification of sloth, lukewarmness and refusal to sacrifice

**Severity:** Major / moral theology · **Control:** DC-10, DC-20, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:47–50`

**Issue.** Acedia is sorrow or aversion toward the divine good and the demands of charity; it is not simply failure to pay a cost. Lukewarmness is related but not a synonym coined by Christ for “the antithesis of sacrifice.” Failure to rise early to pray or missing a game for Mass also needs circumstances and the Sunday obligation stated accurately.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1866, 2094, 2180–2183, 2733; Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae* II-II, q.35.

**Source callout with proposed inline redline:**

**~~The antithesis of sacrifice is sloth — what Christ Himself named lukewarmness.~~ ++Sloth can make the soul shrink from sacrificial love.++**

Sacrifice is the costly self-gift that makes love real; ~~sloth is the refusal to pay. Not the inability, but the refusal. **Editorial consequence.** The warning retains its force while using the Church’s categories rather than treating three related ideas as synonyms.

### CH9-04 · Preserve parental duties without making career sacrifice or divorce outcomes categorical

**Severity:** Major / family and pastoral care · **Control:** DC-23, DC-25, DC-26 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:55–63`

**Issue.** Parents owe children presence and care, but prudence may require demanding employment, and children do not all experience the same schedule in the same way. Separation can be legitimate, and civil divorce can be tolerated for legal rights or children’s care. “They are made whole” promises a spiritual and psychological result from sacrifice itself.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1646–1651, 2201–2231, 2383.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

Children know instinctively what adults in our moment have been taught to forget: *they were supposed to be worth it.* They know a parent is supposed to ~~sacrifice career for bedtime, income for presence, personal dreams~~ ++order career, income, rest and personal hopes prudently around the serious duty++ of raising a child who matters. ++Families in poverty, illness, military service, caregiving or other grave circumstances may bear necessary absences that are not refusals of love.++

When a parent walks away from the covenant — ~~not because of abuse or grave betrayal, but because the covenant has become inconvenient~~ ++apart from legitimate separation, protection from abuse, grave betrayal or other serious circumstances++ — the child ~~learns~~ ++may be wounded by the apparent lesson++ that love lasts only until the cost gets too high.

The parent who sacrifices for her is not diminished by it — ~~they are made whole by it~~ ++rightly ordered self-gift can deepen virtue and communion, even when its earthly fruit is hidden or incomplete++.

**Editorial consequence.** The passage honors fidelity without judging families from appearances or guaranteeing outcomes.

### CH9-05 · Qualify formation by sacrifice and correct the *incurvatus in se* attribution

**Severity:** Major / sanctification and history · **Control:** DC-06, DC-08, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:67`

**Issue.** No isolated sacrifice automatically conforms a soul to Christ; acts formed by grace and charity dispose and shape us, while imprudent or sinful “sacrifices” can deform. The fixed Latin formula *homo incurvatus in se* is commonly associated with Luther’s development of an Augustinian theme, not a verified quotation from Augustine.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

**~~Each act of sacrifice conforms the soul more closely to Christ's.~~ ++Each grace-enabled, rightly ordered act of self-gift can conform the soul more closely to Christ.++** ... Over time we do not just *do* good things; by grace and repeated virtuous action we *become* good persons, conformed to Him. ... It frees us from the prison of the self — ~~what St. Augustine called the soul *curved in on itself* (*homo incurvatus in se*)~~ ++an inward curvature of sin developed in the Augustinian tradition and later expressed as *incurvatus in se*++ — and makes us more fully alive in love.

**Editorial consequence.** Sanctification remains participatory rather than automatic, and a vulnerable historical attribution is removed.

### CH9-06 · Present the sevenfold Old Testament scheme as a selected typology, not the sacrificial system’s own taxonomy

**Severity:** Major / Scripture and typology · **Control:** DC-20, DC-27, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:71–77`

**Issue.** The Day of Atonement includes several rites and offerings, and the scapegoat is a rite involving a live goat, not a separate sacrifice parallel to burnt, purification, reparation and well-being offerings. The selected seven can be a fruitful Christian meditation, but they are not a standard exhaustive biblical classification.

**Controlling sources:** Leviticus 1–7, 16; Hebrews 8–10; CCC 128–130.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

Here I want to touch on ~~seven sacrifices God established at the heart of Old Covenant worship — each a real cost paid by the worshiper~~ ++seven offerings or rites selected from Old Covenant worship. Take them in together — not as seven separate things, but as seven facets of the one act we will encounter at Calvary.

**Editorial consequence.** The rich typology survives without being presented as the Levitical system’s formal structure.

### CH9-07 · Do not generalize hand-laying and confession to every Israelite sacrifice

**Severity:** Major / biblical accuracy and Jewish continuity · **Control:** DC-20, DC-22, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:75`

Romans 11:29 concerns Israel’s election and gifts; it should not be made proof for each cultic assertion. **Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

None of what follows means the Old Covenant sacrifices were empty rehearsals, and none of it diminishes the faith of ~~the Israelite who laid his hand on the head of the lamb and confessed his sin~~ ++faithful Israelites who offered the rites God commanded; particular offerings involved hand-laying, and the Day of Atonement explicitly included confession over the scapegoat++. What the Cross gives us is not contemptuous replacement but fulfillment and perfection — ~~the same God, the same covenant love~~ ++the one faithful God bringing His promises to their divinely intended fullness in the New Covenant++.

**Editorial consequence.** Continuity is affirmed without homogenizing distinct rites or covenants.

The *asham* ~~required not only an animal but full restitution to the wronged party plus an additional fifth~~ ++sometimes accompanied restitution plus an additional fifth, especially when holy things or a neighbor had been defrauded++, because sin can steal and damage what justice requires us to repair. **Editorial consequence.** Satisfaction remains fully Catholic without making the Paschal mystery a single transaction.

**Issue.** Sin truly offends God and Christ truly suffered in His humanity, but the divine nature cannot be injured or diminished. Sin can also arise through weakness and disordered passion, not only a fully lucid free choice of destruction.

### CH9-13 · Correct the claims about sacramental effects and *teleios*

### CH9-14 · Distinguish mortal sin, judgment and the full Catholic doctrine of atonement

**Severity:** Stop-ship / sin, hell and atonement · **Control:** DC-05, DC-10, DC-13 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:121`

**Issue.** Not every sin merits eternal separation; mortal sin requires grave matter, full knowledge and deliberate consent. Hell is definitive self-exclusion, not a punishment God indiscriminately assigns. Mercy does not flow only after a debt has been “completely paid”; the Father lovingly initiates redemption and the Son freely offers Himself.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 393, 599–618, 1033–1037, 1854–1864.

**Editorial consequence.** The chapter no longer implies universal mortal guilt or a Father who becomes merciful only after payment.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1362–1372; Council of Trent, Session XXII, chapters 1–2.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

**Editorial consequence.** The paragraph teaches re-presentation without verbal ambiguity about repetition.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

### CH9-20 · Distinguish faith from obedience while preserving their living relation

**Severity:** Stop-ship / theological virtues · **Control:** DC-09 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:161–173`

**Issue.** Faith and obedience are inseparable in mature discipleship but are not “one thing.” Faith is the supernatural virtue by which we assent to God and what He reveals; obedience is a moral response. A disobedient act does not prove that faith as such is absent.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 142–165, 1814–1816.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

This is also why putting our faith in Jesus and obeying Jesus are ~~not two different things — they are one thing seen from two sides~~ ++distinct but ordered realities: genuine faith calls for obedience, and obedience expresses faith in action++. Obedience is ~~simply the relational word for what trust does when it has hands and feet. It is faith working through love, in the body~~ ++one embodied fruit of living faith working through charity++.

**Editorial consequence.** The organic unity remains without collapsing theological and moral virtues.

### CH9-21 · Do not say dead faith is no faith at all

**Severity:** Stop-ship / justification and faith · **Control:** DC-09 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:175–179`

**Issue.** James calls faith without works dead; Catholic theology calls it unformed faith. It remains faith, though it does not fully unite the believer to Christ or save without charity. **Controlling sources:** CCC 1814–1816; James 2:14–26; Benedict XVI, General Audience, November 19, 2008.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

A faith that produces no truth, no justice, and no mercy in the world is ~~not weak faith or shallow faith — it is no faith at all, because there is nothing for it to be in~~ ++dead or unformed faith: it remains assent, but without charity it does not fully unite the believer to Christ and cannot save++.

Not faith *plus* love. Not faith *and then* love. ~~Faith working through love — one organic reality.~~ ++Faith and charity remain distinct gifts, but saving or formed faith acts through charity as one living Christian life.++

Faith without love is ~~nothing~~ ++spiritually barren; even extraordinary faith avails nothing for salvation without charity++.

**Editorial consequence.** James and Paul are synthesized in the Church’s own categories rather than by denying unformed faith.

### CH9-22 · Restore ordered self-love and Christian hope for one’s own salvation

**Editorial consequence.** The chapter can oppose selfish individualism without condemning a theological virtue or a duty of charity toward oneself.

### CH9-23 · Describe growth in faith and love without making human sacrifice the engine of grace

**Severity:** Major / grace and virtue · **Control:** DC-06, DC-08, DC-09 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:179–181`

**Issue.** “Our sacrifice deepening our faith” and a self-reinforcing spiral can suggest acquired theological virtues. Faith, hope and charity are infused by God; our grace-enabled acts exercise and deepen them, while God remains the first cause.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

There is no either/or. There is only ~~His grace becoming our love through our sacrifice, and our sacrifice deepening our faith, and that faith opening us further to His grace. Faith leads us to love. Love grows us in faith. Round and round it goes ... the spiral of life itself~~ ++God’s grace awakening faith, hope and charity, and our free cooperation exercising those infused gifts in obedience and self-gift. God can increase them as we respond; even that growth remains grace from beginning to end++.

**Editorial consequence.** The lively account of sanctification no longer reads as though repeated works acquire supernatural faith and charity.

### CH9-24 · Qualify the Mass obligation and correct the etymology of *missa*

**Severity:** Major / liturgy and pastoral obligation · **Control:** DC-17, DC-27, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:193–195`

**Issue.** Sunday Mass is a grave obligation subject to legitimate excusing causes; “not optional” can be heard against the sick, caregivers or those without access. *Missa* does not literally mean “sent”; the dismissal gives the term its mission-related resonance.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 2180–2183; General Instruction of the Roman Missal 90.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

This is why the Mass is ~~not optional~~ ++the indispensable center of Catholic life and ordinarily a grave Sunday obligation, while illness, care of infants or other serious reasons can excuse attendance++ and not symbolic in the sense of *merely* symbolic—it is where He forms us into His Body.

*Ite, missa est* — ~~“Go forth; the Mass is ended.” The very word *missa* itself means *sent.*~~ ++rendered in the Roman Missal by dismissal formulas such as “Go forth, the Mass is ended.” The word *missa* is associated with this dismissal, and the rite itself sends the faithful on mission.++

**Editorial consequence.** The exhortation remains strong while respecting both liturgical history and legitimate impediments.

### CH9-25 · Remove the absolute claim that authority without explicit Christian submission always becomes tyranny

**Severity:** Major / authority and natural virtue · **Control:** DC-07, DC-14, DC-26 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:197`

**Issue.** All legitimate authority comes from God and is morally bounded, but non-Christians can exercise real natural virtue and just authority under natural law and actual grace. “Tyranny, every time” is a universal historical assertion the chapter cannot establish.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1897–1904, 1954–1960.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

There is only one way an authority bends rightly: ~~when the one who holds it has first knelt under the authority of Christ~~ ++by serving the true common good under the moral law, whose fullness is revealed in Christ++. Christian leaders should consciously kneel under Christ; others can nevertheless exercise genuine justice according to reason, conscience and grace. Authority without moral truth ~~becomes tyranny, sooner or later, every time~~ ++is vulnerable to becoming domination rather than service++.

**Editorial consequence.** Christ remains the fullness of just authority without erasing natural law or condemning every non-Christian ruler.

### CH9-26 · Describe Eucharistic re-presentation without literal sacred-time convergence

**Severity:** Stop-ship / Eucharistic theology · **Control:** DC-17 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:225–231`

**Issue.** The Mass makes the one sacrifice sacramentally present; it is not literal time travel, and the faithful do not leave chronological time. Revelation portrays the risen Lamb bearing the marks of sacrifice and interceding; it does not mean Christ is continuously being sacrificed in heaven.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 662, 1362–1372; Hebrews 7:25–27, 9:24–28.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

**We Enter Christ's Eternal Offering:** ++We Enter Sacramentally into Christ’s One Offering:++ The Mass makes present Christ's one sacrifice on Calvary—not repeating it or adding to it, but ~~entering into it. It's like a curtain is pulled back, and we are suddenly standing at the foot of the Cross~~ ++uniting the Church sacramentally to the once-for-all offering and its enduring saving power++.

The one sacrifice offered on Calvary is ~~perpetually present before the Father ... what Christ is eternally doing. We step out of ordinary time and into sacred time, where past, present, and future converge~~ ++complete and unrepeatable; the risen Christ eternally intercedes, and the liturgy sacramentally makes His Paschal mystery present here and now. We remain creatures in time while receiving the saving mystery that transcends time++.

**Editorial consequence.** The mystery is not diminished; it is expressed by the Church’s category of sacramental re-presentation.

### CH9-27 · Correct what the faithful offer and the water-and-wine symbolism

**Severity:** Major / Roman rite · **Control:** DC-17, DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:233–237`

**Issue.** We do not place “our sin” on the altar as an offering; we offer ourselves, repentance, work, suffering, prayer and praise united to Christ. The water prayer asks that we share in Christ’s divinity because He shared our humanity; the drop does not simply “represent the humanity of Christ,” nor are our offerings mingled with His Blood by that action.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1368, 2100; General Instruction of the Roman Missal 75; Roman Missal, preparation of the chalice.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

We place our own sacrifices—our ~~sin~~ ++repentance++, struggles, sufferings, mourning, daily acts of love—~~on the altar with Christ's. Like the drop of water the Priest adds to the chalice of wine to represent the humanity of Christ, our small, imperfect human offerings are mingled with His infinite and perfect blood~~ ++spiritually within the Church’s offering, asking that they be united to Christ’s one sacrifice. The water-and-wine prayer asks that we come to share in Christ’s divinity, who humbled Himself to share in our humanity; it should not be made a direct diagram of our offerings entering His Blood++.

**Editorial consequence.** The application now follows the rite’s actual words and Eucharistic doctrine.

### CH9-28 · Do not make conscious personal consent universal to sacrament or say the Incarnation depended on Mary

**Severity:** Stop-ship / grace, Mary and sacraments · **Control:** DC-06, DC-16 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:243–251`

**Issue.** God freely willed Mary’s fiat as real cooperation, but the divine plan is not dependent as though God were contingent on a creature. Infant Baptism and Eastern infant Chrismation show that conscious candidate consent is not universal. Sacraments act by Christ’s power, while fruit in recipients with the use of reason depends on disposition.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 490–494, 1127–1129, 1250–1252, 1306–1308.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

Even the Incarnation ~~depended on free human consent~~ ++was freely willed by God to include Mary’s real and uncoerced fiat++. ... The Church builds this same free-consent into ~~every sacrament—explicitly so in Marriage, Holy Orders, and Confirmation, in which the candidate's own free yes is integral to the sacrament itself~~ ++sacramental discipline according to each sacrament and recipient. Matrimony requires the spouses’ consent, ordination requires a free candidate, and those with the use of reason must not be coerced; yet infant Baptism validly gives grace before personal conscious consent, and sacramental fruit must be distinguished from validity++. Free reception is not an afterthought to grace; ~~it is the very shape grace takes when it lands in a creature made for love~~ ++for adults it is itself a response awakened and enabled by grace++.

**Editorial consequence.** Human cooperation remains genuine without contradicting infant initiation or divine initiative.

### CH9-29 · Replace “physically” and qualify every daily sacrifice as redemptive work

**Severity:** Major / Eucharist and cooperation · **Control:** DC-05, DC-17 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:253–260`

**Issue.** Eucharistic communion is real, sacramental and substantial, but “physically” can imply local bodily contact or digestion of Christ’s glorified body in the ordinary mode. Not every self-described sacrifice is joined fruitfully to Christ; it must be morally good, grace-enabled and offered in charity.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

Christ established the Eucharist so that He can give Himself truly and substantially under sacramental signs and unite us with Him ~~physically, spiritually, and intellectually~~ ++sacramentally, spiritually and ecclesially++. In daily life, ~~every sacrifice we make~~ ++every morally good, grace-enabled self-offering made in charity++ for spouse, children, neighbor or enemy ~~becomes part of Christ's ongoing redemptive work~~ ++can be united to Christ’s one redemptive sacrifice and bear fruit in His Body++.

Our sacrifices, united with Christ’s perfect sacrifice, participate in His redemptive work. This is not ~~allowing His sacrifice to unite us to His perfect sacrifice~~ ++uniting one sacrifice to another; it is Christ drawing our lives into His one perfect offering++ so that we carry His love to others.

**Editorial consequence.** Real Presence and cooperation are affirmed without physicalism or automatic redemptive status.

### CH9-30 · Do not say evil is undone apart from argument, lawful force and Christ’s sovereign action

**Severity:** Major / salvation and social action · **Control:** DC-14, DC-15, DC-19 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:265–275`

**Issue.** Charity overcomes evil, but truthful argument, just laws, institutional reform and proportionate force can be instruments of love. Christ did not cease acting when He ascended or hand salvation over as though the Church replaced Him; He remains the Savior and makes the Church His sacrament and instrument.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 849–856, 1897–1904, 2263–2267; *Lumen Gentium* 1.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

This is how evil is actually undone in the world — ~~not by argument, not by force, but~~ ++ultimately by Christ’s grace, often working through conversion and sacrificial charity, and also through truthful persuasion, just institutions and, when morally legitimate, proportionate protection++.

He ascended to the Father and ~~entrusted the salvation of the world to His Body~~ ++sent His Spirit and made His Body, the Church, the universal sacrament and instrument of His saving mission++. The Church does not replace an absent Savior; the risen Christ continues to act in and through her.

**Editorial consequence.** Personal conversion, social justice and Christ’s unique agency are held together.

### CH9-31 · Correct the martyr paragraph’s language, disputed claims and Tertullian quotation

**Severity:** Major / history and source control · **Control:** DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:281–283`

**Issue.** “Stoners” is jarringly colloquial. Polycarp’s direct discipleship under John is a tradition, not an uncontested historical fact. Tertullian’s actual line is commonly translated “the blood of Christians is seed,” not the later “blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church.” “Every saint ... was already a martyr” collapses technical martyrdom into broad witness.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

Stephen, the first deacon, prayed for ~~his stoners~~ ++those stoning him++. St. Polycarp of Smyrna — ~~the very disciple of John~~ ++whom early tradition associates with apostolic witnesses and, in later accounts, with the Apostle John++ — refused to renounce Christ. ... As Tertullian wrote, more exactly, *“the blood of Christians is seed”* (*Apologeticus* 50); the familiar phrase “the blood of the martyrs is the seed of the Church” is a later rendering of that thought. Every saint is a witness in the broad baptismal sense, but the Church reserves **martyr** in its proper canonical sense for one who dies because of hatred of the faith or a Christian virtue.

**Editorial consequence.** The witnesses remain powerful while the chapter stops turning traditions and paraphrases into exact history.

### CH9-32 · State Friday penance according to universal and particular law

**Severity:** Major / discipline · **Control:** DC-28 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:285`

**Issue.** Friday penance is universal, but the precise obligation to abstain varies by season, age and episcopal conference. In the United States, abstinence is obligatory on Lenten Fridays; outside Lent another penitential practice may be substituted under the bishops’ discipline.

**Controlling sources:** Code of Canon Law 1249–1253; USCCB, *Pastoral Statement on Penance and Abstinence*.

**Source sentence with proposed inline redline:**

**Friday ~~abstinence~~ ++penance++** plants a small weekly sacrifice into every ordinary week, so that the day Christ died is never just another day. ++The universal Church observes Fridays as penitential days; abstinence and permitted substitutions are governed by canon law and the applicable bishops’ conference, with abstinence obligatory on the Fridays of Lent.++

**Editorial consequence.** The book teaches the permanent discipline without overstating one local form as universal every Friday.

### CH9-33 · Do not define every sin as selfishness or every vice as transformed by sacrifice alone

**Severity:** Major / moral theology · **Control:** DC-01, DC-10 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:293–308`

**Issue.** Sin is an offense against reason, truth and right conscience and a failure in love rooted in disordered attachment; choosing one’s own good at another’s expense is common but not exhaustive. Sacrifice is not the direct contrary virtue of all seven capital sins, and joy or visible fruit is not guaranteed.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1849–1876, 1866, 1804–1845.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

**Against Selfishness:** ~~The root of all sin is choosing our own good at others' expense.~~ ++Sin is a disordered choice against God’s law and genuine love; pride and disobedience are deep roots, and selfish preference often expresses them.++ Sacrificial love can reverse selfishness by choosing another’s true good at personal cost.

**Against the Seven Deadly Sins:** ... Sacrificial love ~~transforms each sin~~ ++can assist the contrary virtues by drawing the soul outward, but each vice also requires its proper remedy—humility, generosity, chastity, kindness, temperance, patience and diligence++.

Sacrifice combats despair ~~by producing joy when we see the fruit~~ ++when grace gives hope even before any fruit can be seen++.

**Editorial consequence.** The four-part framework remains a pastoral aid rather than an invented exhaustive taxonomy of sin and virtue.

### CH9-34 · Do not blame an abused wife for “enabling” because she remains

**Severity:** Stop-ship / abuse and victim safety · **Control:** DC-25 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:310–314`

**Issue.** Remaining in an abusive home can result from coercion, danger, poverty, trauma, immigration status or lack of safe options; it does not prove enabling or a failure to love. The text should place responsibility on the abuser, encourage immediate safety and competent help, and distinguish forgiveness from access or cohabitation.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 2263–2265, 2383; Code of Canon Law 1153.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

A wife who stays with an abusive husband ~~isn't practicing sacrificial love—she's enabling his sin and endangering herself and her children~~ ++must not be blamed for the abuse or presumed to be enabling it. Abuse is the abuser’s responsibility, and coercion or lack of safe options can make leaving dangerous++. Authentic love never requires accepting violence. She may seek immediate safety, emergency assistance, pastoral and clinical support, legal protection and legitimate separation for herself and her children. Forgiveness does not require restored access, trust or common life; mercy may seek the abuser’s conversion through firm boundaries and consequences.

**Editorial consequence.** The safety teaching becomes genuinely victim-centered rather than adding culpability to the wounded person.

### CH9-35 · Correct the contraception metaphor and affirm that marriage already constitutes a family

**Severity:** Stop-ship / marriage, life and infertility · **Control:** DC-23, DC-25 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:316–326`

**Issue.** Abortion intentionally ends an existing human life; contraception acts against the procreative meaning of the marital act but ordinarily does not “sacrifice the child.” A valid marriage is already a family and remains fruitful in many ways when conception does not occur. The present wording wounds infertile couples and collapses distinct moral objects.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 1601, 1643–1654, 2366–2379.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

**Attacks on life.** Abortion ~~and contraceptive culture reject the life-giving purpose of marital union itself — a sacrifice of the child, or of fruitfulness~~ ++intentionally takes the life of a child already conceived. Contraceptive acts are morally distinct: they deliberately close the marital act to its procreative meaning++, often in exchange for perceived convenience, career or lifestyle. ~~A family cannot begin where its fruit is treated as optional or disposable.~~ ++Marriage itself establishes a family; spouses who experience infertility retain the full dignity of their covenant and can bear authentic marital, adoptive, spiritual and social fruit.++

**Editorial consequence.** Catholic teaching remains clear while distinct acts, infertile spouses and the reality of a childless family are treated truthfully.

### CH9-36 · Do not prescribe exhaustion or promise that sacrifice delivers joy and completion

**Severity:** Major / caregivers and mental health · **Control:** DC-23, DC-25, DC-26 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:328`

**Issue.** “Work yourself to exhaustion” can encourage self-neglect and shame exhausted caregivers who feel no reward. Sacrifice can carry profound joy, but earthly results and emotions are not guaranteed; prudence, rest and limits can themselves serve vocation.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

When we sacrifice for others, we ~~receive the joy, meaning, and fulfillment~~ ++can discover a deeper joy and meaning, even though fatigue, grief and hidden fruit may remain++. ~~Work yourself to exhaustion serving a stranger~~ ++Give time and strength prudently in service of a stranger++, and you may know that something real has been done. ... Give up career advancement to care for aging parents, ~~and you find a purpose no promotion could have given you~~ ++and the act can embody covenant fidelity, while the real burden still deserves respite and support++. Sacrifice ~~does not diminish us; it completes us ... promises nothing and delivers joy~~ ++is not self-erasure; rightly ordered under grace, it can perfect charity, but it does not guarantee visible success or consoling feelings++.

**Editorial consequence.** Self-gift is commended without romanticizing burnout or making emotional reward its proof.

### CH9-37 · Do not diagnose Peter’s denial as the inevitable result of having practiced no sacrifice

**Severity:** Major / Scripture and moral agency · **Control:** DC-20, DC-26 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:330–334`

**Issue.** Peter had already left home and livelihood to follow Christ. The Gospels portray real fear, weakness, presumption and denial, but do not say he had practiced no sacrifice or that sloth guaranteed betrayal. The present account invents a causal psychology and then applies it universally.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

Peter's betrayal was not malice; it was ~~the inevitable collapse of a love that had never been tested by real sacrifice. He had loved Jesus at the table. He could not love Him at the fire~~ ++a grave denial under fear after an overconfident promise. He had already left much to follow Jesus, yet at the fire discovered that prior sacrifice did not remove his need for grace, humility and conversion++. ... **Editorial consequence.** Peter remains a searching example without the chapter claiming knowledge the text does not give.

### CH9-38 · Mark the closing threefold scheme as the author’s heuristic and consolidate repeated thesis language

**Severity:** Major / authority, logic and structure · **Control:** DC-01, DC-29 · **Location:** `chapter_09.md:351–389`

**Issue.** The close again says sacrifice is the cost that makes truth, justice and mercy real, then labels transaction, barrenness and betrayal as consequences “the Church has long recognized.” The threefold taxonomy is the author’s synthesis, not an established ecclesial list. The thesis has already appeared repeatedly, so the close should state its qualified form once.

**Source passage with proposed inline redline:**

For Sacrifice is not a fourth principle beside truth, justice, and mercy. It is ~~the cost that makes them real~~ ++the self-gift that remains faithful when living truthfully, justly and mercifully carries a cost++. ... This is the call to the fullness of God's love — to pour out your life ~~for all that is authentically true, just, and merciful, as He poured out His~~ ++in union with Christ whenever the true good entrusted to you requires it, while also receiving, resting and caring prudently for the life God has given you++.

What follows are ~~three of the gravest consequences the Church has long recognized — not an exhaustive list, but the principal distortions~~ ++three recurring distortions this chapter proposes as a pastoral summary—not a formal or exhaustive classification by the Church++—when the thread of sacrifice is pulled out of love entirely.

**Editorial consequence.** The chapter ends with one doctrinally controlled thesis and identifies its taxonomy honestly; repeated absolute formulations elsewhere can then be consolidated around this wording.

## Chapter 9 disposition

**Not ready for episcopal review in its present form.** The chapter’s central witness to Christ’s free self-offering and Christian discipleship is worth preserving, but the Trinity/Incarnation distinction, atonement synthesis, priority of grace, faith–charity distinction, Eucharistic explanations, Scripture/history controls and vulnerable-reader passages require correction first. The chapter should remain one volume and one substantial chapter; robustness is not the problem. The editorial need is to consolidate recurring thesis passages so that a single precise teaching—not the repeated absolutist form—governs the whole.

**Manuscript status:** unchanged. No proposal in this report has been applied to `chapter_09.md`.
