Section: Case Studies: Discernment in Action › The Parent Who Asks You to Help Him Die · line 188

… To refuse both the needle and the abandonment — simply to stay — is a single act in which every facet of love is present at once. It honors what is sacred, for his life is God's gift; truth, for his life is still his life; justice, for his dignity is real and does not depend on what he can do; mercy, for his pain is real and must be eased by every honest means; and sacrifice, for it pours out one's own time, sleep, and comfort. And it keeps faith with the covenant between parent and child, a communion that does not end when his usefulness does. This is love that has learned it exists not to be served but to serve, and that a life is honored not for what it can still produce but for the simple, unrepeatable fact that it still is.
