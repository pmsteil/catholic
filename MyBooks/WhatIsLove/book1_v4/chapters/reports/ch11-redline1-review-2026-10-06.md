## 1 · The opening picks up Chapter 10’s Bridge; facets throughout, never pillars or threads

**Why.** Chapter 10's Bridge ends on prudence as "the virtue that teaches us *how* to love, so that truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice are lived as one act toward the real person in front of us." The opening now picks that up ("between the definition and the person in front of you") without re-running the Bridge. **Your 7 October ruling:** every part of the definition is a *facet* of one love, like one diamond seen from every side, and truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice are facets too, not a separate class. "Thread by thread" and the three "pillars" go. "The four facets" goes too, because it suggests love has only four: where the chapter means those four it now names them (truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice), and where it means the whole it says "the facets of love." The other chapters are queued as their own todo (memory.html Future Task 167). "Proved" → "showed" is the Chapter 2 ruling (todo 43 flagged this exact line). The sin definition now lets the Church speak: CCC 1849 calls sin "failure in genuine love for God and neighbor." The old wording ("a perceived good for ourselves at the expense of… another") left out God and sins against oneself.
