Natural law · the Church's words, and why she holds this hope

Section: Natural Law: God's Moral Order Written on Hearts · line 121

… The Church's answer is careful and honest. The four pillars of love All salvation comes from Christ: "the one Christ is the mediator and the way of salvation; he is present to us in his body which is the Church" (CCC 846). Truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice are not a series of tasks that save by completion; they are the shape of love as God designed it. And the fullest, most precisely articulated teaching on what love requires is found in the Catholic Church — including the doctrinal clarity that tells a soul exactly when love has been broken and exactly how it is restored. But God's grace is not imprisoned by human ignorance. This follows the same logic as the sacraments: God binds us to the sacraments as the ordinary means of grace — but God is not Himself bound by them. He can pour grace through any channel He chooses. Those who follow the light they have been given — the natural law, the grace active in conscience, the partial truth written on their hearts — "God has bound salvation to the sacrament of Baptism, but he himself is not bound by his sacraments" (CCC 1257). And so the Church teaches that "those who, through no fault of their own, do not know the Gospel of Christ or his Church, but who nevertheless seek God with a sincere heart, and, moved by grace, try in their actions to do his will as they know it through the dictates of their conscience — those too may achieve eternal salvation" (CCC 847, quoting Lumen Gentium 16). Such people are, in a real sense, doing the will of the Father, even without yet knowing the Father's name. God's perfect justice gives each person what they are due, accounting for what was offered to them and not what was withheld through no fault of their own (CCC 1257, 1260; , and whatever grace reaches them is Christ's grace. Why does the Church hold this hope? Because "since Christ died for all, and since all men are in fact called to one and the same destiny, which is divine, we must hold that the Holy Spirit offers to all the possibility of being made partakers, in a way known to God, of the Paschal mystery" (CCC 1260). Every person bears the same God-given dignity; God's justice does not hold against anyone what he could not know; and His mercy can be at work in the love a person pours out for God, neighbor and self before he has the words to say why. That love does not save by itself; it is grace already at work. The Council is just as honest that "often men, deceived by the Evil One, have become vain in their reasonings" ( Lumen Gentium 16). But none , and the Church "still has the obligation and also the sacred right to evangelize all men" (CCC 848). None of this cancels the necessity of the Church — it only deepens the urgency of bringing the fullness to those who are already responding to its shadow.
