Section: When Love Stops Being Theory · lines 45–51

… And the refusal to give — the quiet refusal most of us make most of the time — has an old name:.

[callout box removed; its text becomes body prose]
The antithesis of The sin directly opposite to sacrifice is sloth —, what Christ Himself named lukewarmness. Sacrifice is the costly self-gift that makes love real; sloth is the refusal to give it. Not the inability, but the refusal. The Christian who loves God "in his heart" but will not rise early to pray, will not give to the poor, will not tell an inconvenient truth, will not miss a game for Mass — this is not love, however much it feels like love. It is love theorized and seldom lived. Christ's verdict is fierce: "So, because you are lukewarm, and neither cold nor hot, I will spew you out of my mouth" (Revelation 3:16). The Catechism names lukewarmness as "hesitation or negligence in responding to divine love," and beyond it acedia (ah-SEE-dee-ah), spiritual sloth, which "goes so far as to refuse the joy that comes from God" (CCC 2094). Aquinas calls acedia contrary to charity itself, because it is sorrow at the very good in which charity rejoices (Summa Theologiae II-II, q.35, a.3).

This chapter will show why love that costs nothing does nothing, and why sloth is not harmless laziness but a quiet betrayal of the God who held nothing back from us.
