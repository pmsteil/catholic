Sent into every sphere.

One Body, Sent into the World to Love: the appeal to the reader

Two spheres were missing that most shape our age: media (the newsroom and the screen, where most people now learn what to believe about love and truth) and science and technology (the laboratory, and the code and AI that increasingly decide what we see and how we are treated). The Church has written on both (Inter Mirifica on media; the 2025 note Antiqua et Nova on artificial intelligence), but the list needs only the images. One clause adds them before the family table, which stays last as the most intimate sphere. Others I considered and left out to keep the sentence moving: the military and police (largely covered by “every form of authority”), the arts, and the trades and farm (the new ending now names “the job site”). The ending (“His truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice”), parents as first teachers, and “drifts toward tyranny” stand.

Section: One Body, Sent into the World to Love · line 271

… Christ sends His Body into every sphere where human beings live and decide and lead. We are to be His mind in the classroom, His judgment in the courtroom and the boardroom, His conscience in the legislature, His care in the hospital, His witness in the marketplace, His voice in the newsroom and on the screen, His wisdom in the laboratory and in the code that now shapes so much of our lives, His patience around the family dinner table. Especially the teachers, parents first among them, because what a generation believes about love and truth begins in the chairs of the ones who teach them it learns from those who teach it. Especially the leaders, in every form of authority, because every authority either bends toward the good of those it serves, or away from it. And there is only one way an authority bends rightly: when the one who holds it has first knelt under the authority of Christ. No leader leads well who has not himself been led; no teacher teaches truth who has not first submitted to the Truth. Christ said it plainly: "It shall not be so among you; but whoever would be great among you must be your servant," (Matthew 20:26). Authority under Christ is service; authority without Christ becomes tyranny drifts, sooner or later, every time toward tyranny. What the world needs is a future filled with good Catholics — disciples in every sphere who understand that the mission of Jesus, now entrusted to His Body, is nothing less than this: that His perfect love is what saves the world. So look at the place where you spend your days: the desk, the kitchen, the classroom, the job site. That is where He is sending you, to make His perfect love known there: His truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice.
