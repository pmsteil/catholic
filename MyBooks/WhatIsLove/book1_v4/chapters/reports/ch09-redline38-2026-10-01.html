Never sin to help another.

The Hierarchy of Sacrifice: say plainly why we may never sin for another

The line meant this: love may give up anything except our own faithfulness to God, so we may never sin “for” someone. But “the image of God within us” was the wrong picture. It was unclear, and theologically loose, because sin wounds and disfigures the image but cannot destroy it. The Church states the principle in one line: “One may never do evil so that good may result from it” (CCC 1789, vatican.va). A plain sentence follows to say why: sin wounds both the one who commits it and the one it claims to serve.

Section: The Hierarchy of Sacrifice · line 295

… We sacrifice our comfort for others, but never our souls: we can never choose sin to "help" another, however noble the intention seems, because the one thing we may never lay on the altar is the image of God within us . The Church puts it simply: "One may never do evil so that good may result from it" (CCC 1789). Sin is never a gift of love; it wounds both the one who commits it and the one it claims to serve.
