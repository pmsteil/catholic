You were right, and the auto-captions confirm your wording (17:54–18:15). The new quotation names the two different aims side by side. Barron's next sentence, "sometimes to love someone means you are going to hurt his feelings," is kept because it makes "Empathy is not to hurt the feelings of another" read unmistakably in print; without it the line could be taken the wrong way. "To will the good of the other" is St. Thomas's own definition (ST I-II q.26 a.4), and the Catechism takes it up at CCC 1766, so the cf. ties Barron to the Church's text. Note 4's timestamp moves to 17:59.

One refinement to how you put it, which the chapter already follows: empathy itself is not the counterfeit. In the same passage Barron says feelings of the heart can "clue you into the direction of love," and Leo XIV calls empathy a call from God. The fault is empathy made the prime value (Barron's words at 18:43) and set in love's place, cut loose from truth and justice. The sentence right after the quotation says exactly that, so it stays.
