Line 226: only Christ founds the Church; the apostles plant it

line 226

… ordered, governed communities, each gathered around its bishop, which the Church still calls particular churches (CCC 833). To "start a church," But notice what they were not doing. They were not founding new Churches. There is one Church, and only Christ had the authority to found it; He did so when He called the Twelve and built her on them (cf. Eph 2:20). Every local church the apostles planted, and every one their successors have planted since, is that one Church made present in a new place, by His authority and not their own: "The Church of Christ is really present in all legitimately organized local groups of the faithful" (CCC 832). And because Christ cannot be divided, neither can His Church or His commission: no one, in any age, has the authority to found a church apart from the Body He founded. St. Paul put the question to the first Christians who began rallying around rival leaders: "Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?" (1 Corinthians 1:13). To plant a local, or particular, church in the New Testament sense, then, is not to gather an affinity group around a charismatic teacher. It is to establish a extend the one governing body for believers that Christ founded, which hands on the deposit of faith intact. …
