Two fixes. (1) The paragraph says the reader “can recite” the love Christ means, but the bold sentence after it is not the definition the reader has learned. It is a one-off phrasing used only in this chapter. The canonical definition never appears in Chapter 9 at all, so this is the natural place for it, word for word. The Aquinas-shaped line follows in plain form, with “sacrificially” becoming “whatever it costs”: to will the good for another, and to give what that costs. (2) “Most readers stop here” could be read as the readers of this book, right before “dear reader.” Now it's “Many hear love here as something sentimental.” The next paragraph also uses “sacrificially will the truth, justice, and mercy of God”; that reads fine as a restatement and is left alone.

Section: One Body, Sent into the World to Love · line 273

… "By this all men will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another" (John 13:35). Most readers stop here and Many hear love here as something sentimental: be nice, be kind, be merciful when convenient. But you, dear reader, by now can recite the love Christ actually means. To love is to be someone who sacrificially wills : God's Perfect Love is the sacred gift of covenant which binds truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice into life-giving communion. To love with that love is to will the truth, justice, and mercy of God for another. , whatever it costs. That is the love by which we will be known, the love the world has been waiting for. …
