And here we need to recover what the word obedience itself actually means. In Latin, oboedire — from which our English "obey" comes — is built from ob- ("toward") and audire ("to listen"): to obey literally means to listen toward someone. , and the Church defines it for us: "To obey (from the Latin ob-audire, to 'hear or listen to') in faith is to submit freely to the word that has been heard, because its truth is guaranteed by God, who is Truth itself" (CCC 144). We obey because we trust the One who speaks. The Greek of the New Testament, hypakouō, says the same thing: to hear under, to listen attentively to the one who speaks. …

Faith Working Through Love: faith's first sacrifice (CCC 143, 155)

Section: Faith Working Through Love · line 169

… one cannot listen to a rulebook the way one listens to a person. ¶The Church also names what faith lays down first: "By faith, man completely submits his intellect and his will to God" (CCC 143). This is the first sacrifice, before any gift of time or money: our own mind and will, placed in the hands of a Teacher we trust. Even that is His gift: we believe because His perfect love first moves the will (cf. CCC 155). Chapter 4 showed that true authority belongs only to the author, and Jesus is the trustworthy Author of love itself. That is why submitting to Him is not a slave's fear but a disciple's trust: we trust the Teacher because we know He is the source of all that is good. Whatever word we use for belonging to Jesus (faith, trust, following, discipleship), none of them is possible without hearing the Teacher, submitting to what He teaches, and then doing it: "Every one then who hears these words of mine and does them will be like a wise man who built his house upon the rock;" (Matthew 7:24). Otherwise, what exactly are we trusting?

Faith Working Through Love: simpler wording

Section: Faith Working Through Love · line 169

… The Greek of the New Testament, hypakouō, says the same thing: to hear under, to listen attentively to the one who speaks. And in the Hebrew Scriptures, the verb is shama, the very word at the heart of the Shema, Israel's daily prayer: "Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD; and you shall love the LORD your God with all your heart, and with all your soul, and with all your might" (Deuteronomy 6:4–5). To hear the Lord, in the biblical idiom, is to obey Him; to obey Him is to hear Him. Obedience is irreducibly relational personal at its root: one cannot listen to a rulebook the way one listens to a person.
