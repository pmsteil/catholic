After the Great Commission: to teach requires authority, and the command outlasts the apostles

line 222

… Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all that I have commanded you; and lo, I am with you always, to the close of the age" (Matthew 28:18–20). ¶Notice two things that command takes for granted. The first is authority. To make disciples is to make learners (in Greek, mathetai), and a disciple learns only by trusting his teacher enough to submit his own judgment to him; a student who accepts only what he already agrees with has learned nothing from anyone. So Christ bound the apostles' teaching to His own: "He who hears you hears me, and he who rejects you rejects me, and he who rejects me rejects him who sent me" (Luke 10:16). The second is time. The command does not end with the death of the last apostle; it runs "to the close of the age." A mission that outlasts the men who first received it needs an authority that outlasts them too. The Church has always read it so: the mission Christ entrusted to the apostles "will continue to the end of time," and so "the apostles took care to appoint successors" (CCC 860).
