    Where: chapters/chapter_12.md · "The Atheist's Dilemma," new paragraph after "...only a ground outside the practice can close that gap." The paragraph that follows ("Every time an atheist says 'That's wrong!'...") is unchanged and moves down.

    ...that we cannot help treating ourselves as precious. But a necessity of our practice is not yet a truth about the world; only a ground outside the practice can close that gap. One last skeptic remains: the one who says rules can bind without a rule-giver — after all, "follow the evidence" binds every honest thinker, and no one seems to stand behind it. But notice what he just did: he told you what you ought to do. If nothing needs to stand behind an ought, then nothing stands behind his, and we may politely set it aside. If something must, then his needs it too. Either way he has conceded the point: an ought is a claim laid on a person — and no thing — no rock, no rule, no equation — has ever laid a claim on anyone. Only a Someone can.
