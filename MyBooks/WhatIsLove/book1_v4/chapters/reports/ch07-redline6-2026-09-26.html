All seven candidates in the order Redline 5 proposed. Green = added, plain = kept, red = cut from the opening. The four kept run: who God is → His justice and love together → what He asks of us → the Church on why justice is His very being. Nothing cut is lost: all three stay in Further Study, and two are already quoted in the body.

Opening quotations, in order

Deuteronomy 32:4

“The Rock, his work is perfect; for all his ways are justice. A God of faithfulness and without iniquity, just and right is he.”

— Deuteronomy 32:4

Keep (new). The nearest Scripture comes to “God is justice”: all His ways are justice. Opens on who God is.

Psalm 89:14

“Righteousness and justice are the foundation of thy throne; steadfast love and faithfulness go before thee.”

— Psalm 89:14

Keep (new). Justice and steadfast love hold up the same throne: the book’s thesis in one verse.

Isaiah 30:18

“Therefore the LORD waits to be gracious to you; therefore he exalts himself to show mercy to you. For the LORD is a God of justice; blessed are all those who wait for him.”

— Isaiah 30:18

Cut. Once quoted whole, its weight falls on mercy, which Psalm 89:14 already joins to justice and which is Ch. 8’s subject. Moves to Further Study.

Micah 6:8

“He has showed you, O man, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness, and to walk humbly with your God?”

— Micah 6:8

Keep. The turn from who God is to what He asks of us.

Isaiah 1:17

“learn to do good; seek justice, correct oppression; defend the fatherless, plead for the widow.”

— Isaiah 1:17

Cut. Already quoted twice in the body (the opposing-sin paragraph and the partiality section) and listed in Further Study; a third appearance at the top adds nothing.

Matthew 23:23

“Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites! for you tithe mint and dill and cummin, and have neglected the weightier matters of the law, justice and mercy and faith; these you ought to have done, without neglecting the others.”

— Matthew 23:23

Cut. Your own note: move it to Further Study if the opening runs long, and it is already there. Its point, justice held with mercy and faith, is the Binding Check’s work.

CCC 271, quoting St. Thomas Aquinas

“In God, power, essence, will, intellect, wisdom, and justice are all identical.”

— CCC 271, quoting St. Thomas Aquinas

Keep (new), last. The Church’s own voice for the chapter’s title.
