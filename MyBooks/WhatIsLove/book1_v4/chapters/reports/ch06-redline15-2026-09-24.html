… But the Scriptures were read inside a life far larger than any list of books. Through those centuries the Church baptized and taught, celebrated the Eucharist every Lord's Day, ordained bishops in unbroken lines, and buried her martyrs through wave after wave of persecution. ¶By the year 300, historians estimate, roughly one person in ten in the Roman Empire was a Christian, some six million souls. In 314 three bishops came from Britain to a council at Arles. In 325 more than two hundred and fifty bishops gathered at Nicaea from Europe, Africa and Asia, and at least one from Persia, beyond the empire's edge. By the middle of the fourth century the kings of Armenia, Georgia and Ethiopia had become Christians. The Creed we still recite was confessed at Nicaea and completed at Constantinople in 381, sixteen years before Carthage. ¶All of this, the baptizing and teaching, the Eucharist on the Lord's Day, the bishops in succession, the witness of the martyrs, the Creed, was the apostolic deposit being handed on: not a book waiting to be compiled, but the Church, "in her doctrine, life and worship," transmitting "to every generation all that she herself is, all that she believes" (CCC 78). …
