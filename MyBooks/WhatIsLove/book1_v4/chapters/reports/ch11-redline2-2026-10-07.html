Section: Prudence: The Cardinal Virtue of Application · line 51

And this is no medieval invention. Centuries before St. Thomas systematized them — and before a word of the New Testament was written — Scripture had already named the cardinal virtues, prudence among them. The Book of Wisdom says of God's own wisdom that "she teaches self-control and prudence, justice and courage; nothing in life is more profitable for men than these" (Wisdom 8:7). There, in those very pages, the fourfold pattern of virtue stands fully drawn, with prudence at its head. What we are unfolding is not a later accretion onto the faith; it is read straight out of Scripture. Long before St. Thomas set them in order, Scripture had already named the four virtues the Church calls cardinal, from the Latin word for "hinge," because "all the others are grouped around them" (CCC 1805). The verse from the Book of Wisdom that opens this chapter names all four: self-control, prudence, justice and courage. What we are unfolding is not something added to the faith later; it is read out of Scripture.
