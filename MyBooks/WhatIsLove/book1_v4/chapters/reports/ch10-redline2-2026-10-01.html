Line 197 · the drop of water, and what love asks

Section: How Love Creates Life-Giving Communion › Sacramental: The Eucharist—Perfect Binding Made Real · line 197

… We do not climb up to God; He pours Himself down into us and overwhelms us with a love we could never produce. Like Mary at the Annunciation—"the And the angel said to her, 'The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore the child to be born will be called holy, the Son of God.'" (Luke 1:35)—we come to the altar empty-handed, receiving what we cannot give ourselves. We bring only the smallest offering: the tiny drop of water the priest mingles into the wine, our poor humanity lost in the abundance of His divinity. "By the mystery of this water and wine," the Church prays, "may we come to share in the divinity of Christ who humbled himself to share in our humanity." That drop adds nothing to the chalice is real, but beside the infinite love it joins, it is almost nothing. It simply consents to be taken up into something infinitely greater than itself. And that is all love ever asks of us—not to add to His gift, but to let Him : to receive His gift of love and let it fill us.

Section: Unity Requires Submission to the Authority of His Perfect Love · line 288

This is why Jesus, praying for our unity, grounds it entirely in God Himself: "that they may all be one; even as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be in us, so that the world may believe that thou hast sent me" (John 17:21). He does not pray that we would merely agree with one another, or like one another, or organize ourselves efficiently. He prays that we would be drawn into the one love of the Trinity. Our unity with each other is the fruit of our shared submission to Him. Two people do not become one by staring at each other; they become one by both surrendering to the same Lord.
