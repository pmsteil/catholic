Your two ideas, kept in your order. "Enables us, and teaches us": grace first, then the lesson. Your draft line ("To repent, to be granted forgiveness so that we can forgive others is the healing…") had the right sequence but ran long as one subject. It is recast as three steps, with grace moving first so repentance is not our own initiative. "The wounds sin leaves in the soul" replaces "the consequences of sin": forgiveness heals the soul, but some temporal consequences of sin can remain even after forgiveness (CCC 1473), so "consequences" would overpromise. "Physician of our souls" is the Catechism's own title for Christ, opening the sacraments of healing (CCC 1421).

1 · Mercy that heals: what Christ's salvation does in the soul

Section: Mercy vs. Mercilessness: The Culture of Condemnation · line 100

… Restoration is not a side-effect of the Gospel; it is the Gospel. The good news is that Christ's sacrifice on the cross teaches us enables us, and teaches us, to give mercy where we ourselves have been given mercy so freely. This is how His salvation heals the wounds sin leaves in the soul: moved by His grace, we repent; we are forgiven; and, forgiven, we become able to forgive. Mercy received and mercy given are one medicine, and Christ, the physician of our souls, is the one who applies it (cf. CCC 1421).
