Section: Christ: The Perfect Sacrifice · line 115

The reason Christ's sacrifice fulfills all seven of them is not that it is a bigger sacrifice in the same series, nor that on the Cross truth, justice, and mercy are at last added up. God is not three things added together. God is truth, God is justice, God is mercy, God is love: one undivided life. What the seven sacrifices each each of those sacrifices spoke truly of, the Cross gathers into a single perpetual act: the One who is all of these, poured out completely. …

Christ: The Perfect Sacrifice: the one perfected sacrifice and the Mass

Section: Christ: The Perfect Sacrifice · line 135

And because the Cross is the one perfected sacrifice — fulfilling all seven every one of them, accomplishing in a single act what generations of repeated offerings reached toward — Christ Himself gave it to us not as a memory but as a perpetual gift. On the night before He died, He took bread and wine into His own hands: "And he took bread, and when he had given thanks he broke it and gave it to them, saying, 'This is my body which is given for you. Do this in remembrance of me.' And likewise the cup after supper, saying, 'This cup which is poured out for you is the new covenant in my blood.'" (Luke 22:19–20). At the Last Supper, Christ instituted the new Passover — His own Body and Blood, offered under sacramental signs — so that the one sacrifice of Calvary would be made present again, in every place and in every age, until He comes. "For from the rising of the sun to its setting my name is great among the nations, and in every place incense is offered to my name, and a pure offering; for my name is great among the nations, says the LORD of hosts" (Malachi 1:11). For two thousand years the Church has done exactly that — not adding to Christ's sacrifice, not repeating it, but offering the one sacrifice He gave her, in every Mass on earth, every day. The seven Old Covenant sacrifices each spoke truly; the Cross speaks finally; and the Mass is where that final word becomes present to us, again and again, until the day we are gathered around the Lamb forever.
