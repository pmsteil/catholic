Owning our failure to teach well

Section: The Scandal of Division · line 322

… We must also acknowledge with sorrow that Catholics have sometimes failed to live the love we profess—scandals, abuses of power, arrogance, and loveless presentations of truth have all contributed to these divisions. And we have failed in a quieter way too: we have often taught what Christ commanded without teaching it well. Too many have received the faith as a list of rules rather than a Love to be known, and have never been shown how truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice come alive together in Christ. That failure is ours to own. Christ sent us to teach all that He commanded (cf. Matthew 28:20), and that means teaching it deeply and meaningfully, until people come to know our Lord in their very souls. It begins with each of us. We cannot hand on what we have not first received, learned and lived, for, as Pope St. Paul VI wrote, "Modern man listens more willingly to witnesses than to teachers, and if he does listen to teachers, it is because they are witnesses" (Evangelii Nuntiandi 41). But the scandal remains: Christ prayed for unity, and we are divided. …
