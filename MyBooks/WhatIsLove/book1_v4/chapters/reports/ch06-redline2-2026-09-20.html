2 · Truth 4: man or woman — one nature, equal dignity, different and complementary

line 71

We are born either male or female, and neither a man nor a woman alone can bring forth new life. Our sex is not one more trait on a list with race, height and birthplace. It is the first distinction within humanity itself, and the only one Scripture names in its first sentence about us (Genesis 1:27). Race, ancestry, height and birthplace describe how human beings vary; sex tells us which of the two ways of being human we have been given. It is not a detail of the body; it marks the whole person, body and soul (CCC 2332)—not assigned, not chosen, but revealed. Our Creator gave it in the making of each of us, body and soul together as one person, and so it is ours to receive, not to rewrite. Man and woman share one human nature and are created with equal dignity: "Man and woman are both with one and the same dignity 'in the image of God'" (CCC 369).¶Yet men and women are not interchangeable. "Each of the two sexes is an image of the power and tenderness of God, with equal dignity though in a different way" (CCC 2335), and their "physical, moral, and spiritual difference and complementarity are oriented toward the goods of marriage and the flourishing of family life" (CCC 2333) — above all in fatherhood and motherhood. They were not left "half-made and incomplete"; God "created them to be a communion of persons" (CCC 372). Our bodies speak a language of self-gift and complementarity, ordered toward union and the new life that flows from it. What the body reveals about how love works isn't oppression—it's truth. And it is far more than a paragraph can hold. The soul is the "form" of the body, and "spirit and matter, in man, are not two natures united, but rather their union forms a single nature" (CCC 365). So being a man or a woman is not something your body has and you merely inhabit; it is part of who you are. The Church has reflected on it for centuries, and it is worth reading what she says (CCC 369–373, 2331–2336; St. John Paul II, Mulieris Dignitatem).

3 · Truth 5 and the close: made for love; the image named

line 73

… We ache to be loved. No one has to teach us this. Each of us, the Church says, is capable "of freely giving himself and entering into communion with other persons" (CCC 357). …

line 75

… "So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them." Each truth above is a feature of that image. God is, and we receive our being from Him. God is the living God, and we live. God knows and wills, and we know and choose. God is a communion of Persons, and we are made man and woman, for one another and for love. We are made in God's likeness and His Son's image—created for love, designed for communion, ordered toward truth.
