## Introduction

### Introduction assessment

The Introduction has a clear evangelical purpose, a strong personal reason for asking “What is love?”, and an effective concern for children. Its central structural problem is that the proposed four-facet definition is repeatedly called the missing interpretive key that unlocks Scripture, salvation, grace, the sacraments, holiness, and the whole Christian life. That assigns a private synthesis work properly belonging to Christ, the apostolic deposit of faith, and the Church’s rule of faith. A second cluster confuses initial justification with lifelong sanctification and universal human dignity with supernatural adoptive filiation. A third cluster uses deterministic predictions and culture-war catalogues where precise propositions, moral objects, culpability distinctions, and paths of hope would make the Catholic case stronger. The remaining proposals address grammar, citation punctuation, capitalization, repetition, production markup, and overlong chapter summaries.
