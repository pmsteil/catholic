    Chapter 6 · Truth: God Is Truth

    Chapter assessment

    The edited chapter is stronger than the earlier whole-book audit reflected. It now correctly calls truth the epistemological floor of love rather than the highest divine attribute; identifies Christ as the eternal Word while preserving Scripture as inspired; describes the Church as receiving rather than creating truth; preserves the Mass-before-written-Gospels insight; and no longer says legitimate authority becomes real only when voluntarily accepted. Its strongest passages unite natural reason, revelation, Christ and ecclesial communion without reducing truth to information.

    The remaining stop-ship problems are concentrated rather than pervasive. Lying is still made the foundational sin behind every sin; inspiration is tied to the human author’s spiritual surrender; the Passion language needs the Church’s Christological and anti-Judaism safeguards; later paragraphs oppose personal truth to propositions, natural reason to revelation and physical truths to moral truths. The long apostolic-authority section contains several historically unequal founder/date claims, makes Matthias prove more than Acts states, accidentally says apostles wrote after their deaths, overstates uniform succession across ancient sees, and describes the whole Bible as written and authenticated by the Church without distinguishing the Old Testament or God’s authorship. The chapter’s Catholic claim can remain confident while becoming more exact, more ecumenically Catholic and more historically credible.

    CH6-01 · Do not make lying the root or necessary condition of every sin

    Severity: Stop-ship / doctrine of sin · Control: DC-10, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:39–50, 164–166

    Issue. Lying is a specific sin against truth. Every sin involves some disordered judgment of an apparent good, but Catholic tradition identifies disobedience and pride in the first sin and does not teach that the act of lying makes every other sin possible.

    Controlling sources: CCC 397–398; CCC 1849–1851; CCC 2482–2487.

    Source passages with proposed inline redline:

    Every corruption of love downstream from here begins with a lie some refusal or distortion of truth — a lie about God, a lie about the human person, or a lie about love itself.

    Satan is called <em>"the father of lies"</em> (John 8:44) because every sin traces back to some falsehood believed or spoken. Lying is not merely one sin among many — it is the foundational sin that makes every other sin possible his temptation draws us away from God’s truth. Lying is a grave and fertile offense against truth, while pride and disobedience are the deeper root dynamics by which the sinner prefers self to God.

    The Eighth Commandment prohibits not only direct lying but "every kind of act contrary to justice and truth" (CCC 2476). Lying is the a foundational destroyer of love because love cannot exist remain rightly ordered without truth.

    Editorial consequence. The chapter can emphasize the generative power of lies without replacing the Church’s account of sin with a one-sin taxonomy.

    CH6-02 · Present existential recognition as experience, not universal self-evident knowledge

    Severity: Major / logic and anthropology · Control: DC-27, DC-29 · Location: chapter_06.md:65–67

    Issue. “Every human being already knows” that they need love, recognize sacrifice and feel the wrongness of injustice is too universal and treats contested or developmentally acquired concepts as incorrigible first principles. The insight works as phenomenological testimony if stated proportionately.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    Here is what every human being already knows before theology begins ordinary human experience discloses before formal theology begins: *I exist. I am a person. I need love.* I feel We can recognize the wrongness of injustice. I We long for mercy when I've we have failed. I We can recognize sacrifice when I see we see it. These are not conclusions reasoned to — they are the ground we already stand on all innate conclusions held with equal clarity; they are realities reason can recognize and experience repeatedly confirms. Truth, in its most primal sense, is simply this: what is actually real. And without it, love has no floor — it becomes airborne, untethered from reality, a word anyone can pour any meaning into.

    Editorial consequence. The argument remains accessible without claiming an empirical or philosophical universality it has not established.

    CH6-03 · Describe sexual differentiation without cellular or anatomical absolutes

    Severity: Stop-ship / anthropology and science · Control: DC-23, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:71

    Issue. Biological sex is bodily, objective and ordered toward generation, but “written into every cell” is not literally true of every cell and “only half of the reproductive organs” is anatomically crude. The stronger Catholic claim concerns the whole person’s sexually differentiated body.

    Controlling sources: CCC 355–373; Dignitas Infinita 60; Male and Female He Created Them 4–6.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    **We are born either male or female, and we are made with only half of the reproductive organs necessary for procreation. Our biological sex is written into every cell of our bodies and the human body is organized as male or female in a sexually differentiated way, including a reproductive system ordered toward generation through the complementarity of the sexes—not assigned, not chosen, but revealed. Our bodies speak a language of self-gift and complementarity, ordered toward union and the new life that flows from it. What the body reveals about how love works isn't oppression—it's truth. This objective pattern can be stated without denying uncommon disorders of sexual development or reducing the person to chromosomes or organs.

    Editorial consequence. The paragraph becomes scientifically defensible while preserving the intended Catholic anthropology.

    CH6-04 · Ground inspiration in God’s action through true human authors

    Severity: Stop-ship / inspiration · Control: DC-20, DC-21 · Location: chapter_06.md:85

    Issue. Prophets, psalmists and apostles were not inspired because they surrendered so fully that divine truth could flow through them. Holiness is not the criterion of biblical inspiration. God acted in and through true human authors making full use of their faculties.

    Controlling sources: Dei Verbum 11; CCC 105–108.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    Through Divine Revelation and His Church: God has not left us to guess at Him from a distance. He broke into human history through prophets, psalmists, and apostles — men and women who surrendered themselves so fully to God's will that His truth could flow through them human instruments whom He freely chose and through whom He acted without cancelling their own faculties, cultures or true authorship. "No prophecy ever came by the impulse of man, but men moved by the Holy Spirit spoke from God" (2 Pet 1:21). And then, when the fullness of time had come, God did not send another prophet. He came Himself.

    Editorial consequence. The paragraph teaches the Catholic doctrine of inspiration rather than a holiness-causes-inspiration model.

    CH6-05 · Do not derive the Church’s governing authority from an overstated etymology

    Severity: Major / ecclesiology, language and reasoning · Control: DC-14, DC-20, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:87

    Issue. Greek ekklesia means an assembly or those called together; particular civic assemblies could exercise public authority, but “governing assembly” is not the word’s universal lexical definition. The Church’s teaching authority follows from Christ’s institution and commission, not from etymology.

    Controlling sources: CCC 751–752; CCC 857–865; Matthew 16:18–19; 18:18; 28:18–20.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    And to ensure that His truth would reach every generation intact, He established His Church. What is the Church? The word itself tells us helps us see part of the answer. The Greek ekklesia did not first mean a building or a loose gathering of the like-minded. In the Greek world it was the ordinary word for a city's governing assembly—the body of citizens called out to gather, deliberate, and decide a word for an assembly, including the civic assembly of citizens called together for public business. And in the Greek Bible the apostles carried, it was the word chosen to translate the Hebrew qahal: the covenant assembly of Israel, the people solemnly summoned by God to stand under His authority and receive His law—"the day of the assembly" at Sinai (Deut 9:10; cf. 4:10–13). Hold the two meanings together and they say exactly what the Gospel means illuminate the people Christ gathers around Himself: in founding His ekklesia, Christ was reconstituting God's own covenant assembly around Himself—a holy governing assembly with real authority to teach in His name whose real teaching authority comes from Christ’s explicit commission, charged with one sacred task: to receive, guard, and hand on everything He had commanded (cf. Matt 28:18–20; 2 Tim 1:14). An assembly summoned by God already stands under His authority—the word itself hints at what Christ's own acts, as we are about to see, confirm the authority He entrusted to the apostles and their successors. She is the pillar and foundation of truth (1 Tim 3:15), and Christ promised the Holy Spirit to guide her into all truth (John 16:13).

    Editorial consequence. The etymology supports the image, while Scripture and apostolic institution bear the doctrinal claim.

    CH6-06 · State canon reception and the one deposit with historical precision

    Severity: Major / canon and revelation · Control: DC-20, DC-28, DC-30 · Location: chapter_06.md:89

    Issue. Hippo and Carthage were regional councils that witnessed and transmitted canonical lists; Trent dogmatically defined the canon amid a later controversy. “Two streams” is serviceable only if their unity is immediately controlling, but Scripture is not merely revelation written down: it is the inspired Word of God in writing, while Tradition transmits the Word entrusted to the apostles.

    Controlling sources: Dei Verbum 8–10; CCC 80–83, 101–141; Council of Trent, Decree Concerning the Canonical Scriptures.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    And it was the Church, guided by that same Spirit, who received, recognized, and handed down the Scriptures — gathering the 73 books Catholics still read today from the apostolic witness, ratifying them at the regional councils of Hippo (393) and Carthage (397), and defining the canon for the whole Church at Trent (1546) receiving the forty-six books of the Old Testament and the twenty-seven of the New as inspired and canonical. Regional councils including Hippo and Carthage witnessed fourth-century canonical lists; the ecumenical Council of Trent later defined that canon dogmatically. But a careful distinction matters: the Word of God is not first a book — He is a Person. "The Word became flesh and dwelt among us" (John 1:14). Christ Himself is the eternal Word, and everything God has revealed reaches us through two inseparable streams flowing from Him: Sacred Scripture (revelation written down) and Sacred Tradition (revelation handed on in the Church's teaching, sacraments, and life) Sacred Scripture, the Word of God consigned to writing under the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, and Sacred Tradition, by which the Word entrusted to the apostles is handed on in the Church’s doctrine, life and worship — "one sacred deposit of the Word of God" (Dei Verbum 10; cf. CCC 80–82).

    Editorial consequence. The canon argument becomes historically and theologically exact without weakening the Church’s indispensable role.

    CH6-07 · Remove speculative dating from the emet paragraph

    Severity: Major / Hebrew and historical evidence · Control: DC-27, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:97

    Issue. Emet carries senses of truth, firmness and faithfulness, and emunah is related, but “older than the prophets, older than the psalms” depends on contested dating. “Israel’s most ancient song” is a scholarly judgment, not a fact the argument needs.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    The Hebrew word for truth—emet—means faithfulness, reliability, and reality itself can signify truth, firmness, reliability and faithfulness. This is not a New Testament innovation. Moses himself, in the Song of Deuteronomy, gives God the title El emunah—"God of faithfulness" (Deut 32:4). In Hebrew, faithfulness and truth grow from a single root: emunah and emet are sisters. And the Psalmist hands the dying that title in its other form—El emet, "God of truth" (Psalm 31:5)—the very words Christ will pray from the Cross before this chapter ends. This title—older than the prophets, older than the psalms, woven into Israel's most ancient song— Together these Old Covenant witnesses declares that God is not merely truthful in the way a reliable witness is truthful. He is the ground of truth itself.

    Editorial consequence. The Hebrew insight remains, but it no longer rests on disputed relative dating or an exhaustive gloss.

    CH6-08 · Preserve propositional truth and identify the Father as the one addressed in John 17

    Severity: Stop-ship / revelation and exegesis · Control: DC-19, DC-20, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:119–133

    Issue. Christ is Truth in person, but this does not make propositions “cold fact” or relationship an alternative to doctrine. In John 17, Jesus asks the Father to sanctify the disciples in truth; the present pronoun makes Christ the grammatical agent of the Father’s act.

    Controlling sources: John 17:17–23; CCC 65–67; CCC 2465–2470; Dei Verbum 2–4.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Truth isn't just propositional statements about reality—it's personal, a Person who embodies reality itself. Because the Person who is Truth speaks and teaches, personal encounter and true propositions belong together rather than competing.

    Truth comes as living relationship, not abstract principle; warm embrace, not cold fact; and therefore also as doctrine and moral truth; it is not reduced to an abstraction or to impersonal information. In Christ, truth is both personally encountered and intelligibly confessed: liberation, not condemnation: "You will know the truth, and the truth will make you free" (John 8:32).

    Look at what Jesus reveals in His final prayer before the Cross. He doesn't ask the Father simply to protect His disciples from lies—He asks the Father to sanctify them in truth: "Sanctify them in the truth; thy word is truth" (John 17:17). To sanctify is to make holy—to transform, to make "whole", to draw into communion with God. For Jesus, truth is not just a standard to uphold. He is the agent of transformation, the very medium through which we are drawn into the life of God Himself. He asks the Father to sanctify His disciples in the truth of the divine word; this saving truth reaches its fullness in Christ and is made living in us by the Holy Spirit.

    Editorial consequence. The chapter can proclaim Christ as Truth without implying that doctrine is dispensable or misidentifying the action in Christ’s prayer.

    CH6-09 · Add Christological and anti-Judaism safeguards to the Passion passage

    Severity: Stop-ship / Christology and responsibility for the Passion · Control: DC-03, DC-04, DC-05, DC-22 · Location: chapter_06.md:145–156

    Issue. The divine Person of the Son truly suffered and died in his human nature; the divine nature is impassible. “The priesthood failed Him” risks transferring responsibility to Judaism or the Jewish priesthood as a whole. Catholic teaching rejects collective Jewish guilt and places the deepest responsibility on sinners universally.

    Controlling sources: CCC 468–469; CCC 595–598; CCC 624–630; Nostra Aetate 4.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Think about the context: Jesus—who IS Truth—was tortured and put to death because of lies. False witnesses testified against Him. Religious leaders accused Him falsely. Some religious leaders conspired and accused Him; this must never be turned into guilt assigned to the Jewish people or priesthood as such. Pilate knew He was innocent but condemned Him anyway for political expediency. The crowd A crowd present at the trial chose Barabbas, a murderer, over Jesus, the Truth. Even His own disciples abandoned Him, with Peter denying three times that he even knew Him. Lies are the one weapon of choice against the truth that saves us.

    Sit with that for a moment. The One who IS Truth—who is the very ground of all that is real, faithful, reliable—was encircled on every side by lies at the moment of His death. Every human institution meant to guard truth failed Him: the courts, the priesthood, the crowd, His closest friends. Representatives of civil power, some religious authorities, members of the crowd and His own friends all failed Him in different ways; more deeply, the Church teaches that the sins of us all are implicated in His Passion.

    This is why the Cross exposes lying for what it really is: the sin that killed God Himself when He walked among us an offense implicated in the condemnation of the incarnate Son. The divine Person truly suffered and died according to His human nature; His divine nature did not suffer or cease to live. Falsehood strikes at the very foundation of love and communion. Every sin involves some form of lying departure from truth and right judgment — to ourselves, to others, or to God.

    And lies are still deadly today—destroying marriages, families, communities, nations, and souls. Every lie is a participation in the same evil that crucified Christ echoes the falsehood that served His condemnation. Every time we speak truth, we participate in His resurrection — the triumph of truth over all the world's deception the risen Christ’s victory and bear witness to the Truth who death could not silence.

    Editorial consequence. The rhetoric retains its force while becoming Chalcedonian, non-collectivizing and faithful to CCC 598.

    CH6-10 · Mark the reported return to the Church as hope, not established trend

    Severity: Major / empirical claim and rhetoric · Control: DC-27, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:164–166

    Issue. “Many are finding their way back to the Church” may be true in some places or cohorts, but the chapter supplies no evidence and treats an observed cultural mechanism as a general trend. The paragraph needs only a possibility and pastoral invitation.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    But here's what's remarkable: the very fullness of the lies of our time seems to be driving people back to truth the brazenness of contemporary lies can awaken a renewed hunger for truth. When lies become so blatant, so destructive, so obviously contrary to reality, people start may begin waking up. *What is actually true? Who can I trust? Where can I find reality?* And many are finding their way back to the Church—the pillar and foundation of truth, standing as a lighthouse in the storm. For some, those questions become a path toward Christ and His Church, “the pillar and bulwark of the truth” (1 Tim 3:15).

    Editorial consequence. The pastoral claim no longer depends on an unsourced sociological trend.

    CH6-11 · Relate truth and authority without saying every definer of reality commands worship

    Severity: Stop-ship / authority and idolatry · Control: DC-14, DC-27 · Location: chapter_06.md:184–192

    Issue. Authorities make judgments within limited competence; they do not thereby “define reality” or command worship. Legitimate authority derives from God and is morally bounded by truth, right reason, human dignity and the common good. The current absolute formulation can make any institutional pronouncement sound divinely binding.

    Controlling sources: CCC 1897–1904; CCC 2104–2109; Acts 5:29.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Once we see that every love bows to something as its ultimate reference point, an unavoidable question follows: who gets to say what is true? how do we recognize truth, and which authorities may teach or command within their proper competence? Truth and authority cannot be separated — whoever defines reality commands our worship are related but not identical. No human authority creates reality, and worship belongs to God alone. If there is no authority higher than the self, then "my truth" becomes the final word, and we are back to idolatry by another name. So before we can speak rightly about truth, we have to speak rightly about authority — what it is, where it comes from, and why submitting to it is not the opposite of freedom but its foundation.

    This means that any authority claimed rightly exercised by a person or institution is borrowed. Governments hold authority because God is the Author of social order (cf. Romans 13:1). Parents hold authority because God is the Author of the family. The Church holds authority because Christ—the Author made flesh—entrusted it to her. Each human authority is limited by its proper competence, the moral law, human dignity and the common good; an unjust command does not bind the conscience. Strip God away, and all authority collapses into raw power. Power can compel. It cannot bind. It cannot command love. Only truth can do that.

    Editorial consequence. The section gains the Catholic limits needed to protect obedience from coercive misuse.

    CH6-12 · Keep Paul’s slavery metaphor while avoiding an unrestricted spirituality of enslavement

    Severity: Major / pastoral safety and freedom · Control: DC-12, DC-14, DC-25 · Location: chapter_06.md:202–206

    Issue. Paul deliberately uses slavery language for belonging wholly to Christ, but “true freedom is voluntary submission to be a slave” can be detached from its divine referent and exploited by abusive human authorities. Grace heals freedom; no human minister becomes “Love itself.”

    Controlling sources: Romans 1:1; 6:15–23; Galatians 5:1, 13; CCC 1730–1742; CCC 1897–1904.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Here's the beautiful paradox: true freedom is the voluntary submission to be a slave to God's love true freedom is fulfilled in belonging wholly and freely to God. St. Paul called himself "a servant of Jesus Christ" (Romans 1:1)—a word that literally means slave. "For you were called to freedom, brethren; only do not use your freedom as an opportunity for the flesh, but through love be servants of one another" (Galatians 5:13). We are always slaves to something Paul contrasts slavery to sin with willing obedience to God—either to sin, which leads to death, or to God, which leads to life.

    When we voluntarily submit to God's authority—grounded in truth and expressed through love—we become slaves to Love itself. And being enslaved to perfect Love is perfect freedom servants of the God who is Love. This divine service is freedom’s perfection, because Love wills our good, knows our nature, and leads us to our true fulfillment. This is why the saints, who were most "enslaved" to God belonged most completely to God, were also the freest people who ever lived. No human superior possesses God’s unlimited authority, and no appeal to obedience can make abuse or an immoral command legitimate.

    Editorial consequence. Paul’s paradox remains powerful without becoming a transferable warrant for domination.

    CH6-13 · Do not make Judges a proof that Israel lacked a binding teaching authority

    Severity: Major / Scripture and ecclesiology · Control: DC-14, DC-20, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:210

    Issue. Judges 21:25 laments Israel’s political and covenant disorder, but Israel already possessed the covenant, Torah, priests, elders and judges. “No king” cannot simply mean “no definitive authority to teach” or serve as a direct proof of New Covenant hierarchy.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    Scripture shows us, across an entire book, what happens when God's people have no binding authority to hand on His truth reject the covenantal order and repeatedly do what is right in their own eyes. The book of Judges ends with a deliberate refrain — "In those days there was no king in Israel; every man did what was right in his own eyes" (Judges 21:25)<sup>3</sup> — and the chapters before it chronicle the predictable fruit: idolatry, injustice, sexual violence, tribal civil war, and a worship that each clan improvised for itself. Without a definitive authority to teach the people what God's love actually requires of them, Although Israel already had the Torah, priests, elders and judges, the people repeatedly abandoned the order God had given; "what is right" collapses into "what I prefer" — and the people tear themselves apart. It is the dictatorship of relativism in Iron Age form an Old Covenant warning about practical relativism and covenant infidelity (as we named it in Chapter 5).

    Editorial consequence. Judges remains a forceful warning without becoming an anachronistic syllogism for the Magisterium.

    CH6-14 · Repair the succession argument and the “after their deaths” error

    Severity: Stop-ship / logic, history and copy · Control: DC-20, DC-27, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:216

    Issue. The sentence literally says the apostles wrote letters after they died. Acts 1 shows replacement of Judas’s vacant apostolic office, but that event alone does not prove that every feature of the apostolate is transmitted identically; episcopal succession rests on the broader apostolic practice and Tradition.

    Controlling sources: Acts 1:15–26; Acts 14:23; 2 Timothy 1:6, 2:2; Titus 1:5; CCC 77; CCC 861–862.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    The answer is written into their actions. They founded churches and appointed elders in every one of them (Acts 14:23). They ordained successors entrusted ministry to co-workers who would in turn teach others (2 Tim 2:2 — "what you have heard from me before many witnesses entrust to faithful men who will be able to teach others also"). They wrote letters to correct doctrine even after their deaths. Their inspired letters continued to correct doctrine after the apostles themselves had died. And from the very start they replaced Judas with Matthias (Acts 1:20–26) — establishing that the apostolic office itself was successive, not personal showing that an apostolic office could be filled after becoming vacant; read with the appointment and laying on of hands of later ministers, this belongs to the scriptural foundation for the Church’s received doctrine of apostolic succession. None of this makes sense if they thought the authority Christ had given them was a one-generation gift. Every one of those acts is an act of handing on.

    Editorial consequence. The paragraph retains its cumulative case while removing a logical overclaim and an impossible sentence.

    CH6-15 · State the hierarchy’s development and sacramental visibility more carefully

    Severity: Major / Holy Orders and ecclesiology · Control: DC-14, DC-20, DC-28 · Location: chapter_06.md:220

    Issue. The three degrees are divinely instituted and ancient, but New Testament terminology for episkopoi and presbyteroi is not yet used with complete later precision in every passage. The hierarchy makes Christ sacramentally present and continues his pastoral mission; it does not make the entire Body “as touchable as Jesus” or guarantee certainty about every application.

    Controlling sources: CCC 861–862; CCC 1536–1600, especially 1550, 1554–1556.

    Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:

    From the apostolic age forward, the Church has been governed by a threefold hierarchy instituted by Christ through the apostles: bishops as successors of the apostles, priests as their co-workers in word and sacrament, and deacons as servants of the Church's charity and proclamation (cf. CCC 1536, 1554–1571). Scripture already shows this structure in place the ministries from which this ancient threefold order is received, although its vocabulary develops in early Christian usage: "appoint elders" (presbyteroi) "in every town" (Titus 1:5), "a bishop" (episkopos) "must be above reproach" (1 Timothy 3:2), "deacons" (diakonoi) "likewise must be serious" (1 Timothy 3:8). By A.D. 107, St. Ignatius of Antioch — a disciple of the Apostle John, on his way to martyrdom — could and did write: "You must all follow the bishop as Jesus Christ followed the Father, and the presbytery as you would the apostles; reverence the deacons as the command of God" (Letter to the Smyrnaeans 8). This was not a later invention. It was Christ's own design, executed by the men He trained and the Spirit He sent — giving the people of God what Judges lamented Israel lacked: a living, visible authority by which every generation could know with certainty what God's love required of them a living, visible ministry that sacramentally teaches, sanctifies and governs, while its human ministers remain fallible outside the charisms and competencies Christ guarantees.

    And all of this is for one purpose — "thy kingdom come, thy will be done, on earth as it is in heaven" (Matt 6:10): to make the Body of Christ as real and as touchable in every age as Jesus Himself was in Galilee, so that every future generation — not only the one that walked with Him — could see Him, hear Him, and know Him to continue Christ’s mission visibly and sacramentally in every age, so future generations may hear His Gospel, receive His sacraments and be gathered into His Body.

    Editorial consequence. The passage still makes a confident Catholic claim without compressing historical development or sacramental mediation into identity.

    CH6-16 · Qualify the apostolic-see founder list and remove its weakest precise claims

    Severity: Stop-ship / historical evidence · Control: DC-27, DC-28, DC-30 · Location: chapter_06.md:222–233

    Issue. The entries have unequal evidence. Jerusalem, Antioch and Paul’s foundations are scriptural; Rome and Smyrna have early patristic evidence; Mark–Alexandria and Thomas–India are later ancient traditions; Andrew–Byzantium and Bartholomew/Jude–Armenia are substantially later and disputed. Exact years such as Peter’s arrival at Rome in A.D. 42 are traditional estimates, not established facts.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    And this is not an abstract claim.
