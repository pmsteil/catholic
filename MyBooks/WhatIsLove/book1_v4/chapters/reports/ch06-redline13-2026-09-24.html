One deposit: Tradition named, and Scripture within it

line 253

… The Creed we still recite was confessed at Nicaea and completed at Constantinople in 381, sixteen years before Carthage. All of this, the baptizing and teaching, the Eucharist on the Lord's Day, the bishops in succession, the witness of the martyrs, the Creed, was the apostolic deposit being handed on: not a book waiting to be compiled, but the Church, "in her doctrine, life and worship," transmitting "to every generation all that she herself is, all that she believes" (CCC 78). The Church calls this living transmission Sacred Tradition. And the Scriptures were never a second deposit standing beside it. The apostles' preaching was written down within that same life; the books were recognized by it (CCC 120); and they are read within it still. Scripture is the Word of God set down in writing, and the Church reveres it Tradition is the same Word handed on in the Church's life, and the Church insists they are one: "For both of them, flowing out from the same divine well-spring, come together in some fashion to form one thing and move towards the same goal" (CCC 80).
