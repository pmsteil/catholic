Chapter 10 quoted no early Father. The best one for this place is St. Ignatius of Antioch, writing early in the second century to the Ephesians on his way to martyrdom: the one bread is “the medicine of immortality” by which “we should live for ever in Jesus Christ” (Letter to the Ephesians 20). It is among the earliest witnesses outside the New Testament, it holds unity and union with Christ together in one breath, and it is the phrase the tradition kept. Checked word for word in the Ante-Nicene Fathers translation on two sites (New Advent, CCEL); the brackets are the translators'. An early Father on the Eucharist

Section: How Love Creates Life-Giving Communion › Sacramental: The Eucharist—Perfect Binding Made Real · line 217

The Church has known this all along. Early in the second century, St. Ignatius of Antioch, bishop and martyr, wrote to the Christians of Ephesus as he was being taken to Rome to die. He urged them to stay one, "breaking one and the same bread, which is the medicine of immortality, and the antidote to prevent us from dying, but [which causes] that we should live for ever in Jesus Christ."<sup>1</sup> Sacred Tradition has preserved this truth for 2,000 years—and it waits for us still, ready to be received by anyone who comes to Him.

Section: Further Study › Christian Thinkers · line Further Study

- St. Ignatius of Antioch (died early 2nd century), Bishop of Antioch and martyr - His letters, written on the road to his martyrdom in Rome, are among the earliest witnesses to the Eucharist as the bread that binds the Church to Christ and to one another - St. Augustine (354-430), Bishop of Hippo and Doctor of the Church - In his Confessions (VII.10.16) he hears Christ say, "you shall be changed into Me"; preaching to the newly baptized (Sermon 272, quoted in CCC 1396), he teaches that our "Amen" at Communion says yes to the Body we are; his City of God (XIV.28) traces the two cities formed by two loves, love of God and love of self
