    1 · A continuity break I introduced, which is what your ¶021 note is really touching

    Your note asks to make the "Methodists on both sides" sentence more precise. Before drafting anything I looked at where that paragraph now sits, and the problem is not precision. It is position, and I caused it.

    That paragraph used to follow directly after the old combined paragraph about the Methodist Church. When I expanded that one paragraph into eight, it got stranded. The order on disk right now is:

    • February 2019 vote
    • Quadrilateral, 2024, the departures
    • "That is when I understood that truth is the ground love has to stand on"
    • "What I was starting to see was a structural problem" and the Chapter 6 pointer
    • five paragraphs about BLM, looting and the family
    • "I noticed something profound: sincere, well-meaning Methodists on both sides…"

    So the reader leaves the Methodists, travels through the riots, and is then returned to a Methodist observation with no signal. It reads as a lost thread, because it is one.

    The fix is to move it, not to expand it. It belongs immediately after the 2024 paragraph, where it is an observation feeding the conclusion rather than an afterthought trailing it:

    1. February 2019 vote
    2. Quadrilateral, 2024, the departures
    3. "I noticed something profound: sincere, well-meaning Methodists on both sides…" — moved here
    4. "That is when I understood…"
    5. Structural problem, and the Chapter 6 pointer
    6. "During this same time, our society…" and the BLM passage

    Nothing is rewritten. One paragraph moves up four places, and the Methodist account becomes continuous.

    2 · On adding BLM to that sentence — I would not

    Your note also suggests making it precise that Methodists were defending the sexuality errors and joining the BLM movement. Three reasons to leave it alone, and I would rather argue them than quietly drop it.

    The sentence is about one disagreement. "Both sides" means the two sides of the sexuality question, each claiming Scripture. That is precise, and it is the whole point: a method with no settling authority produces two confident opposite readings. BLM is not a side of that question, so adding it does not sharpen the sentence — it changes the subject inside it.

    It needs sourcing we do not have. Some Methodist bodies did publish statements supporting the movement, and that is documentable. But "Methodists were lining up to join BLM" as a characterization is not, and we have just spent four rounds making this paragraph survive a hostile reader.

    It re-muddies what we worked to separate. The BLM passage is defensible right now because it keeps the organization, the movement and the rioters distinct. Adding "Methodists" as a fourth category, linked by assertion, reopens exactly the vulnerability we closed.

    If you still want the connection, the honest version is a separate sentence about a specific published statement, sourced. But my counsel is that it buys you little and costs the paragraph its footing.

    3 · The contrast insight — worth adding, and it is the book's method in one line

    Your note: "it is the contrast of seeing what is definitely not good for society that has helped me understand more deeply what love is."

    That is not an aside. It is how the book actually works, and stating it early tells the reader why the next four hundred pages keep examining counterfeits. Proposed as a new sentence closing the opening paragraph, before "What is Love?":

    …all of it boils down to a failure to understand the most fundamentally human question that lies at the very heart of who we are as persons created in God's image:

    And I should admit that I learned much of this backwards. It was watching what is plainly not good for people, and asking what was missing from it, that taught me most of what I now understand about love.

    What is Love?

    Alternative, if you want it plainer: "Much of what I understand about love, I learned by contrast — from watching what is plainly not good for people and asking what was missing."

    4 · Cutting the salvation paragraph — yes, but one knock-on

    You are right that it argues rather than introduces. Cutting it:

    This is not the salvation the Bible teaches. Scripture speaks of salvation as transformation, as dying to self and rising with Christ, as heeding St. Paul's command to "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" (Philippians 2:12), as becoming "partakers of the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4). Biblical salvation is nothing less than being caught up into God's own life of perfect love—learning to love as He loves, to give as He gives, to sacrifice as He sacrificed. It's comprehensive, demanding, glorious, and utterly transformative.

    The knock-on. The next paragraph opens "How did we lose sight of this transformative vision?" — and the vision it points back to is the one in the paragraph you are cutting. Without it the pronoun reaches back to "stripped of its transformative power," which is the negative, so it still just about works but reads thin.

    One-clause repair, if you want it:

    How did we lose sight of this transformative visionthe transformation salvation is meant to be? By separating what God has joined together.

    5 · The Journey Ahead — I agree with your instinct, and I would shorten rather than cut

    That section is 720 words, and your spoiler objection is sound. But I would keep the four parts and drop the per-chapter blurbs, rather than removing the section, for a reason beyond orientation.

    Those blurbs are where two doctrinal errors were hiding. The Chapter 13 blurb was the place that said justification is "not a single moment," and the Chapter 15 blurb was the place that said "incomplete love isn't love at all." Both were flagged by the audit; both were fixed this week. A summary written to compress a chapter will keep producing overstatements like that, and a censor reads every line of it. Less surface, fewer places for an error to hide.

    Proposed replacement, roughly 120 words for 720:

    Part I — The Question and the Answer (Chapters 1–2). Where evil comes from, and why love is the answer to it.

    Part II — The Facets of Perfect Love (Chapters 3–10). The definition itself, unpacked one facet at a time: sacred, covenant, binding, truth, justice, mercy, sacrifice, and the communion they exist for.

    Part III — Living Love, and Its Failures (Chapters 11–16). What this looks like in an actual decision, why it holds up under examination, how virtue and sin follow from it, and what its counterfeits cost us — and our children.

    Part IV — Love Fulfilled in Christ (Chapter 17). The Gospel: Perfect Love revealed in a Person.

    "Test everything; hold fast what is good." (1 Thessalonians 5:21)

    The Scripture line moves up to close the section rather than sitting inside Part III, where it currently interrupts.

    One cost you should weigh: some readers, and some reviewers, do use a chapter map, and a bishop's reader may want to see the shape of the argument without reading straight through. If that worries you, the compromise is this short version in the Introduction and a fuller table of contents up front, which the build already generates.
