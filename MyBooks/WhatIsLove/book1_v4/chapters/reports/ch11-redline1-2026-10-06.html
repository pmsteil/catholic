Each evening, walk slowly back through your day in the presence of God, and ask four questions of the moments that mattered. Truth: where did I see clearly today, and where did I let myself believe a comfortable lie? Justice: what did I owe the people in front of me, and did I give it? But that raises a deeper question, and we cannot go further until we face it. We have spent this entire book speaking of love as a law — as real, as objective, and as binding as the law of gravity. We have spoken of truth that must be obeyed, justice that is owed, a good that is genuinely true and not merely preferred. But a law this real, this universal, this authoritative — where does it come from? Laws do not write themselves. The very moral order we have just spent a chapter learning to obey points beyond itself to its source. A Law of Love this real requires a Lawgiver.
