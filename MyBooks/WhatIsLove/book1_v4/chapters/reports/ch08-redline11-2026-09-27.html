1 · Christ's authority to forgive, first and plainly His

Section: The Sacrament of Reconciliation · line 209

On the evening of His resurrection, Jesus did something the world had never seen. Only God can forgive sins; His critics had said so, and on that point they were right: "Why does this man speak thus? It is blasphemy! Who can forgive sins but God alone?" (Mark 2:7). Now the risen Christ gives that authority to men. St. John records it: "Jesus said to them again, 'Peace be with you. As the Father has sent me, even so I send you.' And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and said to them, 'Receive the Holy Spirit. If you forgive the sins of any, they are forgiven; if you retain the sins of any, they are retained'" (John 20:21-23).¶The power is not theirs; it is His. The Catechism is exact: "Only God forgives sins," and "by virtue of his divine authority he gives this power to men to exercise in his name" (CCC 1441). The Church forgives as she baptizes and teaches, under the authority Christ claimed before He sent the apostles out: "And Jesus came and said to them, 'All authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me'" (Matthew 28:18; cf. 28:19–20). The priest speaks the words; it is Christ who absolves. ¶The Catholic Sacrament of Reconciliation embodies mercy in concrete form. …
