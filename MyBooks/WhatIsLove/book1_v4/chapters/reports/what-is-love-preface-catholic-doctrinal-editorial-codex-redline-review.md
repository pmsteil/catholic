## Preface

### Preface assessment

The Preface has a strong testimonial purpose, an inviting ecumenical tone, transparent gratitude, and an unusually candid AI disclosure. Its principal vulnerability is not the testimony itself but a cluster of sentences that simultaneously deny human authorship, call the Holy Spirit the book’s author, and describe the author’s synthesis as revelation. Taken together, those sentences can imply inspiration or private revelation even though the Preface verbally denies that implication. The proposed redlines preserve the gratitude and personal voice while clearly identifying Patrick Steil as the responsible human author, public revelation as complete in Christ, and the four-facet framework as a fallible synthesis submitted to the Church.

### PREF-01 · Distinguish human authorship from the source of revealed truth

**Severity:** Major · **Control:** DC-20, DC-21 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:4`

**Issue.** “I cannot claim to be the author” is literally inconsistent with the later AI disclosure and with the moral and legal responsibility of a human author. In this context it also prepares the reader for the later claim that the Holy Spirit is the book’s author.

**Why it matters.** Catholic doctrine calls the Holy Spirit the principal author of inspired Scripture while also affirming its true human authors. A private devotional manuscript is not inspired Scripture. The Preface can deny originality as to the truths received without denying Patrick’s real authorship of the book’s selection, arrangement, reasoning, and language.

**Controlling sources:** *Dei Verbum* 11; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-20–21.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

I collected all the words and strung them together. But I cannot claim to be ~~the author~~ ++the source of the truths they seek to express++.

**Editorial consequence.** This keeps the humility and cadence of the opening while preventing a false authorship claim.

### PREF-02 · Acknowledge that the framework’s organization and expression are the author’s work

**Severity:** Major · **Control:** DC-01 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:6`

**Issue.** The absolute claim that no idea or principle was created or developed by the author conflicts with the book’s central four-facet synthesis, its exact canonical wording, its virtue and vice matrices, and its many original applications.

**Why it matters.** The underlying Catholic truths are received; the precise framework is the author’s fallible synthesis. Denying that distinction makes the synthesis sound revealed rather than proposed and leaves the reader unable to tell what carries ecclesial authority.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 65–67; *Dei Verbum* 7–10; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-01.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

~~There is not a single idea or principle in this book that I created or came up with on my own.~~ ++The truths I draw upon are not mine to invent, though their organization and expression in this book are my own fallible work.++

**Editorial consequence.** This is the Preface’s first explicit statement of the framework’s proper status and should govern the rest of the manuscript.

### PREF-03 · Separate the deposit of faith from the author’s synthesis

**Severity:** Major · **Control:** DC-01, DC-20 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:10`

**Issue.** “I have received this truth” has no clear antecedent and can identify the exact four-facet framework with revealed truth. “Organize facts and synthesize them” also makes theology sound like a neutral data exercise and omits the Church’s interpretive and guarding office.

**Why it matters.** Scripture and Tradition transmit the one deposit of faith; the Catechism authoritatively presents Church teaching; saints and faithful witnesses assist understanding. The author then organizes what he has learned. Those levels should remain visible.

**Controlling sources:** *Dei Verbum* 7–10; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-01, DC-20.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

Just as ~~the role of the Church is to receive the truth from God and faithfully transmit it through the ages~~ ++the Church receives, guards, interprets and faithfully transmits what God has revealed++, I have received ~~this truth from God through His Church—through Scripture, the Catechism, the saints, and two thousand years of faithful witnesses~~ ++the truths on which this book depends through her—through Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition, as taught in the Catechism and witnessed by the saints and two thousand years of faithful Christians++. God gave me a desire to ~~organize facts and synthesize them, and a heart to share what I've received. That's all~~ ++organize what I have learned, draw connections and share what I have received. That is all++.

**Editorial consequence.** The paragraph still describes dependence on the Church while accurately locating the author’s creative and intellectual work.

### PREF-04 · Recast a personal awakening without implying new revelation

**Severity:** Major · **Control:** DC-19, DC-21 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:12`

**Issue.** “God first revealed to me this life-changing truth” can mean that the author received a new revelation, although the sentence immediately quotes public revelation already given in Scripture.

**Why it matters.** The experience can be described strongly as grace, encounter, awakening, or illumination without suggesting that Christ’s definitive revelation was privately supplemented. The book’s front matter already states that Scripture quotations use the RSV-2CE unless otherwise noted, so individual quotations do not need a redundant translation label.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 65–67; *Dei Verbum* 2; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-19, DC-21.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

When I was 19, on a Catholic retreat in Panama in February 1988, ~~God first revealed to me this life-changing truth:~~ ++the Lord used Sacred Scripture to awaken me to a life-changing truth already revealed in Christ and entrusted to the Church:++ "So we know and believe the love God has for us. God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God abides in him~~." (1 John 4:16)~~++" (1 John 4:16).++ It made intuitive sense—I felt its truth deep in my bones—but I couldn't fully explain *why* it was true or *what* it meant. That moment ignited a lifelong quest.

**Editorial consequence.** The testimony remains personal and providential while the object of faith remains the Church’s received public revelation.

### PREF-05 · Replace scientific-verification language with an accurate theological aim

**Severity:** Review · **Control:** DC-26 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:14`

**Issue.** “Almost scientific” and “verify” imply an empirical method and a level of demonstration the book does not supply. The same paragraph repeats private-revelation language and ends one sentence with a rhetorical fragment.

**Why it matters.** Theology can be clear, systematic, rational, testable for internal coherence, and open to examination without becoming experimental science. The conclusion should claim only what the argument can earn.

**Controlling source:** Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-21, DC-26.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

My prayer ever since has been to understand and share the ~~deep love that God revealed to me~~ ++depth of the love proclaimed in this passage++ in a way that just makes sense to people. This book is the culmination of that journey—an attempt to explain, in ~~an almost scientific~~ ++a clear and systematic++ way, just how profoundly true the statement "God is ~~Love~~ ++love++" really is~~. Not~~++—not++ by inventing new truths, but by systematically unpacking the ancient truths in a way our generation can understand, ~~verify, and~~ ++examine and++ embrace.

**Editorial consequence.** This preserves the author’s ambition while removing a category mistake and an overclaim.

### PREF-06 · Clarify who formed the author and restore the scriptural order

**Severity:** Clarity · **Control:** DC-28 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:18`

**Issue.** “Saints in my life” can be read as calling every person in the subsequent family, priest, mentor, and teacher lists a saint in the canonized or ecclesial sense. The Great Commandment allusion also changes the order of heart, soul, mind, and strength.

**Why it matters.** The broader biblical use of “saints” is legitimate, but this wording is ambiguous in a Catholic acknowledgments section that includes people from different ecclesial or philosophical backgrounds. A recognizable biblical allusion should preserve its sequence.

**Controlling sources:** Mark 12:30, RSV; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-28.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

The faith I'm sharing didn't come to me in a vacuum. I came to know, love, and serve Jesus through real people—~~saints in my life who were faithful witnesses and~~ ++family members, priests, mentors and teachers who helped form me and++ taught me to love the Lord with all my heart, ~~mind, soul and~~ ++soul, mind, and++ strength.

**Editorial consequence.** The sentence becomes inclusive of every named influence without assigning an unintended ecclesial status.

### PREF-07 · Repair the family acknowledgments’ theological emphasis and grammar

**Severity:** Copy / clarity · **Control:** DC-26 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:20–24`

**Issue.** Calling the author’s wife “the most important reason” to desire God’s love unintentionally places a created relationship above God as the motive of charity. The final bullet has a dangling construction: “through whose search … helped.” Punctuation is inconsistent across the list.

**Why it matters.** The intended testimony is strong: human love can deepen desire for God and assist growth in virtue. Clear grammar allows that meaning to land without a theological hierarchy the author likely does not intend.

**Source list with proposed inline redline:**

- **MamaLou and DaddyBob** (my grandparents)—faithful Catholics with 11 children++,++ including my Dad, who showed me what a faithful husband and father looks like++.++
- **My parents, Barry and Magda**—who gave me life and raised me in the faith++.++
- **My wife Tyra**—~~who is the most important reason for me to desire God's love and who helped teach me to be a good~~ ++whose love has deepened my desire to know God's love and who has helped me become a better++ man, husband and father++.++
- **My six sons**—with special thanks to the Lord for Keaton's journey~~, through whose search for truth from atheism back to Catholicism helped~~++; his search for truth—from atheism back to Catholicism—helped++ bring me back to His Church.

**Editorial consequence.** The list retains every person and every intended act of gratitude.

### PREF-08 · Repair an awkward image in the mentors list

**Severity:** Copy · **Location:** `2_preface.md:26–30`

**Issue.** “Poured out their love for Jesus on so many youth and adults” is grammatically possible but semantically awkward: the object being poured “on” people is the mentors’ love *for* Jesus, not love directed toward those people. “Youth” also reads bureaucratically when referring to persons.

**Why it matters.** Acknowledgment prose should be especially clear because readers cannot infer the intended ministry from surrounding argument.

**Source list with proposed inline redline:**

- **Father Rutledge**—who cared so much for the younger generation
- **Father Kane**—who was a true shepherd and a true friend
- **Laura and Ramon Reyes**—who followed the Holy Spirit and ~~poured out their love for Jesus on~~ ++shared their love of Jesus with++ so many ~~youth~~ ++young people++ and adults
- **John Husum and Berta Briceno**—who lovingly supported the revival in our youth

**Editorial consequence.** The proposal changes only the unclear image and leaves the testimonial claim intact.

### PREF-09 · Keep teacher acknowledgments personal rather than revelatory or universal

**Severity:** Review · **Control:** DC-20, DC-21, DC-26 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:32–38`

**Issue.** Jordan Peterson is credited with showing “so many” what it means for the Bible to be “true,” an undefined and unsupported public claim. Scott Hahn is said to have “revealed” doctrine, repeating the Preface’s revelation ambiguity, and “but inspires” creates an unintended contrast. The phrase “understanding the Old Covenants” is intentional: it records the author’s formative recognition of the Old Testament’s covenantal structure and its influence on the word “covenant” in the book’s central definition. It is not an error and should remain.

**Why it matters.** An acknowledgment may respectfully name Catholic, Protestant, or secular influences as personal testimony without using them as the book’s theological authority. Personalizing the effect removes the need to prove broad claims about an audience.

**Controlling sources:** *Dei Verbum* 2, 7–10; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-20–21, DC-26.

**Source list with proposed inline redline:**

- **Bill LaBarr**—who taught me faith begins with gratitude
- **Timothy Keller**—who taught me what an idol actually is
- **Jordan B. Peterson**—who ~~showed so many in our time what it means that the Bible is "true"~~ ++prompted me to think more seriously about how biblical truth engages human experience++
- **Bishop Robert Barron**—who makes the beauty of Catholicism accessible ~~but~~ ++and++ inspires us to know our faith deeply
- **Scott Hahn**—who ~~revealed~~ ++helped me see++ the importance of understanding the Old Covenants in order to understand the New Covenant and why the Eucharist is the source and summit of the Christian life
- **Trent Horn, Joe Heschmeyer, Tim Staples, and Catholic Answers**—who defend the faith with deep understanding, rigorous theological thinking, clarity, and charity

**Editorial consequence.** Timothy Keller remains named because this is a respectful acknowledgment of personal influence, not a controlling theological citation. Scott Hahn’s connection to “understanding the Old Covenants” also remains exactly as intended; only “revealed” changes to avoid confusing personal instruction with divine revelation. The proposal improves precision without erasing the author’s actual intellectual history.

### PREF-10 · Remove the implication that the manuscript is inspired by the Holy Spirit

**Severity:** Stop-ship · **Control:** DC-20, DC-21 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:44`

**Issue.** The sentence explicitly calls the Holy Spirit the author of whatever is true in the book and then denies divine revelation. The denial does not cure the preceding inspiration claim. “The same grace that makes my entire life, and yours, ‘super-natural’” also collapses created life, actual grace, sanctifying grace, and supernatural participation, and presumes the reader’s state of grace.

**Why it matters.** Inspiration belongs properly to Sacred Scripture. Divine assistance, providence, prayer, study, and grace do not make a private manuscript inspired. The human author must assume responsibility for the book’s wording and errors and submit its claims to ecclesial judgment.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 65–67; *Dei Verbum* 11; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-06, DC-20–21.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

~~If anything in this book is true, its author is the Holy Spirit working through the Church.~~ ++If anything in this book faithfully expresses the truth, I owe that truth to the Holy Spirit's work in the Church and to the grace by which He has guided my study. I remain the human author and am responsible for the book's reasoning, wording and errors.++ I am not claiming any semblance of ~~"divine revelation" here—only the same grace that makes my entire life, and yours, "super-natural"!~~ ++divine revelation or inspiration for this manuscript; I submit it to the judgment of the Church.++

**Editorial consequence.** This is the Preface’s most important redline and should be settled before later chapters use revelation or inspiration language.

### PREF-11 · Distinguish revealed sources from later witnesses and teaching instruments

**Severity:** Major · **Control:** DC-20, DC-21 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:46`

**Issue.** The current syntax says the Creator’s wisdom is “revealed through” Scripture, the Catechism, the saints, and a two-thousand-year tradition in the same sense. It also repeats “Through the Holy Spirit” immediately after the inspiration-risk paragraph.

**Why it matters.** Public revelation is given in Christ and transmitted in Scripture and Tradition. The Catechism presents Church teaching; saints witness, contemplate, and explain; the Holy Spirit assists the Church and the author without making every later formulation revealed.

**Controlling sources:** CCC 65–67; *Dei Verbum* 7–10; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-20–21.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

~~Through the Holy Spirit, I am simply gathering the wisdom of our Creator that has been revealed through Sacred Scripture, the Catechism, the saints, and the Church's 2,000-year tradition~~ ++With prayer for the Holy Spirit's guidance, I am gathering what I have learned from Sacred Scripture and Sacred Tradition, as taught in the Catechism and witnessed by the saints and the Church's two-thousand-year life++—hoping to present it in a way that is meaningful to all Christians.

**Editorial consequence.** The paragraph still credits the Holy Spirit and the Church while preserving the distinct authority of each source.

### PREF-12 · Make the AI disclosure precise about authorship, authority, and responsibility

**Severity:** Major · **Control:** DC-20, DC-26, DC-28 · **Location:** `2_preface.md:50`

**Issue.** The opening timeline is now out of date: the manuscript has been refined over a year and a half, following 37 years of prayer, study and teaching. The disclosure is commendably candid, but “this book was not written by AI” sits uneasily beside AI-assisted research, organization, and editing. “What God has revealed to me” repeats the private-revelation ambiguity. “It only helped me say more clearly what I already knew to be true” understates the admitted research role and could sound as though the author did not need to verify generated claims.

**Why it matters.** A bishop-facing disclosure should be current, name AI as a tool, identify the human author, deny any ecclesial authority or accuracy guarantee to the tool, and place responsibility for the final words and claims squarely on the author.

**Controlling sources:** *Dei Verbum* 11 by analogy concerning true human authorship and responsibility; Catholic Doctrinal Control Sheet DC-20, DC-26, DC-28.

**Source paragraph with proposed inline redline:**

A note on how this book was written: this is the fourth full version of this manuscript, refined over ~~about a year~~ ++a year and a half++ of writing after 37 years of prayer~~ and~~++,++ study++ and teaching++. Throughout that year, I used AI tools—Claude and ChatGPT—to help research, organize, and edit these words, especially in tracing the connections between Scripture, the Catechism, and the saints. ~~But this book was not written by AI. I directed every word based on what God has revealed to me through His Scripture and His Church. No AI can know the depths of the human heart, or the depths of God's love—it only helped me say more clearly what I already knew to be true.~~ ++I remain the human author and take responsibility for every sentence and claim. I reviewed, revised, accepted or rejected the tools' suggestions in light of what I have learned from Sacred Scripture and the teaching of the Church. AI supplies no ecclesial authority and guarantees neither doctrinal nor factual accuracy; it served only as a research and editorial aid, while the judgment and responsibility for the final text are mine.++

**Editorial consequence.** This disclosure is transparent enough for ecclesiastical and public review without making claims about AI involvement that a reader could reasonably dispute.
