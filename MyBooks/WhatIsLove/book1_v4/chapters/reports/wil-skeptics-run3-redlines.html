    Now:

    > And here is the logic the skeptic refuses to face: There is no freedom to choose good without the freedom to choose evil. You cannot have one without the other. The moment God creates a being capable of genuine love, He creates a being capable of genuine rejection. To demand a world without evil is to demand a world without freedom—which is to demand a world without love.

    Proposed (lead-in changed, and a new paragraph follows):

    > And here is the logic at the heart of freedom: There is no freedom to choose good without the freedom to choose evil. You cannot have one without the other. The moment God creates a being capable of genuine love, He creates a being capable of genuine rejection. To demand a world without evil is to demand a world without freedom—which is to demand a world without love. > > And if you are already thinking of heaven—where the saints are perfectly free and sin no more—you have seen the point exactly. The freedom of the blessed is not freedom taken away but freedom fulfilled: a heart so filled with God that it no longer wants anything less. That fullness cannot be installed at the start, because it is the fruit of a love freely given and freely kept, day after day, until it is complete. The road of freedom must be walked for the arrival to be an arrival.

    This was the strongest unanswered objection in the whole review — the book's own Chapters 10 and 17 describe sinless freedom in glory. Two sentences close it, in the register of the chapter. The old lead-in also goes: the skeptic has faced this logic; now the paragraph answers his best reply instead of claiming he has none.

    Edit 9 · Mercy is not between the Persons (finding 2)

    Now:

    > They're participating in the mercy that flows eternally between Father, Son, and Spirit.

    Proposed:

    > They're participating in the mercy that flows from the eternal love of Father, Son, and Spirit.

    Mercy presupposes a wound to forgive; between the divine Persons there is nothing to forgive. The book keeps this rule perfectly for sacrifice — eternal self-gift, economic Cross — and this one sentence was the exception.

    Edit 10 · The Trinity dilemma, held as revelation's answer (finding 11)

    Now:

    > And see what this means: a God who is love before anything exists must have a Beloved before anything exists. Either love is eternal in God—and God is eternally a communion of Persons—or love began only when creation finally gave Him something to love, and "God is love" would name something He became rather than who He is.

    Proposed:

    > And see what this means: if God is love before anything exists, then love was being given and received before anything existed. Whom did the Father love before there was a world? Reason can only stand before that question; revelation answers it: from all eternity, God is a communion of Persons, eternally giving and receiving the one love that God is. Were it otherwise, love would have begun only when creation finally gave Him something to love, and "God is love" would name something He became rather than who He is.

    The Church herself teaches that the Trinity cannot be demonstrated by reason alone — and the surrounding callout already has the right register ("the Trinity, just ahead, shows how it can be true"). This keeps the full force of the insight while letting revelation, not a syllogism, carry it. The next sentence ("The Trinity is not a puzzle bolted onto the Gospel…") stands unchanged.

    Edit 13 · "Every civilization" (finding 7)

    Now (two sentences, same section):

    > We see this pattern across cultures, throughout history, in every civilization: husband-father who protects and provides…

    > This is why every civilization that has endured has protected the permanent union of husband and wife ordered toward children—not as one lifestyle choice among many, but as the essential foundation of human flourishing.

    Proposed:

    > We see this pattern across cultures and throughout history: husband-father who protects and provides…

    > This is why every civilization that has endured has built itself on the union of husband and wife ordered toward children—and has flourished in the measure it held that union sacred and stable—not as one lifestyle choice among many, but as the essential foundation of human flourishing.

    Rome and other long-lived civilizations had easy divorce; a hostile reader refutes the exceptionless claim in one line. The recalibrated claim — every enduring civilization builds on this union and flourishes as it protects it — is nearly as strong and cannot be refuted by a counterexample.

    Edit 14 · Turning the normalization concession into evidence (finding 10)

    Now (paragraph ends):

    > It would never occur to a child that parents could separate—unless society has already normalized it, unless they've seen it happen to friends, unless the unthinkable has become thinkable.

    Proposed (same paragraph, with an addition):

    > It would never occur to a child that parents could separate—unless society has already normalized it, unless they've seen it happen to friends, unless the unthinkable has become thinkable. And notice what normalization actually changes: the child's belief, never the child's wound. A child can be taught to expect divorce; no child has ever been taught to stop grieving one. The constancy of the wound—in every culture, under every arrangement—is the witness.

    As written, the concession hands the skeptic his mechanism (the "assumption" varies with environment, like anything learned). The added sentences flip it: what never varies is the wound, and the wound is the testimony.
