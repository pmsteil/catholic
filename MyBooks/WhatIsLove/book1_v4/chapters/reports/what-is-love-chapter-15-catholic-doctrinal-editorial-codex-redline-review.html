    Chapter 15 · Counterfeit Love in the Modern World

    Chapter assessment

    Chapter 15 has a legitimate Catholic purpose: test cultural claims, defend objective truth and human dignity, oppose abortion and pornography, uphold sexual difference and marriage, reject atheistic collectivism, affirm Scripture–Tradition–Magisterium, insist that faith must live through charity, and call Christians to perseverance. It also occasionally acknowledges the genuine good that makes an error attractive.

    In its current form, however, this chapter is a major obstacle to episcopal review. It treats amorphous labels (“postmodernism,” “wokeism,” “self-actualization”), distinct moral acts, schools of thought and separated Christian traditions as twelve unitary systems; assigns each motives and inevitable outcomes; and uses the author’s seven boxes as both definition and verdict. Catholic doctrine recognizes real natural virtue, affection, sacrifice, marriage-like commitment and truth outside explicit faith while locating their ultimate fulfillment in Christ. Catholic social doctrine teaches both personal sin and “structures of sin,” so “only individual hearts” is not Catholic. Gender distress, identity claims, social transition, puberty blockers, hormones and surgeries need separate moral and factual treatment; changing medical claims require current primary sources. The abortion section is especially unsafe: “easy way out,” universal guilt and shame, inability to love afterward, “post-abortion syndrome,” dismissal of therapists and mapping all seven deadly sins violate Evangelium Vitae 99’s pastoral standard. The religious sections caricature diverse Protestant teachings and ignore Vatican II’s acknowledgement of shared gifts and shared guilt for division; Benedict XVI explicitly describes a Catholic sense in which “faith alone” is true when faith is not opposed to charity. This chapter needs exact propositions, Catholic sources, distinctions, evidence and remedies—not softer convictions, but better Catholic reasoning.

    CH15-01 · Withdraw the claim that one missing component proves no love exists

    Severity: Stop-ship / natural love and framework authority · Control: DC-01, DC-07 · Location: chapter_15.md:17–27

    Issue. Fallen people can show real natural affection, sacrifice and virtue while love remains incomplete or disordered. A missing facet can reveal a defect in a claim without proving “incomplete love isn’t love at all” or that the author’s definition exhausts every act.

    Controlling sources: CCC 1804–1811, 1812–1829.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    We can test claims about love against Scripture, natural law and Church teaching, using the book’s definition as one subordinate lens. If any component is missing or distorted, it's counterfeit—and counterfeits always cause harm. Incomplete love isn't love at all A missing or disordered good may expose a counterfeit claim, but real though imperfect natural love can remain. The task is to identify the precise truth retained, the precise error introduced, the actual moral object and the harm supported by evidence.

    Editorial consequence. The chapter can expose counterfeits without denying every natural good in imperfect people and communities.

    CH15-02 · Replace the seven-component tables as verdicts with proposition-level analysis

    Severity: Stop-ship / method and circularity · Control: DC-01, DC-26 · Location: chapter_15.md:31–45

    Issue. The seven elements and four ratings are author-created, not a Catholic doctrinal instrument. Whole traditions are graded as though each has a single “framework,” and “absent anywhere” is an impossible universal claim to establish.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Our definition reveals seven essential components ... Remove any component, and love becomes its opposite. Each table rates every component names seven themes this chapter can use as prompts. These are not exhaustive doctrinal criteria or quantitative grades. For each topic, define one exact proposition, acknowledge the genuine good or concern, state the Catholic correction with primary sources, distinguish persons from ideas and acts, and identify evidence-based consequences. The tables should be labeled interpretive summaries, not verdicts on every adherent or instance.

    Editorial consequence. The chapter becomes an examination of claims rather than a prosecution by spreadsheet.

    CH15-03 · Define postmodernism and relativism instead of assigning one self-refuting proposition to both

    Severity: Major / philosophy and copy · Control: DC-26, DC-28 · Location: chapter_15.md:48–70

    Issue. Postmodernism includes diverse analyses of language, power and metanarratives; not every postmodern thinker asserts “there is no objective truth.” Relativism also has multiple forms. The typo “object truth” must be “objective truth.” Critique exact propositions, not labels.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Postmodernism & Relativism Relativist Claims About Truth and Meaning

    The Appeal: Some contemporary claims stress perspective, social power and the limits of human interpretation. These concerns can expose bias and overconfidence. The error examined here is the exact proposition that no truth or moral norm is objectively valid across persons or cultures, together with the slogan “live your truth” when it means self-authored reality. Do not attribute that proposition to every thinker called postmodern.

    Postmodernism makes the bold foundational claim—that there is no object truth no single claim as a school; replace with named authors and propositions if the label remains. Correct “object truth” to “objective truth.”

    Editorial consequence. The philosophical target becomes refutable because it is finally defined.

    CH15-04 · Remove motive assignment and deterministic family claims from the relativism table

    Severity: Major / evidence and tone · Control: DC-22, DC-26 · Location: chapter_15.md:56–68

    Issue. The table says relativists idolize self, leave marriage when it stops serving them, demand others sacrifice truth and can resolve conflict only by force. These may describe particular outcomes but are not entailed motives of everyone who holds contextual or relativist views.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Relativism can make covenant promises difficult to justify when present preference becomes the highest norm; it binds you only ... makes it easy to walk away ... demands others sacrifice ... communion never escapes the self does not prove that every adherent is selfish, unfaithful or coercive. Many people live real fidelity inconsistently with their theory. Critique the theory’s capacity to explain permanent obligation while recognizing the actual virtues of persons. Social conflict can be settled only by force can become more vulnerable to power when no shared rational standard is acknowledged, though institutions and inherited norms may still restrain it.

    Editorial consequence. The section evaluates implications without pretending to read hearts or predict every marriage.

    CH15-05 · Critique Marxism using Catholic social doctrine rather than denying all dignity or structural sin

    Severity: Stop-ship / social doctrine · Control: DC-15, DC-22 · Location: chapter_15.md:72–92

    Issue. The Church rejects atheistic materialism, class hatred, collectivism and coercive communism, but also recognizes exploitation, class conflict, social sin, unjust structures, solidarity and the need for institutional reform. “Only individual hearts” contradicts Catholic social teaching.

    Controlling sources: CCC 1886–1889, 1939–1942, 2425; Centesimus Annus 13–15; Sollicitudo Rei Socialis 36–40.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Marxism correctly identifies real exploitation and structural injustice but, in its atheistic and revolutionary forms, reduces the person to material and class relations, treats class struggle as history’s engine and subordinates persons to collectivist ends. It has no doctrine of individual dignity ... the individual heart is the only place evil originates and can be overcome ... groups cannot sin Marxist thinkers do speak of human dignity and alienation, though the Church judges their materialist anthropology inadequate. Personal sins create and sustain “structures of sin”; conversion of hearts and reform of unjust institutions are both required. Groups are not persons with souls, but institutions and collective practices can embody responsibility and injustice. Solidarity must not be reduced to envy or a claim on “other men’s goods.”

    Editorial consequence. The critique becomes recognizably Catholic rather than individualist.

    CH15-06 · Source and qualify the historical-death claim

    Severity: Major / historical evidence · Control: DC-26, DC-28 · Location: chapter_15.md:90

    Issue. “Tens of millions dead under Marxist regimes” may be broadly defensible but depends on definitions, countries, periods and causal attribution. A bishop-ready book needs a named scholarly source and must distinguish ideology, regime policy, war, famine, repression and contested estimates.

    Source sentence with proposed inline redline:

    Tens of millions dead in the 20th century under Marxist regimes. Marxism creates resentment ... totalitarianism. Self-described Marxist-Leninist regimes committed mass repression, executions, deportations and disastrous policies that contributed to enormous loss of life. Cite a current, reputable historical source, give its scope and date, distinguish direct killing from famine and war, and avoid implying one number is uncontested or every Marxist analysis entails every regime crime.

    Editorial consequence. A serious historical indictment is documented rather than sloganized.

    CH15-07 · Remove or define “wokeism” and do not call it Marxism proven by a moving target

    Severity: Stop-ship / terminology and argument · Control: DC-15, DC-26, DC-28 · Location: chapter_15.md:94–120

    Issue. “Wokeism” has no stable analytical definition and is used for anti-racism, identity politics, corporate practices, medical claims and speech norms. Saying it is Marxism recast and that changing targets prove a power grab is assertion, not analysis.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Wokeism: “Love Is Affirming Identity” Collective-Guilt and Identity-Reduction Claims

    Wokeism promises ... It is Marxism recast ... the machinery has not ... proof ... seize power. Avoid “wokeism” unless defined from a specific source. The propositions examined here are: assigning personal guilt or innocence primarily by group membership; treating lived experience as immune from rational examination; and imposing collective penalties without individual responsibility. Catholic teaching rejects collective blame while also recognizing solidarity, historical effects, social structures and duties to repair demonstrable injustice.

    Editorial consequence. Readers can assess exact errors without a partisan catch-all.

    CH15-08 · Acknowledge structures of sin and forms of collective responsibility

    Severity: Stop-ship / social doctrine · Control: DC-15 · Location: chapter_15.md:102–118

    Issue. “Only individual hearts oppress” and “groups cannot sin” ignore institutions, laws, markets and cultural practices shaped by personal sins and capable of perpetuating injustice. Collective responsibility does not erase personal culpability, but Catholic teaching requires both levels.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Only individual hearts oppress other hearts. Only individual hearts can seek forgiveness and reconcile. Fighting systemic injustice becomes license for personal injustice. Only persons bear moral guilt in the strict sense, but personal sins consolidate into “structures of sin” that influence conduct and distribute harm. Catholics must convert hearts, reform unjust laws and institutions, make proportionate reparation, avoid unjust collective punishment, and judge each person’s culpability individually. “Systemic injustice” is neither automatically true nor automatically a license; it is a factual and moral claim requiring evidence.

    Editorial consequence. The chapter no longer contradicts St. John Paul II’s social doctrine.

    CH15-09 · Remove the unsourced medical and sex-based claims from the “wokeism” section

    Severity: Stop-ship / medicine and evidence · Control: DC-25, DC-26, DC-28 · Location: chapter_15.md:116–120

    Issue. “Children are sterilized,” sports, spaces, speech and self-destruction combine distinct factual and policy questions. Medical effects and legal availability vary by intervention, age, jurisdiction and date. Current primary medical evidence is required and should be kept separate from moral anthropology.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Children are sterilized for ideology. Women lose spaces and sports to biological males. Disagreement becomes violence ... compassion becomes complicity in self-destruction. Delete this catalogue from the undefined section. If the manuscript addresses sports, privacy, compelled speech or pediatric interventions, create separate proposition-level subsections. For each, state the Catholic principle, current jurisdiction, exact population and intervention, benefits and risks reported by authoritative primary medical bodies, evidence quality and date. Do not infer every participant’s motive or outcome.

    Editorial consequence. Volatile empirical claims are no longer embedded in a theological label.

    CH15-10 · Remove the claim that the Church is history’s greatest justice force and “never forces”

    Severity: Major / history and ecclesial humility · Control: DC-22, DC-26 · Location: chapter_15.md:120

    Issue. The Church has made immense contributions to justice and also includes members and institutions guilty of coercion, abuse, complicity and delayed reform. “Greatest force” is unmeasurable; “never forcing” is historically false.

    Source sentence with proposed inline redline:

    The Church has been the greatest force for justice in history ... inviting ... never forcing has given the world profound witnesses and institutions of justice because of the Gospel, while her members have also committed grave injustices and used coercion contrary to the Gospel. Her credibility requires repentance as well as proclamation. Authentic evangelization proposes truth without coercing faith.

    Editorial consequence. Ecclesial confidence is joined to truth and penitence.

    CH15-11 · Separate gender distress, identity, social transition and medical intervention

    Severity: Stop-ship / anthropology and pastoral care · Control: DC-24, DC-25 · Location: chapter_15.md:122–142

    Issue. “Gender ideology” collapses persons, distress, theories, pronoun practices, social identity, blockers, hormones and surgeries. Catholic anthropology affirms body–soul unity and sexual difference while requiring respect, compassion and no unjust discrimination. Pastoral responses differ by person and act.

    Controlling sources: CCC 2331–2336; Dignitas Infinita 55–60.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Gender Ideology Claims That Sexed Embodiment Is Subordinate to Self-Defined Gender

    The exact claim criticized is that personal identity can be determined independently of or contrary to bodily sex. Do not use that critique to define every person experiencing gender dysphoria or distress. Distinguish involuntary experience from theory, moral choice, social transition and each medical intervention. Every person retains equal dignity and must be treated with respect and compassion and protected from unjust discrimination, bullying and violence. The body and soul form one person; sex is a received bodily reality, and medicine must serve the integral good rather than treat the body as raw material.

    Editorial consequence. Catholic anthropology is retained while persons and actions are no longer collapsed.

    CH15-12 · Replace categorical fertility and pediatric claims with current sourced evidence

    Severity: Stop-ship / medicine · Control: DC-25, DC-26, DC-28 · Location: chapter_15.md:131–140

    Issue. “Children sacrifice fertility,” “irreversible interventions before understanding,” and “sterilized before puberty” are not accurate as universal descriptions. Puberty suppression, sex hormones and surgeries have different effects, reversibility, ages and legal status. This is temporally unstable high-stakes medicine.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Children sacrifice their own fertility ... undergo irreversible interventions ... Children sterilized before puberty may never become parents. Some interventions can impair fertility or sexual function, with risks differing by puberty suppression, hormones, surgery, duration and developmental stage. State no universal effect. Before publication, cite current primary clinical guidance and systematic evidence, identify jurisdiction and date, acknowledge uncertainty, and distinguish ethical objections from empirical outcome claims. Encourage families to seek competent medical and pastoral care consistent with Catholic anthropology.

    Editorial consequence. The book stops making medically overbroad claims that may age or mislead.

    CH15-13 · Do not reduce fulfillment to marriage and family

    Severity: Major / vocation and infertility · Control: DC-23 · Location: chapter_15.md:138–142

    Issue. Embodied sexual difference is real and marriage is the union of man and woman, but not every person is called to marriage. Consecrated celibacy, priesthood, widowhood, faithful singleness and infertility can be profoundly fruitful.

    Source sentence with proposed inline redline:

    This truth helps us receive our bodies as integrated persons and discern our vocation. Find fulfillment in the complementary communion we were made for: the life-giving love of marriage and family. Some are called to marriage and family; others to consecrated celibacy, priesthood or faithful single life. Every vocation is ordered to holiness and spiritual fruitfulness, and infertility does not make a marriage or person unfulfilled.

    Editorial consequence. Sexual anthropology does not become compulsory marriage.

    CH15-14 · Recognize atheists’ real truth, virtue, covenant, mercy and sacrifice

    Severity: Stop-ship / natural good and nonbelievers · Control: DC-07, DC-22 · Location: chapter_15.md:144–168

    Issue. The table says atheism cannot ground truth, justice or sacrifice and implies atheists cannot form real communion or permanent bonds. Catholic teaching recognizes natural law, conscience, acquired virtue, affection, marriage and actual grace among nonbelievers. Atheists can sacrifice without secretly reducing it to genes or advantage.

    Controlling sources: CCC 31–38, 1804–1811, 1954–1960; Gaudium et Spes 16.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Atheists can know empirical and moral truths, form real friendships and marriages, forgive, serve justice and make heroic sacrifices. Sacrifice ✗ ... no account possible ... every apparent one reduced ... communion only two minds in parallel ... covenant revocable ... mercy theorized These goods are real, not simulations. Catholic philosophy argues that their ultimate ground and supernatural fulfillment are in God, even when the person does not acknowledge Him. Do not infer from a contested metaphysical account that the concrete act lacks love or virtue. Atheism as a proposition denies God; individual atheists have diverse motives, ethics and lives.

    Editorial consequence. The chapter makes the Catholic claim about fulfillment without denying observable goodness.

    CH15-15 · Remove the Holocaust/eugenics causal shortcut

    Severity: Stop-ship / history and reputation · Control: DC-22, DC-26, DC-28 · Location: chapter_15.md:160–166

    Issue. Eugenics, Nazism and the Holocaust were multicausal and drew on racism, nationalism, pseudoscience, state power, antisemitism and other currents; Christians were perpetrators, bystanders, resisters and victims. Presenting death camps as the direct result of losing belief in the image of God is historically irresponsible.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    The eugenics movements and death camps followed exactly this move: strip away the doctrine ... human value becomes negotiable. Eugenics and the Holocaust demonstrate how pseudoscience, racism, antisemitism, nationalism, bureaucracy and state power can deny whole classes of people equal dignity. Some perpetrators rejected Christian anthropology; others acted within nominally Christian cultures, and Christian failures require acknowledgement. The history warns against making dignity contingent, but it cannot be reduced to atheism as a single cause.

    Editorial consequence. The moral warning remains without exploiting the Holocaust as a one-step apologetic.

    CH15-16 · Engage atheistic philosophies individually and correct “reason alone could never discover dignity”

    Severity: Major / natural reason · Control: DC-07, DC-22, DC-26 · Location: chapter_15.md:164–168

    Issue. Nietzsche, utilitarianism and nihilism are not interchangeable, and utilitarian theories explain binding through impartial welfare even if Catholic critique finds them insufficient. Reason can recognize human dignity; revelation gives its fullest light and supernatural destiny.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Various atheistic philosophies propose different goods and require separate treatment. Each collapses ... denies essential components ... reason alone could never discover ... only revelation gives human worth Catholic critique should state the strongest version of each and identify precise limits—for example, whether aggregate welfare can protect inviolable persons. Natural reason can recognize intrinsic dignity, as the Church explicitly teaches; revelation confirms, purifies and discloses its deepest origin and destiny in God.

    Editorial consequence. Natural theology and fair philosophical engagement replace sweeping dismissal.

    CH15-17 · Restore ordered self-love as a good in itself, not merely an instrument to be spent

    Severity: Stop-ship / charity and self-care · Control: DC-07, DC-23, DC-25 · Location: chapter_15.md:170–196

    Issue. The chapter rightly rejects narcissistic self-construction but says self-love is merely “instrumental” so the self can be poured out. A person is willed for their own sake, owes care to body and soul, and may enjoy legitimate growth, rest and achievement under God. Self-gift is not self-erasure.

    Controlling sources: CCC 1809, 1825, 2288; Gaudium et Spes 24.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Self-actualization retains a genuine good: developing gifts and caring responsibly for a person God wills for their own sake. Genuine love of self is instrumental ... cared for so it can be poured out ... the self is found by being given, not built Ordered self-love is itself part of charity’s measure—“as yourself”—and also disposes us for service. Growth, therapy, boundaries, rest and vocation can be genuine goods when ordered to God and duties; they become counterfeit only when autonomous self-fulfillment is made the supreme end or used to abandon justice and covenant. Self-gift fulfills rather than annihilates the person.

    Editorial consequence. The critique does not teach caregivers or wounded readers that they exist only as resources for others.

    CH15-18 · Remove caricatures of therapists and unsupported mental-health causation

    Severity: Stop-ship / mental health · Control: DC-25, DC-26 · Location: chapter_15.md:188–194

    Issue. Therapists do not all teach one “liturgy,” and licensed care can help people accept limits, honor duties and love well. Claims that anxiety, depression and loneliness rise “in lockstep with self-focus” require evidence and ignore economic, technological, familial, medical and social factors.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Therapists, influencers, and self-help books all teach the same liturgy ... Anxiety, depression, and loneliness rise in lockstep with self-focus. Some commercial self-help messages make autonomous fulfillment supreme. Do not group licensed therapists with influencers or assume clinical care is narcissistic; competent therapy can cooperate with pastoral and sacramental care. Mental-health and loneliness trends are multicausal. Any empirical association with self-focus requires current peer-reviewed evidence and probabilistic language.

    Editorial consequence. The book no longer alienates clinicians or discourages necessary care.

    CH15-19 · Describe pornography’s objective evil without universal addiction and family-outcome claims

    Severity: Major / sexuality and mental health · Control: DC-10, DC-25, DC-26 · Location: chapter_15.md:200–222

    Issue. Pornography gravely violates dignity and chastity, but not every performer is trafficked, not every viewer is clinically addicted, and not every use causes a broken marriage or emotional absence. Culpability and effects vary; treatment and sacramental help can cooperate.

    Controlling sources: CCC 2354, 1735.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Pornography removes sexual acts from intimacy and displays persons for others’ pleasure; production can involve exploitation or coercion and consumption can gravely harm the user and relationships. “Consenting adults” launders the coercion, trafficking and exploitation that supply the industry ... habitual use devastates ... destroying ability to love ... Addiction. Broken marriages. Emotional absence. Consent does not by itself make pornographic production moral, but coercion and trafficking must be alleged only where evidenced. Use can become compulsive or addictive and is associated with varied harms; do not diagnose every user or predict every marriage. Encourage Confession, accountability and qualified clinical treatment where needed.

    Editorial consequence. Catholic moral clarity is joined to factual and clinical precision.

    CH15-20 · Remove motive assignment and “easy way out” from abortion

    Severity: Stop-ship / abortion and pastoral care · Control: DC-10, DC-25 · Location: chapter_15.md:224–242

    Issue. Abortion intentionally kills an unborn human being, but women may face coercion, abandonment, abuse, poverty, fear, misinformation or grave pressure. “Easy way out,” failure to rise to vocation and autonomy-idolatry judge motives and culpability. The father has duties and interests, but no “right to his own child” as possession.

    Controlling sources: CCC 2270–2275; Evangelium Vitae 58–63, 99.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Abortion’s objective moral object is the intentional killing of unborn human life. The adult takes the easy way out ... loving neither self, neighbor, nor God ... denies her capacity to rise ... idolizes autonomy Do not infer that every woman acts from ease, selfishness or autonomy. Pressure from a partner or family, fear, poverty, abuse, isolation and misinformation can diminish responsibility without changing the act’s objective gravity. Fathers owe protection and support and suffer real loss; describe parental duties and the child’s right to life rather than a possessive “father’s right to his own child.”

    Editorial consequence. The defense of unborn life follows the Church’s pastoral voice toward women and men in crisis.

    CH15-21 · Remove universal guilt, shame, impaired-love and post-abortion-syndrome claims

    Severity: Stop-ship / trauma and evidence · Control: DC-25, DC-26 · Location: chapter_15.md:233–248

    Issue. Emotional outcomes after abortion vary. Some experience grief, guilt or trauma; others report different responses. “Post-abortion syndrome” is not a universally recognized diagnosis. The text cannot say a woman cannot love as fully, destroys part of herself, or becomes bound to guilt and secrecy.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    The guilt and shame that follow ... cannot love again as fully ... lost something inside ... grief follows ... post-abortion syndrome ... destroyed a part of herself ... bound to guilt, grief, secret reshaping every relationship. Abortion takes the child’s life and can leave women and men with grief, guilt, numbness, trauma or complicated relational and spiritual wounds; experiences vary, and no single psychological syndrome or emotional sequence should be asserted universally. No past sin makes a person incapable of future love or sanctity. Use current clinical evidence, avoid diagnosing readers, and offer sacramental, pastoral and professional support.

    Editorial consequence. The passage stops imposing a psychological script on every woman.

    CH15-22 · Do not dismiss therapy or make Project Rachel exclusively spiritual

    Severity: Stop-ship / medical and pastoral safety · Control: DC-18, DC-25 · Location: chapter_15.md:248–252

    Issue. Christ alone forgives sin and saves, but therapists can treat grief, PTSD, depression and relationship wounds. Project Rachel itself can refer participants to professional care. “No therapist ... can reach this wound” creates a false competition.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    No therapist, no political movement ... can reach this wound ... only through Him. No clinical care can sacramentally absolve sin or replace Christ, and no slogan can guarantee healing. Yet competent therapy can treat psychological wounds and cooperate with pastoral care. Christ offers forgiveness and grace through His Church; Project Rachel and similar ministries can integrate prayer, Confession, compassionate accompaniment and referral to qualified clinicians.

    Editorial consequence. Spiritual and clinical care are complementary rather than rivals.

    CH15-23 · Delete the seven-deadly-sins and “anti-sacrament” abortion anatomy

    Severity: Stop-ship / moral theology and rhetoric · Control: DC-01, DC-10, DC-25 · Location: chapter_15.md:238–252

    Issue. Calling abortion the “ultimate anti-sacrament,” most diagnostically complete sin, violation of the First and Tenth Commandments, and embodiment of all seven capital sins is an invented system that assigns lust, envy, gluttony, wrath and other motives without evidence. Gravity does not come from category count.

    Source passage with proposed inline redline:

    Abortion is the ultimate anti-sacrament ... most diagnostically complete sin ... breaks First and Tenth ... embodies all seven deadly sins ... no other act maps ... only a human remedy cannot reach. Delete this catalogue. State instead: abortion is objectively grave because it directly and intentionally takes innocent human life. Personal sins and pressures surrounding it vary and must not be assigned by a matrix. Christ’s mercy is greater than every sin; reconciliation requires truth, contrition, sacramental absolution for Catholics, and appropriate penance and healing—not an invented claim that one act contains every vice.

    Editorial consequence. The section remains uncompromising without sensationalism or motive diagnosis.
