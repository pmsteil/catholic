… To plant a local, or particular, church in the New Testament sense, then, is not to gather an affinity group around a charismatic teacher. It is to extend the one governing body for believers that Christ founded, which hands on the deposit of faith intact. The Great Commission did not produce a movement; it produced ¶None of this rests on the Great Commission alone. Christ said plainly what He was doing: "And I tell you, you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my church, and the powers of death shall not prevail against it" (Matthew 16:18). Not a church, and not churches: my Church, one, and His. Everything traced above was that building: the Twelve, with Peter at their head (CCC 765); the New Covenant in His Blood, instituted at the Last Supper and fulfilled on the Cross (cf. Luke 22:20; CCC 766); and, on the evening of His Resurrection, His own breath: "And when he had said this, he breathed on them, and said to them, 'Receive the Holy Spirit.'" (John 20:22). The Greek verb is emphusao, "to breathe into," and it appears nowhere else in the New Testament. The Greek Old Testament uses it when God breathed the breath of life into Adam (Genesis 2:7), and when the prophet called the breath of God upon the dry bones so that they would live (Ezekiel 37:9). St. Paul uses a kindred word, theopneustos, "God-breathed," for Scripture itself (2 Timothy 3:16). The same Breath that made Adam a living being and raised the dry bones is the Breath Christ gave His Church, the Breath that inspired her Scriptures, and the Breath that filled the house at Pentecost like a mighty wind (Acts 2:2; CCC 767). The Commission sent that Church into the world, and what went out was not a movement but a Church.
