It is what the conscience itself recognizes the moment it stops to think. A love that bypasses the will is not love; a gift forced into a closed hand is not a gift. And so the Catechism reminds us what reason and conscience already see So the Catechism holds both together: faith itself "is a gift of God, a supernatural virtue infused by him" (CCC 153), and at the same time, "believing is an authentically human act" in which "the human intellect and will co-operate with divine grace" (CCC 154–155 (CCC 154). God loves us first; we say yes. The initiative is entirely His love; the reception is genuinely ours. "We love, because he first loved us" (1 John 4:19), and faith is not a separate thing standing beside that love. Faith is the open hand of the soul toward the One who is loving us; it is how love is received before it is returned. To believe Him is already to begin to love Him, because the One being believed is Love. Faith and love cannot be pried apart without breaking both. Close the fist against His love, and in the same motion you have closed it against trusting Him.
