## The Marketplace of Counterfeits

In the previous chapter, we saw that sin is fundamentally a failure to love—a rejection of truth, justice, mercy, or sacrifice. But what leads us to fail? Often, it's embracing counterfeit loves that promise fulfillment but deliver destruction.

Modern culture floods us with competing definitions of love. "Love is love." "Love wins." "Love means acceptance." How do you distinguish authentic from counterfeit?

We now have a diagnostic tool. Just as a jeweler tests gold against a standard, we can test any claim about love against our definition: **God's Perfect Love is the sacred gift of covenant which binds truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice into life-giving communion.** If any component is missing or distorted, it's counterfeit—and counterfeits always cause harm.

This chapter applies this diagnostic tool to twelve modern counterfeits, showing precisely what each eliminates or distorts, why it fails, and how God's Perfect Love provides the authentic good these counterfeits promise but cannot deliver.

**Incomplete love isn't love at all**—it's a distortion that creates the very evils it wants to save us from.

---

## How to Recognize a Counterfeit

Every counterfeit can be identified by what it's missing. Our definition reveals seven essential components: **Sacred, Covenant, Truth, Justice, Mercy, Sacrifice, and Life-giving Communion**. (Count the definition's words and you will find more: *gift* lives inside Sacred and Covenant—love freely given, never seized—and *binds* is not an eighth component but the integration itself, the very thing these tables test.) Remove or distort any component, and love becomes its opposite.

Each diagnostic table below rates every component using four markers, applied the same way throughout this chapter:

- **✓ Present & Rightly Ordered** — the counterfeit affirms this component correctly, aimed at its true end.
- **Partial** — the counterfeit retains a genuine but truncated piece of this component: true as far as it goes, incomplete in scope.
- **Distorted** — the counterfeit has a real, functioning analog of this component, but redirects it toward a false standard or object.
- **✗ Absent** — no functioning analog of this component exists anywhere in the counterfeit's framework.

The line between Distorted and ✗ matters: a counterfeit that talks about "justice" while defining it as vengeance, or as whatever power or preference currently favors, has not eliminated justice—it has hijacked it.

The counterfeits we'll examine fall into three categories: **ideological** (worldviews that eliminate components), **relational** (patterns that destroy covenant), and **religious** (incomplete Christianity). The diagnostic tables reveal the pattern.

## Section 1: Ideological Counterfeits

### Postmodernism & Relativism: "There Is No Objective Truth" / "Live Your Truth"

**The Appeal:** These twin counterfeits promise liberation from "oppressive" truth claims and the "freedom" to create your own meaning. Postmodernism makes the bold and foundational claim—that there is no object truth - and any claim of objective truth itself is only a mask for power. Relativism makes it personal—"live your truth," as though each of us authored our own reality.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | Postmodernism & Relativism | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Sacred | Distorted—idolizes the self, and with it a self-authored reality; personal "authenticity" and inner experience are treated as sacrosanct and beyond question, occupying the place transcendence should hold | ✓ |
| Covenant | Distorted—binds you only to those who share your subjective truth, and only for as long as it still feels true, making it easy to walk away even from the most important covenants—marriage, family—once they stop serving the self's truth | ✓ |
| Truth | ✗—self-refuting: it asserts "there is no objective truth" as an objective truth, borrowing the very thing it denies in order to deny it | ✓ |
| Justice | Distorted—re-centered on self-assertion: the right to define reality on one's own terms, whether that's choosing your own pronouns, authoring your own morality, or seizing outright power over others, rather than any standard outside the self | ✓ |
| Mercy | Distorted—"who am I to judge" and radical affirmation are offered as mercy, but both are themselves judgments, and mercy divorced from justice is not mercy at all; and refusing to name any wrong leaves nothing to forgive in yourself and no way to answer real injustice done to others | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | Distorted—inverted and demanded of others: for "my truth" to stand, everyone else must sacrifice their own claim to objective truth and defer to mine | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | Distorted—tribal "epistemic communities" offer the feeling of belonging, but since each member is really only affirming his own self-authored truth, communion never reaches beyond the self—you end up in fellowship with your own reflection, not truly united to another | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** At root, both idolize the self. Postmodernism denies truth exists at all; relativism relocates truth entirely inside the self—either way, the self ends up occupying the place only God's transcendence should hold, and both are self-refuting from the start: postmodernism asserts "there is no objective truth" as an objective truth, and relativism appeals to "my truth" even though truth, by definition, must correspond to reality outside the self. Once truth is no longer received but authored, everything reorganizes around the self. Justice becomes self-assertion—the right to define reality on your own terms. Covenant binds you only to those who share your truth, and only for as long as it still feels true, which is why even marriage and family become negotiable. Mercy collapses into "who am I to judge" or its mirror, radical affirmation—both are judgments themselves, and neither leaves anything real to forgive. Sacrifice gets inverted: instead of giving yourself for another, you demand others sacrifice their claim to truth so yours can stand. And communion never actually escapes the self—you end up in fellowship with your own reflection, not truly united to anyone else.

**Why It Fails:** Moral paralysis and social fragmentation. If truth is merely constructed—whether by power or by preference—then nothing is truly wrong, love becomes arbitrary, and conflict can be settled only by force. A society needs a shared reality it did not invent; strip that away and communities splinter into competing subjective claims.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love is rooted in Truth—the eternal Logos who became flesh (John 1:14). Objective truth, grounded in reality—His Word, which encompasses all truth - all of creation, all of His revelation, the truth of His love written on our hearts—liberates us from the slavery of subjective opinion and enables authentic justice, mercy, and communion. "If you continue in my word, you are truly my disciples, and you will know the truth, and the truth will make you free" (John 8:32). And He did not leave that truth for each of us to assemble alone. There is "one Lord, one faith, one baptism" (Ephesians 4:5), and one holy Catholic Church—"the pillar and bulwark of the truth" (1 Timothy 3:15)—where the fullness of God's revealed truth is kept whole and handed on intact from one generation to the next. Where relativism leaves every man to author his own reality, Christ left us a Church in which to receive His.

### Marxism: "Love Is Class Solidarity"

**The Appeal:** Marxism recognizes injustice and promises solidarity with the oppressed.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | Marxism | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| Sacred | Distorted—the coming classless society is treated as sacrosanct and beyond question: a redemption promised by history itself rather than by God, with the oppressed class cast as its messiah. Envy of the oppressor is the fire kept burning beneath it | ✓ |
| Covenant | Distorted—solidarity binds comrades only around whichever "oppressor" the cause currently objectifies, reforming around a new enemy as the target shifts | ✓ |
| Truth | Distorted—ignores human nature, and so denies the dignity of the individual person; it relocates both the cause of evil and its cure to economic systems and class structures, ignoring that the individual heart is the only place evil actually originates and the only place it can be overcome | ✓ |
| Justice | Distorted—guilt and innocence belong to classes, not persons; there is no sin to confess and no one to forgive, so whatever serves the collective counts as good, whatever it costs the people it is done to | ✓ |
| Mercy | Distorted—mercy is extended freely within the oppressed class, but withheld entirely from "class enemies"; because a "class enemy" is a category, not a person, mercy has no way to reach the individual inside it | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | Distorted—true sacrifice is a person's free gift of self for another's good; Marxism inverts this into the involuntary sacrifice of individuals for the collective's good, because no individual life carries worth that the collective's needs cannot override | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | Distorted—what it calls communion is a claim on other men's goods; the bond it offers is not the union of hearts but a shared appetite for what someone else has—greed wearing solidarity's clothes | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** At its root, Marxism has no doctrine of the individual person's dignity. Once the individual is reduced to a class position, everything else follows: truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice all get redefined around the collective's needs instead of the person. Sin becomes something classes commit, not hearts—so there is no individual guilt to confess and no individual to forgive, only "class enemies" to defeat. Sacrifice stops being a person's free gift and becomes the involuntary cost paid by individuals for the collective's good. Even Marxism's instinct toward communion is real but inverted: it starts from the collective instead of the individual heart, where communion actually has to be built, one act of repentance and forgiveness at a time. It appeals to envy and self-righteousness rather than building authentic solidarity. And because it is not built on the truth of human nature, it cannot solve the very problems it identifies. Greed and oppression are real sins—but no system abolishes a sin. They are overcome only by conversion, one heart at a time, freely submitting itself to the laws of God's love.

**Why It Fails:** Tens of millions dead in the 20th century under Marxist regimes. Marxism creates resentment, not solidarity; violence, not peace; totalitarianism, not justice.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love establishes true justice through solidarity that recognizes every person's sacred dignity. It calls us to sacrificial love for all—including enemies—building communion through covenant rather than coercion—which is the only thing that actually answers the oppression Marxism rightly sees.

### Wokeism: "Love Is Affirming Identity"

**The Appeal:** Wokeism promises compassion for 'marginalized groups' and celebrates 'diversity'. It is Marxism recast for a modern audience: the same story of oppressor and oppressed, with race, sex, and identity standing in for economic class. The target has moved; the machinery has not—which is itself the proof that "the oppressor" is whoever the ideology needs to blame next in order to seize power, not a truth discovered once and applied consistently.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | Wokeism | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| Sacred | Distorted—group identity and victim status are treated as sacrosanct and beyond question, occupying the place only the individual soul's God-given dignity should hold | ✓ |
| Covenant | Distorted—coalition membership functions as a bond, but conditional on shared grievance and political alignment, not sacred or permanent | ✓ |
| Truth | Distorted—a claim is judged true or false by the group identity of who makes it, not by whether it corresponds to reality; "lived experience" of group identity overrides any truth that could be checked or shared | ✓ |
| Justice | Distorted—guilt and merit are assigned by group identity rather than individual action, so a person may be condemned or exonerated for the category he belongs to, not what he as an individual has done | ✓ |
| Mercy | Distorted—mercy is assigned by group identity, not individual repentance: victim-group members are absolved unconditionally, while oppressor-group members have no path back at all—cancel culture runs only one direction, with no mechanism for repentance to ever be received | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | Distorted—true sacrifice costs the self and transforms one's own heart; wokeism substitutes cheap, performative gestures (public confession of privilege, symbolic allyship) that cost little, while demanding real sacrifice from others based on their group identity | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | Distorted—coalition and belonging form around shared grievance and a common "oppressor," lasting only until a member of the coalition is named oppressor in turn | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** Wokeism makes a fatal category error: it treats groups as moral agents, elevating group identity and victim status to something sacrosanct, and treating a claim's truth as a matter of who is making it rather than whether it corresponds to reality. But groups cannot sin, and groups cannot repent. Only individual hearts oppress other hearts. Only individual hearts can seek forgiveness and reconcile.

Yes, individual sins ripple outward—one heart's distorted love can harm many and spread counterfeit loves across generations. Real injustice exists. Real victims suffer. But wokeism's solution—assigning collective guilt to "oppressor groups" and collective victimhood to "marginalized groups"—makes authentic justice impossible. Why? Because once you point the finger at a group, the only options become coercion, discrimination, or punishment of that group. Fighting "systemic injustice" becomes license for personal injustice. The oppressed become oppressors. The cycle continues.

Wokeism also inverts virtue itself. Instead of costly sacrifice that transforms one's own heart, it offers cheap virtue through grievance—virtue signaling in the place of actual virtue. Instead of the hard work of loving enemies, it offers the satisfaction of identifying them. Instead of examining the plank in one's own eye, it obsesses over the speck in the "oppressor's" eye. The result? Self-righteousness without righteousness. Moral superiority without moral transformation.

**Why It Fails:** Denying reality harms those it claims to help. Children are sterilized for ideology. Women lose spaces and sports to biological males. Disagreement becomes "violence." Truth becomes "hate speech." Compassion becomes complicity in self-destruction. And the promised liberation never arrives—because you cannot liberate hearts through coercion. You will only harden them—and a hardened heart, certain of its own grievance, is perfectly equipped to become the next oppressor.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love addresses the actual source of evil: the individual human heart. It speaks truth to each person—affirming their infinite dignity as image-bearers while calling them to conversion. It offers real mercy: not affirmation of sin, but forgiveness of sin and power to change. It demands real sacrifice: not blaming others, but taking responsibility for one's own heart first. "Why do you see the speck that is in your brother's eye, but do not notice the log that is in your own eye?" (Matthew 7:3).

This is why the Gospel transforms cultures while **ideologies only rearrange oppressors**. Christ doesn't redistribute guilt—He removes it. He doesn't create new victim classes—He heals all who come to Him. The Church has been the greatest force for justice in history precisely because it addresses hearts one at a time, inviting them to healing through covenant—never forcing it—rather than assigning collective guilt. "Rather, speaking the truth in love, we are to grow up in every way into him who is the head, into Christ" (Ephesians 4:15).

### Atheism: "There Is No God"

**The Appeal:** Atheism promises autonomy from transcendent authority and moral accountability.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | Atheism | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| Sacred | ✗—denies any transcendent source of being outside the material universe, so nothing has a nature or purpose grounded beyond itself | ✓ |
| Covenant | Partial—secular marriage, contracts, and the social contract function as real bonds, but are revocable human agreements with no sacred permanence | ✓ |
| Truth | Partial—can make and test empirical observations, but calling something "true" already imports a value judgment (that correspondence to reality deserves belief); atheism has no ground for that judgment once all value is self-referential | ✓ |
| Justice | Partial—simulates justice through social consensus or utility, but without a transcendent ground, value is self-referential (projected by each mind, not discovered), so conflicting valuations have no neutral standard to appeal to—only preference, or power | ✓ |
| Mercy | Partial—secular compassion and charity are real, but rest on a theorized (not demonstrated) evolutionary instinct or social utility, not forgiveness of moral guilt | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | ✗—on materialism's own terms, no account of true sacrifice is possible; every apparent one must be reduced to something else: self-fulfillment, reciprocal advantage, or the survival of one's genes. **What's Missing:** At root, atheism denies any transcendent source of being, so value becomes self-referential: each mind projects worth onto people and things rather than discovering it already there. This runs deeper than morality—even calling something "true" smuggles in a value judgment atheism has no ground for. Justice becomes competing preference or power, with no neutral standard to settle conflicts. Communion between people is real, but without shared truth to unite around, it's two minds running in parallel, not real union. Covenant survives only as a revocable human agreement—marriage vows and contracts either party may dissolve, not a sacred and permanent bond. Mercy survives as compassion, but resting on theorized evolutionary instinct or social utility, not the forgiveness of moral guilt. What atheism cannot supply is exactly what reason alone could never discover and only revelation gives: that every human being is made in the image and likeness of God, who Himself declared what He made "good" (Genesis 1:27, 31). Worth is not something a mind assigns—it is spoken over every person by the God who made them, before any human being weighs in. Atheism provides no answer for our deepest human longings for truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice.

**Why It Fails:** Atheism cannot ground the very realities it depends on. Why value truth, seek justice, show mercy, or sacrifice for others? Evolutionary psychology reduces love to survival mechanism. Utilitarianism makes it arbitrary calculation. And this isn't merely academic: once human worth is no longer grounded in the fact that God made and values each person, a society can redefine who counts—excluding some group as "other," "subhuman," or simply outside the circle of concern. The eugenics movements and the death camps of the twentieth century followed exactly this move: strip away the doctrine that all people are made in God's image, and human value becomes negotiable.

**Atheistic Frameworks That Deny Love as the Highest Good:**

Various atheistic philosophies propose alternative "highest goods," but each collapses: Nietzschean will-to-power confuses domination with greatness; utilitarianism reduces goodness to a sum of feelings—and can demand heroic sacrifice while never explaining why the demand binds anyone; nihilism rejects intrinsic human worth, obliterating the possibility of love or justice. Each fails because it denies essential components of God's Perfect Love.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love recognizes our need for truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice—and provides the only coherent explanation: because God IS love in His very nature, and we are made in His image. This is not a conclusion reason could reach on its own; it is revealed—God Himself declaring, at the dawn of creation, that the human person He made is "very good" (Genesis 1:31). Worth is not negotiated by us; it is spoken over us. Our deepest longings can only be fulfilled in Him. "God is love, and he who abides in love abides in God, and God abides in him" (1 John 4:16).

### Self-Actualization: "Be All You Can Be"

**The Appeal:** Self-actualization promises that the highest goal of life is to become the fullest, most authentic version of yourself — to discover your potential, follow your dreams, love yourself first, and live your best life. *"Be all you can be."* *"You can be anything you put your mind to."* *"Self-care is self-love."* It echoes a real and good intuition: that you are a soul of intrinsic worth, entrusted to your own stewardship, and meant to flourish—which is why it deserves a careful hearing before we weigh what it is missing.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | Self-Actualization | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Sacred | Distorted—"personal growth" and the self-defined "authentic self" are treated as sacrosanct and beyond question; the self has become love's destination rather than its steward | ✓ |
| Covenant | Distorted—marriage, parenthood, and vocation are held provisionally, kept only while they serve the self's "growth"; a self that terminates in itself is not obligated to bind itself to anything that would cost it future fulfillment | ✓ |
| Truth | Partial—assumes we are created for our own growth, success, or fulfillment rather than for love—to love and be loved—which is reached only through the selfless gift of ourselves | ✓ |
| Justice | Distorted—recast as the self's entitlement to its own fulfillment ("I don't owe anyone anything"); a self made for its own growth owes nothing beyond itself | ✓ |
| Mercy | Partial—turned inward as "self-compassion" and cut off the moment it would cost us outwardly; a self made for its own growth has no reason to spend mercy on another's need | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | Distorted—cost is rarely paid purely for another's good; an ulterior motive lingers—I'll pay this cost for you as long as it still serves my own growth | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | Distorted—this is our divorce culture: "till death do us part" quietly becomes "till you no longer meet my needs"; communion lasts only as long as it serves the self; a self made for its own fulfillment cannot give itself away unconditionally | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** At root, self-actualization mistakes what the self is for. Growth, success, and fulfillment are real goods — but they are not the final end; they exist to serve others, ordered outward the way love itself is ordered: first our covenant relationships — spouse, children, family — then the wider communities we belong to. We were made for love: to love and be loved, through the selfless gift of ourselves. Genuine love of self is **instrumental** — the soul is cared for as a charge entrusted by God so it can be poured out in love of God and neighbor. Self-actualization inverts this into **terminal** self-love: the self becomes love's destination rather than its steward, quietly occupying the place only God's image should hold (Sacred). Once that root error is in place, every outward thread comes untied. Covenant is kept only as long as it serves the self's growth (Covenant). Justice shrinks to "I don't owe anyone anything," because a self made for its own growth owes nothing beyond itself (Justice). Mercy turns inward as self-compassion, then stops the moment it would cost something outward (Mercy). Sacrifice survives only when it doubles as self-investment, rarely purely for another's good (Sacrifice). And the cost is real: the discipline, the boundaries, the hours spent "investing in yourself" are not nothing. What is missing is not effort but direction — every one of those costs is paid toward a self that has nowhere further to send it. And communion collapses into our divorce culture's quiet substitution — "till death do us part" becomes "till you no longer meet my needs" — rather than the covenant that still wills the other's good, even their sanctity, after they've sinned against it (Communion). A self that terminates in itself cannot enter communion with anyone.

**Why It Fails:** This counterfeit shows up everywhere precisely because it sounds like wisdom. Recruiters say *"Be all you can be."* Parents say *"You can be anything you put your mind to."* Therapists, influencers, and self-help books all teach the same liturgy: discover yourself, prioritize yourself, fulfill yourself. But the human person was never built to terminate in himself. Three failures follow inevitably:

It lies to children about reality. *"You can be anything you put your mind to"* is not encouragement; it is a metaphysical falsehood. We are not pure will. We are embodied creatures with given gifts, limits, vocations, and a Creator who has already willed us *for* something specific. When a child raised on this slogan collides with reality — a talent they don't have, a body they cannot redesign, a vocation they did not choose — the result is not resilience but despair, because the slogan offered the wrong measure of worth. Worth was never about *becoming anything*; it was always about *being the image of God you already are*.

It also dissolves covenant. A self that exists to fulfill itself cannot bind itself to anything that would cost it future fulfillment. Marriage, parenthood, vocation, parish, friendship — every covenant becomes provisional, kept only while it serves the self's "growth." *"I have to be true to myself"* becomes the universal solvent of every promise.

And it produces the loneliest culture in human history. A society of self-actualizers has no one left to actualize *for*. Anxiety, depression, and loneliness rise in lockstep with self-focus, because we were made for self-gift, not self-construction. The cup that pours itself for itself never fills. Saint Bernard warned his monks to be reservoirs and not canals—filled first, then overflowing, "without loss to itself." But he never imagined a cup that could fill itself. Ours is filled at the altar, from the cup of the new covenant, and it is filled for one reason: so that it can be poured out into the cups of others.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love recovers self-love as the *measure* of neighbor-love, not its replacement. *"You shall love your neighbor as yourself"* (Mark 12:31) presumes you already love yourself rightly — with the same attentive, ordered care a good parent gives a child entrusted to them. *"Life and physical health are precious gifts entrusted to us by God. We must take reasonable care of them..."* (CCC 2288). The body is a temple of the Holy Spirit (1 Cor 6:19); the soul bears His image. Both have been *placed* in your care — and the whole point of that care is that you might have a self capable of pouring itself out for God and neighbor in love. Christ did not say *"be all you can be"*; He said *"whoever loses his life for my sake will find it"* (Matthew 16:25). The self is found by being given, not by being built. *"It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me"* (Galatians 2:20). That is what authentic flourishing actually looks like — not a curated self displayed, but a soul transparent to Christ, poured through into a world that desperately needs Him.

**The Appeal:** Pornography promises sexual gratification without the cost of real relationship.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

**What's Missing:** At root, pornography idolizes private gratification, treating another person's body — and one's own — as an object to be consumed rather than a gift to be given and received within covenant. Once self-gratification becomes the end, every outward thread of Perfect Love is severed: the body's God-given dignity is displaced by pleasure on demand (Sacred); no bond is ever formed, because an image consumed is not a person given (Covenant); the other becomes a sensation to extract, not a whole person to know (Truth); "consenting adults" launders real coercion and harm, because the other's actual good was never the point (Justice); nothing is owed, so nothing is left for mercy to reach (Mercy); no cost is paid for another, because there is no other in view (Sacrifice); and self-gratification is the opposite of communion — you can't do communion alone (Communion). Habitual use devastates individuals, destroying their ability to love spouse and family through addiction and emotional detachment. **Why It Fails:** Addiction. Broken marriages. Emotional absence from children. Pornography promises intimacy but delivers isolation.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love calls spouses to total self-gift in covenant marriage where they know and are known completely—authentic intimacy that honors the sacred dignity of the person and builds life-giving communion. "Therefore a man leaves his father and his mother and cleaves to his wife, and they become one flesh" (Genesis 2:24).

### Abortion: "My Body, My Choice"

**The Appeal:** Abortion promises compassion for women in difficult circumstances and attempts to honor individual autonomy.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | Abortion | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|---------|-------------------|
| Sacred | Distorted—"bodily autonomy" is treated as absolute self-ownership over a life that was received as a gift, not chosen | ✓ |
| Covenant | ✗—the parent-child bond is not weakened but ended outright; a self claiming absolute autonomy leaves no one to bind to. The guilt and shame that follow leave the mother's own heart broken, making it harder for her to love the rest of her family as fully as before | ✓ |
| Truth | Distorted—personhood is redefined or denied ("just a clump of cells," not yet a person) precisely because autonomy can only remain absolute if the one it overrides is first defined out of existence as a person | ✓ |
| Justice | Distorted—"bodily autonomy" and "reproductive justice" are lies borrowing the language of rights: they recognize only the sovereign self's claim, erasing the child's prior and equal right to life and the father's right to protect and raise his own child | ✓ |
| Mercy | Distorted—"compassion" kills the child instead of helping her circumstances—and often leaves them worse—denying her the God-given capacity to rise to the occasion as her child's protector and advocate | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | Distorted—rather than voluntarily stepping up to whatever difficulty the circumstances present, the adult takes the easy way out and eliminates the one who would require the sacrifice—loving neither self, neighbor, nor God | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | ✗—the choice is rarely made against the child so much as without him in view at all, autonomy having crowded him out of sight; abortion is the ultimate anti-sacrament of communion—one person is dead, and the other cannot love again as she could before, apart from grace | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** At root, abortion idolizes personal autonomy — treating the self as owning its own existence absolutely, though no one chose to exist and every body ultimately belongs to God (Sacred). Once autonomy is treated as absolute, everything else follows. The child's personhood must be redefined or denied, because autonomy can only remain intact if the one it overrides is first defined out of existence (Truth). Rights language recognizes only the sovereign self's claim, erasing the child's prior and equal right to life and the father's right to his own child (Justice). "Compassion" kills the child rather than helping the mother's circumstances or forming her for the vocation of protecting and advocating for her child — a culture that had formed her for that vocation beforehand would have kept her from this crossroads in the first place (Mercy). Rather than stepping up to whatever the circumstances demand, the adult takes the easy way out and eliminates the one who would require the sacrifice (Sacrifice). The parent-child bond is not weakened but ended outright, and the guilt and shame that follow often leave the mother's own heart too broken to love the rest of her family as fully as before (Covenant). And because the child is rarely killed in malice so much as simply crowded out of view, abortion becomes the ultimate anti-sacrament of communion — one person dead, the other unable to love again as she could before, apart from grace (Communion). The debate focuses on adult autonomy while ignoring the child's death — the ultimate injustice.

**Why It Fails:** Over 60 million children dead in America since 1973.<sup>1</sup> Mothers wounded. An entire generation eliminated. The "solution" to crisis pregnancy is killing the child—the opposite of love—confirming the truth that "the wages of sin is death."

But the statistics hide the personal devastation. The woman who chose abortion in the name of her own autonomy did not gain freedom—she lost something irreplaceable inside herself. She sought to be the author of her own story, but the act of ending her child's life wrote a chapter she cannot unwrite. The grief that follows is unlike ordinary grief: it cannot be spoken easily, because to name the loss is to name what was lost—a person, her child. Many women carry this wound for decades in silence: the anniversary dates, the wondering, the dreams. Some clinicians call it post-abortion syndrome; the Church calls it what it is—a wound to the soul that touches the deepest place a human being can be wounded, the place where love and life and communion meet.

This is the bitter irony of counterfeit autonomy: in the act of asserting absolute control over her own body, she exercised absolute and irreversible power over another person's existence—and in doing so, destroyed a part of herself. The self-determination she sought became the instrument of her own interior destruction. She did not become more free. She became bound—to guilt, to grief, to a secret that reshapes every relationship that follows.

No therapist, no political movement, no amount of cultural affirmation can reach this wound. "No Regrets" is a slogan, not a healing. Only One who is both perfectly just and perfectly merciful can forgive what no human court can try and heal what no human hand can reach. Jesus Christ, who said "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28), is the only answer to this particular darkness. His mercy is not cheap—it does not pretend the child did not exist—but it is real, and it is offered. The Church's *Project Rachel* ministry exists precisely because the wound is real and the healing is possible, but only through Him.

It is worth pausing to recognize what this reveals about the nature of sin itself. Abortion may be the most diagnostically complete sin in human experience: it violates every component of Perfect Love simultaneously—breaking the First Commandment (placing self above God as lord of life and death) and the Tenth (coveting the unburdened life of others) in the same act. It also embodies all seven deadly sins: the pride that claims sovereignty over life, the lust that treated a person as an object, the sloth that refuses the sacrifice of parenthood, the greed that hoards an unencumbered future, the envy of others' freedom, the gluttony of self-comfort, and the wrath turned against the most innocent. This is why Catholics intuitively feel the full weight of this sin in a way that secular culture cannot explain—we are formed by the very framework that abortion violates completely. No other single act so thoroughly maps onto the full anatomy of sin, which is precisely why no merely human remedy can reach it, and why only the One who conquered sin itself can heal it.

This is precisely why our calling is not only to oppose this evil but to lead every person we meet — the woman wounded by an abortion, and the one still tempted toward one — into the fullness of God's truth and the holy communion only He offers: forgiveness and healing for the first, and the strength to sacrifice comfort for life in the second. Whoever fights for the unborn must fight with God's Perfect Love held always in view for the person in front of them, not the cause alone.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love recognizes every human life as sacred gift from conception, calling parents to welcome and protect life through sacrifice—even when costly or inconvenient. "Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, and before you were born I consecrated you; I appointed you a prophet to the nations" (Jeremiah 1:5). Authentic love is ordered to life and builds communion across generations, providing support for mothers in crisis rather than eliminating their children.

### Same-Sex "Marriage": "Love Is Love"

**The Appeal:** Same-sex "marriage" promises equal access to marriage and the recognition of a family.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | Same-Sex "Marriage" | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Sacred | Distorted—romantic and emotional bonding alone is treated as sacred and sufficient to make a union | ✓ |
| Covenant | Distorted—commitment is detached from the complementary union that covenant, by nature, is ordered to form | ✓ |
| Truth | Distorted—claims emotional bonding alone determines what a union is, denying the biological fact that the marital act, and the organs themselves, are ordered by design to beget children — a truth about what the union *is*, not about which couples happen to be fertile | ✓ |
| Justice | Distorted—a union missing either a father or a mother loses the complementary structure meant to secure justice alongside mercy for the spouses, the children, and society alike | ✓ |
| Mercy | Partial—mercy is detached from the complementary structure that would order it, alongside justice, toward the good of spouses, children, and society | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | Partial—sacrifice is not ordered to life; without the complementary union open to procreation, it serves the couple's bond rather than the self-gift that begets and raises the next generation | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | Distorted—rooted in bonding alone rather than the complementary gift of husband and wife, it is structurally closed to the procreative communion marriage is ordered toward | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** At root, this counterfeit idolizes emotional bonding — treating the feeling of love as sacred and sufficient in itself to make a union, apart from the complementary, sacrificial covenant that actually founds a family (Sacred). Truth is denied, since the marital act and the organs themselves are ordered by biological design to beget children, not to feeling (Truth). Covenant survives as real commitment, but detached from what it exists to found (Covenant). Justice and mercy come apart wherever either a father or a mother is missing, since neither parent can supply what only the other's complementary presence secures (Justice, Mercy). Sacrifice may be real between the partners, but it is not ordered to life, since it cannot open to the self-gift that begets and raises the next generation (Sacrifice). And communion, however emotionally real, is structurally closed to the procreative communion marriage is ordered toward (Communion).

**Why It Fails:** Marriage is not a loose arrangement between two people who "love" each other—it is a covenantal, familial relationship with God Himself, entered into by a man and a woman whose complementary bodies are the only union capable of, and ordered toward, producing the family that covenant requires. This is not a restriction imposed on marriage from outside; it is what marriage *is*. A union that is by nature closed to producing family (a union closed by its nature, not a fertile couple who happen to be infertile) is not a different kind of marriage—it is not marriage at all, any more than a triangle with four sides is a different kind of triangle. Redefining marriage to mean something other than the covenantal union of man, woman, and God open to new life does not expand marriage; it dissolves it. Children need both mother and father, yet we've made marriage about adult satisfaction rather than the next generation's flourishing—and in doing so, we have not liberated love but abandoned it.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love establishes marriage as the complementary union of man and woman whose bodies are designed for one-flesh communion naturally ordered to creating and nurturing new life. This alone images the Trinity's life-giving love. "Male and female he created them. And God blessed them, and God said to them, 'Be fruitful and multiply'" (Genesis 1:27-28).

## Section 3: Religious Counterfeits

### "Faith Alone": Belief Without Works

**The Appeal:** "Faith alone" promises salvation without the cost of sacrificial obedience.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | "Faith Alone" | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Sacred | ✓ | ✓ |
| Covenant | Partial—commitment to God is oriented toward securing salvation rather than the covenant of love salvation is meant to serve | ✓ |
| Truth | Partial—narrows saving truth to whatever minimal beliefs secure salvation, often leaving out love, truth, and Jesus Himself as the cornerstone, and missing that we are made by love and for love — capable, in Him, of a whole and perfected love, not merely justified for justification's own sake | ✓ |
| Justice | Distorted—because salvation, not love, is the good being sought, acting justly toward others is left optional once salvation is secured | ✓ |
| Mercy | Partial—receives God's mercy as the salvation being sought, without recognizing that mercy received is required to become mercy given | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | Partial—costly obedience is not required once salvation is secured, although all who have come in contact with Jesus do try to live this way | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | Distorted—treats salvation as the finish line, when communion with Christ — not salvation as a possession — is the actual goal salvation exists to begin; this is exactly why James says faith without works is dead and bears no fruit | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** At root, this counterfeit, in its extreme form, idolizes salvation itself — placing the good of being saved above the Love of God that salvation exists to serve. Commitment to God becomes about securing that good rather than an ongoing covenant of love (Covenant). Saving truth narrows to whatever minimal beliefs secure salvation, often without love among them (Truth). Because the good sought was salvation, not love, acting justly toward others is left optional once salvation is secured — which is exactly why the Church insists faith and love can never be separated, and why devoted Christians do live justly despite what the doctrine technically permits (Justice). Mercy is received as the very salvation sought, without recognizing that mercy received is meant to become mercy given (Mercy). Costly obedience is not required once salvation is secured, though true love of Christ produces sacrifice anyway, which is why most adherents live out the very obligation their doctrine denies (Sacrifice). And it treats salvation as the finish line, when communion with Christ — not salvation as a possession — is the actual goal salvation exists to begin, which is exactly why James says faith without works is dead and bears no fruit (Communion).

**Why It Fails:** Scripture explicitly contradicts it: "You see that a man is justified by works and not by faith alone" (James 2:24). Faith that doesn't transform the whole person and bear fruit in acts of truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice is not united with the God who is perfect truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice.

**A Note on Charitable Reading:** Some Christians who speak of being saved by "faith alone" do not mean a faith stripped of works; they readily affirm that saving faith will bear fruit in love. But if there are no good works — no love — has such a person actually become a new creation in Christ? The counterfeit addressed here is the more extreme claim that separates faith entirely from its fruits—a claim that most who use the phrase would themselves reject.

**The Larger Context:** Grace is God's Perfect Love poured into our hearts. Christ's saving work is the perfect integration of truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice displayed on the Cross. Justification exists within this larger reality—God's grace to justify is not a time-stamped event but an aspect of His Perfect Love that begins with Him as Author of Love and ends with Him as our eternal Communion.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love works through "For in Christ Jesus neither circumcision nor uncircumcision is of any avail, but faith working through love" (Galatians 5:6). Authentic faith transforms the whole person and bears fruit in acts of truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice, because love is not just believed but lived. "Not every one who says to me, 'Lord, Lord,' shall enter the kingdom of heaven, but he who does the will of my Father who is in heaven" (Matthew 7:21).

### "Bible Alone": Private Interpretation

**The Appeal:** "Bible alone" promises direct access to the truth of God's Word without institutional mediation.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | "Bible Alone" | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|--------------|-------------------|
| Sacred | Distorted—private judgment is placed above the interpretive authority Christ entrusted to His Church, so submission to God's authority is never completely made; and since God's authority is His love, incomplete submission to it is incomplete submission to His love | ✓ |
| Covenant | Distorted—because each reader is his own final interpretive authority, no teaching authority (denomination, pastor, teacher) can bind him; his own covenant with God stays incomplete, since he has not entrusted his understanding of it to the Church God established to guard it, and his covenant with fellow believers fractures wherever their readings diverge | ✓ |
| Truth | Distorted—private judgment stands in for the interpretive authority Christ entrusted to His Church, which taught and unified believers through a standardized liturgy long before there was a codified Bible to argue over, and still forms the faithful through it today, so "what I think it means" displaces what the Church's Tradition and liturgy actually teach | ✓ |
| Justice | Partial—private judgment leaves justice's definition to vary by reader rather than rest on settled authority; this is itself an injustice to those outside the faith, who are owed a clear, unified witness to God's justice, not competing versions | ✓ |
| Mercy | Partial—recognizes mercy but has no authority to mediate it. Jesus gave this authority to the Disciples (see John 20:23 and 2 Corinthians 5:18) | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | Partial—personal sacrifice, but doesn't call the individual to complete submission to Jesus and the Church He started | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | Distorted—real fellowship exists within each denomination or congregation, but fractures at every boundary between them, with no visible unity across the whole Body of Christ | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** "Bible alone" makes each person their own interpretive authority, destroying truth (contradictory interpretations), covenant (no binding Church authority), and communion (endless division). This division provides damaging witness to the world—dividing Christ himself and bolstering postmodernist claims that there is no objective truth.

**Why It Fails:** Thousands of Protestant denominations, each claiming biblical authority. Scripture spans thousands of years, multiple languages, diverse cultures, and complex theological connections—expecting each individual to achieve interpretive certainty without authoritative guidance places an impossible burden on private judgment. The result: contradictory interpretations, doctrinal chaos, and endless division with no mechanism to resolve disputes.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** Complete submission to God's Perfect Love transmits truth through Scripture, Sacred Tradition, and the Magisterium (the teaching authority given by Jesus to teach all nations everything He commanded) working together—this three-legged stool ensures faithful interpretation, maintains covenant unity under apostolic authority, and preserves communion across time and cultures. "I hope to come to you soon, but I am writing these instructions to you so that, if I am delayed, you may know how one ought to behave in the household of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and bulwark of the truth" (1 Timothy 3:14-15).

### "Once Saved, Always Saved": Presumption

**The Appeal:** "Once saved, always saved" promises eternal security regardless of subsequent sin.

**Diagnostic Analysis:**

| Facet | "Once Saved, Always Saved" | God's Perfect Love |
|-----------|---------------------------|-------------------|
| Sacred | Partial—adherents claim it is backed by Scripture, but ignore much Scripture to the contrary, and ignore God's Perfect Love as the primary hermeneutical lens for reading all of Scripture | ✓ |
| Covenant | Distorted—leads people to a merely "personal salvation" without any demand, never drawing them into the family, parish, or societal covenant that should follow | ✓ |
| Truth | Partial—recognizes salvation but denies ongoing accountability | ✓ |
| Justice | ✗—justice is sidelined as optional | ✓ |
| Mercy | Distorted—heavily weights the mercy of Jesus, but mercy without justice is not true mercy | ✓ |
| Sacrifice | ✗—demands no sacrifice, just "rest" in Jesus, but there is no rest, no peace, without truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice leading to communion with God and others | ✓ |
| Life-giving Communion | Distorted—no ongoing transformation or growth: if there is no communion, no love, what were you saved from? | ✓ |

**What's Missing:** "Once saved, always saved" presumes eternal security regardless of sin (no justice, and no mercy for the offender or the offended), destroying sacrifice (no obligation to ongoing discipleship), covenant (no perseverance), and transformation (no continued conversion). Christ came to free us from sin's slavery, but ignoring sin means we remain enslaved.

**Why It Fails:** Contradicts hundreds of places in the New Testament—the Gospels and all the Epistles—that direct us to turn away from sin and turn toward God's perfect love. "Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, so now, not only as in my presence but much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling" (Philippians 2:12). "If we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us" (1 John 1:8). The doctrine also traps itself in a circle: when someone who once professed faith abandons it entirely, adherents often explain it away by saying "they were never really saved to begin with"—which quietly reintroduces perseverance as the very proof of salvation the doctrine claims isn't required. This is not merely a theoretical danger — it enables moral complacency and presumption in those who are not deep in the faith, a pattern seen again and again in pastoral experience.

**How Good Overcomes Evil:** God's Perfect Love calls us to persevere in grace—to persevere in our relationship with God and others by doing His truth, justice, mercy and sacrifice—through ongoing conversion and cooperation with the Holy Spirit until death. Authentic love requires faithful covenant living, costly sacrifice, and continuous transformation. "You, therefore, must be perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect" (Matthew 5:48).

## The Pattern Revealed

<div class="callout" style="background-color: #073642; padding: 26px 34px; margin: 34px 0; border-radius: 14px; font-size: 18px; line-height: 1.45; color: #2aa198; border-top: 4px solid #2aa198; border-bottom: 4px solid #2aa198; box-shadow: 0 0 18px rgba(42, 161, 152, 0.12);">
Every counterfeit is identifiable by what it's missing. Partial love does not merely fall short of love; it curdles into the very thing it claims to oppose. Only God's Perfect Love has all seven components working together, which is why only it can answer the problem of evil completely.
</div>

**Every counterfeit eliminates or distorts essential components** of God's Perfect Love, producing exactly the harm it set out to defeat.

**Ideological counterfeits** eliminate God as source, reducing love to preference or power.

**Relational counterfeits** destroy covenant and communion, treating relationships as means to gratification rather than commitments ordered to life.

**Religious counterfeits** fragment what God joined—separating faith from works, Scripture from Tradition, conversion from transformation.

Each counterfeit promises good things but delivers the opposite: slavery, harm, division, insecurity. **Incomplete love cannot answer the problem of evil because incomplete love IS evil.**

The diagnostic tables reveal why our definition matters. It's not arbitrary. It's not one option among many. It's the complete picture of what love actually is—and anything less causes predictable harm.

The counterfeits we've examined aren't just theoretical errors. Each creates real suffering—especially for children, as the next chapter will demonstrate. When adults embrace counterfeit love, children pay the price. The ultimate test of any "love" is simple: **Does it protect and nurture the next generation?**

Twelve counterfeits. Twelve failures of this test. Only God's Perfect Love passes—because only it has everything children need to flourish.
