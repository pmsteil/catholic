## R1. Modern slavery and forced labor

**Claim in the draft.** The first draft said "about fifty million people worldwide are enslaved today."

**Finding.** The ILO, Walk Free and IOM estimate 49.6 million people lived in modern slavery on a given day in 2021. Of these, 27.6 million were in forced labor and 22 million were in forced marriage. Of the 27.6 million in forced labor: 17.3 million were in the private economy (manufacturing, construction, services, agriculture), 6.3 million in forced commercial sexual exploitation, and 3.9 million in state-imposed forced labor. About one person in 150 is affected. Women and girls are 54% of victims. Children are 15%. The total rose by about 10 million from 2016, mostly from forced marriage.

**Decision (2026-10-31).** Do not write "enslaved" for 49.6 million, because 22 million of it is forced marriage. Use "about 28 million people are trapped in forced labor, and more than 6 million of them in forced sexual exploitation."

**Sources.**
- [Global estimates of modern slavery, Global Slavery Index](https://www.globalslaveryindex.org/projects/global-estimates-of-modern-slavery/) (secondary summary)
- [Down To Earth summary of the 2022 report](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/governance/there-are-50-million-people-in-situations-of-modern-slavery-on-any-given-day-report-84907) (secondary)
- [ILO report PDF at Alliance 8.7](https://alliance87.org/sites/default/files/2023-06/Global%20Estimates%20of%20Modern%20Slavery%20Forced%20Labour%20and%20Forced%20Marriage.pdf) (primary, not opened)

**Open.** Open the primary PDF to confirm the 6.3 million figure.

## R4. Canada assisted death (MAID)

**Finding.** Health Canada's fifth annual report, released December 2024, covers 2023. It records 19,660 requests. 15,343 people received MAID. That is about 4.7% of all deaths, nearly one in twenty. This is 15.8% above 2022, and the rise is slowing. About 96% of cases were people whose natural death was reasonably foreseeable.

**Law (checked 2026-10-31).** Canada legalized MAID in 2016 for people near death. Bill C-7 received Royal Assent on 17 March 2021. It removed the requirement that natural death be reasonably foreseeable and created two tracks of safeguards. A person whose only medical condition is a mental illness is not eligible. Source: [Library of Parliament, Legislative Summary of Bill C-7](https://lop.parl.ca/sites/PublicWebsite/default/en_CA/ResearchPublications/LegislativeSummaries/432C7E) (primary; seen in search excerpts only).

**Decision (2026-10-31).** Write "By 2023 it was about one death in twenty, and the law has been widened to people whose natural death is not reasonably foreseeable."

**Sources.**
- [Health Canada, Fifth Annual Report on MAID, 2023](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/publications/health-system-services/annual-report-medical-assistance-dying-2023) (primary, not read in full)

## R6. No-fault divorce and the Reagan remark

**Finding.** California became the first state to adopt no-fault divorce, in 1969. Governor Ronald Reagan signed the Family Law Act. Sources give 4 or 5 September 1969. The Uniform Law Commission had begun a model statute in 1967. Sources do not agree on the speed of adoption: one says 45 states within five years, another says all states offered some form by 1985. New York was the last state, in 2010.

**Reagan remark.** Michael Reagan wrote in *Twice Adopted* that his father said, late in life, that signing the law was one of the worst mistakes he made in office. The Washington Times (22 September 2009) and Catholic Exchange repeat it. The Daily Signal paraphrases it. No primary recording or document was found. Context: Reagan's own divorce from Jane Wyman, and the bill's author, Assemblyman James Hayes, was in a bitter divorce.

**Decision (2026-10-31).** Keep the remark with the wording "is reported by his son Michael." Name the book in a footnote.

**Sources.**
- [The Washington Times, 22 Sept 2009](https://m.washingtontimes.com/news/2009/sep/22/no-fault-law-marks-its-40th/) (secondary)

## R7. Jamel Dunn

**Finding.** Jamel Dunn drowned in a retention pond in Cocoa, Florida, in July 2017 (most reports say 9 July). Teenagers filmed him, laughed and mocked him. None called 911. His sister, Simone Scott, said he was disabled and walked with a cane. A family friend found the video on social media and gave it to the police. His body was recovered on 14 July.

**Legal outcome (checked 2026-10-31).** Cocoa Police first said no law was broken. Later the chief said a misdemeanor charge of failure to report a death under Florida Statute 406.12 "could be applicable." On 22 June 2018 State Attorney Phil Archer declined to charge four juveniles and one adult. The Associated Press reported his reason: no Florida law requires a person to give emergency aid in this case. A bill to create such a duty was proposed in 2018 and failed. The chief called the conduct "utterly inhumane and cruel."

**Age.** Sources disagree. July 2017 reports say 32. CBS Miami (July 2017) and the Associated Press (June 2018) say 31. Do not state his age.

**Teenagers.** Reports give ages 14 to 16 or 14 to 18, and four or five teenagers. Do not state a number.

**Decision (2026-10-31).** Write "a disabled man." Say the State Attorney declined to charge anyone because no Florida law required them to help. Do not write "Police called their conduct inhumane but not illegal", because the police position changed.

**Sources.**
- [Associated Press via Fox 4, 22 June 2018](https://www.fox4news.com/news/no-charges-for-teens-who-taunted-drowning-disabled-man) (secondary; read in full)

## R9. The Ulma family

**Finding.** Józef and Wiktoria Ulma, farmers in Markowa, Poland, hid Jews. The Germans killed them on 24 March 1944, with their six children and the child Wiktoria was carrying. Counting the unborn child, the family was beatified on 10 September 2023 as nine people. The first draft's "all seven of their children, including the baby" matches this count. Eight Jews were hidden. Sources name the Didner, Grünfeld and Goldman families, and one memorial account names the Szall family. Yad Vashem recognized the Ulmas as Righteous Among the Nations in 1995. The Vatican treated the unborn child as a martyr, "baptized in the martyred mother's blood." One source says a Polish policeman, Włodzimierz Leś, denounced them.

**Sources.**

## R12. Mother Teresa and her critics

**What is contested.** Critics argue about the quality of care in her hospices, the handling of donations, her political contacts and her view of suffering.

**Finding.**
- Christopher Hitchens made a 1994 documentary and wrote against her. Aroup Chatterjee, a physician, published a 2003 book and worked on the documentary.
- In 2013 Serge Larivée, Geneviève Chénard and Carole Sénéchal published a review in *Studies in Religion*. It questioned her "rather dubious way of caring for the sick," her political contacts and her handling of money. It leans heavily on Hitchens. It includes claims about reused needles.
- Defenders (the Catholic League, First Things) answer that Hitchens's book has no footnotes, that his film interviewed no people in her care, and that the 2013 authors did not visit Calcutta. These rebuttals are advocacy.
- A blog says the editor of *The Lancet* visited the Kolkata hospice in 1994 and called the care "haphazard." This is unverified.

**Why it matters for Premise 2.** Premise 2 needs a saint whom believers and unbelievers judge the same way. Skeptics dispute Teresa, so she weakens the test.

**Decision (2026-10-31).** Keep her. The author chose Mother Teresa over St. Damien (R13). The dispute above stays on record.

**Sources.**
- [First Things, Mother Teresa and Her Critics](https://firstthings.com/mother-teresa-and-her-critics/) (secondary, partisan)
- [Catholic League response](https://www.catholicleague.org/?p=41213) (secondary, partisan)
- [Why Evolution Is True, 2013 post](https://whyevolutionistrue.com/2013/03/05/a-new-expose-on-mother-teresa-shows-that-she-and-the-vatican-were-even-worse-than-we-thought/) (secondary, partisan)

**Open.** Because the book keeps her, read the 2013 paper and the 1994 *Lancet* correspondence.

## R17. Birth rates and "demographic winter"

**Finding.**
- A 2024 *Lancet* study (IHME Global Burden of Disease) projects that 155 of 204 countries (76%) will be below replacement fertility by 2050 and 198 (97%) by 2100. It projects global fertility of 1.83 in 2050.
- United Nations, *World Population Prospects 2024*: 131 of 237 countries and areas (55%) are below replacement fertility (2.1), home to 68% of the world's people. Checked 2026-10-31 in secondary summaries of the UN release; the UN data portal was not opened.

**Decision (2026-10-31).** Write "the 'demographic winter' Pope Francis has warned of", not "the Popes." "Below replacement across most of the developed world" is supported by the OECD and UN figures above. Cite the OECD and the UN in the footnote, not the *Lancet* projection.

## R18. China's one-child policy

**Finding.**

## R19. Romanian orphanages (optional dignity paragraph)

**Finding.** After the fall of the Ceaușescu regime in 1989, researchers found children in state institutions. The Bucharest Early Intervention Project (Nelson, Fox, Zeanah) recruited 136 abandoned infants from six Bucharest institutions. It is the only randomized trial of institutional care.
